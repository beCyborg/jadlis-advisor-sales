# advisor-voss — core

The method's components, ranked by how strongly each is supported, strongest first. The unit of ranking is the evidence ladder: `field-study` → `external-research` → `practitioner-n-many` → `practitioner-anecdote` → `assertion`.

Read this file when the question is *how much weight does this claim carry*, not *what do I say next* (that is moves.md).

**Headline calibration:** the composed system has no tier-1 support. Nothing in this tradition rests on a `field-study` of the method. The named research it cites supports isolated psychological components — labeling, anchoring, placebic reasons, range offers, anger — never the assembly, and never the transfer from crisis rooms to commercial deals. Every "this works" statement in the source is either firm practice or a retold case, told by its protagonist.

---

## Tier 0 — `field-study`: empty

No controlled test of tactical empathy as a system, and no test of its transfer to B2B selling. Treat any council claim of the form "Voss proves X about deals" as unsupported at this tier by construction. The two strongest-sounding numbers in the book — a 23% lift from a No-oriented fundraising script and roughly 90% success on disputed-invoice sequences — are single self-reports with no denominators (see myths-and-demotions.md).

## Tier 1 — `external-research`: components with named outside support

These are the load-bearing parts. Note carefully: the research supports the *mechanism*, and the author supplies the *application*. The application is one ladder rung lower in every case.

1. **Naming an emotion reduces its grip.** The core mechanism under [VOSS:LABEL], and derivatively under [VOSS:UNDERLYING], [VOSS:NEGFIRST], [VOSS:FOLLOWLABEL] and [VOSS:LEVNEG]. Support: affect-labeling imaging work (Lieberman et al., 2007), cited in the book's own notes. This is the single best-supported idea in the lens — the *why* is externally grounded, the *sentence stems* are not.
2. **Loss aversion and the certainty effect make value positional.** Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) is the stated engine under [VOSS:ACCANCHOR], [VOSS:GHOSTMAIL], [VOSS:LEVNEG] and the general preference for framing an ask as avoiding a loss. The theory is robust; the specific sales choreography built on it is not tested.
3. **A reason, even a thin one, raises compliance on small requests.** [VOSS:BECAUSE] rests on Langer, Blank & Chanowitz (1978): roughly 60% compliance with no reason against over 90% with one, including a meaningless one. The strict boundary matters — the effect came from trivial requests, so extending it to high-stakes asks is the author's, not the research's.
4. **Range offers outperform point offers in compensation talk.** [VOSS:RANGE] cites Ames & Mason (2015). The author reports rather than reproduces it, and the "expect the low end" caveat is his practical overlay.
5. **Anger has asymmetric, mostly bad effects.** [VOSS:UMBRAGE] cites Sinaceur & Tiedens (2006) on extracting concessions through anger and Ames & Wazlawek (2014) on people who provoke umbrage rating themselves as over-assertive. The doctrine that *faked* anger backfires and that anger-won concessions fail in implementation is stated with these citations behind it.
6. **Linguistic markers of overselling.** [VOSS:PINOCCHIO] uses deception-marker work credited to Van Swol, Braun & Malhotra (2012). The marker is external; treating it as a live authority-and-budget test in a sales call is the author's.
7. **Hiding a deadline raises impasse risk.** [VOSS:DEADOPEN] leans on cited work by Don A. Moore (Berkeley), reported second-hand, and is explicitly positioned against the classic advice to conceal your clock.
8. **Attention is narrow.** The ~7-item conscious-processing figure (Miller, 1956) is the stated reason a single listener misses material — the justification under [VOSS:LISTENTEAM] and the review-it-twice half of [VOSS:RELIGION].

**Demoted within this tier:** the 7/38/55 words/tone/body split. It comes from Mehrabian's studies of *liking*, and the author concedes they were not negotiation research. Use [VOSS:CONGRUE] as an attention heuristic — watch for a mismatch — and never as a ratio. Any claim that quantifies how much of a message is nonverbal is refuted for this lens's purposes.

## Tier 2 — `practitioner-n-many`: repeated field and client use, no measurement

This is the book's own ceiling and where most of the method lives. These components were run many times by the author's unit and firm, across many counterparts, and survived. That is real evidence of *usability under pressure*; it is not evidence of *effect size*, and it carries hostage-room selection bias.

- **Refusal restores autonomy; a refusal is safer to elicit than agreement.** [VOSS:NOPERMIT], [VOSS:NODECODE], [VOSS:NOSETUP], [VOSS:ASKNOT], [VOSS:BADTIME], [VOSS:NOSERIES]. Credited upstream to Jim Camp's right-to-veto. Repeatedly used, never tested; note the whole family is one school, so agreement between Camp and Voss is echo, not confirmation.
- **The target state is felt understanding, not agreement.** The "that's right" doctrine, the Behavioral Change Stairway (active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → change) rooted in Rogers, and its instruments [VOSS:ALSTACK], [VOSS:SUMTRIG], [VOSS:MIRROR]. Mirroring is FBI-wide practice; the tipping figure attributed to Wiseman in the book has no verifiable reference and should not be cited.
- **Calibrated what/how questions convert demands into requests for help.** [VOSS:CALIBQ], [VOSS:STANDBYQ], [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED], [VOSS:IMPLQ]. Described as the unit's core tool after 2002. Its theoretical backing is borrowed from Dutton's *Split-Second Persuasion* — remove unbelief rather than install belief — which the author endorses rather than tests.
- **Verbal agreement is unreliable evidence.** [VOSS:YESTRIAGE] (counterfeit / confirmation / commitment), [VOSS:RIGHTALARM], [VOSS:RULE3]. The taxonomy is the author's own and the "hard to lie three times" rationale is asserted; but the underlying behavioural test — demand a next action, not a word — is sound practice independent of the theory.
- **Preparation format over improvisation.** [VOSS:ONESHEET], [VOSS:GOALHIGH], [VOSS:SUMPREP], [VOSS:LABELFILL], [VOSS:NONCASH-PREP], [VOSS:HYPOTH]. Firm-standard client exercise. The attached 7:1 payback claim is vendor self-report and is not part of the evidence.
- **Counterpart typing.** [VOSS:TYPEID] / [VOSS:PLAYTYPE] — Analyst / Accommodator / Assertive, plus the "I am normal" projection error and the arithmetic that roughly two thirds of counterparts differ from your own type. Consolidated by the firm from archetype literature; no validation study is offered, and the arithmetic is not evidence of predictive validity.
- **The precise-number effect.** [VOSS:ODDNUM], and the terminal rung of [VOSS:ACKERMAN]. Asserted from repeated use; the book openly concedes the effect is irrational and uses it anyway.
- **Ackerman laddering.** [VOSS:ACKERMAN], [VOSS:NOSERIES], [VOSS:NAMEFIRST]. Heavy repeated use in Haitian kidnapping cases plus classroom replication. The Raiffa endorsement is second-hand and anecdotal. This is the strongest-practised component with the narrowest valid domain: single-issue, locked-in counterpart, your clock cheaper than theirs.
- **Leverage taxonomy.** [VOSS:LEVPOS] / [VOSS:LEVNEG] / [VOSS:LEVNORM]. Positive (what they want), negative (what they fear losing), normative (their own stated rules). Practised, unmeasured; the normative branch is the most transferable to B2B because it does not depend on live paraverbal signal.
- **Behind-the-table reality.** [VOSS:BTT] as a doctrine — assume a team stands behind your counterpart. The doctrine is n-many; its two supporting stories are anecdotes, and the whole treatment of committees is thin (see myths-and-demotions.md).
- **Non-neediness.** [VOSS:WALKAWAY], [VOSS:NOSPLIT]. Repeated across cases, argued by analogy (one black shoe and one brown), never measured.

## Tier 3 — `practitioner-anecdote`: one retold case, usually by its protagonist

Treat these as existence proofs — the move can work — never as base rates. Survivorship is heavy: nearly every story is a *resolved* case, and where failures appear they are used to reinforce doctrine rather than bound it.

- [VOSS:AUDIT] and [VOSS:ACCANCHOR] — the accusation audit. Its single strongest case (contractors accepting a 75% rate cut without a counteroffer) is exactly the shape of outcome that should invite scepticism, not adoption.
- [VOSS:MISLABEL] — deliberate wrong labels. One property case. Manipulation-adjacent by the author's own framing.
- [VOSS:PRONOUN] — authority read from pronoun ratio. Inferred retrospectively from two hostage cases; the author concedes the pattern-fitting.
- [VOSS:DJVOICE], [VOSS:PLAYFUL] — the voice register doctrine. Anecdote plus an unsourced claim about positive mood and thinking speed.
- [VOSS:RELIGION], [VOSS:SIMILARITY] — worldview reconstruction and in-group vocabulary. One standoff, one CEO engagement; the cited "research by social scientists" on in-group trust is unnamed.
- [VOSS:SWAPIN], [VOSS:DISAGREE4], [VOSS:APOLWARM], [VOSS:NOSETUP], [VOSS:STATUSTRADE], [VOSS:LABELASK], [VOSS:SALPERSIST], [VOSS:RECAPOPEN], [VOSS:THIRDPRICE], [VOSS:FAIRDEF], [VOSS:FAIRMIRROR] — one case each.
- [VOSS:BITETONGUE] — supported by a *failure* anecdote, which is why it belongs here despite being the precondition for everything else: emotional regulation gates the entire toolkit, and the book's own case shows the toolkit collapsing without it.

## Tier 4 — `assertion`: stated, illustrated at best, never tested

- [VOSS:ANTIBATNA] — that rehearsing the walk-away silently caps your ask. Argued from a general claim about attention under stress; no source anywhere in the appendix. This is a genuinely useful reframe and a genuinely unsupported one.
- [VOSS:NOSCRIPT] — sequence by situation, not by playbook. Presented as the lesson of experience, with nothing behind it, and in direct tension with any council lens selling a repeatable process.
- [VOSS:HOPES] — speaking to unattained goals. Illustrated only by public rhetoric, with no negotiation outcome attached.
- **~3 Black Swans per side** ([VOSS:BS3]) — explicitly the author's hypothesis. The *habit* of hunting unknown unknowns survives; the number does not.
- **"Why" is accusatory in every language** ([VOSS:WHYFLIP]) — a universal claim from field impression. Cross-cultural reads are the lens's least reliable surface.
- **Detection is assumed away** — the reassurance that the counterpart will never notice the technique is asserted, never demonstrated. Given that this is the best-selling title in its category and the appendix's stems circulate widely, the assertion is not merely unsupported but likely false, and it degrades over the life of a relationship rather than within a single call.

## Structural claims that hold up better than the tactics

Three ideas survive the demotions above and are what this lens should actually contribute to a council verdict:

1. **System 1 first.** Shape how the counterpart feels about the offer and their stated reasoning follows. This is the mechanism claim, and it is the reason the lens beats every other layer on de-escalation, silence discipline and extracting what a rational-actor frame never surfaces.
2. **Emotion is not separable from the problem.** The rational-problem-solving school is named as having measurably failed FBI negotiators after Ruby Ridge and Waco. This is the tradition's founding rejection, and it is a historical claim rather than a laboratory one.
3. **Behavioural tests beat verbal agreement.** [VOSS:YESTRIAGE], [VOSS:RULE3], [VOSS:IMPLQ] and [VOSS:RIGHTALARM] are one idea in four instruments: never accept a word as evidence of a commitment. This is the lens's most exportable contribution to forecast hygiene, and it survives even if every sentence stem in moves.md is discarded as scripted.
