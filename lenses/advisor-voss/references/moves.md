# advisor-voss — moves

Layer: the language micro-layer of a live negotiation — what to say, in what tone, in the next sixty seconds, when the counterpart is emotional, defensive, or holding a position logic will not move.

Single canonical source: Never Split the Difference (Voss & Raz, 2016). Evidence ceiling for the lens: `practitioner-n-many`. No move may claim `field-study`; components borrowing named outside studies are marked `external-research` in the Evidence line and say which study carries the weight.

Codes are unique within this file. Cross-references between entries reuse codes deliberately; only `###` headings are unique.

## Stage A — Preparation and stance

### [VOSS:ONESHEET] Five-section negotiation one sheet, not a script
**Move:** Prepare one page before the call: the ambitious goal, a two-sentence neutral history, three to five labels forming an accusation audit, three to five calibrated questions, and the non-cash items they could hand over. Attach the research justifying the target and the Ackerman ladder if a price fight is likely. Carry it, but break sequence freely when you clearly hold the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live negotiation moment — pricing call, renewal, redline session, a one-shot multi-stakeholder meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** you extend it into a turn-by-turn script; the source states plainly that full scripting makes you rigid, readable and exploitable by a more agile counterpart.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the exercise the author's firm assigns every client, plus the Mishary lease case; the attached 7:1 prep-payback figure is vendor self-report and must not be quoted.

### [VOSS:GOALHIGH] Commit only the ambitious number to paper
**Move:** Think through both extremes privately, then write down a single optimistic-but-defensible target, define it concretely, say it out loud to a colleague so retreat costs face, and bring the written version into the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** you catch yourself anchoring internally on the minimum you would accept; before any price or rate conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** you hold hard information that the ambitious figure is unreachable and voicing it ends the relationship; also do not treat the written target as a ceiling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — client-practice prescription; the appeal to "decades of goal-setting research" names nothing, so that support is assertion.

### [VOSS:ANTIBATNA] Demote the walk-away from centrepiece to boundary
**Move:** Keep the walk-away as a line you will not cross, but stop rehearsing it as the plan; spend preparation attention on the ambitious outcome, because the number you rehearse most becomes the focal point under stress.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal reviews where the team has spent most of its prep modelling the fallback or the discount floor.
**AVOID WHEN:** alternatives are genuinely thin and ruin risk is real — this reorders attention, it does not abolish the floor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from a general claim about limited attention under stress; no study named anywhere in the appendix.

### [VOSS:SUMPREP] Pre-write the two-sentence shared-facts summary
**Move:** Draft a compact neutral account of the history that produced this conversation, including their version of it; open the call with it and wait for confirmation before touching terms. The test is behavioural — they confirm it, or your summary was wrong.
**APPLY WHEN:** renewals, escalations and renegotiations with history; any call where the two sides carry different stories about how things got here.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes your own position dressed as neutrality — a self-serving summary earns polite agreement and kills the diagnostic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard element of the firm's client preparation exercise.

### [VOSS:AUDIT] Accusation audit
**Move:** List every worst thing they could accuse you of, however unfair — gouging, past failure, self-interest — compress to at most five "it seems…" statements, rehearse them aloud, and say them yourself in their voice before they can. Spoken out loud they sound exaggerated, and the counterpart tends to deny them and reach for common ground.
**APPLY WHEN:** delivering a price increase, a scope cut, bad news, or a renegotiation after a soured relationship; stored grievance from a failed implementation; win-back and save conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** the accusations are true and legally material (you are handing over admissions); no real negative exists, so self-accusation reads as technique; stacking beyond a handful becomes self-flagellating theatre; written channels, where it becomes quotable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Anna's contractor renegotiation, self-reported; the affect-labeling research cited in the book's notes supports the label mechanism ([VOSS:LABEL]), not the audit.

### [VOSS:REHEARSE] Rehearse the audit with a partner and an observer
**Move:** Role-play both sides with colleagues — one plays the counterpart, one disarms with the prepared labels, a third watches. Decide in advance where each label lands and where the pauses go.
**APPLY WHEN:** a single high-value meeting with an adversarial counterpart and a pre-writable set of accusations.
**AVOID WHEN:** rehearsal turns into recital — over-scripted delivery is exactly what makes this toolkit audible in a live room.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one prepared negotiation.

### [VOSS:LABELFILL] Keep fill-in-the-blank label stems loaded
**Move:** Carry a handful of reusable stems — it seems X matters to you / it seems you dislike X / it seems X would make this easier / it seems you are hesitant about X — so you can place the right one without composing under time pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** live calls where you must respond in real time and cannot pause to phrase carefully.
**AVOID WHEN:** fired mechanically in sequence — these stems are the most recognizable surface of the book, and repeated verbatim they read as script, the failure mode that damages long-cycle relationships.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — templates the firm issues to clients.

### [VOSS:STANDBYQ] Standing bank of what/how questions
**Move:** Keep three to five open questions ready: what are we really trying to achieve, what is the core issue, what is your biggest challenge, what about this matters to you, how does this fit the objective, how can I make this better for us both, how would you like me to proceed, what brought this about, how can the problem be solved.
**APPLY WHEN:** first discovery calls; reopening a stalled deal; any point where the conversation has collapsed into positions and counter-positions.
**AVOID WHEN:** read aloud as a checklist — sequence without listening is an interrogation; and when the questions steer toward a foregone conclusion, which the counterpart hears.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's recurring set, reused across client and teaching work.

### [VOSS:NONCASH-PREP] Inventory their cheap-but-valuable assets before the call
**Move:** List in advance the non-money things they could give — introductions, a case study, logo rights, publication, data, a reference, faster access, scope or timing trades — and bring the list, so you have somewhere to move when price is capped. Framing question: what could they give that would nearly make you do the work for free?
**APPLY WHEN:** their budget is genuinely constrained, or their internal win is spending less cash; also whenever a hard discount demand is expected.
**AVOID WHEN:** the item carries real cost or risk to them (reference logos, exclusivity) — then it is a priced concession, not a free trade.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/saas/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the lawyers'-association magazine-cover trade is the only example offered.

### [VOSS:HYPOTH] Treat your read of the account as testable hypotheses
**Move:** Write down what you believe about the buyer's motive, authority and constraints as explicit hypotheses, then spend each call trying to disconfirm them; discard on new evidence rather than defending the original read.
**APPLY WHEN:** entering any account, especially when the intel came from a single source, or when an early "obvious" read is already driving the plan.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating everything as permanently open stalls qualification — time-box it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing FBI doctrine, illustrated by the 1993 Brooklyn bank case where every initial assumption proved wrong.

### [VOSS:TYPEID] Diagnose the counterpart's bargaining type first
**Move:** Classify as Analyst (time = preparation; silence = thinking; hypersensitive to reciprocity; hates surprises), Accommodator (time = relationship; silence = anger; warm rapport with nothing accomplished; hides objections), or Assertive (time = money; silence = your turn to be quiet; cannot listen until they feel heard). Read pace, depth of preparation, emotional display, voice, appetite for small talk, and above all what their silence means. Assume you are probably not facing your own type.
**APPLY WHEN:** before the first price conversation, and after any interaction that felt inexplicably off.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have a single data point; misreading an Analyst's coolness as hostility or an Accommodator's silence as thought.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the firm's consolidation of archetype literature cross-referenced with field and business-school cases; no validation study offered.

### [VOSS:PLAYTYPE] Type-matched tactics
**Move:** With an Analyst: prepare heavily, lead with data and comparisons, warn early about problems, avoid surprises, do not open with a barrage of questions, let silence run, expect a delayed counterproposal. With an Accommodator: be sociable, let them talk, convert talk into action with implementation questions, dig actively for the objections they are hiding, verify they can deliver what they cheerfully promised. With an Assertive: let them be heard first, then mirror, label, summarize toward "that's right", and expect fast reciprocity demands in their own favour. Correct your own default too — Analysts should smile while speaking, Accommodators should voice objections and cut chitchat, Assertives should deliberately soften tone.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after the type read, for the whole bargaining phase.
**AVOID WHEN:** committee calls with several types present — one register cannot serve all three; and when type-play substitutes for substance in data-scored procurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — firm practice, illustrated by an Assertive mirrored for nearly an hour without noticing.

### [VOSS:NOSCRIPT] Sequence by situation, not by playbook order
**Move:** Refuse fixed offer/counteroffer choreography. Hold several live hypotheses about what they want and pick the next tool from what just happened, skipping steps freely.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deal is volatile, their stated demand does not match their behaviour, or your standard sequence has stalled.
**AVOID WHEN:** a team needs a repeatable coachable process; regulated or committee-driven procurement where deviating from a documented process is itself a risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as the lesson of field experience, with no data attached.

### [VOSS:LISTENTEAM] Put more than one listener on the important call
**Move:** Staff the call with a lead speaker plus at least one person whose only job is to listen — take notes, gauge mood, flag tells, pass silent input — then compare what each of them heard afterwards, because a single listener always misses material.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-stakes calls; complex or hostile counterparts; anything you intend to dissect afterwards.
**AVOID WHEN:** small or early conversations where a second attendee signals disproportionate pressure or intimidates a solo buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing FBI protocol, justified by the claim that listening well is genuinely difficult.

## Stage B — Opening and access

### [VOSS:BADTIME] Ask whether this is a bad time, not whether they have a minute
**Move:** Open with a question whose natural answer is a refusal — is now a bad moment — rather than requesting permission for their time. Both branches work: agreement gets you a real slot or a clean exit, refusal gets you their attention.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold and semi-cold calls, unscheduled interruptions, follow-ups into a busy account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the meeting is already booked and the framing sounds falsely tentative; with buyers who have read the book and will name the trick.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard teaching point for sellers; no measured comparison given.

### [VOSS:NOPERMIT] Grant explicit permission to refuse
**Move:** Say up front that a refusal is a completely acceptable outcome of this conversation and that you will not push past one — then let that stand without immediately qualifying it away.
**APPLY WHEN:** first contact, cold outreach, or any moment the counterpart looks cornered or over-pitched; also when a previously warm buyer has gone stiff.
**AVOID WHEN:** they have already committed and you are in execution or paperwork — reopening the veto invites re-litigation; also avoid stacking it with hard pressure elsewhere in the same call, which reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — taught to students and used across hostage cases; derived from Jim Camp's right-to-veto framing, untested.

### [VOSS:OWNNAME] Use your own first name, not theirs
**Move:** Rather than hammering their name, introduce yourself by first name in a light friendly way and ask for the concession by that name. Humanizing yourself creates forced empathy and gives them a reason to bend a rule for a person rather than a transaction.
**APPLY WHEN:** first contact, gatekeepers, front-line staff, list-price situations, de-escalating hostility.
**AVOID WHEN:** formal enterprise procurement where whimsy reads as unserious; when the counterpart controls no discretionary discount and the ask embarrasses them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a bar de-escalation in Kansas and a 10% outlet-store discount; entertaining, not systematic.

### [VOSS:RIFF] Label → mirror → label → ask
**Move:** Open on someone under pressure by labelling the unpleasantness they just absorbed, mirror their reply to draw detail, label again to acknowledge how they handled it, then make the request — framed as riding on what they just volunteered. Stay silent after the ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold asks to gatekeepers or support staff; anyone just mistreated by the previous person in line; walking in after a competitor or colleague burned the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** there is no relationship time available; a counterpart with no discretion to grant the ask regardless of rapport.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the airline gate-agent story, a single instance resolved in under two minutes.

### [VOSS:YOUSCRIPT] Rewrite the outreach around them
**Move:** Take the standard payment-demand or follow-up template and reframe it: possessive framing that makes the relationship theirs, explicit acknowledgment of the pressure they are under, an offer to work with their specific situation, and a request to talk it through — with no discount attached.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, overdue renewals, re-engaging accounts that stopped replying; any templated outreach drawing silence.
**AVOID WHEN:** the acknowledgment is not backed by real flexibility — hollow empathy plus an unchanged demand reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Washington Redskins season-ticket collections case, self-reported.

### [VOSS:GHOSTMAIL] One-line ghosting email
**Move:** Break silence with a single short question implying they have abandoned the project — have you given up on settling this, on closing this deal this year — which can only be rebutted by re-engaging and simultaneously signals you are prepared to walk. If even that draws nothing, treat the silence as data pointing at a constraint they are embarrassed to name, and start investigating through third parties.
**APPLY WHEN:** two or more unanswered follow-ups; a previously responsive contact goes dark after a proposal, especially against a budget-year boundary.
**AVOID WHEN:** the delay has a known cause (holiday, budget freeze, reorg) — the implied accusation looks careless; the relationship is early and cold, where loss framing closes the door; never twice on the same contact, since its power is in being one-shot.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the firm's classic email claimed to "always work", which is self-report; the chapter's own worked case is the one where it failed.

### [VOSS:ASKADVICE] Ask for suggestions instead of stating requirements
**Move:** Describe what you are looking for and request recommendations rather than announcing requirements; then, instead of a hard offer, say the price exceeds your budget and ask for their help.
**APPLY WHEN:** buying side, vendor selection, any counterpart whose expertise can be flattered into cooperation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart is compensated purely on price and holds no discretion — the request wastes both sides' time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a general-purpose consumer and vendor tactic, unquantified.

### [VOSS:MENTORQ] Ask what it takes to be successful here
**Move:** Put that exact question to an interviewer or an executive sponsor; whoever answers acquires a stake in your following their advice and tends to become an informal mentor and advocate.
**APPLY WHEN:** interviews; first meetings with an executive sponsor; partner onboarding.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no intention of acting on the answer — the stake they acquire cuts both ways.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=stage/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated student reports that interviewers said nobody had asked before; the exact wording is claimed to be load-bearing, without test.

## Stage C — Discovery, listening and diagnosis

### [VOSS:MIRROR] The three-word mirror
**Move:** Repeat back the last three words — or the one to three carrying the weight — with a curious upward lift, then stop talking. The intent to convey is help me understand; they restate differently and add material.
**APPLY WHEN:** you want clarification without interrogating; their tone contradicts their words; you need them to keep talking or need a beat to regroup; objection handling.
**AVOID WHEN:** you step on it with a follow-up question, which kills the effect; mechanical repetition of every sentence becomes obvious and irritating.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — FBI-wide practice; the in-book waiter-tipping figure attributed to Wiseman is cited without a verifiable reference and should not be quoted as data.

### [VOSS:LABEL] Label the emotion with an impersonal stem
**Move:** Name what you observe them feeling with a neutral observation stem — it seems / it sounds like / it looks like — never a first-person stem, then go silent and let them respond. If they reject the label, retreat to the fact that you only said it seemed that way.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is tense or hedging, or their tone contradicts their words; a stalled deal with an unnamed objection.
**AVOID WHEN:** you smother it with your own follow-up question; the label targets surface behaviour rather than the driver, which merely annoys; "I" phrasing, which triggers defensiveness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research (mechanism) — affect labeling supported by the UCLA imaging work of Lieberman et al. (2007) cited in the book's notes; the negotiating application itself remains practitioner-n-many.

### [VOSS:UNDERLYING] Label the driver, not the behaviour
**Move:** Separate presenting behaviour from the feeling underneath and address the second. Voice the underlying state non-judgmentally, pause to let it register, then offer the constructive reframe.
**APPLY WHEN:** a counterpart is picky, cranky, or rejecting every option without a coherent reason.
**AVOID WHEN:** you guess the driver wrong and sound presumptuous about their inner life; the objection is genuinely technical.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Girl Scouts fundraiser case, where two successive labels closed a donation with no project selected.

### [VOSS:WMD] Words, music and dance
**Move:** Read emotion from three channels at once — words, vocal tone, body — and above all from the changes that occur immediately after you say something. A flat voice on a colleague's name, a downturned mouth, restless feet: each is a cue to probe.
**APPLY WHEN:** live calls and meetings, especially when you need what nobody has said out loud.
**AVOID WHEN:** email or async deals with no signal channel; over-reading single cues — the book compares the skill to how psychics operate, which cuts both ways.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — instructional doctrine from the author's course, unmeasured.

### [VOSS:NEGFIRST] Neutralize the negative before selling the positive
**Move:** Go at the negative dynamic directly and early — voice their fear or the awkwardness instead of tiptoeing — and only then offer the positive framing or the ask. Label positives too, to reinforce them.
**APPLY WHEN:** the relationship has soured, communication has gone silent, or you are about to ask for something after friction.
**AVOID WHEN:** naming a negative they had not considered plants it; deals where the negative is contractual and naming it invites a legal position.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Harlem standoff, the Canada country-clearance call, and the Redskins collections case.

### [VOSS:ALSTACK] Active-listening stack
**Move:** Run the six tools together rather than singly — deliberate silence to drain emotion, minimal encouragers to show attention, mirroring of their last words, labeling of the feeling, paraphrasing to prove comprehension, then a summary fusing the last two.
**APPLY WHEN:** entrenched positions repeated across meetings; an emotional or aggrieved counterpart; discovery where the stated ask makes no economic sense.
**AVOID WHEN:** short transactional exchanges where they just want a price; applied mechanically it becomes audible parroting — the exact failure paraphrase exists to prevent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the unit's operating arsenal, deployed via a written two-page instruction in the Schilling case.

### [VOSS:SUMTRIG] Summary engineered to trigger "that's right"
**Move:** Rearticulate their entire version of the situation — including the parts you consider nonsense — plus the emotions attached, completely enough that no correction is available, and stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** deadlock on a demand that will not move under logic; an objection that has survived three rebuttals; internal disagreement with a colleague before you attempt to redirect them.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not actually gathered their worldview — a thin summary produces "you're right" or a correction; also avoid summarizing a position you intend to publicly refute immediately afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Schilling case, where a four-month damages demand evaporated after one summary, plus coaching episodes with the author's son and a pharma-rep student.

### [VOSS:RIGHTALARM] Treat "you're right" and "I'll try" as failure alarms
**Move:** When agreement arrives phrased as you being right, or as an intention to try, treat the persuasion attempt as failed — the first ends an unwanted conversation while changing nothing, the second is a plan to fail. Do not move to paperwork; return to calibrated how-questions until they restate the terms of success in their own words, then summarize to a "that's right".
**APPLY WHEN:** you have just pushed for a commitment, a next step, or an internal action item on their side; call reviews and deal inspection; any suspiciously fast concession.
**AVOID WHEN:** the phrase is a genuine factual concession on a small verifiable point; also beware over-reading one phrase in a second-language or heavily scripted conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — repeated coaching failures with the author's son, generalized into a field heuristic without measurement.

### [VOSS:RECAPOPEN] Open the next meeting with them, not your product
**Move:** Start the follow-up by recalling what mattered to them last time — their mission, their people, their constraints — as a labeled summary, let them expand, and only after the walls drop describe how your thing serves the outcome they just named.
**APPLY WHEN:** a hostile or dismissive technical buyer who cuts off feature talk; second and third meetings; incumbent-vendor situations.
**AVOID WHEN:** you learned nothing real in meeting one — recycled flattery is transparent; also weak with buyers who want a quote and nothing else.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pharma sales rep who converted the largest prescriber in her territory.

### [VOSS:WANTNEED] Talk wants to find needs
**Move:** Get them talking at length about what they want — aspirational and safe to voice — and use the volume of talk to infer the need: the minimum that would actually make them act, which they will not state directly.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery; a buyer whose stated ask is obviously a wish-list; before pricing anything.
**AVOID WHEN:** you mistake stated wants for the requirement and quote against them; time-boxed calls where the meander costs you the agenda.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as core listening doctrine, unquantified.

### [VOSS:ASKNOT] Ask what they would refuse
**Move:** Explicitly invite them to lay out what they would say no to, and treat that list as the working definition of the deal space.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery stalls on vague requirements; scoping a service or consulting engagement; committee deals where constraints go unspoken.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart is already in a negative spiral — inviting more refusals compounds it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as routine practice, unquantified.

### [VOSS:CALIBQ] Calibrated open questions
**Move:** Convert demands and closed asks into questions beginning with what or how; strip out can/is/are/do/does, and drop who/when/where, which only harvest facts. Design each question toward a chosen destination rather than asking openly at random.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery, objection handling, any point where a direct statement would provoke a defence; also as a de-escalation reflex under attack.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart needs a decision or a fact from you and questions read as stalling; committee settings where endless open questions consume the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the unit's core post-2002 tool, taught widely; the supporting theory is borrowed from Dutton's *Split-Second Persuasion* and not tested by the author.

### [VOSS:QCLUSTER] Ask related questions in clusters of two or three
**Move:** Group two or three near-identical questions so they circle the same issue from different angles instead of answering once and closing the topic.
**APPLY WHEN:** the first answer was thin or rehearsed.
**AVOID WHEN:** short calls or an already-defensive counterpart — near-identical repetition reads as pressure-testing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as an observed pattern from practice.

### [VOSS:DEALKILLER-Q] Surface the blocker and the cost of doing nothing
**Move:** Ask in this shape: what are we up against, what is your biggest challenge here, how does dealing with us change things, what happens if you do nothing, what does inaction cost, how does this fit what your company prides itself on.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled mid-funnel deals where the stated objection is price but movement has stopped.
**AVOID WHEN:** early rapport-building, or delivered as a challenge — the do-nothing question lands as pressure if the relationship has not earned it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — client prep list; the status-quo-bias rationale behind it is asserted, not sourced.

### [VOSS:FOLLOWLABEL] Pre-stage second-layer labels for their answers
**Move:** Keep follow-up stems ready — it seems X is important / it seems you feel we are uniquely positioned to X / it seems you are worried that X — and place one after each answer so they keep expanding instead of stopping.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery calls where answers are coming but staying shallow.
**AVOID WHEN:** every single answer gets a label — the rhythm becomes audible and they start performing for the technique.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — issued as a client template.

### [VOSS:SLOWDOWN] Deliberately slow the conversation
**Move:** Stretch the exchange out. Do not drive toward resolution; let time pass, ask, pause, revisit. Speed reads as not listening and burns the rapport you built.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is agitated; you feel urgency to close; the conversation has become a ping-pong of positions.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuine external deadline exists and delay costs the buyer money; long enterprise cycles where slowness is already the failure mode.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — field doctrine; the book gestures at research on time as a tool without naming any.

### [VOSS:DJVOICE] Late-night FM DJ voice
**Move:** For statements you want treated as settled, drop the pitch at the end of the sentence and speak slowly, calmly, quietly. Make declarations, not questions. On a non-negotiable term, state it plainly and offer no alternative, since offering one reopens debate.
**APPLY WHEN:** you must state something non-negotiable, take over a stalled conversation, or calm a spiralling counterpart.
**AVOID WHEN:** most of the conversation — the book says use it selectively; rising inflection is correct whenever you actually want a response.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Watts handover and the author's own contract-clause practice; effect claimed, never measured.

### [VOSS:PLAYFUL] Positive/playful voice as the default register
**Move:** Default to a light good-natured tone and physically smile while speaking, including on the phone, because the tonal change carries. Reserve the direct assertive voice for near-never — it signals dominance and provokes push-back.
**APPLY WHEN:** virtually all routine contact — prospecting, discovery, haggling.
**AVOID WHEN:** the moment demands gravity (bad news, a firm boundary); with a counterpart already reading you as unserious.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an instructor's girlfriend outbargaining him in Istanbul spice markets, plus an unsourced claim that positive mood speeds thinking.

### [VOSS:BS3] Assume three hidden Black Swans and hunt them
**Move:** Enter every negotiation assuming each side holds roughly three pieces of unrecognized decisive information. Replace confirmation-seeking research with the standing question — why are they communicating this, right now? Ask many questions, voice your nonverbal observations back to them, and stay focused on the next step rather than the end objective.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where behaviour does not fit your model — silence, stalling, odd conditions, unexplained urgency.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as licence for endless discovery on a small transactional deal; conventional research still covers the known knowns.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the firm's stated preparation format; the three-per-side figure is the author's hypothesis, never measured.

### [VOSS:RELIGION] Reconstruct their worldview, and listen twice
**Move:** Work out the counterpart's worldview and reason for being, not just their interests, then position demands inside it. Operationally: review everything you heard afterwards, because you missed things the first time; compare notes with teammates; assign backup listeners whose only job is to hear between the lines.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-stakes, emotionally loaded or values-driven counterparts; long cycles with several conversations to mine.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot deploy a second listener and are guessing a worldview from thin signal; and when invoking someone's beliefs would be manipulative enough to detonate the relationship if noticed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Dwight Watson tractor standoff, resolved when an agent inferred devout faith and framed the surrender inside it.

### [VOSS:SIMILARITY] Shared-identity vocabulary
**Move:** Find the word the counterpart's in-group uses for what they are struggling to articulate and say it back — the book's example is naming a born-again CEO's dilemma as stewardship. The point is not the similarity but the understanding it demonstrates.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart signals a strong subculture (faith, profession, alumni, craft) and expresses feeling misunderstood.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are performing a membership you do not hold — a wrong or hollow term reads as manipulation; the book itself frames this as instrumental rapport.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one Ohio CEO engagement; the supporting "research by social scientists" on in-group trust is cited without a name.

### [VOSS:HOPES] Speak to their unattained goals
**Move:** Once you know their worldview, name what they have always wanted, express genuine belief in their ability to reach it, and attach a purposeful plan. People follow whoever draws a credible map to the thing they had given up on.
**APPLY WHEN:** founder or executive counterparts with visible ambition; partnership and investor conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** the aspiration is guessed rather than evidenced; procurement-driven buys where aspiration is irrelevant to the scorecard.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=investor/stage/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a motivational claim illustrated only by public rhetoric (Ted Leonsis), with no negotiation outcome attached.

### [VOSS:FRINGE] Mine the unguarded moments
**Move:** Treat the first minutes before business starts and the last minutes as everyone leaves as the highest-yield observation windows; watch closely during interruptions, odd exchanges and anything that breaks the flow, noting both who cracks and how the others react.
**APPLY WHEN:** in-person or video meetings, site visits, dinners, conferences.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no attention to spare from running the meeting — this is where a second listener earns their seat.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated practice, argued by analogy to reporters never switching off the recorder.

### [VOSS:MISLABEL] Deliberate mislabel to trigger a correction
**Move:** State a plainly wrong reading of their motive, emotion or situation as a label, so the only comfortable response is to correct you. The correction is where the withheld fact arrives.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is not engaging, gives one-word answers, or repeats a position without content; a visible inconsistency (a cash cow being sold, urgency with no stated reason); mid-discovery with a talkative broker or intermediary.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is already tense or the mislabel touches a real grievance, where being wrong on purpose reads as mockery; with an Analyst, who logs it as your error and disengages; never where the wrong label could go on record.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Charleston student's mixed-use property case, where the mislabel exposed that the seller needed cash for distressed properties elsewhere.

### [VOSS:PINOCCHIO] Lie markers in word volume and pronouns
**Move:** Treat a sudden jump in word count, a shift to third-person pronouns and more complex sentence construction as flags that a statement is being oversold. Probe that specific claim rather than accusing.
**APPLY WHEN:** verifying claims about budget, competing bids, authority, or timelines.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart is naturally verbose, presenting on behalf of a committee (where "we" is accurate), or speaking a second language.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research (marker) — the deception-marker work is credited to Van Swol, Braun & Malhotra (2012); the negotiating application is the author's own and untested.

### [VOSS:PRONOUN] Pronoun read for real authority
**Move:** Score how often they say I/me/my versus we/they/them. Heavy first person suggests the real decision sits elsewhere; someone who avoids first person and defers to unnamed others is more likely the actual decision-maker preserving room to defer. Route your behind-the-table questions accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** mapping the buying committee; qualifying a champion's real power; when you cannot ask about decision authority without insulting them.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deferral is literally true (a junior contact in an enterprise committee) or the corporate culture mandates "we"; never let a linguistic tell override org-chart evidence — treat it as a hypothesis to test.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — inferred from two hostage cases plus the author's read of the distancing finding; he concedes it is retrospective pattern-fitting.

### [VOSS:RULEPROBE] Probe the assumed rule, then hypothesize their real motive
**Move:** Where a blocker cites policy, first check whether the rule actually exists, then ask what led them to that decision, then state your hypothesis of their underlying interest as a summary they can only confirm — the confirmation usually drags out the hidden driver.
**APPLY WHEN:** procurement or legal blocks framed as fixed policy; a champion's boss vetoing without a stated reason; anything where "we can't do that" is unverified.
**AVOID WHEN:** the constraint is genuinely regulatory or contractual — probing it wastes credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one MBA student's internal transfer negotiation, which surfaced two unstated motives.

### [VOSS:EMODRIVER] Sell the vision of their problem
**Move:** Draw out their problems, pain and unmet objectives, then frame the offer against what they are actually buying — the babysitter sells a relaxed evening, the locksmith sells security — rather than against the feature list.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery; positioning against a cheaper functional equivalent; any pitch that keeps collapsing into specification comparison.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a technical evaluator scoring measurable criteria, where emotional framing reads as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — borrowed from *How to Become a Rainmaker*, which the author endorses rather than tests.

## Stage D — Objections, stalls and conflict

### [VOSS:NODECODE] Decode "no" before answering it
**Move:** Treat a refusal as one of roughly seven real messages — not ready, uncomfortable, does not understand, cannot afford it, wants something different, needs more information, needs to consult someone — then pause and respond either with a solution-seeking question (what about this does not work, what would it take to work) or with a plain label of what seems to be bothering them.
**APPLY WHEN:** any objection, especially the first one; late-stage stalls; silence after a proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** the refusal is a hard disqualification — no budget, no authority, wrong problem — where decoding becomes pestering.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter's central operating rule, repeated across cases without counts.

### [VOSS:NONOGO] No refusal at all is a disqualification signal
**Move:** If, after deliberate attempts, the counterpart will never refuse anything, read it as indecision, confusion or a hidden agenda, and end the pursuit.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline hygiene; deals that agree with everything and advance nothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart's culture or role makes explicit refusal costly — absence of "no" may then be politeness, not evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted as a pattern from repeated cases, no counts given.

### [VOSS:YESTRIAGE] Triage which "yes" you just heard
**Move:** Before forecasting, classify the agreement as counterfeit (an escape hatch), confirmation (reflex affirmation to a binary question) or commitment (the real one that ends in signature). The three sound nearly identical, so test by asking for a next action rather than accepting the word.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of a call that felt too easy; enthusiastic buyers with no calendar movement; forecast reviews.
**AVOID WHEN:** the classification changes nothing unless you follow it with an implementation question — do not stop at labelling it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's taxonomy, illustrated by the crisis-hotline review episode.

### [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED] Answer an impossible demand with a request for help
**Move:** Summarize the situation, then ask how you are meant to comply — genuinely asking, no accusation in the delivery — and stay quiet while they solve your constraint. The claimed effect is forced empathy: they must inspect your position, and reciprocity pulls them toward improving their own offer. Keep re-asking variants as each new demand arrives.
**APPLY WHEN:** take-it-or-leave-it price or term demands; unpayable discount asks; unpaid invoices; scope demands beyond contract; especially strong right after they have asked you for something extra.
**AVOID WHEN:** you can and should simply agree; you cannot keep your tone neutral, where it lands as the accusation it paraphrases; with an Analyst who needs data rather than appeals; in a collaborative multi-issue deal where trading across issues creates more value than stalling; and the book itself calls it passive-aggressive when repeated with the same person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas/product · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the standard reply to ransom demands across many kidnappings plus consulting stories; "it never backfired" is the author's self-report.

### [VOSS:MEACULPA] Front-load the apology as self-accusation
**Move:** When you caused the offence, open by accusing yourself in blunt, slightly comic terms before they can, and acknowledge their anger explicitly rather than explaining yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** you missed a deadline, went around someone's authority, or shipped a failure; the counterpart's status has been slighted.
**AVOID WHEN:** written channels, where the self-accusation becomes evidence; repeat offences, where it reads as a substitute for fixing anything.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Canada country-clearance call and the author's habitual use.

### [VOSS:APOLWARM] Apology plus first name to blunt a bulldozer
**Move:** Prefix a resistant reply with a light apology and their first name, so the refusal lands as warmth rather than opposition, keeping the confrontation from hardening while you hold your line.
**APPLY WHEN:** the other side is escalating tone or trying to steamroll you; early in a contested exchange.
**AVOID WHEN:** cultures or buyers who read repeated apologizing as weakness; when the apology implies fault you should not concede in writing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single recorded role-play against Mnookin and Blum.

### [VOSS:DISAGREE4] Four-step disagreement without confrontation
**Move:** To push back on a dominant counterpart: DJ voice, a soft apology stem, a mirror of their instruction, then silence for at least four seconds — and repeat the cycle. They talk themselves through the flaws in their own request.
**APPLY WHEN:** an aggressive boss or buyer issues a poorly-thought-out demand; you need them to withdraw it without being seen to refuse.
**AVOID WHEN:** the request is reasonable; a counterpart who has read the same book and recognizes the ritual; repeated use with the same person erodes candour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one student's account of avoiding a week of duplicate paperwork, self-reported.

### [VOSS:CRAZYTRIAGE] Triage apparent irrationality
**Move:** When they act against their own interest, run three hypotheses in order: bad information — find what they do not know and supply it, ideally through a neutral third party they trust; undisclosed constraints — legal advice, prior promises, precedent risk, no authority; hidden interests — fiscal timing, internal politics, fairness, promotion, an unannounced exit. Then negotiate against the real cause.
**APPLY WHEN:** ghosting after momentum, refusal of an objectively good offer, inexplicable deadlines or scope demands.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to rationalize a dead deal indefinitely — some counterparts are simply not buying.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research (framework) — credited to Malhotra and Bazerman's *Negotiation Genius*; the author's illustrations (a commission dispute settled by outside audit, a contact who had lost internal influence) are anecdotal.

### [VOSS:FACETIME] Buy face time outside the building
**Move:** Move the interaction off email and out of their office — invite them somewhere informal with an explicit promise not to discuss business, framed as a question they answer by refusing. Direct human contact is what lets an embarrassed contact admit a constraint and hand you to the right executive.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled deal, suspected hidden constraint, a contact whose authority you doubt.
**AVOID WHEN:** compliance-restricted buyers with gift or hospitality policies; travel cost exceeding deal value; remote-only relationships where insistence reads as pressure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Coca-Cola pursuit, which still took over a year to sign after the dinner.

### [VOSS:WHYFLIP] Weaponized "why" that makes them defend your position
**Move:** Treat "why" as accusatory and avoid it, with one exception: aim the defensiveness at the option that favours you — why would you ever switch to us, why would you ever leave a supplier doing great work — delivered deferentially, so they argue your case for you.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive displacement; a wobbling prospect who needs to hear their own reasons for change.
**AVOID WHEN:** diagnosing past decisions or failures — reframe as what caused…; and when they may take the invitation and reaffirm the incumbent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a cross-language rule from field impression; the scripted pattern carries no case outcome and the universality claim carries no evidence.

### [VOSS:BITETONGUE] Emotional regulation as the precondition
**Move:** When verbally attacked, do not counterattack: pause, let the reaction pass, then reply with a calibrated question or a short apology conceding your own harshness. Buy framing time deliberately — the book's example is executives using an interpreter they do not need.
**APPLY WHEN:** hostile buyers, patronizing counterparts, escalations over price or blame.
**AVOID WHEN:** never contraindicated — but note it is the binding constraint on every other move here; without it the toolkit does not run at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the marketing strategist who executed step one perfectly, then lost the deal ten minutes later on the phone.

### [VOSS:IMSG] "I" messages to set a boundary
**Move:** Use the first-person singular to pause a bad dynamic without escalating: state that this does not work for you, or use the I-feel-X-when-you-Y-because-Z construction to demand a time-out. Keep the tone level and cool.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is being unproductive, repetitive, or personally aggressive.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered with heat — the same words in an aggressive tone start the argument they are meant to stop.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard boundary-setting technique presented as firm practice.

### [VOSS:SWAPIN] Fresh voice, flat handover
**Move:** When a conversation has hardened around one person, substitute a different negotiator without preamble and open with a plain downward-inflected statement that they are now dealing with you.
**APPLY WHEN:** the relationship with the current owner is stuck and the only alternative is escalation; a closer is brought in for final terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** the incumbent rep holds the only trust in the account; the source itself notes this broke protocol and could easily have backfired.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single standoff, acknowledged by the author as a gamble.

### [VOSS:NOSETUP] "No"-oriented setup question
**Move:** Frame the ask as a question they answer by refusing something they clearly do not want — for example whether they want the organization embarrassed — then immediately follow with an open question inviting them to define your next step.
**APPLY WHEN:** the other side holds formal power and is about to impose a decision; stalled internal negotiations; renewals where a sponsor is defaulting to cancel.
**AVOID WHEN:** the implied bad outcome is a stretch — an invented consequence reads as a threat and burns the relationship; avoid in writing, where tone is lost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one colleague's dismissal meeting that she reversed on the spot.

## Stage E — Price, leverage and bargaining

### [VOSS:NOSPLIT] Refuse the midpoint reflex
**Move:** Do not propose or accept a halfway number as a way of resolving a gap; treat no-deal as an acceptable outcome and say so internally before the call.
**APPLY WHEN:** procurement opens with an unjustified number; end-of-quarter pressure to concede; any counterpart running a win-lose playbook.
**AVOID WHEN:** the residual gap is small relative to relationship value, or the deal is genuinely multi-issue and a trade across issues (not a split) is available — the book does not distinguish that case, and this is the lens's weakest point.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — unit doctrine that no deal beats a bad deal, argued by analogy rather than data.

### [VOSS:ANCHORLAST] Let them anchor, then absorb the punch
**Move:** Default to letting them name the first number — you rarely hold full market information and their opening may exceed your target. When an extreme anchor lands, do not counter-bid and do not get angry: deflect with a version of No, or refocus the room on what we are actually trying to accomplish. Prepare psychologically in advance to hear an absurd number without adjusting toward it.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are information-poor about their budget or market; rate and salary talks; new categories; unfamiliar buyers; when the split-the-difference trap is forming.
**AVOID WHEN:** you hold a strong reference price and anchoring first is worth more than the read (the author anchors first in his own car-purchase and Ackerman cases); when going second stalls a deal against a hard deadline; against an experienced counterpart who will anchor absurdly and drag you — the book explicitly refuses "never open" as a rule.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — self-inflicted underpricing cases plus hostage haggling and student cases; no controlled first-versus-second-mover comparison.

### [VOSS:RANGE] Quote a bolstering range via a comparable
**Move:** Instead of naming your number, cite what comparable deals or comparable employers pay as a range whose low end is what you actually want; expect to land near that low end.
**APPLY WHEN:** salary and rate discussions; a first pricing conversation with a new buyer; anything where a flat number triggers a defensive reaction.
**AVOID WHEN:** the comparable is easily falsified, or the buyer treats the low end as your list price forever.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** external-research (component) — the range-offer effect is credited to Ames & Mason (2015, Columbia), reported second-hand by the author rather than reproduced.

### [VOSS:THIRDPRICE] Deflect "you go first" with someone else's number
**Move:** If forced to name a price first, do not name yours — quote an implausibly high figure a third party would charge (the book cites a $2,500 per student per day academic rate). It sets a reference point without committing you.
**APPLY WHEN:** early pricing pressure, RFP-style ballpark requests, services with no list price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer can trivially show the comparison is irrelevant; procurement contexts where a non-answer disqualifies you outright.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted engagement.

### [VOSS:ODDNUM] Use precise, non-round numbers
**Move:** State figures that look calculated rather than rounded; round numbers read as placeholders inviting negotiation, precise ones read as the output of analysis and as a computed limit.
**APPLY WHEN:** any offer or counteroffer you intend to defend; final concessions.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot explain the derivation if asked, or industry convention is round pricing and precision looks contrived.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted from repeated use (the ransom case closed at $4,751); the book concedes the effect is irrational and uses it anyway.

### [VOSS:ACKERMAN] Ackerman offer ladder
**Move:** Set the target you actually want; open at 65% of it; plan raises to 85%, 95% and 100%, each increment half the previous; between raises use empathy and the No-series so they counter before you move; make the final number precise and non-round; on the last offer add a non-monetary item they probably do not want, to signal you have hit your limit. Decreasing raises are meant to convince them they squeezed you dry.
**APPLY WHEN:** true single-issue price haggling against a hard bargainer; renewals, buyouts, procurement showdowns.
**AVOID WHEN:** relationship-first or committee-consensus sales where a 65% opener reads as insulting or unserious; a competing bidder who takes the business while you ladder; any context where the opener burns credibility you need during delivery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — used heavily in Haiti across many kidnappings plus student cases; Howard Raiffa's endorsement is second-hand and anecdotal.

### [VOSS:NOSERIES] Four ways to refuse before saying the word
**Move:** Refuse up to four times without the word: ask how you are supposed to do that; call their offer generous, apologize, say it simply does not work for you, with no counteroffer; say you are afraid you just cannot do that, expressing inability rather than unwillingness; then a short apologetic no. Only then a plain No with downward inflection and warmth. Making no counteroffer is what invites them to keep improving their own number.
**APPLY WHEN:** price or terms pressure where you have time and they have more urgency than you; buyouts, renewals, single-buyer/single-seller situations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a competing buyer or seller exists and delay costs you the deal — the book says outright the method suited its case only because the counterpart had no other buyer; also when repeated non-answers exhaust a busy enterprise buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Spanish grow-shop buyout, the author's own vehicle purchase, and repeated kidnapping use.

### [VOSS:NAMEFIRST] Attrition ladder that makes them bid against themselves
**Move:** Decline to name any number: block with an inability statement avoiding the word no, question the fairness of the demand, then answer each demand with a calibrated how-question so the only way forward is for them to lower their own figure. Only after they have dropped twice do you place an extreme low anchor, follow it with a precise odd number, and close with a token non-monetary sweetener.
**APPLY WHEN:** an opening demand with no cost basis; heavy discount demands; buyers who insist you go first.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart has real alternatives and low switching cost — attrition requires them locked in; also unusable where slow-rolling burns your own deadline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Haitian kidnapping case, a $150,000 demand walked to $4,751 plus a CD player in one day.

### [VOSS:GIFTSHOCK] Extreme anchor, then an unrelated gift
**Move:** Stake an aggressive anchor, absorb the rejection, then offer a small unrelated gift or gesture that costs you little, invoking reciprocity instead of conceding on price.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stalled haggle where you refuse to move on the number; long-horizon relationships where a returned favour has value.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gesture could read as an inducement or breach the buyer's procurement rules — a live compliance risk in enterprise and public-sector deals that the book does not address at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — grounded in reciprocity and illustrated politically (Sadat's 1977 Knesset address) rather than commercially.

### [VOSS:NONMONEY] Pivot from price to terms and non-monetary value
**Move:** Park price explicitly and ask what would make this a good deal, or what else they could offer that would make that price work for you. Name items cheap for them and valuable to you (or the reverse) and seed the brainstorm with concrete examples — logos, references, case studies, exposure, data, introductions, scope, timing, payment terms, exclusivity — so they start hunting their own.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is anchoring hard on price or is capped on budget but rich in other assets; multi-issue deals where non-price terms are tradeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely single-issue transactions with nothing to trade; the counterpart cannot actually deliver the non-monetary item; the buyer reads a terms pivot as evading a straight answer; or you are trading cash you need now for goodwill you cannot bank.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the bar-association trade of a magazine cover story against a steep discount from a $25,000/day rate; this is the one point where the book brushes against multi-issue logrolling without naming it.

### [VOSS:FAIRDEF] Defuse "we just want what's fair"
**Move:** Do not concede to the implied accusation. Stop, apologize, and invite them to walk back to the exact point where you began treating them unfairly so it can be fixed.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart plays the fairness card to extract a discount; emotional escalation over price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the complaint is substantively correct — then fix the substance instead of running the counter.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a friend's home sale where the phrase alone raised the buyer's offer.

### [VOSS:FAIRMIRROR] Mirror the fairness jab back
**Move:** When told you have already been given a fair offer, repeat the word fair as a question, hold the pause, then label their apparent readiness to show the evidence behind the claim — which forces disclosure or exposes the bluff.
**APPLY WHEN:** procurement asserts market fairness without data; everyone-else-pays-this claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** you could not use the data if they produced it, or the relationship cannot absorb a challenge on record.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — modelled on an NFL lockout exchange, presented as the author's standard reply.

### [VOSS:FAIRFRAME] Pre-license fairness as a stop word
**Move:** Early in the engagement, state that you want them to feel treated fairly throughout and invite them to stop you the moment they think you are not — then honour it.
**APPLY WHEN:** kickoff of a long or repeated negotiation; multi-year contracts; partner and channel relationships where reputation compounds.
**AVOID WHEN:** you intend to run hard anchoring tactics in the same negotiation without slack to absorb a challenge.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own repeated opening line, framed as reputation management.

### [VOSS:ACCANCHOR] Accusation audit plus loss framing before bad news
**Move:** Before delivering an unattractive proposition, voice every negative thing they are about to think about you, in their words, until the expectation floor is low; then present the offer as an opportunity you are bringing to them first, before it goes elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** price reductions you must impose, scope cuts, delivery delays, renegotiating rates with contractors or partners.
**AVOID WHEN:** the negatives you voice are ones they had not considered and would materially damage trust; risky in writing, where self-accusation becomes quotable evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the UAE subcontract case, where contractors accepted a cut from $2,000/day to $500/day without a counteroffer.

### [VOSS:UMBRAGE] Real anger, poised threats, strategic umbrage
**Move:** When an offer is genuinely insulting, allow a controlled amount of real anger, aim it at the proposal rather than the person, and say you do not see how that could ever work. Deliver any threat with poise — confidence and self-control. Faked anger is out.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stalled negotiation, a deliberately abusive offer, or a counterpart who reads politeness as weakness.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be faking it, which is documented to produce intractable demands and destroy trust; when the concession extracted will be implemented badly, since anger degrades their cognitive resources; and recognize the same move when aimed at you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** external-research (component) — supported by named work from Sinaceur & Tiedens (2006) on anger and Ames & Wazlawek (2014) on targets of umbrage rating themselves over-assertive; the tactical packaging is the author's.

### [VOSS:WALKAWAY] No neediness — be genuinely ready to leave
**Move:** Fix your bottom line in advance and be genuinely willing to walk; no deal beats a bad deal, and if you feel you cannot refuse, you have taken yourself hostage. Before any punch-back, offer de-escalation — suggest a time-out so the other side regains a sense of agency.
**APPLY WHEN:** every bargaining session; especially when you catch yourself rationalizing a concession because you need the quarter to close.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are posturing a walk-away you cannot execute — bluffing here is the same error as fake anger; also when the relationship's long-term value exceeds this deal's terms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a principle repeated across the author's cases, asserted rather than measured.

### [VOSS:DEADOPEN] Disclose your own deadline
**Move:** Tell the counterpart your cutoff instead of hiding it, on the logic that when the clock runs out for you it runs out for them too, and secrecy only makes you concede against yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** budget-cycle or fiscal-year constraints; procurement processes that drift; any negotiation where the other side appears to be waiting you out.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deadline is soft and you will visibly blow through it — disclosing a fake cutoff you then ignore destroys the tool permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research (component) — contradicts Herb Cohen's classic advice and leans on cited impasse-risk research by Don A. Moore (Berkeley), reported second-hand.

### [VOSS:DEADHUNT] Locate their self-imposed clock
**Move:** Identify their internal cycle — quarter close, month end, weekend, board meeting — and time your firmest position just before it; read escalating specificity in their threats (what, who, when, how) as the signal the real deadline is near.
**APPLY WHEN:** vendors and sellers on quota; durable-goods purchases at month end; renewal negotiations near a fiscal boundary.
**AVOID WHEN:** your own hard cutoff lands earlier; also when stalling risks losing an internal champion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — pattern extracted from the Haitian kidnapping wave and generalized by analogy to quarterly sales behaviour.

### [VOSS:LEVPOS] Build positive leverage from their stated wants
**Move:** Treat every declaration of wanting something as the moment leverage transfers to you, then delay your own offer, since going first gives it away. Use their desire to create optionality — real or implied competing interest — and to restructure rather than discount: sell a partial stake or a phased purchase at your price rather than the whole thing at theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** they have declared intent to buy; you have any alternative buyer, even a casual one.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are manufacturing competition you do not have and can be caught out on; when delay costs you a live deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a business-sale worked example plus the Watson standoff, where the desire was to be heard.

### [VOSS:LEVNEG] Label negative leverage instead of threatening
**Move:** Identify what they fear losing — audience, status, reputation, an on-time-payment record — from their own oblique remarks or from a third party who knows them, then make it visible with a label rather than a threat.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, breached commitments, stalled implementations where loss aversion is the only lever left.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be making a direct threat — the book calls those nuclear, with residue that poisons the process; and whenever it would be experienced as loss of autonomy, which triggers shutdown (the harder the push, the more resistance).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — hostage and consulting practice; the loss-aversion basis is asserted with reference to unnamed psychology in the move itself, though the book elsewhere cites prospect theory.

### [VOSS:LEVNORM] Normative leverage from their own rules
**Move:** Learn the standards, valuation formulas and moral rules the counterpart says they live by, then frame your ask inside them — including pointing out inconsistency between their stated beliefs and their proposed action. If they mention paying a certain multiple of cash flow, price your ask in that multiple.
**APPLY WHEN:** counterparts with articulated policies, published values, professional codes, or a stated valuation method.
**AVOID WHEN:** it lands as calling them a hypocrite; when their rule was invented on the spot as a negotiating device.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Watson standoff (military honour rules) plus the valuation-multiple example.

### [VOSS:BECAUSE] Give a reason, framed in their worldview
**Move:** Attach a because-clause to requests, delivered in a reasonable tone. On substantive issues, make the reason reference the counterpart's own norms — for example declining a lowball by citing a duty of responsible stewardship to someone who thinks in those terms.
**APPLY WHEN:** small procedural asks (scheduling, access, sequencing) and as the framing for refusals on larger ones.
**AVOID WHEN:** complex or high-stakes issues where an empty reason is transparent — the compliance effect in the cited experiment came from trivial requests.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** external-research (component) — grounded in Langer, Blank & Chanowitz (1978) copy-machine line-cutting experiments; the worldview-framing extension is the author's.

### [VOSS:STATUSTRADE] Buy the blocker's standing instead of cutting price
**Move:** When resistance traces to someone's personal exposure rather than budget, hold price and offer a term that protects them — the book's example guarantees a 90-day installation so the buyer's review four months out is not endangered.
**APPLY WHEN:** you detect timing fear, reputational risk, or an internal review cycle behind the hesitation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the constraint really is budget; or the term becomes a promise you cannot keep — missing a guaranteed window converts a status gift into the exact loss you were protecting against.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single illustrative accounting-system example in the appendix.

### [VOSS:LABELASK] Label their expertise, then hand them the problem
**Move:** Once the negatives are on the table, label the counterpart's competence or track record, then ask how they would restructure the deal so everyone still makes money. Credited as the expert, they tend to propose the concession themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** you need a concession you cannot demand; a committee member with pride of ownership over the current structure.
**AVOID WHEN:** they propose something worse than your target and you are now anchored to their idea; flattery that lands as insincere kills the rest of the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one contract renegotiation with a self-reported seven-figure swing.

### [VOSS:SALPERSIST] Pleasant persistence on non-cash terms
**Move:** In compensation or rate talks, keep pressing cheerfully on non-cash terms; the persistence surfaces the full option set and often produces a cash counter when the non-cash item turns out to be impossible.
**APPLY WHEN:** offer-stage hiring; rate negotiations with a constrained buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered without warmth, where it becomes nagging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one student who traded a vacation request into a higher salary offer.

## Stage F — Commitment and execution

### [VOSS:RULE3] The Rule of Three
**Move:** Get agreement on the same point three times in one conversation, varying the instrument so it does not sound like nagging: the initial commitment; a label or summary that pulls a "that's right"; a calibrated how/what question about implementation. Alternatively, the same question asked three different ways.
**APPLY WHEN:** you suspect a counterfeit yes; before allocating delivery resources; after a suspiciously fast agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is already strained by scrutiny; low-stakes transactional buys where the friction costs more than the risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — trained FBI and consulting practice; the claim that it is hard to lie three times is asserted, not measured.

### [VOSS:CONGRUE] Label the mismatch between words and delivery
**Move:** Watch tone and body language against the literal words. When a yes arrives with hesitation, name the incongruence aloud — you heard the yes but it sounded hesitant, and it matters that this be gotten right. The label surfaces the real objection and reads as respect rather than pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** confirming agreement, especially in person or on video; anywhere a soft yes could become a silent no later.
**AVOID WHEN:** text or email-only channels with no paraverbal signal; cross-cultural reads where your baseline for hesitation is unreliable; with Analysts, who sound flat by default.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the technique is the author's; the 7/38/55 split comes from Mehrabian's liking-and-attitude studies, which the author admits were not about negotiation, so discount any variant leaning on the ratio.

### [VOSS:IMPLQ] Implementation-defining questions to lock buy-in
**Move:** Before agreeing, make them define success: ask how we will know we are on track and how we handle it if we drift. Then summarize their answer back until you get "that's right" — their own words become the implementation plan, and the questions force their internal coordination.
**APPLY WHEN:** late stage, right before or right after verbal agreement; any deal where delivery, onboarding or rollout risk sits on the buyer's side.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart has no authority to define implementation (you are talking to a gatekeeper); very early discovery, where it reads as presumptive closing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing practice in the author's business negotiations plus the Ecuador case; no dataset.

### [VOSS:BTT] Map and influence the players behind the table
**Move:** Assume a team stands behind your counterpart and build influence that reaches them. Ask how the deal lands on the rest of their team, how bought-in the people not on this call are, what their colleagues see as the main challenge here, and how we make sure the right material reaches the right people. Treat a change of negotiator on their side as a signal of a hardening position, and account for whoever was displaced — they can still break the deal. Get absent blockers named, and ideally invited in.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where implementation runs through a committee; you are talking only to an executive sponsor; a new procurement contact appears mid-cycle; champion turnover; channel deals with hidden principals.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuine single decision-maker with budget authority and no delivery dependency — the questions then manufacture phantom stakeholders and stall momentum; when raising it insults a buyer who has asserted sole authority (soften to a label); and going around your contact reads as betrayal, since the move is about influence reaching them, not bypassing them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Dos Palmas, where a displaced negotiator with physical control of the hostages destroyed a completed $300,000 payment, plus the author's own lost training deal, killed by a division head nobody had asked about.

### [VOSS:SUCCESSTERMS] Attach success metrics to the number
**Move:** Once compensation or scope is agreed, define what success looks like and the metrics that trigger the next increase — free for the counterpart to grant, and it schedules the next negotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** offer close; consulting engagements; expansion agreements where the next uplift needs a pre-agreed trigger.
**AVOID WHEN:** the metrics would be set by someone who cannot control them, which converts the tool into a trap.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — taught to MBA classes; illustrated by the Angel Prado case.

### [VOSS:COLLECT8] Eight-step sequence for a disputed unpaid invoice
**Move:** Run in order: a No-oriented email to reopen contact; a statement engineered for "that's right" about their view of the bill; a calibrated question asking how the bill violates the agreement; a battery of No-oriented questions removing unspoken accusations; labeling and mirroring any unacceptable answer back at them; a calibrated question to any partial offer asking how you are supposed to accept it; a label flattering their self-image as a principled operator; then a long pause and a final No-oriented question about whether they want a reputation for not honouring agreements.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, disputed final invoices, a counterpart gone silent after part-payment.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot regulate your own emotions — the book's own case failed at step one when anger took over; also inappropriate where legal process has begun or the counterpart genuinely disputes delivery quality on the merits.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author claims roughly a 90% success rate for scripts of this shape; self-reported, no denominator, conditional on composure, and not quotable as a benchmark.
