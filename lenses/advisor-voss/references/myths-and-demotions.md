# advisor-voss — myths and demotions

What this tradition itself debunks, what must be demoted inside it, and the numbers that circulate as facts but are self-report. Read this file whenever the lens is about to make a confident claim, and whenever another lens quotes "Voss says…".

---

## 1. Myths this tradition explicitly kills

- **The yes ladder.** Chaining small agreements to reach the order is presented as actively counterproductive: in the book's worked case every step succeeded and the final answer was still no. Any council recommendation built on agreement momentum is refuted from inside this lens.
- **"Yes" as a win.** Counterfeit (an escape hatch), confirmation (reflex affirmation) and commitment yeses sound nearly identical. Verbal agreement, praise and gratitude are not evidence of change — being thanked and praised by the counterpart is graded as a *bad* outcome, because it means they own nothing ([VOSS:YESTRIAGE]).
- **"You're right" and "I'll try."** The first ends an unwanted conversation while changing nothing; the second is a plan to fail. Both are failure alarms, not progress ([VOSS:RIGHTALARM]).
- **Separate the people from the problem.** Impossible when the emotion *is* the problem. The rational-problem-solving school is named as having measurably failed FBI negotiators after Ruby Ridge and Waco — this rejection is the tradition's founding act.
- **BATNA-centric preparation.** Rehearsing the walk-away makes it the psychological focal point and silently caps the ask; "win-win goals" often function as wimp-win, a modest target defended because victory is easy to declare ([VOSS:ANTIBATNA], [VOSS:GOALHIGH]). Note the demotion below — this correction is itself only an assertion.
- **ZOPA-style rational bargaining models** are dismissed as academic. The counter-model is emotional and anchor-driven. Treat this as a school-level disagreement, not a settled finding.
- **Splitting the difference** is the predictable lacklustre default the Ackerman ladder exists to beat — one black shoe and one brown ([VOSS:NOSPLIT]).
- **Hiding your deadline** is named as the worst available position: you concede faster while they wait, i.e. you negotiate against yourself. Deadlines are described as nearly always self-inflicted and flexible ([VOSS:DEADOPEN]).
- **The Golden Rule in negotiation.** Treat people as *they* need to be treated. The "I am normal" projection error is the root failure that makes type diagnosis necessary; blunt assertiveness is counterproductive most of the time ([VOSS:TYPEID]).
- **The poker face**, and softening criticism by denying the negative ("I don't want to sound harsh"), which lends the negative credence ([VOSS:NEGFIRST]).
- **Anger as a tool** — almost never productive, including your own, because it degrades judgement while inflating confidence. Faked anger produces intractable demands and destroys trust; anger that "works" still buys concessions that fail in implementation ([VOSS:UMBRAGE]).
- **Threats** are a last-resort weapon with lasting contamination, not a tool. Direct pressure produces resistance — the harder the push, the more the counterpart shuts down ([VOSS:LEVNEG]).
- **Calling the counterpart crazy** is a research failure, not a diagnosis, and guarantees no deal ([VOSS:CRAZYTRIAGE]).
- **Over-using the counterpart's name** is a salesman's tell that buyers are tired of ([VOSS:OWNNAME]). Conversely, apologizing-as-weakness is rejected: "I'm sorry" is load-bearing in the No-series.
- **Parroting instead of paraphrasing.** Repeat their concern in *your* words, not theirs, or they hear the technique. The breakthrough is supposed to be invisible; noticed, it is gone.
- **Formal meetings as the place to learn things.** They are the least revealing face time; email-first negotiating suppresses Black Swans by giving the counterpart time to re-centre ([VOSS:FRINGE], [VOSS:FACETIME]).
- **Wimp-win deals.** Accepting the first acceptable counter and folding early to avoid conflict is named as the default failure mode across business and family alike. Avoiding honest conflict — not losing it — is what destroys outcomes.

## 2. Demotions inside the lens — claims that must be down-weighted

- **7 / 38 / 55.** Words / tone / body comes from Mehrabian's studies of *liking and attitude*, and the author concedes they were not negotiation research. Use [VOSS:CONGRUE] as an attention heuristic only. Any quantified claim about how much of a message is nonverbal is REFUTED for this lens.
- **~3 Black Swans per side.** The author's hypothesis, never measured. Keep the hunting habit ([VOSS:BS3]), drop the number.
- **"Why is accusatory in every language."** A universal claim from field impression, with no cross-cultural evidence. Culture is a footnote in this book, and reads of hesitation, silence and refusal are exactly where these tools misfire.
- **"They will never notice."** Detection is assumed away, not demonstrated. This is the most-read title in its category and its literal sentence stems circulate widely; ritual delivery is recognizable. Any advice premised on invisibility gets a hard discount in a repeat-game B2B relationship.
- **The waiter-mirroring tipping figure.** Attributed in-book to Wiseman without a verifiable reference. Do not cite it as data for [VOSS:MIRROR].
- **The "decades of goal-setting research"** invoked for [VOSS:GOALHIGH] names nothing. The move is practice; the research backing is assertion.
- **[VOSS:ANTIBATNA] itself.** The reframe is useful and entirely unsourced — no study named in the appendix. Do not present it to a council as a research-backed correction of Harvard-school preparation.
- **[VOSS:PRONOUN].** The author concedes it is retrospective pattern-fitting from two hostage cases. Never let a linguistic tell override org-chart evidence; treat it as a hypothesis to test.
- **Agreement between Camp's right-to-veto and this book's No-doctrine** is one school talking to itself. Same for any other tactical-empathy or crisis-negotiation source. It is echo, not independent confirmation.

## 3. Numbers that must never be quoted as fact

All of these are self-reported by the author or his firm, with no denominator, no control and no independent replication.

- **23% better return** from a No-oriented fundraising script versus a yes-pattern script — one student's test-market group, sample size unstated.
- **~90% success rate** for the disputed-invoice script family ([VOSS:COLLECT8]) — no denominator, and conditional on the negotiator staying calm, which is precisely the variable that failed in the book's own case.
- **7:1 return** on preparation time versus later renegotiation — vendor self-report (Black Swan Group). Never cite as a benchmark.
- **">90% of open-heart surgery patients had changed nothing two years later"** — attributed to "study after study", no named source.
- **Case outcomes** — a $150,000 ransom demand walked to $4,751 in a day; a $5M demand settled at $16,500; a buyout ladder ending at €23,567; a $36,000 sticker paid at $30,000; a lease anchored at $1,730 accepted at $1,829; a $4.3M asking price closed at $3.6M; contractors cut from $2,000/day to $500/day; a seven-figure swing on one renegotiation; a $130,000 commission claim reversed by an outside audit. Every one is a resolved case retold by its protagonist. Usable as an illustration of a move's *shape*, never as an expected value.
- **Externally sourced numbers that ARE quotable** (with their real provenance, not as the book's findings): Langer/Blank/Chanowitz ~60% versus >90% compliance on trivial requests; Miller (1956) ~7 items of conscious attention; the lawyer-negotiator survey across two US cities reporting 65% cooperative and 24% truly assertive styles, with >75% of those graded effective being cooperative against 12% assertive — cited by footnote without a name in the text, so attribute cautiously.

## 4. Documented failure modes of the techniques

These are recorded in the source itself, which makes them the most reliable content in the lens.

- **Ritual delivery.** The sentence stems ([VOSS:LABELFILL], [VOSS:FOLLOWLABEL], [VOSS:STANDBYQ]) fired in sequence become audible. The book's own instruction — do not parrot, do not label every answer, do not read the question bank as a checklist — is an admission that the failure mode is the default under pressure.
- **Emotional regulation collapse.** [VOSS:BITETONGUE] is the binding constraint: the book's marketing strategist executed the opening perfectly and lost the deal ten minutes later on the phone. [VOSS:COLLECT8] failed at step one for the same reason. A user who cannot hold composure should not be handed this toolkit at all.
- **Anchoring too fast.** The Charleston property case is preserved as a failure: the student anchored before running the labels that would have made the broker bid against himself.
- **Confrontational mode in a long engagement.** Dos Palmas is diagnosed in-source as the root cause of a 13-month failure that ended with two hostages dead — and separately as proof that a *displaced* negotiator can destroy a completed agreement ([VOSS:BTT]).
- **Repetition against the same person.** [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED] is called passive-aggressive when reused on one counterpart; [VOSS:DISAGREE4] erodes candour; [VOSS:GHOSTMAIL] is one-shot by construction.
- **Wrong-audience misfire.** Appeals, forced empathy and emotional framing land badly with an Analyst or any evaluator scoring measurable criteria — [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED] and [VOSS:EMODRIVER] both misfire there by the book's admission. [VOSS:MISLABEL] with an Analyst is logged as your error and ends engagement.
- **Written-channel liabilities.** [VOSS:AUDIT], [VOSS:MEACULPA], [VOSS:ACCANCHOR] and [VOSS:MISLABEL] become quotable evidence in writing. [VOSS:NOSETUP] loses its tone and reads as a threat.
- **Compliance and procurement collisions.** [VOSS:GIFTSHOCK] can breach gift policy outright; non-answers ([VOSS:THIRDPRICE], [VOSS:NAMEFIRST]) disqualify you in scored RFPs; a 65% Ackerman opener reads as unserious in a committee consensus sale. The book addresses none of this.
- **A doctrine that beat orthodoxy and still never shipped.** A measurably improved fundraising script was never rolled out because it violated institutional convention — preserved in-source as a warning that being right is not adoption.

## 5. Anti-recommendations — do not do this, with the reason

1. **Do not run this lens as a negotiation strategy.** It allocates nothing, models no buying process, and cannot tell you *which* deals to work — only what to say inside one. Strategy belongs to another layer; using this one for it is the lens's largest documented overreach.
2. **Do not apply [VOSS:NOSPLIT] to a multi-issue deal.** The book has no concept of logrolling: it treats every gap as single-axis distributive conflict and attacks compromise without distinguishing it from trading across issues. Refusing a split when a trade is available destroys value rather than protecting it.
3. **Do not deploy the full stem set on a long-cycle, repeat-relationship account.** The toolkit is optimized for one-shot extraction from a counterpart you will not meet again. Recognition cost compounds across renewals, references and word-of-mouth — which is where B2B actually lives.
4. **Do not use manipulation-adjacent moves the user would not show the buyer.** [VOSS:MISLABEL], [VOSS:GIFTSHOCK], extreme anchoring and accusation audits are manipulation-adjacent by the author's own admission, and the book's only ethical safeguard is a stance (human irrationality is patterned; seek connection first), not a control. The council's ethical gate overrides here.
5. **Do not assume your clock is cheaper than theirs.** Attrition ladders, deliberate slowness and no-counteroffer refusals ([VOSS:NAMEFIRST], [VOSS:NOSERIES], [VOSS:SLOWDOWN]) all rest on that assumption, which in quota-carrying sales is usually false. There is no cost-of-time model anywhere in the source.
6. **Do not transpose the hostage frame without flagging it.** The originating profile — a counterpart in crisis, no coherent demand, no alternative supplier, no next quarter — is precisely what a normal B2B buyer is not. This transposition is the lens's largest untested assumption and should be named out loud in any verdict that leans on it.
7. **Do not treat the prep overhead as free on small deals.** One sheet, rehearsal, second listener and Black Swan hunting exceed the value of a micro-ACV transaction; the book itself warns against endless discovery on a small deal.
