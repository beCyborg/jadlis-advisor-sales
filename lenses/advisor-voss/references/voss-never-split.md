# Never Split the Difference — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens's language micro-layer: what to say, in what tone, in the next sixty seconds of a live conversation, when the counterpart is emotional, defensive, or holding a position that logic will not move. Its mechanism claim is System-1-first — shape how the counterpart feels about the offer and their stated reasoning follows — so it beats every other source in the council on de-escalation, objection handling, silence discipline, and extracting information a rational-actor frame would never surface. It is deliberately a *tactic* layer: it does not allocate value across issues, does not model procurement, and does not build pipeline. Use it inside a deal strategy owned elsewhere.

## Core model

Five stacked ideas, chapter-anchored:

- **Tactical empathy (Ch. 2–3)** — recognize the counterpart's perspective *and* voice that recognition, aimed at the driver under the presenting emotion. Mechanism: naming a fear drains it. Delivery outranks argument; three voices (late-night FM DJ, positive/playful, assertive-almost-never).
- **"No" over "Yes" (Ch. 4)** — three yeses exist (counterfeit, confirmation, commitment); chasing yes manufactures the counterfeit. A refusal restores felt autonomy, which is the precondition for real examination. Credited to Jim Camp's right-to-veto.
- **"That's right" over "You're right" (Ch. 5)** — the target state is felt understanding, delivered by summary = paraphrase + label, running the Behavioral Change Stairway (active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change) rooted in Rogers.
- **Bending reality and the illusion of control (Ch. 6–7)** — prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky 1979): loss aversion and the certainty effect make value positional. Calibrated what/how questions convert demands into requests for help, so the counterpart proposes your solution (Dutton: remove unbelief rather than install belief).
- **Execution, style and Black Swans (Ch. 8–10 + Appendix)** — "yes" is nothing without "how"; three negotiator types (Analyst / Accommodator / Assertive) with the "I am normal" projection error; three leverage kinds (positive / negative / normative); ~3 unknown unknowns per side; the Ackerman ladder (65/85/95/100 + odd number + noncash sweetener); the one-sheet prep format that demotes BATNA from centerpiece to boundary.

## Moves

### Stage A — Preparation and stance

### [VOSS:ONESHEET] Five-section negotiation one sheet, not a script
**Move:** Pre-write a single page: (1) the ambitious high-end goal, (2) a two-sentence factual summary of how we got here, (3) three to five labels forming an accusation audit, (4) three to five calibrated questions, (5) the noncash items the other side could give. Add the research that justifies the target and the Ackerman ladder if a price fight is coming. Carry it in — and allow yourself to break sequence when you clearly hold control, because under pressure you drop to your automatic preparation level rather than rising to the moment.
**APPLY WHEN:** any real negotiation stage — pricing call, renewal, contract redline, a multi-stakeholder meeting where you get one live shot.
**AVOID WHEN:** you extend it into a turn-by-turn script; the book states outright that a fully scripted flow makes you rigid, readable and exploitable by a more agile counterpart.

**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the exercise the author's firm assigns every client, plus the classroom slogan and the Mishary lease case; the attached 7:1 payback figure is vendor self-report.

### [VOSS:GOALHIGH] Write down only the ambitious goal, never the floor
**Move:** Reason through both extremes privately, then commit to paper a single optimistic-but-defensible number, define it concretely, say it aloud to a colleague so backing down costs face, and bring the written version with you.
**APPLY WHEN:** you notice yourself anchoring internally on the minimum you would accept; before any price or salary conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have hard information the ambitious figure is unachievable and voicing it would end the relationship; also do not treat the written goal as a ceiling — stay open to more than you knew to ask for.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — client-practice prescription; the "decades of goal-setting research" support is unnamed, so treat that part as assertion.

### [VOSS:ANTIBATNA] Demote the walk-away from centerpiece to boundary
**Move:** Keep a known walk-away as a line you refuse to cross, but stop rehearsing it as the plan; spend your prep attention on the ambitious outcome instead, because whatever number you rehearsed most becomes the psychological focal point under stress.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal reviews where the team has spent most of its prep modeling the fallback or the discount floor.
**AVOID WHEN:** alternatives are genuinely thin and ruin risk is real — this reorders attention, it does not abolish the floor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from a general claim about limited attention under stress; no study named in the appendix.

### [VOSS:SUMPREP] Pre-write the two-sentence shared-facts summary
**Move:** Draft in advance a compact neutral account of the history that produced this conversation, including their version of it, open the call by delivering it, and wait for confirmation before touching terms. The pass/fail test is behavioral: they confirm the description is right, or the summary is wrong.
**APPLY WHEN:** renewals, escalations, and renegotiations with history; any call where the two sides carry different stories about how things got here.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes your own position dressed as neutrality — a self-serving summary earns polite agreement and destroys the diagnostic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard prescription in the firm's client exercise.

### [VOSS:AUDIT] Accusation audit
**Move:** List every worst thing the other side could accuse you of, however unfair — price gouging, past failure, self-interest — compress them into at most five "it seems like…" statements, rehearse them aloud, and open the meeting by saying them yourself in their voice before they can be levelled. Spoken out loud they sound exaggerated, and the counterpart tends to deny them and reach for common ground.
**APPLY WHEN:** delivering a price increase, a scope cut, bad news, or a renegotiation after a soured relationship; stored grievance from a bad prior implementation or a missed date; win-back and save conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** the accusations are true and legally material — you are handing over admissions; no real negative exists to name, so the self-accusation reads as a technique; stacking more than a handful turns into self-flagellating theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Anna's contractor renegotiation, self-reported; underlying affect-labeling support (Lieberman et al., 2007) is cited in the book's own notes for the label mechanism, not for the audit.

### [VOSS:REHEARSE] Rehearse the audit with a partner and an observer
**Move:** Role-play both sides of the coming meeting with a colleague — one plays the counterpart, the other disarms with the prepared labels — while a third person watches. Decide in advance where each label lands and where the pauses go.
**APPLY WHEN:** a single high-value meeting with an adversarial counterpart and a pre-writable set of accusations.
**AVOID WHEN:** rehearsal turns into recital — over-scripted delivery is precisely what makes these tools sound fake in a live room.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one prepared negotiation.

### [VOSS:LABELFILL] Keep fill-in-the-blank label stems loaded
**Move:** Carry a handful of reusable stems — it seems X matters to you / it seems you dislike X / it seems X would make this easier / it seems you are hesitant about X — so you can drop the right one in without composing under time pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** live calls where you must respond in real time and cannot pause to phrase carefully.
**AVOID WHEN:** fired mechanically in sequence — these stems are the most recognizable surface of this book, and repeated verbatim they read as script, which is the failure mode that damages long-cycle relationships.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — templates the firm issues to clients.

### [VOSS:STANDBYQ] Standing bank of what/how questions
**Move:** Keep three to five reusable open questions ready: what are we actually trying to achieve here; what is the core issue; what is the biggest challenge you face; what about this is important to you; how does this fit the objective; how can I make this better for us both; how would you like me to proceed; what brought this situation about; how can the problem be solved.
**APPLY WHEN:** first discovery calls; reopening a stalled deal; any point where the conversation has collapsed into positions and counter-positions.
**AVOID WHEN:** read aloud as a checklist — sequence without listening produces an interrogation; and when the questions are steering toward a foregone conclusion, which the counterpart hears.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own recurring set, repeated in client and teaching use across many negotiations.

### [VOSS:NONCASH-PREP] Inventory their cheap-but-valuable assets before the call
**Move:** List in advance the non-money things the counterpart could hand over — introductions, a case study, logo rights, publication, data, a reference, faster access, scope or timing trades — and bring the list, so you have somewhere to move when price is capped. Framing question: what could they give that would nearly make you do the work for free?
**APPLY WHEN:** their budget is genuinely constrained or their internal win is "spent less cash"; also whenever a hard discount request is expected.
**AVOID WHEN:** the noncash item carries real cost or risk to them (reference logos, exclusivity) — then it is a concession to be priced, not a free trade.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/saas/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the lawyers'-association magazine-cover trade is the sole example given.

### [VOSS:HYPOTH] Treat your read of the account as testable hypotheses
**Move:** Write down what you believe about the buyer's motive, authority and constraints as explicit hypotheses, then use each call to disconfirm them; discard on new evidence instead of defending the original read.
**APPLY WHEN:** entering any account, especially where the intel came from one source; when early "obvious" reads are already driving the plan.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating everything as open indefinitely stalls qualification — time-box it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing FBI doctrine, illustrated by the 1993 Brooklyn bank case where every initial assumption proved wrong.

### [VOSS:TYPEID] Diagnose the counterpart's bargaining type first
**Move:** Classify as Analyst (time = preparation; silence = thinking; hypersensitive to reciprocity; hates surprises), Accommodator (time = relationship; silence = anger; great rapport with nothing accomplished; hides objections), or Assertive (time = money; silence = your turn to shut up; cannot listen until they feel heard) using pace, preparation depth, emotional display, voice quality, appetite for small talk, and above all what their silence means. Assume you are probably not facing your own type.
**APPLY WHEN:** before the first price conversation, and after any interaction that felt inexplicably off.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have a single data point; misreading an Analyst's coldness as hostility or an Accommodator's silence as thinking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the firm's consolidation of archetype literature cross-referenced with field and business-school cases; no validation study offered.

### [VOSS:PLAYTYPE] Type-matched tactics
**Move:** With an Analyst — prepare heavily, lead with data and comparisons, warn early about problems, avoid surprises, do not open with a question barrage, let silence run, expect delayed counterproposals. With an Accommodator — be sociable, let them talk, convert talk to action with implementation questions, dig actively for the objections they are hiding, verify they can deliver what they cheerfully promised. With an Assertive — let them be heard first, then mirror, label, summarize toward "that's right", and expect fast reciprocity demands in their own favor. Correct your own default too: Analysts smile while speaking, Accommodators keep their objections and cut chitchat, Assertives deliberately soften tone.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after the type read, for the whole bargaining phase.
**AVOID WHEN:** committee calls with several types present — one register cannot serve all three; and when type-play substitutes for substance in data-driven procurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — firm practice; illustrated by an Assertive who was mirrored for nearly an hour without noticing.

### [VOSS:NOSCRIPT] Sequence by situation, not by playbook order
**Move:** Refuse the fixed offer/counteroffer choreography. Hold several live hypotheses about what the other side wants and pick the next tool by what just happened, jumping steps freely.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deal is volatile, the buyer's stated demand does not match their behavior, or your standard sequence has stalled.
**AVOID WHEN:** a team needs a repeatable coachable process; regulated or committee-driven procurement where deviating from a documented process is itself a risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as the lesson of FBI experience, no data given.

### [VOSS:LISTENTEAM] Put more than one listener on the important call
**Move:** Staff the call with a lead speaker plus one or more people whose only job is to listen — take notes, gauge mood, flag tells, pass silent input, and afterwards compare what each of them heard, since a single listener always misses material.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-stakes calls; complex or hostile counterparts; anything you intend to dissect afterwards.
**AVOID WHEN:** small or early conversations where a second attendee signals disproportionate pressure or intimidates a solo buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing FBI protocol, justified by the claim that listening well is genuinely hard.

### Stage B — Opening and access

### [VOSS:BADTIME] "Is now a bad time?" instead of "got a minute?"
**Move:** Open with a question whose natural answer is a refusal — ask whether this is a bad moment — rather than asking permission for their time. Both branches are useful: an affirmative gets you a real slot or a clean exit, a refusal gets you their attention.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold or semi-cold calls, unscheduled interruptions, follow-ups into a busy account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the meeting is already booked and the framing sounds falsely tentative; with buyers who have heard the trick and will name it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard teaching point for sellers; no measured comparison given.

### [VOSS:NOPERMIT] Grant explicit permission to refuse
**Move:** Say up front that a refusal is a completely acceptable outcome of this conversation and that you will not push past one — then let that stand without immediately qualifying it away.
**APPLY WHEN:** first contact, cold outreach, or any moment the counterpart looks cornered or over-pitched; also when a previously warm buyer has gone stiff.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart has already committed and you are in execution or paperwork — reopening the veto invites re-litigation; also avoid stacking it with hard pressure elsewhere in the same call, which reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — taught to students and used across hostage cases; derived from Camp's right-to-veto framing, not tested.

### [VOSS:OWNNAME] Use your own first name, not theirs
**Move:** Instead of hammering their name, introduce yourself by first name in a light friendly way and ask for the concession by that name ("is there a Chris discount?"). Humanizing yourself creates forced empathy and gives them a reason to bend a rule for a person rather than a transaction.
**APPLY WHEN:** first contact, gatekeepers, front-line staff, list-price situations, de-escalating hostility.
**AVOID WHEN:** formal enterprise procurement where whimsy reads as unserious; when the counterpart controls no discretionary discount and the ask embarrasses them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Kansas bar de-escalation and a 10% outlet-store discount; entertaining, not systematic.

### [VOSS:RIFF] Label → mirror → label → ask
**Move:** Open on someone under pressure by labelling the negative they just endured, mirror their reply to draw out detail, label again to acknowledge how they handled it, and only then make the request — framed as riding on the information they volunteered. Stay silent after the ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold asks to gatekeepers, support staff, or anyone just mistreated by the previous person in line; walking in after a competitor or colleague has burned the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** there is no relationship time available; a counterpart with no discretion to grant the ask regardless of rapport.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the airline gate-agent story, one instance, resolved in under two minutes.

### [VOSS:YOUSCRIPT] Rewrite the outreach around them
**Move:** Take the standard demand-for-payment or follow-up template and reframe it: possessive framing that makes the relationship theirs, explicit acknowledgment of the pressure they are under, an offer to work with their specific situation, and a request to talk it through — with no discount attached.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, overdue renewals, re-engaging accounts that have stopped replying; any templated outreach getting silence.
**AVOID WHEN:** the acknowledgment is not backed by real flexibility — hollow empathy plus an unchanged demand reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Washington Redskins season-ticket collections case, self-reported.

### [VOSS:GHOSTMAIL] One-line ghosting email
**Move:** Break silence with a single short question implying they have abandoned the project — have you given up on settling this / on finalizing this deal this year — which they can only rebut by re-engaging, and which simultaneously signals you are prepared to walk. If even that draws nothing, treat the silence itself as data pointing at a constraint they are embarrassed to admit, and start investigating their situation through third parties.
**APPLY WHEN:** two or more unanswered follow-ups; a previously responsive contact goes dark after a proposal, especially against a budget-year boundary.
**AVOID WHEN:** the delay has a known cause (holiday, budget freeze, reorg) — the implied accusation looks careless; the relationship is early and cold, where loss framing closes the door; and never twice on the same contact, its power is in being one-shot.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the firm's classic email that "always works", which is self-report; the chapter's own worked case is the one where it failed.

### [VOSS:ASKADVICE] Ask for suggestions instead of stating requirements
**Move:** Describe what you are looking for and request recommendations rather than announcing requirements; then, instead of a hard offer, say the price exceeds your budget and ask for their help.
**APPLY WHEN:** buying side, vendor selection, any counterpart whose expertise can be flattered into cooperation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart is compensated purely on price and has no discretion — the request wastes both parties' time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a general-purpose consumer and vendor tactic.

### [VOSS:MENTORQ] "What does it take to be successful here?"
**Move:** Ask that exact wording of an interviewer or an executive sponsor; whoever answers acquires a stake in your following their advice and tends to become an informal mentor and advocate.
**APPLY WHEN:** interviews; first meetings with an executive sponsor; partner onboarding.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no intention of acting on the answer — the stake they acquire cuts both ways.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=stage/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated student reports that interviewers said nobody had asked before; the wording is claimed to be load-bearing.

### Stage C — Discovery, listening and diagnosis

### [VOSS:MIRROR] The three-word mirror
**Move:** Repeat back the last three words — or the one to three words carrying the weight — of what they just said, with a curious questioning lift, then stop talking. The intent to convey is "help me understand"; they restate it differently and add material.
**APPLY WHEN:** you want clarification without interrogating; their tone contradicts their words; you need them to keep talking, or need a beat to regroup; objection handling.
**AVOID WHEN:** you step on it with a follow-up question, which kills the effect; mechanical repetition of every sentence becomes obvious and irritating.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — FBI-wide practice plus an in-book waiter-tipping study attributed to Richard Wiseman, cited without a verifiable reference.

### [VOSS:LABEL] Label the emotion with an impersonal stem
**Move:** Name what you observe them feeling using a neutral observation stem — it seems / it sounds like / it looks like — never a first-person stem, then go silent and let them respond. If they reject the label, retreat to the fact that you only said it seemed that way.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is tense or hedging, or their tone contradicts their words; a stalled deal with an unnamed objection.
**AVOID WHEN:** you smother it with your own follow-up question; the label targets surface behavior rather than the driver, which merely annoys; "I" phrasing, which triggers defensiveness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated field and classroom use, supported in-book by a cited UCLA brain-imaging study (Lieberman) on naming emotions.

### [VOSS:UNDERLYING] Label the driver, not the behavior
**Move:** Separate presenting behavior from the underlying feeling and address the second. Voice the underlying state non-judgmentally, pause to let it register, then offer the constructive reframe.
**APPLY WHEN:** a counterpart is picky, cranky, or rejecting every option without a coherent reason.
**AVOID WHEN:** you guess the driver wrong and sound presumptuous about their inner life; the objection is genuinely technical.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Girl Scouts fundraiser case, where two successive labels closed a donation with no project selected.

### [VOSS:WMD] Words, music and dance
**Move:** Read emotion from three channels at once — words, vocal tone, body — and specifically from the *changes* that occur immediately after you say something. A flat voice on a colleague's name, a downturned mouth, restless feet: treat each as a cue to probe.
**APPLY WHEN:** live calls and meetings, especially when you need to find what nobody has said out loud.
**AVOID WHEN:** email or async deals with no signal channel; over-reading single cues — the book itself compares the skill to how psychics work, which cuts both ways.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — instructional doctrine from the author's course.

### [VOSS:NEGFIRST] Neutralize the negative before selling the positive
**Move:** Go at the negative dynamic directly and early — voice their fear or the awkwardness rather than tiptoeing — and only afterwards offer the positive framing or the ask. Label positives too, to reinforce them.
**APPLY WHEN:** the relationship has soured, communication has gone silent, or you are about to ask for something after friction.
**AVOID WHEN:** naming a negative they had not considered plants it; deals where the negative is contractual and naming it invites a legal position.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Harlem standoff, the Canada country-clearance call, and the Redskins collections case.

### [VOSS:ALSTACK] Active-listening stack
**Move:** Run the six tools together rather than singly — deliberate silence to drain emotion, minimal verbal encouragers to show attention, mirroring of their last words, labeling of the feeling, paraphrasing to prove comprehension, then a summary fusing the last two.
**APPLY WHEN:** entrenched positions repeated across several meetings; an emotional or aggrieved counterpart; discovery where the stated ask makes no economic sense.
**AVOID WHEN:** short transactional exchanges where the counterpart just wants a price; applied mechanically it becomes audible parroting, the exact failure paraphrase exists to prevent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the FBI unit's operating arsenal, deployed via a written two-page instruction in the Schilling case.

### [VOSS:SUMTRIG] Summary engineered to trigger "that's right"
**Move:** Rearticulate their entire version of the situation — including the parts you consider nonsense — plus the emotions attached, completely enough that no correction is available to them, and stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** deadlock on a demand that will not move under logic; a stated objection that has resisted three rebuttals; internal disagreement with a colleague before you attempt to redirect them.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not actually gathered their worldview yet — a thin summary produces "you're right" or a correction; also avoid summarizing a position you intend to publicly refute right afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Schilling case, where a four-month "war damages" demand evaporated after one summary, plus the author's son and a pharma-rep student.

### [VOSS:RIGHTALARM] Treat "you're right" and "I'll try" as failure alarms
**Move:** When agreement arrives phrased as you being right, or as an intention to try, treat the persuasion attempt as failed — "you're right" ends an unwanted conversation while changing nothing, "I'll try" is a plan to fail. Do not proceed to paperwork; go back to calibrated how-questions until they restate the terms of success in their own words, then summarize to a "that's right".
**APPLY WHEN:** you have just pushed for a commitment, a next step, or an internal action item on their side; call reviews and deal inspection; any suspiciously fast concession.
**AVOID WHEN:** the phrase is a genuine factual concession on a small verifiable point; also beware over-reading a single phrase in a second-language or heavily scripted conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — repeated coaching failures with the author's son, generalized into a field heuristic without measurement.

### [VOSS:RECAPOPEN] Open the next meeting with them, not your product
**Move:** Start the follow-up call by recalling what mattered to them last time — their mission, their patients, their constraints — as a labeled summary, let them expand, and only after the walls drop describe how your thing serves the outcome they just named.
**APPLY WHEN:** a hostile or dismissive technical buyer who cuts off feature talk; second and third meetings; incumbent-vendor situations.
**AVOID WHEN:** you learned nothing real in meeting one — recycled flattery is transparent; also weak with buyers who want a quote and nothing else.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pharma sales rep who converted the largest prescriber in her territory.

### [VOSS:WANTNEED] Talk wants to find needs
**Move:** Get them talking at length about what they want — aspirational and safe to voice — and use that volume of talk to infer the need, the minimum that would actually make them act, which they will not state directly.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery; a buyer whose stated ask is clearly a wish-list; before pricing anything.
**AVOID WHEN:** you mistake stated wants for the requirement and quote against them; time-boxed calls where the meander costs you the agenda.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as core FBI listening doctrine.

### [VOSS:ASKNOT] Ask what they would refuse
**Move:** Explicitly invite them to lay out what they would say no to, and treat that list as the working definition of the deal space.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery stalls on vague requirements; scoping a service or consulting engagement; committee deals where constraints are unspoken.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart is already in a negative spiral — inviting more refusals compounds it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as routine practice, unquantified.

### [VOSS:CALIBQ] Calibrated open questions
**Move:** Convert demands and closed asks into questions starting with what or how; strip out can/is/are/do/does, and drop who/when/where, which only harvest facts. Design each question toward a predetermined destination rather than asking openly at random.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery, objection handling, any point where a direct statement would provoke a defense; also as a de-escalation reflex when attacked.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart needs a decision or a fact from you and questions read as stalling; committee settings where endless open questions consume the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the unit's core post-2002 tool, taught widely; supporting theory borrowed from Dutton's *Split-Second Persuasion*, not tested by the author.

### [VOSS:QCLUSTER] Ask related questions in clusters of two or three
**Move:** Group two or three near-identical questions so the counterpart circles the same issue from different angles instead of answering once and closing the topic.
**APPLY WHEN:** the first answer was thin or rehearsed.
**AVOID WHEN:** short calls or an already-defensive counterpart — repetition of near-identical questions reads as pressure-testing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise

### [VOSS:SLOWDOWN] Deliberately slow the conversation

### [VOSS:DJVOICE] Late-night FM DJ voice

### [VOSS:PLAYFUL] Positive/playful voice as the default register

### [VOSS:BS3] Assume three hidden Black Swans and hunt them

### [VOSS:RELIGION] Reconstruct their "religion", and listen twice

### [VOSS:FRINGE] Mine the unguarded moments

### [VOSS:MISLABEL] Deliberate mislabel to trigger a correction

### [VOSS:PINOCCHIO] Lie markers in word volume and pronouns

### [VOSS:PRONOUN] Pronoun read for real authority

**APPLY WHEN:** mapping the buying committee; qualifying a champion's real power; when you cannot ask "are you the decision maker" without insulting them.

### [VOSS:RULEPROBE] Probe the assumed rule, then hypothesize their real motive

### Stage D — Objections, stalls and conflict

### [VOSS:NODECODE] Decode "no" before answering it
**Move:** Treat a refusal as one of roughly seven real messages — not ready, uncomfortable, does not understand, cannot afford it, wants something different, needs more information, needs to consult someone — then pause and respond either with a solution-seeking question (what about this does not work, what would it take to work) or with a plain label of what seems to be bothering them.
**APPLY WHEN:** any objection, especially the first one; late-stage stalls; silence after a proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** the refusal is a hard disqualification — no budget, no authority, wrong problem — where decoding becomes pestering.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter's central operating rule, repeated across cases.

### [VOSS:NONOGO] No refusal at all is a disqualification signal
**Move:** If, after deliberate attempts, the counterpart will never say no to anything, read it as indecision, confusion, or a hidden agenda, and end the pursuit.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline hygiene; deals that agree with everything and advance nothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart's culture or role makes explicit refusal costly — absence of "no" may then be politeness, not evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted as a pattern from repeated cases, no counts given.

### [VOSS:YESTRIAGE] Triage which "yes" you just heard
**Move:** Before forecasting, classify the agreement as counterfeit (an escape hatch), confirmation (reflex affirmation to a binary question), or commitment (the real one that ends in a signature). The three sound nearly identical, so test by asking for a next action rather than accepting the word.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of a call that felt too easy; enthusiastic buyers with no calendar movement; forecast reviews.
**AVOID WHEN:** the classification changes nothing unless you follow it with an implementation question — do not stop at labelling it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's taxonomy, illustrated by the crisis-hotline review episode.

### [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED] "How am I supposed to do that?"
**Move:** Answer a demand you cannot meet with a request for help rather than a refusal: summarize the situation, then ask how you are meant to comply — genuinely asking, no accusation in the delivery — and stay quiet while they solve your constraint. The claimed effect is forced empathy: they must inspect your position, and reciprocity pulls them toward improving their own offer. Keep re-asking variants of the same two or three questions as each new demand arrives.
**APPLY WHEN:** take-it-or-leave-it price or term demands; unpayable discount asks; unpaid invoices; scope demands beyond contract; especially strong right after they have asked you for something extra.
**AVOID WHEN:** you can and should simply say yes; you cannot keep your tone neutral, where it lands as the accusation it paraphrases; with an Analyst who needs data rather than appeals; in a collaborative multi-issue deal where trading across issues creates more value than stalling; and the book itself calls it passive-aggressive when repeated with the same person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas/product · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the standard reply to ransom demands across many kidnapping cases plus consulting stories (a PR consultant's unpaid invoice paid in 48 hours; Kelly the accounting consultant); "it never backfired" is the author's self-report, and the Harvard role-play version is a single self-reported exercise.

### [VOSS:MEACULPA] Front-load the apology as self-accusation
**Move:** When you caused the offence, open by accusing yourself in blunt, slightly comic terms before the other side gets to, and acknowledge their anger explicitly rather than explaining yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** you missed a deadline, went around someone's authority, or shipped a failure; the counterpart's status has been slighted.
**AVOID WHEN:** written channels where the self-accusation becomes evidence; repeat offences, where it starts to read as a substitute for fixing anything.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Canada country-clearance call and the author's habitual use.

### [VOSS:APOLWARM] Apology plus first name to blunt a bulldozer
**Move:** Prefix a resistant reply with a light apology and use the counterpart's first name, so the refusal lands as warmth rather than opposition, keeping the confrontation from hardening while you hold your line.
**APPLY WHEN:** the other side is escalating tone or trying to steamroll you; early in a contested exchange.
**AVOID WHEN:** cultures or buyers who read repeated apologizing as weakness; when the apology implies fault you should not concede in writing.

### [VOSS:DISAGREE4] Four-step disagreement without confrontation
**Move:** To push back on a dominant counterpart: DJ voice, a soft apology stem, a mirror of their instruction, then silence for at least four seconds — and repeat the cycle. They talk themselves through the flaws in their own request.
**APPLY WHEN:** an aggressive boss or buyer issues a poorly-thought-out demand; you need them to withdraw it without being seen to refuse.
**AVOID WHEN:** the request is reasonable; a counterpart who has read the same book and recognizes the ritual; repeated use with the same person erodes candour.

### [VOSS:CRAZYTRIAGE] Triage apparent irrationality
**Move:** When they act against their own interest, run three hypotheses in order: (1) bad information — find what they do not know and supply it, ideally through a neutral third party they trust; (2) constraints they will not disclose — legal advice, prior promises, precedent risk, or no authority; (3) hidden interests — fiscal timing, internal politics, fairness, promotion, an unannounced exit. Then negotiate against the real cause.
**APPLY WHEN:** ghosting after momentum, refusal of an objectively good offer, inexplicable deadlines or scope demands.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to rationalize a dead deal indefinitely — some counterparts are simply not buying.

### [VOSS:FACETIME] Buy face time outside the building
**Move:** Move the interaction off email and out of their office — invite them somewhere informal with an explicit promise not to discuss business, framed as a no-oriented question along the lines of whether it would be a bad idea to take them to their favorite steakhouse. Direct human contact is what lets an embarrassed contact admit a constraint and hand you to the right executive.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled deal, suspected hidden constraint, a contact whose authority you doubt.
**AVOID WHEN:** compliance-restricted buyers with gift or hospitality policies; travel cost exceeding deal value; remote-only relationships where insistence reads as pressure.

### [VOSS:WHYFLIP] Weaponized "why" that makes them defend your position
**Move:** Treat "why" as accusatory in every language and avoid it, with one exception: aim the defensiveness at the option that favors you — why would you ever switch to us, why would you ever leave a supplier who is doing great — delivered deferentially, so they argue your case for you.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive displacement; a wobbling prospect who needs to hear their own reasons for change.
**AVOID WHEN:** diagnosing past decisions or failures — reframe as "what caused…"; and when they may take the invitation and reaffirm the incumbent.

### [VOSS:BITETONGUE] Emotional regulation as the precondition
**Move:** When verbally attacked, do not counterattack: pause, let the reaction pass, then reply with a calibrated question or a short apology conceding your own harshness. Buy framing time deliberately — the book's example is Japanese executives using an interpreter they do not need.
**APPLY WHEN:** hostile buyers, patronizing counterparts, escalations on price or blame.
**AVOID WHEN:** never contraindicated, but note it is the binding constraint on every other move here — without it the toolkit does not run.

### [VOSS:IMSG] "I" messages to set a boundary
**Move:** Use the first-person singular to pause a bad dynamic without escalating: state that this does not work for you, or use the I-feel-X-when-you-Y-because-Z construction to demand a time-out. Keep the tone level and cool.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is being unproductive, repetitive, or personally aggressive.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered with heat — the same words in an aggressive tone start the argument they are meant to stop.

### [VOSS:SWAPIN] Fresh voice, flat handover

**APPLY WHEN:** the relationship with the current owner is stuck and the only alternative is escalation; a closer is brought in for final terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** the incumbent rep holds the only trust in the account; the source itself notes this violated protocol and could easily have backfired.

### [VOSS:NOSETUP] "No"-oriented setup question

### Stage E — Price, leverage and bargaining

### [VOSS:NOSPLIT] Refuse the midpoint reflex
**Move:** Do not propose or accept a halfway number as a way of resolving a gap; treat "no deal" as an acceptable outcome and say so internally before the call.
**APPLY WHEN:** procurement opens with an unjustified number; end-of-quarter pressure to concede; any counterpart running a win-lose playbook.
**AVOID WHEN:** the residual gap is small relative to relationship value, or the deal is genuinely multi-issue and a trade across issues (not a split) is available — the book does not distinguish that case, and this is where the lens is weakest.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as unit doctrine ("no deal is better than a bad deal"), argued by analogy rather than data.

### [VOSS:ANCHORLAST] Let them anchor, then absorb the punch
**Move:** Default to letting the counterpart name the first number — you rarely hold full market information and their opening may exceed your target. When the extreme anchor lands, do not counter-bid and do not get angry: deflect with a version of No ("how am I supposed to accept that?") or refocus the room on what we are actually trying to accomplish. Prepare psychologically in advance to hear an absurd number without adjusting toward it.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are information-poor about their budget or their market; hiring and salary talks; new categories; unfamiliar buyers; when the split-the-difference trap is forming.
**AVOID WHEN:** you hold a strong reference price and anchoring first is worth more than the read (the author anchors first in the 4Runner and Ackerman cases); when going second stalls a deal against a hard deadline; against an experienced shark who will anchor absurdly and drag you — the book explicitly refuses "never open" as a rule.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Chandler and "Jerry" salary cases of self-inflicted underpricing, plus hostage haggling and student cases; no controlled first-versus-second-mover comparison.

### [VOSS:RANGE] Quote a bolstering range via a comparable
**Move:** Instead of naming your number, cite what comparable deals or comparable employers pay as a range whose low end is what you actually want; expect to land near that low end.
**APPLY WHEN:** salary and rate discussions; the first pricing conversation with a new buyer; anything where a flat number would trigger a defensive reaction.
**AVOID WHEN:** the comparable is easily falsified, or the buyer will treat the low end as your list price forever.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard coaching, referencing a Columbia Business School study on range-quoting applicants (Ames & Mason) that he reports rather than reproduces.

### [VOSS:THIRDPRICE] Deflect "you go first" with someone else's number
**Move:** If forced to name a price first, do not name yours — quote an implausibly high figure a third party would charge (the book cites a Harvard Business School rate of $2,500 per student per day to a hospital chain). It sets a reference point without committing you.
**APPLY WHEN:** early pricing pressure, RFP-style ballpark requests, services with no list price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer can trivially verify the comparison is irrelevant; procurement contexts where a non-answer disqualifies you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted engagement.

### [VOSS:ODDNUM] Use precise, non-round numbers
**Move:** State figures that look calculated rather than rounded; round numbers read as placeholders inviting negotiation, precise ones read as the output of analysis and as a computed limit.
**APPLY WHEN:** any offer or counteroffer you intend to defend; final concessions.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot explain the derivation if asked, or industry convention is round pricing and precision looks contrived.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted from repeated use; the ransom case closed at $4,751 rather than a round figure. The book concedes the effect is irrational and uses it anyway.

### [VOSS:ACKERMAN] Ackerman offer ladder
**Move:** (1) Set the target you actually want. (2) Open at 65% of it. (3) Plan raises to 85%, 95%, 100% — each increment half the previous one. (4) Between raises, use empathy and the No-series so they counter before you move. (5) Make the final number precise and non-round. (6) On the last offer add a non-monetary item they probably do not want, to signal you have hit your limit. The decreasing raises are meant to make them believe they squeezed you dry.
**APPLY WHEN:** true single-issue price haggling against a hard bargainer; renewals, buyouts, procurement showdowns.
**AVOID WHEN:** relationship-first or committee-consensus sales where a 65% opener reads as insulting or unserious; a competing bidder who will simply take the business while you ladder; any context where the opener burns credibility you need for delivery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — used heavily in Haiti across many kidnappings plus student cases (Mishary's lease); Howard Raiffa's endorsement is second-hand and anecdotal.

### [VOSS:NOSERIES] Four ways to refuse before saying the word
**Move:** Refuse up to four times without using "no": (1) ask how you are supposed to do that; (2) call their offer generous, apologize, say it simply does not work for you — with no counteroffer; (3) say you are afraid you just cannot do that, expressing inability rather than unwillingness; (4) a short "I'm sorry, no." Only then a plain No, delivered with downward inflection and warmth. Making no counteroffer is what invites them to keep improving their own number.
**APPLY WHEN:** price or terms pressure where you have time and they have more urgency than you; buyouts, renewals, single-buyer/single-seller situations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a competing buyer or seller exists and delay costs you the deal — the book says outright the method suited its case only because the counterpart had no other buyer; also when repeated non-answers will exhaust a busy enterprise buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Spanish grow-shop buyout, the author's 4Runner purchase, and repeated kidnapping use.

### [VOSS:NAMEFIRST] Attrition ladder that makes them bid against themselves
**Move:** Decline to name any number: block with an inability statement that avoids the word "no", question the fairness of their demand, then answer each demand with a calibrated how-question so the only way forward is for them to lower their own figure. Only after they have dropped twice do you place an extreme low anchor, follow it with a precise odd number, and close with a token nonmonetary sweetener.
**APPLY WHEN:** an opening demand with no cost basis; heavy discount demands; buyers who insist you go first.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart has real alternatives and low switching cost — attrition requires them locked in; also unusable where slow-rolling burns your own deadline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Haitian kidnapping case, a $150,000 demand walked down to $4,751 plus a CD player in one day.

### [VOSS:GIFTSHOCK] Extreme anchor, then an unrelated gift
**Move:** Stake an aggressive anchor, absorb the inevitable rejection, then offer a small unrelated gift or gesture that costs you little, invoking reciprocity instead of conceding on price.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stalled haggle where you refuse to move on the number; long-horizon relationships where a returned favor has value.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gesture could read as an inducement or violate the buyer's procurement rules — a live compliance risk in enterprise and public-sector deals the book does not address.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — grounded in reciprocity, illustrated politically (Sadat's 1977 Knesset address) rather than commercially.

### [VOSS:NONMONEY] Pivot from price to terms and nonmonetary value
**Move:** Park price explicitly and ask what would make this a good deal, or what else they could offer that would make that price work for you. Name items cheap for them and valuable to you (or the reverse), and seed the brainstorm with concrete examples — logos, references, case studies, exposure, data, introductions, scope, timing, payment terms, exclusivity — so they start hunting for their own.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is anchoring hard on price or is capped on budget but rich in other assets; multi-issue deals where non-price terms are tradeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely single-issue transactions with nothing to trade; the counterpart cannot actually deliver the nonmonetary item; the buyer reads a terms pivot as evasion of a straight answer; or you are trading cash you need now for goodwill you cannot bank.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Memphis Bar Association trade of a magazine cover story against a steep discount from a $25,000/day rate; this is also the one point where the book brushes against multi-issue logrolling without naming it.

### [VOSS:FAIRDEF] Defuse "we just want what's fair"
**Move:** Do not concede to the implied accusation. Stop, apologize, and invite them to walk back to the exact point where you began treating them unfairly so it can be fixed.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart plays the fairness card to extract a discount; emotional escalation over price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the complaint is substantively correct — then fix the substance rather than running the counter.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a friend's home sale where the phrase alone raised the buyer's offer.

### [VOSS:FAIRMIRROR] Mirror the fairness jab back
**Move:** When told you have already been given a fair offer, repeat the word "fair" as a question, hold the pause, then label their apparent readiness to show the evidence behind the claim — which forces disclosure or exposes the bluff.
**APPLY WHEN:** procurement asserts market fairness without data; "everyone else pays this" claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** you could not use the data if they provided it, or the relationship cannot absorb a challenge on record.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — modeled on the NFL lockout exchange, presented as the author's own standard reply.

### [VOSS:FAIRFRAME] Pre-license fairness as a stop word
**Move:** Early in the engagement, state that you want them to feel treated fairly throughout and invite them to stop you the moment they think you are not — then honor it.
**APPLY WHEN:** kickoff of a long or repeated negotiation; multi-year contracts; partner and channel relationships where reputation compounds.
**AVOID WHEN:** you intend to run hard anchoring tactics in the same negotiation without slack to absorb a challenge.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own repeated opening line, framed as reputation management.

### [VOSS:ACCANCHOR] Accusation audit plus loss framing before bad news
**Move:** Before delivering an unattractive proposition, voice every negative thing they are about to think about you, in their words, until the expectation floor is low; then present the offer as an opportunity you are bringing to them first, before it goes elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** price reductions you must impose, scope cuts, delivery delays, renegotiating rates with contractors or partners.
**AVOID WHEN:** the negatives you voice are ones they had not considered and would materially damage trust; risky in writing, where self-accusation becomes quotable evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the UAE subcontract case, where every contractor accepted a cut from $2,000/day to $500/day without a counteroffer.

### [VOSS:UMBRAGE] Real anger, poised threats, strategic umbrage
**Move:** When an offer is genuinely insulting, allow a controlled amount of real anger, aim it at the proposal rather than the person, and say you do not see how that could ever work. Deliver any threat with poise — confidence and self-control, e.g. that it simply does not work for you. Faked anger is out.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stalled negotiation, a deliberately abusive offer, or a counterpart who reads politeness as weakness.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be faking it, which is documented to produce intractable demands and destroy trust; when the concession you extract will be implemented badly, since anger degrades their cognitive resources; and recognize the same move when it is aimed at you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — supported in the text by named work from Sinaceur (INSEAD) and Tiedens (Stanford) on anger, and Ames & Wazlawek (Columbia) on targets of umbrage rating themselves over-assertive.

### [VOSS:WALKAWAY] No neediness — be genuinely ready to leave
**Move:** Fix your bottom line in advance and be genuinely willing to walk; no deal beats a bad deal, and if you feel you cannot say No you have taken yourself hostage. Before any punch-back, offer de-escalation — suggest a time-out so the other side regains a sense of agency.
**APPLY WHEN:** every bargaining session; especially when you catch yourself rationalizing a concession because you need the quarter to close.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are posturing a walk-away you cannot execute — bluffing here is the same error as fake anger; also when the relationship's long-term value exceeds this deal's terms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a principle repeated across the author's cases, asserted rather than measured.

### [VOSS:DEADOPEN] Disclose your own deadline
**Move:** Tell the counterpart your cutoff instead of hiding it, on the logic that when the clock runs out for you it runs out for them too, and secrecy only makes you concede against yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** budget-cycle or fiscal-year constraints; procurement processes that drift; any negotiation where the other side appears to be waiting you out.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deadline is soft and you will visibly blow through it — disclosing a fake cutoff you then ignore destroys the tool permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — contradicts Herb Cohen's classic advice; supported by cited research from Don A. Moore (Haas, Berkeley) on impasse risk, reported second-hand.

### [VOSS:DEADHUNT] Locate their self-imposed clock
**Move:** Identify the counterpart's internal cycle — quarter close, month end, weekend, board meeting — and time your firmest position just before it; read escalating specificity in their threats (what, who, when, how) as the signal the real deadline is near.
**APPLY WHEN:** vendors and sellers on quota; durable-goods purchases at month end; renewal negotiations near a fiscal boundary.
**AVOID WHEN:** your own hard cutoff lands earlier; also when stalling risks losing an internal champion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — pattern extracted from the Haitian kidnapping wave (four-day, weekend-driven cycles) and generalized to quarterly sales behavior.

### [VOSS:LEVPOS] Build positive leverage from their stated wants
**Move:** Treat every "I want…" as the moment leverage transfers to you, then delay your own offer, since going first gives it away. Use their desire to create optionality — real or implied competing interest — and to restructure rather than discount: sell a partial stake or a phased purchase at your price rather than the whole thing at theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** they have declared intent to buy; you have any alternative buyer, even a casual one.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are manufacturing competition you do not have and can be caught out on; when delay costs you a live deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a business-sale worked example plus the Watson standoff (his desire to be heard).

### [VOSS:LEVNEG] Label negative leverage instead of threatening
**Move:** Identify what they fear losing — audience, status, reputation, an on-time-payment record — from their own oblique remarks or from a third party who knows them, then make it visible with a label rather than a threat: observing that they seem to value always having paid on time, or that it seems they do not care what position this leaves you in.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, breached commitments, stalled implementations where loss aversion is the only lever left.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be making a direct threat — the book calls those nuclear, with residue that poisons the process; and whenever the counterpart would experience it as loss of autonomy, which triggers shutdown (the paradox of power: harder push, more resistance).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — hostage and consulting practice; the loss-aversion basis is asserted with reference to unnamed psychology.

### [VOSS:LEVNORM] Normative leverage from their own rules
**Move:** Learn the standards, valuation formulas and moral rules the counterpart says they live by, then frame your ask inside them — including pointing out inconsistency between their stated beliefs and their proposed action. If they mention paying a certain multiple of cash flow, price your ask in that multiple.
**APPLY WHEN:** counterparts with articulated policies, published values, professional codes, or a stated valuation method.
**AVOID WHEN:** it lands as calling them a hypocrite; when their "rule" was invented on the spot as a negotiating device.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the Watson standoff (military honor rules: withdraw with honor after three days) plus the valuation-multiple example.

### [VOSS:BECAUSE] Give a reason, framed in their worldview
**Move:** Attach a "because" clause to requests, delivered in a reasonable tone. On substantive issues, make the reason reference the counterpart's own norms — e.g. declining a lowball by citing a duty of responsible stewardship to a counterpart who thinks in those terms.
**APPLY WHEN:** small procedural asks (scheduling, access, sequencing) and as the framing for refusals on larger ones.
**AVOID WHEN:** complex or high-stakes issues where an empty reason is transparent — the compliance effect in the cited experiment came from trivial requests.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — grounded in Langer, Blank & Chanowitz's late-1970s copy-machine line-cutting experiments, applied by the author.

### [VOSS:STATUSTRADE] Buy the blocker's standing instead of cutting price
**Move:** When resistance traces to someone's personal exposure rather than budget, hold price and offer a term that protects them — the book's example is guaranteeing a 90-day installation so the buyer's review four months out is not endangered.
**APPLY WHEN:** you detect timing fear, reputational risk, or an internal review cycle behind the hesitation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the constraint really is budget; or the term becomes a promise you cannot deliver — missing a guaranteed window converts a status gift into the exact loss you were protecting against.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single illustrative accounting-system example in the appendix.

### [VOSS:LABELASK] Label their expertise, then hand them the problem
**Move:** After the negatives are on the table, label the counterpart's competence or track record, then ask how they would restructure the deal so everyone still makes money. Credited as the expert, they tend to propose the concession themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** you need a concession you cannot demand; a committee member with pride of ownership over the current structure.
**AVOID WHEN:** they propose something worse than your target and you are now anchored to their idea; flattery that lands as insincere kills the rest of the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one contract renegotiation with a self-reported $1M swing.

### [VOSS:SALPERSIST] Pleasant persistence on nonsalary terms
**Move:** In compensation talks, keep pressing cheerfully on non-cash terms; the persistence surfaces the full option set and often produces a cash counter when the non-cash item turns out to be impossible.
**APPLY WHEN:** offer-stage hiring; rate negotiations with a constrained buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered without warmth, where it becomes nagging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one student who traded a vacation request into a higher salary offer.

### Stage F — Commitment and execution

### [VOSS:RULE3] The Rule of Three
**Move:** Get agreement on the same point three times in one conversation, varying the instrument so it does not sound like nagging: (1) the initial commitment, (2) a label or summary that pulls a "that's right", (3) a calibrated how/what question about implementation, e.g. what we do if we get off track. Alternatively, the same question asked three different ways.
**APPLY WHEN:** you suspect a counterfeit yes; before allocating delivery resources; after a suspiciously fast agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is already strained by scrutiny; low-stakes transactional buys where the friction costs more than the risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as trained FBI and consulting practice; "it is hard to lie three times" is asserted, not measured.

### [VOSS:CONGRUE] Label the mismatch between words and delivery (7-38-55)
**Move:** Watch tone and body language against the literal words. When a yes arrives with hesitation, name the incongruence aloud — say you heard the yes but it sounded hesitant, and that it matters this be gotten right. The label surfaces the real objection and reads as respect rather than pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** confirming agreement, especially in person or on video; anywhere a soft yes could become a silent no later.
**AVOID WHEN:** text or email-only channels with no paraverbal signal; cross-cultural reads where your baseline for "hesitant" is unreliable; with Analysts, who sound flat by default.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the technique is the author's; the 7/38/55 split comes from Mehrabian's liking-and-attitude studies, which the author admits were not about negotiation, so discount any move leaning on the ratio.

### [VOSS:IMPLQ] Implementation-defining questions to lock buy-in
**Move:** Before agreeing, make them define success: ask how we will know we are on track and how we will handle it if we drift off track. Then summarize their answer back until you get "that's right" — their own words become the implementation plan, and the questions also force their internal coordination.
**APPLY WHEN:** late stage, right before or right after verbal agreement; any deal where delivery, onboarding or rollout risk sits on the buyer's side.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart has no authority to define implementation (you are talking to a gatekeeper); very early discovery, where it reads as presumptive closing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing practice in the author's business negotiations plus the Ecuador case, where repeated questions kept the captors' crew aligned; no dataset.

### [VOSS:BTT] Map and influence the players behind the table
**Move:** Assume a team stands behind your counterpart and build influence that reaches them. Ask directly how the deal lands on the rest of their team, how bought-in the people not on this call are, what their colleagues see as their main challenge in this area, and how we make sure the right material reaches the right people. Treat a change of negotiator on their side as a signal of a hardening position, and account for whoever was displaced — they can still break the deal. Get absent blockers named, and ideally invited in.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where implementation runs through a committee; you are talking only to an executive sponsor; a new procurement contact appears mid-cycle; champion turnover; channel deals with hidden principals.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuine single decision-maker with budget authority and no delivery dependency — the questions then manufacture phantom stakeholders and stall momentum; when raising it would insult a buyer who has already asserted sole authority (soften to a label); and going around your contact reads as betrayal, since the move is about influence reaching them, not bypassing them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Dos Palmas, where a displaced negotiator with physical control of the hostages torpedoed a completed $300,000 payment, plus the author's own lost Florida training deal, killed by a division head nobody had asked about.

### [VOSS:SUCCESSTERMS] Attach success metrics to the number
**Move:** Once compensation or scope is agreed, define what success looks like and the metrics that trigger the next increase — free for the counterpart to grant, and it schedules the next negotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** offer close; consulting engagements; expansion agreements where the next uplift needs a pre-agreed trigger.
**AVOID WHEN:** the metrics would be set by someone who cannot control them, which converts the tool into a trap.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — taught to MBA classes; illustrated by the Angel Prado case.

### [VOSS:COLLECT8] Eight-step sequence for a disputed unpaid invoice
**Move:** Run in order: a "No"-oriented email to reopen contact (have they given up on settling this amicably); a statement engineered for "that's right" about their view of the bill; a calibrated question asking how the bill violates the agreement; a battery of "No"-oriented questions removing unspoken accusations (misled, failed to deliver, reneged, failed them); labeling and mirroring any unacceptable answer back at them; a calibrated question to any partial offer asking how you are supposed to accept it; a label flattering their self-image as a principled operator; then a long pause and a final "No"-oriented question about whether they want a reputation for not honoring agreements.
**APPLY WHEN:** collections, disputed final invoices, a counterpart who has gone silent after part-payment.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot regulate your own emotions — the book's own case failed at step one when anger took over; also inappropriate where legal process has begun or the counterpart genuinely disputes delivery quality on the merits.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author claims roughly a 90% success rate for scripts of this shape, self-reported, no denominator, conditional on the negotiator staying calm.

## Where this book stops working

- **Multi-issue B2B value creation.** The book has no concept of logrolling. It treats every gap as a single-axis distributive fight and attacks compromise without distinguishing it from trading across issues. Harvard PON's structural correction applies directly: in multi-issue deals value comes from trades, not from holding one position. Only [VOSS:NONMONEY] gestures at it, and the one-sheet's noncash section is one-directional value capture, not a trade list. Discount every strategic claim here by roughly 0.4.
- **Committee and procurement-scored enterprise motion.** Almost every case is one counterpart in one room: a hostage taker, a car dealer, a landlord, a single consulting buyer. Committee dynamics appear only as "there is a team behind them" ([VOSS:BTT]) and one Coca-Cola constraint story. In scored RFPs, non-answers ([VOSS:THIRDPRICE], [VOSS:NAMEFIRST]) disqualify you; a 65% Ackerman opener reads as unserious; gifts ([VOSS:GIFTSHOCK]) can breach procurement policy outright.
- **High-ACV, long-cycle, repeat-relationship deals.** The toolkit is optimized for one-shot extraction from a counterpart you will not meet again. The book's own defense — that the counterpart will never notice — is the tell. Recognizable, ritual delivery of the sentence stems is the documented degradation mode: the buyer hears a routine, and the trust cost compounds across a multi-year relationship.
- **Analysts and data-driven evaluators.** Appeals, forced empathy and emotional framing land badly with a buyer scoring measurable criteria; [VOSS:HOWSUPPOSED] and [VOSS:EMODRIVER] both misfire there by the book's own admission.
- **Async and written channels.** Half the arsenal (voice, mirrors, incongruence reads, labels landing in a pause) needs a live paraverbal channel. The book explicitly says email-first negotiating suppresses Black Swans, and several moves become quotable liabilities in writing ([VOSS:AUDIT], [VOSS:MEACULPA], [VOSS:ACCANCHOR], [VOSS:MISLABEL]).
- **Micro-ACV transactional volume.** The prep overhead (one sheet, rehearsal, second listener, Black Swan hunting) exceeds the value of small deals; the book itself warns against endless discovery on a small transaction.
- **Hostages are not buyers.** The originating situation profile — a counterpart in crisis, no coherent demand, no alternative supplier, no next quarter — is precisely what a normal B2B buyer is not. The transposition is the lens's largest untested assumption.

## What the book debunks

- **The yes ladder / agreement-momentum script.** Chaining small yeses to reach the order is presented as actively counterproductive: every step succeeded and the final answer stayed no.
- **"Yes" as a win.** Counterfeit and confirmation yeses sound identical to commitment; verbal agreement, praise and gratitude are not evidence of change. Being thanked and praised by the counterpart is graded as a bad call, because it means they own nothing.
- **"You're right" and "I'll try."** The first ends an unwanted conversation while changing nothing; the second is a plan to fail.
- **Separating the people from the problem** — impossible when emotion *is* the problem. The rational-problem-solving school is named as having measurably failed FBI negotiators after Ruby Ridge and Waco.
- **BATNA-centric preparation.** Rehearsing the walk-away makes it the psychological focal point, silently capping your ask. "Win-win goals" often function as wimp-win: a modest target defended because it is easy to declare a victory.
- **ZOPA-style rational bargaining models** are dismissed as academic; the counter-model is emotional and anchor-driven.
- **Splitting the difference** is the predictable lackluster default the Ackerman model exists to beat — one black shoe and one brown.
- **Hiding your deadline** is named as the worst position: you concede faster while they wait, i.e. you negotiate against yourself. Deadlines are described as nearly always self-inflicted and flexible.
- **The Golden Rule in negotiation** — treat people as *they* need to be treated, not as you would want. The "I am normal" projection error is the root failure that makes type diagnosis necessary; blunt assertiveness is counterproductive most of the time.
- **The poker face** and softening criticism by denying the negative ("I don't want to sound harsh"), which lends the negative credence.
- **Anger** — almost never productive, including your own, because it degrades judgement while inflating confidence. Faked anger produces intractable demands and destroys trust. Anger that "works" still buys concessions that later fail in implementation.
- **Threats** are treated as a last-resort weapon with lasting contamination, not a tool; direct pressure produces resistance (paradox of power).
- **Calling the counterpart crazy** is a research failure, not a diagnosis, and guarantees no deal.
- **Over-using the counterpart's name** is a salesman's tell buyers are tired of; conversely, apologies-as-weakness is rejected — "I'm sorry" is load-bearing in the No-series.
- **Parroting vs paraphrase** — repeat their concern in your words, not theirs, or they hear the technique. The breakthrough is supposed to be invisible; if they notice you engineering it, the effect is gone.
- **Formal meetings are the least revealing face time**; email-first negotiating suppresses Black Swans by giving the counterpart time to re-center.
- **Wimp-win deals** — accepting the first acceptable counter, folding early to avoid conflict — are the stated default failure across business and family alike. Avoiding honest conflict, not losing it, is what destroys outcomes.
- **Documented self-inflicted failures the book preserves:** the Dos Palmas confrontational mode, diagnosed as the root cause of a 13-month failure that killed two hostages; a technically improved fundraising script never rolled out because it violated orthodoxy; the marketing strategist who executed step one and lost the deal ten minutes later on the phone; the Charleston case where the student anchored too fast and skipped the labels that would have made the broker bid against himself.

## Numbers that matter

- **Ackerman ladder:** open at 65% of target, raise to 85 / 95 / 100%, each increment half the last; final number precise and non-round (the book's ransom closed at $4,751).
- **Rule of Three:** the same agreement confirmed three times in one conversation. **Four** expressions of "no" available before the word itself. **Seven** real meanings of a refusal.
- **Mirror = last 3 words** (or the critical 1–3). **Silence ≥ 4 seconds.** **Three voice tones.**
- **7 / 38 / 55 percent** — words / tone / body in Mehrabian's *liking* studies, transposed to negotiation by the author, who concedes it was not negotiation research. Treat as an attention heuristic, not a law.
- **~3 Black Swans per side per negotiation** — the author's hypothesis, not a measurement.
- **~66%** chance the counterpart's type differs from yours (arithmetic from three types).
- **3–5 labels** and **3–5 calibrated questions** on the one sheet; questions asked in clusters of **2–3**; summary target **a couple of sentences**; **five** sections on the one sheet.
- **Loss stings at least 2× the equivalent gain**; prospect-theory example: a 95% chance of $10,000 versus a certain $9,499 flips choice direction when framed as losses.
- **23% better return** from a "No"-oriented fundraising script versus the yes-pattern script — self-reported, one student's test-market group, no sample size.
- **~90% claimed success rate** for the disputed-invoice script family — self-reported, no denominator, conditional on composure.
- **7:1 claimed return** on prep time versus later renegotiation and clarification — vendor self-report (Black Swan Group); never cite as a benchmark.
- **>90% of open-heart surgery patients** had changed nothing two years later — cited generically as "study after study", no named source.
- **Langer copy-machine experiments:** ~60% compliance with no reason, >90% with a reason, even a meaningless one.
- **Lawyer-negotiator study (two US cities):** 65% cooperative style, 24% truly assertive; among those graded effective, >75% cooperative and only 12% assertive. Cited by footnote, no name in the text.
- **~7 pieces of information** processable in conscious attention (Miller, 1956).
- **Waiter mirroring:** 70% higher average tips than praise-based service (Wiseman, cited in-book without a verifiable reference).
- Self-reported case outcomes: Haiti $150,000 → $4,751 (plus a CD player) in one day; Ecuador $5M demand → $16,500; Spanish buyout €30,812 → €28,346 → €25,000 → accepted €23,567; 4Runner $36,000 sticker → $30,000 paid; Mishary lease anchored $1,730 → $1,829 accepted against a $1,830 target; Charleston property asking $4.3M, anchor $3.4M → closed $3.6M; UAE contractors $2,000/day → $500/day; Memphis Bar Association against a $25,000/day standard rate; Angel Prado's boss moving 120 → 127 → 134.5 against himself; a $1M swing on one contract renegotiation; a $130,000 commission claim reversed to $25,000 owed by an independent audit.
- **Provenance ledger (Notes):** externally sourced components — Miller (1956); Stephens/Silbert/Hasson (2010) on speaker–listener neural coupling; Lieberman et al. (2007) on affect labeling; Ames & Mason (2015) on range offers; Van Swol/Braun/Malhotra (2012) on deception markers; Mehrabian (1971/1981); Sinaceur & Tiedens (2006); Ames & Wazlawek (2014); Langer/Blank/Chanowitz (1978). Everything composed — the Ackerman model, the one sheet, the accusation audit, "that's right", calibrated questions as a system — rests on FBI cases and firm practice, not on trials of the method.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No controlled test of the method itself.** The cited studies support component psychology (affect labeling, anchoring, placebic reasons); none test the composed system, and none test its transfer from hostage rooms to commercial deals. The strongest numbers in the book are two self-reports (23% script lift, ~90% invoice success) with no denominators.
- **Survivorship in the case base.** Nearly every story is a resolved case retold by its protagonist. Where failures appear (Dos Palmas, the hotline call, the strategist who blew the deal) they are used to reinforce the doctrine, not to bound it.
- **No model of the buying process.** Pipeline, qualification, forecast, procurement scoring, legal review, security review, multi-threading beyond "there is a team behind them" — absent. The lens cannot answer *which* deals to work, only what to say inside one.
- **No repeat-game accounting.** The book optimizes a single encounter. It has almost nothing on what the tactics cost across renewals, references and word-of-mouth when the counterpart later recognizes them — which is where B2B actually lives.
- **Logrolling and issue trading are invisible** to the framework, as is any notion of expanding the pie through structured multi-issue exchange.
- **Detection is assumed away.** The reassurance that the counterpart will never catch you is asserted, not demonstrated, and the appendix's literal sentence stems are now widely circulated — the most-read book in its category is the least likely to go unrecognized.
- **Ethics are handled by a single move.** The defense against the manipulation charge is that human irrationality is patterned and that connection should be the first goal — a stance, not a safeguard. Mislabeling, extreme anchors, gift-then-reciprocity and accusation audits are manipulation-adjacent by the author's own admission.
- **Culture is treated as a footnote.** Strong universal claims ("why is accusatory in every language") are made from field impression; cross-cultural reads of hesitation, silence and refusal are precisely where the tools misfire.
- **No cost-of-time model.** Attrition ladders, deliberate slowness and no-counteroffer refusals assume your clock is cheaper than theirs, which in quota-carrying sales is usually false.
