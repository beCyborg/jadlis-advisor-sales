# advisor-resonate — myths and demotions

What this tradition itself debunks, what must be demoted when carried into a sales deal, and which
numbers may never be quoted as fact. Source: Duarte, *Resonate* (2010). Prefix `[RESO:]`.

Two separate lists live here. **Section A** is what the book knocks down — usable as ammunition.
**Sections B–D** are demotions the lens applies *to the book*, because the deal layer is not the
keynote layer.

---

## A. Debunked inside the tradition

- **"A deck is a presentation."** A document distributed as slides is a report. Documents are
  dense on purpose, presentations sparse on purpose; they are two artifacts `[RESO:SLIDEDIET]`.
  Corollary: a request for "a five-slide overview" is a request for a document.
- **"Better-looking slides fix a weak message."** Polish is downstream of the message, never a
  substitute — the author positions her own earlier book as the visual layer and this one as the
  message layer.
- **"Facts persuade on their own."** Fact-after-fact delivery offloads onto the audience the job
  of deciding what matters. Logical agreement is not personal conviction `[RESO:FACTPLUSFEEL]`.
- **"Then pile on emotion instead."** The mirror failure: an analytical room reads missing proof
  as lost credibility, and emotional overshoot is read directly as manipulation
  `[RESO:KAIROSCALIBRATE]`.
- **"Presentations fail from too little information."** They fail from too much. Nobody has ever
  asked for a longer presentation `[RESO:UNDERTIME]`.
- **"The audience will follow my associative path."** Subject-matter experts link ideas that are
  adjacent only inside their own heads; an unorganised talk is a private path that walks the
  audience into dead ends `[RESO:MECE]`.
- **"The form is a formula."** Rigid adherence is self-defeating: presentations oscillate rather
  than peak once, so Field's single-climax paradigm only partly applies, and Hero's Journey stages
  can be reordered because a presentation is not bound by chronology. Following a form does not
  force formulaic output.
- **"Memorable moments are spontaneous."** They are planned and rehearsed — Armstrong spent
  6 hours 40 minutes on the lunar surface composing his first-step line. A moment that lands but
  distracts from the big idea is still a failure, and kitschy execution reads as a bad camp skit
  `[RESO:STARPLAN]`.
- **"Great presenters are naturals."** Called false outright; the book's exemplars agonised over
  wording, structure and delivery. Mastery also does not transfer across formats — Graham was a
  master communicator in dance and froze as a speaker `[RESO:FEARREPS]`.
- **"Superior analysis wins on its merits."** The anchor case has the smarter consulting team
  losing a multimillion-dollar engagement because its findings were locked inside dense,
  smart-sounding slides. Density is not rigour; "brilliance obscured" is the named failure, and
  McClintock's genuine discovery went unrecognised for roughly three decades partly on delivery
  `[RESO:USABLENOTSMART]`, `[RESO:DEJARGON]`.
- **"Content validity survives a bad first impression."** Aristotle's contrary position is cited
  and then rejected as unrealistic for audiences that judge within seconds `[RESO:PREFRAME]`.
- **"Friendly internal review is review."** Six named dysfunctional environments — Conceited
  Captain, Political Paranoia, Message Magic, Vacuum Visionary, Lackey Leader, Customer
  Cold-Shoulder — mean the honest critic often has to be sourced outside the organisation
  (Conway's Law) `[RESO:HOSTILESCREEN]`.
- **"Demographics tell you who the audience is."** Rejected as a basis for connection; so is the
  imagine-them-in-their-underwear advice. Ownership of any misunderstanding sits with the
  presenter `[RESO:HEROGRID]`.
- **"Resistance is irrational."** Usually rational from the buyer's seat — reputational risk from
  spending company money on an unpredictable outcome. A benefit stated only from the presenter's
  side counts as no benefit, and leaving resistance to Q&A is the named failure `[RESO:INOCULATE]`.
- **"I communicated it once, so I'm done."** Adoption needs repeated deliveries across the life
  cycle `[RESO:MILESTONESERIES]`.
- **"Absence of objection means agreement."** Enron routed internal dissenters to other
  departments; silence measured nothing. A competent presenter who hedges is functionally silent —
  the room hears the tone, not the appendix `[RESO:PLAINRISK]`.
- **"Presentation skill is morally neutral in effect."** Amoral in the strong sense: the same
  structure lands a lie faster than an honest idea, and in regulated selling the transmitted
  presentation is itself the charged artifact `[RESO:SLICKCHECK]`.
- **"Announce the successor and skip the failure."** Not acknowledging a prior failure before
  announcing its replacement makes the audience assume the new one will fail too
  `[RESO:TOPICTOMESSAGE]`.
- **"Slides and speaker reinforce each other."** They compete for one processing channel — the
  audience reads or listens, never both — and overusing slides erodes the human connection that
  justified the meeting `[RESO:ONEIDEASLIDE]`.

## B. Demoted when carried into a deal

Each of these is a legitimate keynote move that must lose rank inside a sales motion, with the
reason. This is where the lens must contradict its own source.

| Move | Demoted to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `[RESO:APPLAUSEBEAT]` | do not use below stage scale | There is no applause mechanism in a committee room or a video call; engineered response lines land as grandstanding. The study behind it measured applause, not decisions. |
| `[RESO:PACEBURST]`, `[RESO:PAUSEBREAK]` | stage only | Tempo crescendos need 15+ minutes of runway; engineered pauses in a two-person call read as theatrical or manipulative. |  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
| `[RESO:FASTCONTOUR]`, `[RESO:TRIPLECONTRAST]` | warm rooms only | Fast what-is / what-could-be swings without established facts read as hype on a first call or in front of a hostile committee. |
| `[RESO:SONATA3]` | not in dialogue | A pre-composed three-part arc stops you following the buyer; discovery is buyer-led by construction. |
| `[RESO:PROPDEMO]` | needs a stage | Physical theatre in a small meeting room or on a call feels forced, and overstating the data with a prop is the manipulation failure mode. |
| `[RESO:FOURACTORS]`, `[RESO:MECE]`, `[RESO:ROLESHIFT]` | committee instruments | Aimed at a single decision-maker they dilute the ask; a menu of four actions is worse than one clear one. |
| `[RESO:NOABOUTUS]` | inverted in procurement | Credentials are the agenda item in vendor-qualification and security review; deleting them there is a scored miss. |
| `[RESO:SAFETYORANGE]`, `[RESO:OWNANGLE]` | penalised in regulated buying | Conservative committees punish novelty in the framing itself, and the book's remedy (be more distinctive) points the wrong way. |
| `[RESO:EMBODY]`, `[RESO:VISUALWORD]`, `[RESO:FACTPLUSFEEL]`, `[RESO:STATVISUAL]` | evasion in scoring contexts | Where the audience scores against a spec sheet or explicitly asked for data, emotional layering reads as dodging the question. |
| `[RESO:DEEPPREP]`, `[RESO:ROUND34]`, `[RESO:REHEARSE20]`, `[RESO:HOSTILESCREEN]`, `[RESO:SPATIALLAYOUT]` | unrecoverable below mid ACV | The prep economics only close above mid/high ACV; below that the leverage is a reusable script, on which the book is silent. |
| `[RESO:REJECTREPS]` | needs an external stopping rule | The book supplies no criterion separating persistence from denial, so this move can absorb true disconfirming feedback indefinitely. |
| `[RESO:NOBACKDOWN]` | needs a calibration test | With no test for being wrong, it degrades into stubbornness — and is actively dangerous where the person contradicted holds budget and has no face-saving exit. |

## C. Documented failure modes of the techniques

- **Manufactured urgency.** The gap must match real severity. The author's own 2007 internal
  downturn talk is the documented case: a technically defensible urgency frame read as
  manipulation by an audience with no experience to validate it, costing about a year to recover
  `[RESO:PAINPLEASURE]`, `[RESO:CALLADVENTURE]`.
- **Invented common ground.** Fabricated or thin overlap is detectable and costs more than it
  buys `[RESO:COMMONGROUND]`.
- **Pre-empting a dead objection.** Raising an objection the room did not hold plants it
  `[RESO:INOCULATE]`.
- **Generic current-state slide.** A baseline written without discovery signals no homework and
  destroys the credibility it was meant to build `[RESO:WHATIS-BASELINE]`.
- **Unresolved cliffhanger.** Breaking the narrative for the data block and running out of time
  reads as disorganisation, not suspense `[RESO:CLIFFHANGER]`.
- **Ignored screening.** A review you solicit and then disregard burns the reviewers
  `[RESO:HOSTILESCREEN]`.
- **Committee deck by open modification.** Letting collaborators expand the vision before a spine
  exists produces exactly the deck the method targets `[RESO:TEAMLIFT]`.
- **Manufactured humility.** A rehearsed "humble" failure is read instantly as technique
  `[RESO:HONESTFLAW]`, and a confession touching the current deal's competence question is read
  as risk `[RESO:CONFESS]`.
- **Unearned deviation from form.** An anti-deck from someone who has not demonstrated command of
  the standard format reads as unprofessional rather than as a signal `[RESO:RULESFIRST]`.
- **Polish exceeding evidence.** Slick delivery that leaves a room confused but agreeing is the
  named ethical failure, and the only safeguard offered is the presenter's own honesty check
  `[RESO:SLICKCHECK]`.

## D. Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

**Self-reported / vendor-run — never cite as evidence:**

- The executive survey: 86.1% saying clear communication affects career and income; only 25.2%
  spending more than two hours preparing a high-stakes presentation (29.2% one-to-two hours,
  17.0% 30–60 min, 16.2% 5–30 min, 12.1% seldom practising); hardest part 35.7% message, 13.8%
  delivery, 8.9% slide quality, 41.1% all of the above. Vendor-run, sample size not given.
- "Works with half of the world's top fifty brands" — firm self-description.
- "The clever ideas arrive in the 3rd–4th round" `[RESO:ROUND34]` — no data.
- The Covert case: 20 rehearsals, a 15-minute talk plus 15 minutes Q&A, a $2.5M NIH Pioneer Award
  (2009), "don't change a thing" arriving only at rehearsals 19–20 — the causal attribution to
  rehearsal is the participant's own `[RESO:REHEARSE20]`.
- The Rauch Foundation "reaction was electric" claim — the client's own report `[RESO:STATVISUAL]`.
- GE figures (company-reported): $2–3 billion a year in new revenue from a marketing-led
  innovation portfolio; marketers on teams driving 8–10 percent organic growth described as more
  than double the historic rate `[RESO:MOVEMATRIX]`.
- Ranjay Gulati cited without a specific paper for the claim that customer-focused firms investing
  through a downturn stay ahead for up to five years after recovery.

**Vanity metrics with no established link to outcomes:** Pausch's last lecture over 12 million
YouTube views; TED.com over 100 million views; King's speech over 15 million views. These support
`[RESO:REPEATSPREAD]` as an *assertion* only — views are not decisions.

**Form constants — conventions, not findings:** beginning ≤10% of runtime; 2 turning points;
3 contrast types; 4 audience action types; 12 Hero's Journey stages; 8 organisational patterns;
3 (up to 5) topics with 3–5 supporting ideas; 5 S.T.A.R. types; 4 noise types; slides processable
under 3 seconds; never over 2 minutes on one slide; 40 minutes out of 60. All are rules of thumb.
The ~25-seconds-to-read-a-text-slide figure and the 10-minutes-to-squirm figure are stated without
measurement.

**Safe to quote as illustration (public record, still not evidence of persuasion):** the
Heritage & Greatbatch 1986 corpus (476 speeches, 19,000+ sentences, ~half of applause bursts after
contrast) — but only for contrast and applause, never for a decision; Gettysburg at 278 words in
just over 2 minutes against Everett's 2 hours; King's 16 minutes with applause 27 times, roughly
every 35 seconds; Reagan's ~4-minute Challenger address; Cummings rejected by 14 publishers.

## E. The tradition's own blind spots (state these when you speak)

1. **Selection on success, no control group.** The form was derived by fitting patterns onto
   speeches already known to have worked. No failed presentation was analysed, so sparkline
   conformance is a heuristic and never a predictor.
2. **No deal mechanics whatsoever.** Nothing on discovery questioning, live objection handling in
   dialogue, negotiation, pricing, qualification, forecasting or pipeline. The single nod to a
   sales process is an unsourced awareness→loyalty ladder used as a targeting device.
3. **One-to-many by construction.** Every mechanism assumes a presenter with a stage, an agenda
   and controlled time; the asymmetry of a buyer-run meeting is never addressed, and coverage of
   remote or asynchronous selling is nil — a 2010 vintage predating the video call as the default
   sales room.
4. **Effect claims stop at the wrong outcome.** Applause, views, and a self-attributed grant win.
   Nothing in the source measures a purchase, a renewal or a decision.
5. **Ethics chapter is a conscience, not a control.** It correctly names the craft as amoral but
   offers only the presenter's own honesty check — no external gate, no test the buyer can apply.
6. **Visual source loss.** The book is heavily visual; sparklines, the Hero's Journey wheel, the
   presentation-form diagram and the four-actor table carried content in figures that did not
   survive text conversion. Some figure-only detail behind these moves is unrecoverable here, so
   treat diagram-derived specifics as approximate.
