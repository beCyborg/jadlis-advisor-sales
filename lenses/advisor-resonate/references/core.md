# advisor-resonate — core principles, ranked by evidence

Layer: **архитектура выступления**. Source: Nancy Duarte, *Resonate* (2010). Evidence ceiling
for this lens: `external-research` — nothing here may be asserted at `field-study` strength.

Ranked strongest → weakest by the ladder token that actually supports the component, not by how
central the book treats it. The book's own spine (the oscillating sparkline) sits in the middle
tiers, not the top: it was derived by fitting a pattern onto speeches already known to have
worked. Read this file when you need to know *how hard to push* a recommendation, not what to do.

---

## Tier 1 — `external-research`: borrowed from outside the tradition

These are the only components resting on something the author did not generate herself. Note the
outcome measured is never a purchase.

**1. Contrast is what audiences respond to audibly.** The single real empirical citation in the
book — Heritage & Greatbatch, *American Journal of Sociology*, 1986, 476 British political
speeches and over 19,000 sentences — found roughly half of applause bursts followed a moment of
contrast, and that the best-received speeches drew applause nearly twice a minute. This is the
strongest support in the lens, and it is support for **contrast as an attention device**, not for
persuasion or for a decision. It underwrites the contrast half of `[RESO:TRIPLECONTRAST]`,
`[RESO:GAPFRAME]`, `[RESO:CALLADVENTURE]` and `[RESO:PAIREDIMAGE]`; it does **not** underwrite
`[RESO:APPLAUSEBEAT]` as a persuasion tactic, which reads the same study backwards.

**2. Divergence must precede convergence.** The generate-then-filter sequence is credited to
J. P. Guilford (1967) on divergent and convergent thinking; the specific filtering rule is
practitioner method (Tim Brown's funnel). Supports the ordering constraint in
`[RESO:MURDERDARLINGS]` and `[RESO:ROUND34]` — the *sequence* is the defensible part, the
"third or fourth round" number is not.

**3. Persuasion has three separable appeals.** Ethos, logos and pathos are Aristotle's, and the
head/heart/gut/groin register split is Randy Olson's named framework. Both are external and both
are rhetorical schemes rather than measured constructs — external in provenance, not in
validation. Supports `[RESO:THREEAPPEALS]` and `[RESO:FOURORGANS]`. The practical claim that
survives is diagnostic: an audit that finds one appeal missing is more reliable than any ratio
the book prescribes.

## Tier 2 — `practitioner-n-many`: repeated across a corpus of engagements or cases

The firm's method applied across many clients and the book's speech corpus. No control group and
no failed cases, so treat these as reliable *craft defaults*, not as predictions.

**4. Fix the destination before generating content.** One-sentence big idea with a point of view
and a stake `[RESO:BIGIDEA3]`, a from/to map on both belief and behaviour `[RESO:FROMTO]`, and
only then material. This is the most transferable principle in the lens because it is a
constraint, not a technique — it costs minutes and it kills topic-shaped decks.

**5. The audience is the protagonist; you are the mentor.** `[RESO:MENTORSTANCE]`,
`[RESO:MENTORGIFT]`, `[RESO:NOABOUTUS]`. Twenty years of client decks stand behind the
recommendation; the *mechanism* is argued by analogy to the Campbell/Vogler mentor archetype and
has never been tested. Strong as an editing rule ("if the subject of the sentence is your
company, move it"), weak as a theory of why buyers move.

**6. Resistance is structural and priced.** Adoption costs a sacrifice, which must be named
`[RESO:SACRIFICE]` and answered with a proportional reward `[RESO:REWARD7]`, and the strongest
objections belong inside the presentation rather than in Q&A `[RESO:INOCULATE]`. The Kotter and
Kotter & Schlesinger HBR pieces are named for the change-resistance framing; the six buckets and
seven reward categories are the author's own checklists, so the *principle* is Tier 2 and the
*taxonomies* are Tier 4.

**7. Numbers do not carry themselves.** Every decision-relevant figure needs scale, cross-domain
comparison or causal context `[RESO:NUMBERNARRATIVE]`; the worked cases (WaterPartners, Intel,
Gates, Pollan) are consistent enough to treat as method. Constraint attached: the comparison must
be honest to the underlying ratio, or it converts into the manipulation failure mode.

**8. Structure must be seen, not scrolled.** Laying messages out spatially before slide
production `[RESO:SPATIALLAYOUT]`, choosing an arrangement pattern deliberately
`[RESO:PICKPATTERN]`, clustering into 3–5 non-overlapping topics `[RESO:MECE]`, and handing a
senior reviewer an outline `[RESO:OUTLINEFIRST]`. The claim underneath — that structure is
absorbed faster from an outline than from slides — is craft observation, but it is repeated
across engagements and it is cheap to test yourself.

**9. Presentations and documents are two artifacts, not one.** Sparse on screen, dense on paper
`[RESO:SLIDEDIET]`, `[RESO:ONEIDEASLIDE]`. Rests on a single-channel-processing claim the book
does not source to a study, plus Hollywood scene-length practice — so the *rule* is Tier 2 by
repetition, the *cognitive explanation* is Tier 4.

**10. One planned moment does the remembering.** `[RESO:STARPLAN]`, `[RESO:SOUNDBITE]`,
`[RESO:RHETDEVICE]`. Four named worked cases plus firm practice. The load-bearing claim for sales
is narrow and useful: when the decision happens days after you leave, what survives is one moment
and one phrase, not the argument.

**11. Review must come from outside a friendly system.** A screening with chosen naysayers at
roughly 3× runtime `[RESO:HOSTILESCREEN]`, and preparation sized to what is at stake
`[RESO:PROPINVEST]`. Conway's Law is invoked as the rationale: the presentation cannot exceed the
process that made it. This is the one place the book supplies a real quality gate.

**12. Adoption needs a series, not a delivery.** `[RESO:MILESTONESERIES]` — a pattern observed
across the book's featured presenters, with Gore's hundreds of deliveries as the anchor. Maps
cleanly onto long enterprise cycles; collapses to overhead below mid ACV.

## Tier 3 — `practitioner-anecdote`: one case, often a famous one

Use these as options to reach for, never as reasons. Nearly all are selected on success.

**13. The set-piece oscillates rather than climaxes.** The sparkline itself — beginning ≤10% of
runtime, a call to adventure, a middle alternating on content, register and delivery mode, a call
to action, an ending above the start `[RESO:WHATIS-BASELINE]`, `[RESO:CALLADVENTURE]`,
`[RESO:TRIPLECONTRAST]`, `[RESO:NEWBLISS]`. It was fitted post hoc onto the 2007 iPhone launch
and the 1963 King speech. It is the book's central claim and it sits at Tier 3: a heuristic with
no predictive standing, because no failed presentation was ever analysed against it.

**14. Delivery craft is scored, not felt.** Pauses written into the script `[RESO:PAUSEBREAK]`,
tempo arcs `[RESO:PACEBURST]`, oscillation speed matched to room energy `[RESO:FASTCONTOUR]`,
planned mode changes `[RESO:MODESHIFT]`. Each rests on the author's reading of one recording.
These are the moves that transfer worst to a two-person call — see `myths-and-demotions.md`.

**15. Emotion is shown, not asserted.** `[RESO:EMBODY]`, `[RESO:VISUALWORD]`,
`[RESO:HUMANIZEFEATURE]`, `[RESO:PAIREDIMAGE]`, `[RESO:STATVISUAL]`. Single named cases each
(Graham, Cisco, Rauch Foundation). Directionally sound, and the AVOID side is the operative part:
all of them read as evasion the moment a buyer has asked for data.

**16. Credibility is negotiated before content starts.** Pre-meeting materials and staging
`[RESO:PREFRAME]`, arriving early `[RESO:ARRIVEEARLY]`, naming overlap before difference
`[RESO:COMMONGROUND]`, borrowing the audience's own canon `[RESO:SHAREDTEXT]`. Anchored on the
author's own failed 2007 internal talk as the counter-case — which makes it the rare Tier 3 item
supported by a *failure*, and therefore worth more than its token suggests.

**17. Two composition passes beat one.** Script first, visuals second, with different
collaborators `[RESO:TWOFRAMEWORKS]`, `[RESO:TEAMLIFT]`. Hitchcock biographies only.

**18. Rehearsal is the differentiator that looks like talent.** `[RESO:REHEARSE20]`,
`[RESO:FEARREPS]`, `[RESO:RULESFIRST]`. Two named cases; the grant-win attribution is the
participant's own. The debunking half of this (naturals are a myth) is better supported than the
prescription half (twenty rehearsals).

**19. Optimise the artifact for the retelling, not for your own rigour.**
`[RESO:USABLENOTSMART]`, `[RESO:REFRAIN]`, `[RESO:MASTERSTORY]`. One dinner-conversation account
of a lost engagement carries most of this weight — but it is the principle with the highest
sales relevance in the lens, because it is the only one that treats the champion's re-delivery
as the real channel.

## Tier 4 — `assertion`: stated, illustrated, never supported

Offer these as prompts, and say out loud that they are unsupported when you do.

**20. Persuasion reduces to raising pain and lowering pleasure on the "no."**
`[RESO:PAINPLEASURE]` — the two-emotion reduction is bare assertion; only the Kotter citation
beside it is sourced.

**21. Audience taxonomies.** Four actor temperaments `[RESO:FOURACTORS]`, seven reward categories
`[RESO:REWARD7]`, six resistance buckets `[RESO:INOCULATE]`, broad-versus-analytical audience
profiles `[RESO:KAIROSCALIBRATE]`, the sales-cycle and adoption ladders `[RESO:PROCESSSEG]`.
Useful checklists; measured constructs they are not. None should be quoted as a finding.

**22. Distinctiveness beats safety.** `[RESO:SAFETYORANGE]`, `[RESO:OWNANGLE]` — a camouflage
analogy and a transparency list, with no data. Note the built-in contradiction: the book also
concedes conservative committees punish novelty, and supplies no rule for telling the two rooms
apart before you are in one.

**23. Vulnerability buys openness.** `[RESO:HONESTFLAW]`, `[RESO:CONFESS]` — reciprocity of
openness stated as principle with a Guber quotation. No boundary is given on how much
vulnerability is too much, which is exactly where it fails in a live deal.

**24. Recency governs the close.** `[RESO:NEWBLISS]` invokes a "principle of recency" that is
named but not sourced.

**25. Attention physiology.** `[RESO:MODESHIFT]`'s fight-or-flight rationale and
`[RESO:FACTPLUSFEEL]`'s body-chemistry-during-stories claim are unsourced. Per the lens's ethical
contour, neuro-flavoured justifications are **refuted by design** — use the moves, drop the
mechanism story.

**26. Persist through rejection; hold conviction under pushback.** `[RESO:REJECTREPS]`,
`[RESO:NOBACKDOWN]`, and the ethics check `[RESO:SLICKCHECK]`. Moral exhortation with folk-tale
and biographical illustration. All three share the same defect: no stopping rule. Nothing here
distinguishes an idea that failed from an idea that was communicated badly, or conviction from
stubbornness — the lens must supply that judgment from outside the book.

## What the ranking implies for how this lens speaks

- Push hard on: destination-before-content `[RESO:BIGIDEA3]` `[RESO:FROMTO]`, contrast as a
  structural device, the document/presentation split, hostile screening, and building for the
  champion's retelling.
- Offer with a caveat: the full sparkline arc, delivery-craft scoring, the audience taxonomies.
- Never present as evidence: applause counts, view counts, self-reported executive-survey
  percentages, or the rehearsal-won-the-grant attribution. See `myths-and-demotions.md`.
- The lens has **no** Tier 1 support for any claim about a purchase, a renewal or a decision.
  Every measured outcome in the source is applause, views, or the participant's own report.
