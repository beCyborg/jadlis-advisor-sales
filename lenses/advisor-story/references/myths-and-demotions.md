# advisor-story — myths, demotions and documented failure modes

What this layer itself rejects, plus what the layer's own sources assert that must not leave this lens as fact.

## Demoted in full: the pop-neuroscience layer

The second source explains its techniques with brain mechanisms. The techniques are kept; the explanations are not transmitted, and must never be said to a buyer or used as an argument in a verdict. Each is pop-science framing, mechanism unproven — the move stands on practitioner evidence only.

- **Mirror neurons as the reason a told story transfers feeling.** Sourced to trade books and a conference talk, not primary studies, and contested in the literature it borrows from. Affected moves keep their practitioner standing: `[STORY:ACT-OUT]`, `[STORY:CHECK-IN]`, `[STORY:TEND-STORY]`.
- **Oxytocin / bonding-chemistry framing of disclosure and trust.** No measurement anywhere in the source; disclosure moves survive on workshop practice alone: `[STORY:GO-FIRST-DISCLOSE]`, `[STORY:MISTAKE-STORY]`, `[STORY:SHARED-LOW-POINT]`.
- **Left brain / right brain division of labour.** The stated rationale for handwriting cards in keywords and for drilling until the mechanics disappear. Treat `[STORY:HANDWRITE]` as an anti-perfectionism device and `[STORY:DRILL-FIRST]` as ordinary rehearsal.
- **Limbic-first decision making and the triune brain** ("decisions are made emotionally, then justified") — sourced to "research now tells us" with no named study in either book. The behavioural advice does not need it.
- **7% words / 38% tone / 55% nonverbal**, and the 93%-is-nonverbal remainder. The underlying studies concerned judging feelings from mismatched single-word cues, not general communication. `[STORY:EYE-LISTEN]` and `[STORY:WHOLE-BRAIN-LISTEN]` keep the practice of watching the other channels; the ratio is folklore.
- **10,000 hours** as the practice frame — cited secondhand and then deflated by the same authors, who concede storytelling needs far less.
- **Storytelling as humanity's primary means of conveying ideas for ~95,000 years** — attributed to an anthropologist friend. Rhetoric, not a citation.
- **~6,000 emotion words in English** — unsourced.
- **The lens spec is stricter than the books here:** neuro-justifications are REFUTED by design in this council. If a verdict of this lens reaches for one, that is a defect in the verdict.

## Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

- **"20% of sellers make 80% of revenue, and storytelling is what separates them."** Unaudited self-assertion. The 87/13 variant comes from a vendor index quoted secondhand from a keynote; the 90/10 variant is one executive's impression; the ancestor is a 1970s corporate rule of thumb. None of it establishes that story training moves the ratio — neither book reports a before/after on any client's distribution. `[STORY:RATIO-AUDIT]` is the honest version: run your own concentration numbers.
- **Client outcome claims from the second source's own training clients** — 43% revenue growth, reach expanded 300%, dependence on an incumbent cut to zero, doubled dating matches (n=1, outside sales entirely). All self-reported by parties with an interest, none controlled.
- **Vendor-reported practice numbers** — usable version by the fifth or sixth take, ~30 minutes to perfect a story, 30–90 minutes of practice per week — come from a training-software vendor. `[STORY:RECORD-ITERATE]` keeps the pattern (middle takes get worse), drops the counts.
- **Practice ratios** (Olympic swimmer >1,000:1, footballer 200:1, salesperson 0–5 minutes per call hour) — one expert's estimate, self-reported.
- **Value-lift by story** — ~100 thrift objects averaging ~$1.29 resold with invented stories for ~$3,612, quoted as a ~2,800% lift. The canon author himself hedges that part of the gain may be listing effects; it measured eBay listings, not sales calls.
- **Recall figures** — 63% remembered story details versus 5% remembering any individual statistic — come from one classroom exercise, not a controlled study.
- **Trust surveys** — a 1999 general-trust poll and a 2007 commercial ranking of family, journalists, advertising and telemarketers. Neither measures storytelling; the bridge to it is the author's inference.
- **The 51%-of-a-herd-moves threshold** and the adoption-curve percentages are borrowed analogies with unnamed sources.
- **Quota figures** (under 50% of B2B IT sellers hitting quota; ~17% judged likely to) — self-reported, unsourced.

## Debunked inside this tradition

- **"Any meaningful set of words is a story."** Mission statements, message tracks, capability claims and testimonials are useful and are not stories; treating them as stories is the common category error. `[STORY:SIXTEST]`
- **"Passing the story test means it is good."** Bad stories pass; and a well-built pitch can fail four of six markers and still work.
- **"Any motivational story helps."** Directly contradicted: only benefit-to-others moved output; the self-benefit group matched the control. `[STORY:MOTIVESTORY]`
- **"Ask them for a story."** Reliably fails — people encode experience as things that happened, and the request triggers performance anxiety. The one exception is salespeople. "Tell me your best story" is additionally ambiguous. `[STORY:INTERVIEW-ELICIT]`, `[STORY:CUSTOMER-QUESTIONS]`
- **"Ask questions and discovery takes care of itself."** Closed questions produce one-word answers and a follow-up chain that never reaches narrative. The second source goes further and judges three decades of question-based models as not having changed outcomes — that judgment is its own assertion, not a finding. `[STORY:STORYQ]`, `[STORY:QUEST-FROM-HEAR]`
- **The doctor/patient framing of discovery.** Buyers recognise pain-elicitation questions from every prior vendor, so the move signals what the seller hoped to hide. `[STORY:ABORT-INTERROGATION]`
- **"State the trait and it transfers."** Promising you will always tell them when you are not the best option, or listing credentials, is a low-credibility substitute for an enacted past case. Answering yes confidently to every hard question can *reduce* credibility — the buyer infers you did not understand. `[STORY:TRUSTCANDOR]`, `[STORY:HONEST-UNKNOWN]`
- **"Rebut the competitor's smear directly."** Backfires twice: it sounds desperate and seeds the idea in listeners who had not heard it. `[STORY:NOTWHO]`
- **"Justify price by explaining your costs."** Rent, equipment and cash-flow needs explain your need, not the buyer's gain. `[STORY:PRICE-BENEFIT-STORY]`
- **"Restate the benefits when they say not now."** Dead weight; the leverage is the problem created by waiting. `[STORY:DELAY-REGRET]`
- **"We're flexible, anything you want."** Inert. One concrete past accommodation tells the customer what to ask for. `[STORY:GOTOBAT]`
- **"Lead with the conclusion."** Inverted pyramid, memo structure and tell-them-what-you-will-tell-them are named as wrong for stories. `[STORY:LESSON-TO-END]`
- **"Use all five senses; set the season."** Fiction advice that damages sales stories — irrelevant scene-setting announces a performance. `[STORY:DATE-ANCHOR]`
- **"Delivery polish is the skill."** Ranked far below selection and construction; performance that upstages the pitch counts as failure even when enjoyed. Memorization is a failure mode, not preparation. `[STORY:INVISIBLE]`, `[STORY:NO-VERBATIM-SCRIPT]`
- **"Emotion clouds judgment."** Rejected — reasoning with emotion stripped out is worse. Note this rests on a borrowed neurology popularization and is an argument, not a result.
- **"Only exaggeration is dangerous."** Under-telling is a real failure: hedging every detail buries the story in qualifiers. `[STORY:ACCURACY-NOT-PRECISION]`
- **"Never let the truth get in the way of a good story."** Flagged as convenient cover; the stated cost of drift is misleading the audience and permanently damaging the relationship. `[STORY:WITNESS-TEST]`
- **"Top performers succeed because they run the methodology."** The second source's own affiliate numbers collapse that inference, and the authors concede bottom performers abandon a taught methodology within about ten days.
- **"Relationship skill is an unteachable gift."** Attacked as a dodge — but the counter (everyone is wired for story) is asserted from popular-science books, not demonstrated.
- **"More slides and more detail persuade."** The named failure is overload and confusion; analytic appetite is unbounded, so product detail turns the call into endless product discussion. `[STORY:VISCERAL-SLIDE]`, `[STORY:DECK-BREAK]`
- **Named anti-patterns:** "Why I'm So Great" masquerading as a personal story; the About Us timeline masquerading as a company story; treating the resolution as the turning point; telling a story only as an opener and abandoning it for the rest of the cycle; telling a story and then not yielding the floor; sending a recap of a story the buyer never told.

## Documented failure modes of the techniques

- **An unrehearsed live demonstration** in front of a buyer produced the opposite proof and killed the deal for over a year. Rule extracted: never run a tactic whose outcome you cannot predict.
- **The "if I could show you a way to save X, would you buy today" opener** — reported as universally repellent to the procurement informants.
- **Manufactured personal connection is detected.** Both sources rely on it and neither offers a test that separates a genuine disclosure from a staged one; the second source's own opening exemplar is a negotiator deliberately setting aside his real feelings to manufacture similarity. `[STORY:CLUEHUNT]`, `[STORY:SHARED-LOW-POINT]`
- **A mismatched feeling-story hero** — a wealthy suburban peer told to a poor rural district — kills the "just like you" mechanism outright. `[STORY:SPONSOR-FEELING-STORY]`
- **A predictable "surprise"**, heavy background music, and performing the emotion yourself all read as staging. `[STORY:FLAT-DELIVERY]`
- **"Everything got better after I arrived" stories** rate worse than unreachable-hero stories.
- **Memory drift** — the telephone game in which, over years of retelling, all the children in the story become you. `[STORY:FACT-PARITY-LINE]`
- **The sob-story sale** that distracts from quality and price criteria the seller knows the product fails — the canon's own example of illegitimate emotional use. `[STORY:MANIP-CHECK]`
- **Emotion-probing that overshoots the setting.** Workshop participants wept; a business meeting cannot hold that, and the source gives no safety layer, no consent norm and no guidance on the limits of a salesperson's competence. `[STORY:EMO-LADDER]`, `[STORY:TEND-STORY]`
- **Disclosure entering a record.** In procurement-scored, regulated or litigated contexts, admitted mistakes become risk findings and "I have never done this" becomes a capability gap. Every vulnerability move inverts here. `[STORY:DUMBASS-BEAT]`, `[STORY:MISTAKE-STORY]`, `[STORY:HONEST-UNKNOWN]`
- **The guilt lever.** `[STORY:FAVOR-TRIAL]` trades an unrelated favour for compliance; the authors themselves call it uncomfortable and used it for lack of another path. Ethically borderline — if you would not state the ask plainly to the buyer, do not run it.
- **Third-party exposure without consent.** The second source's own examples use a mentor's child's addiction and clients' mistakes as raw material without discussing consent. `[STORY:HUMOR-THEN-HEART]`, `[STORY:MISTAKE-LIST]`

## Structural demotions of the layer as a whole

- **On discovery, questions beat stories.** The layer's own structure chapters make the seller the talker for the story's duration, and the one move that returns the floor is rated the hardest of the three exits to execute. Only `[STORY:STORYQ]`, `[STORY:SILENCE]`, `[STORY:THREEASKS]`, `[STORY:OFFICEOBJ]`, `[STORY:GOFIRST]` and `[STORY:POST-STORY-SILENCE]` survive that objection cleanly, and `[STORY:GOFIRST]` only if immediately followed by shutting up.
- **Opportunity cost is never priced.** No move asks what a two-minute story displaces in a thirty-minute call, and the wish list points one way only: more stories.
- **The manipulation limiters are self-administered.** `[STORY:MANIP-CHECK]`, `[STORY:WITNESS-TEST]` and `[STORY:GODIN-BUYER-TEST]` all ask the seller to grade their own honesty, with no external check and no consequence structure.
- **The method is unfalsifiable as taught.** Discomfort is pre-framed as evidence of learning, early failure is promised as part of the arc, and no failure case of the method itself appears in either book — a rep who tries it and loses has no way, inside this framework, to conclude that it did not fit. `[STORY:PREFRAME-DISCOMFORT]`
- **Authority confound, untouched.** The best confession cases are told by CEOs, founders and owners; the counterargument that the CEO could sell that way *because* he was CEO is answered rhetorically and never tested.
- **Channel decay.** Silence, mirroring, body cues and acting out degrade or invert on video and asynchronous channels; the second source is a 2012 in-person book and the canon a 2016 one. Named tools of that era are period examples, not recommendations.
