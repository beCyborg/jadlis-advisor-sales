# advisor-story — core principles, ranked by evidence

Reading rule: the rank below is about *what holds the claim up*, not about how often the move is used. The lens ceiling is `practitioner-n-many` — nothing in either source is a controlled trial of sales storytelling, and no before/after on win rate, cycle time or quota attainment appears anywhere. Where a component is an assertion, it is named as one. Agreement between the two sources is a same-school echo (both are practitioner storytelling books, one author explicitly citing the other), never independent confirmation.

## Tier A — practitioner-n-many, sourced from the buy side

The strongest material in this layer, because the informants sit through more pitches than any seller does and have no stake in a storytelling method selling.

1. **Buyers ask for a small, predictable set of company stories.** Founding, values, product origin, how it is made, integrity — these were the recurring answers when procurement people were asked what they want to hear. `[STORY:BUYERWANT]`, `[STORY:FOUNDING]`
2. **Enacted candour outranks stated candour.** Two past cases — you told a customer you were not the right answer; you disclosed your own mistake first — were named by procurement informants as what earns trust immediately. The equivalent promise about the future is unverifiable and worth little. `[STORY:TRUSTCANDOR]`, `[STORY:DIFFSTORY]`
3. **Scripted-sounding delivery is the top tell that triggers "I am being sold to".** This is the buy-side finding under the whole no-script rule, not a stylistic preference. `[STORY:NO-VERBATIM-SCRIPT]`, `[STORY:CONVERSATIONAL]`, `[STORY:INVISIBLE]`
4. **A hero the buyer cannot map onto themselves does not work.** Sellers who always star in their own stories were flagged by a procurement informant; unreachable celebrity heroes fail the same way. `[STORY:MIRROR-HERO]`, `[STORY:HERO-RECAST]`
5. **Your own procurement department is the cheapest buy-side critic available.** The canon author's going-in hypothesis — that sellers would be the primary teachers — was reversed by these interviews. `[STORY:PROCUREMENT-FEEDBACK]`

## Tier B — practitioner-n-many, sourced from the sell side or from workshops

Same ladder token, weaker position: the informants are sellers recalling wins, or attendees of the authors' own paid training. Selection bias by construction — no failed telling is in the corpus, and no frequencies are reported.

6. **Selection beats delivery.** Fix the objective or the belief first, then pick the story that carries exactly that; a story chosen for entertainment value fails regardless of polish. Both sources converge here, and it is the single most load-bearing craft claim in the layer. `[STORY:LESSON-FIRST]`, `[STORY:STORY-SEARCH-SFC]`, `[STORY:25-STORY-MAP]`
7. **A told story needs a visible struggle, and an internal one beats an external one.** A flawless-hero story is the documented failure mode in both sources; the workshop version insists on the beat where the teller was plainly wrong. `[STORY:SURFACE-STRUGGLE]`, `[STORY:DUMBASS-BEAT]`, `[STORY:CONFLICT-HUNT]`
8. **The seller discloses first, then yields the floor.** Reciprocity is the engine: the buyer's admission is bought with the seller's. This is the second source's core loop and it is measured nowhere. `[STORY:GO-FIRST-DISCLOSE]`, `[STORY:PASS-TORCH]`, `[STORY:POST-STORY-SILENCE]`
9. **Context is the most-skipped element and its absence blocks listening.** Who, when, where, what they wanted, and the stakes — two or three sentences, then cut to action. `[STORY:CONTEXT-4W]`
10. **Length is a constraint, not a target: 1–3 minutes, with rehearsed short and long variants.** Derived from the canon author's own corpus (median ~280 words) and the second source's delivery experience; the tier list is descriptive of what those practitioners tell, not of what works better. `[STORY:LENGTH-TARGET]`, `[STORY:CUT-BY-TEMPLATE]`, `[STORY:MINI-STORY]`
11. **Emotion is placed, not sprayed.** Map the beats, pick one or two, escalate from naming to showing; spreading emotional load evenly flattens the story and raises the manipulation risk. `[STORY:EMO-MAP]`, `[STORY:TELL-ME]`, `[STORY:SHOW-ME]`
12. **Build backward from the point, on prompts rather than prose.** Point → resolution → setting → complication → turning point, captured as keywords, is a repeatable construction drill run across many workshop attendees. Its colour-and-card mechanics are the authors' preference, not evidence. `[STORY:CARD-BUILD]`, `[STORY:BLUE-CARD]`, `[STORY:BOARD]`
13. **Questions should be derived from what was just heard, not from a sequence.** The second source's authors built two of the dominant question-based methodologies and then retracted the sequencing rule; that retraction is data about the authors' beliefs, not about buyer outcomes. `[STORY:QUEST-FROM-HEAR]`, `[STORY:ARC-NOT-PAIN]`, `[STORY:ABORT-INTERROGATION]`
14. **The transportable artifact is the buyer's own story written back to them.** A recap in the five elements is forwardable inside the account and simultaneously qualifies the contact. `[STORY:RECAP-EMAIL]`, `[STORY:SILO-CRUMBS]`
15. **Story supply is an organizational problem, not a personal one.** Interview cadence, shared venues, a database and a wish list are what keep stories alive past a single rep's tenure. `[STORY:SOURCE-OTHERS]`, `[STORY:STORY-VENUES]`, `[STORY:STORY-DATABASE]`, `[STORY:WISH-LIST]`
16. **Interviews produce stories only if you never ask for one.** People encode experience as things that happened; the request itself triggers performance anxiety. The single exception is salespeople. `[STORY:INTERVIEW-ELICIT]`, `[STORY:CUSTOMER-QUESTIONS]`, `[STORY:SALESREP-QUESTIONS]`
17. **Honesty rules are explicit and hard-edged, but self-administered.** Accuracy matters and precision does not; hard points are frozen and soft points have leeway; embellish no more than you would embellish a number in a business review. `[STORY:HARD-SOFT-POINTS]`, `[STORY:ACCURACY-NOT-PRECISION]`, `[STORY:EXPECTATION-PREFACE]`, `[STORY:MANIP-CHECK]`

## Tier C — practitioner-anecdote

One case each, usually self-reported by the person who won. Treat as existence proofs — this can work somewhere — never as base rates.

18. **A first-person mistake story can reset a cold room.** The flagship instance is one call: a three-minute mistake story, zero questions asked, the real requirement surfacing about 45 minutes in. The teller's authority is uncontrolled. `[STORY:MISTAKE-STORY]`, `[STORY:RIGHT-TOOL]`
19. **Admitting a company-level failure can buy back credibility after a public misstep** — but only when the turning point and the delivered fix are real. `[STORY:DIRTY-LAUNDRY]`, `[STORY:HONEST-UNKNOWN]`
20. **A cost-of-waiting story moves a "not now" better than restating benefits.** One reused deferred-deal account. `[STORY:DELAY-REGRET]`
21. **A champion can be armed with a feeling story for a room you cannot enter.** The only move in the layer that addresses closed-door committee decisions, and it depends entirely on an existing sponsor. `[STORY:SPONSOR-FEELING-STORY]`
22. **Non-office contact time produces narrative that the meeting room suppresses.** One executive's account. `[STORY:OFFSITE]`, `[STORY:OFFICEOBJ]`
23. **Bottom-up tribal spread can beat calling high** — one contrast case against a cold call to a president. Presented by the authors as a retraction of their own earlier rule. `[STORY:TRIBAL-LEADER-HUNT]`, `[STORY:ENTRY-ALTITUDE]`
24. **Borrowed public cases substitute for a thin reference list**, provided no phrasing implies you did the work. `[STORY:BORROWED-ICON]`

## Tier D — assertion

Stated by the authors, sometimes borrowed from screenwriting, therapy or popular-science books. Usable as craft heuristics; not evidence of anything.

25. **The six-marker definition of a story.** Time, place, character, obstacle, goal, events — the gate the whole layer runs on is itself an unmeasured criterion borrowed from another storytelling author. `[STORY:SIXTEST]`
26. **Selling reframed as helping someone decide to change.** The second source's founding move; synthesized from outside disciplines with no sales-outcome data. `[STORY:REDEFINE-SELL]`, `[STORY:SHIP-CHECK]`
27. **Enter the call with no predetermined outcome.** Imported from a therapy-dialogue model; unverifiable by construction, since compliance cannot be observed. `[STORY:NO-MEMORY-NO-DESIRE]`, `[STORY:BUYER-AUTHORITY]`
28. **Silence as an elicitation device.** Argued as obvious-but-underused; no study, no case count, in either source. `[STORY:SILENCE]`, `[STORY:GOFIRST]`
29. **Structure rules borrowed from screenwriting** — arrive late and leave early, choose the audience's position relative to the hero, build character before harm. `[STORY:ARRIVE-LATE]`, `[STORY:AUDIENCE-POSITION]`, `[STORY:CHAR-KNOW]`
30. **Body-language vocabularies.** Chin, nod and hand-to-face readings are folk knowledge with unsourced rationale; usable only as prompts to probe. `[STORY:CUE-READ]`, `[STORY:WHOLE-BRAIN-LISTEN]`
31. **Card mechanics beyond the build order** — handwriting, colour coding, visual intensity scaling. The rationale offered for them is the hemisphere metaphor this lens does not carry. `[STORY:HANDWRITE]`, `[STORY:EMO-SCALE]`
32. **Never used as an argument here: the neuro layer.** Mirror neurons, limbic-first decisions, the 7/38/55 split and the 10,000-hours frame are demoted in full — see myths-and-demotions.md. Every move that rested on them is retained on practitioner grounds alone. `[STORY:CHECK-IN]`, `[STORY:DRILL-FIRST]`, `[STORY:VISCERAL-SLIDE]`, `[STORY:EYE-LISTEN]`

## The one external-research item, and why it is still capped

`[STORY:MOTIVESTORY]` is the only move in the layer touching a randomized study: three groups, ten minutes of reading before a shift, the benefit-to-others group more than doubling both pledge count and money raised while the control and benefit-to-self groups showed no significant change. It is carried at the lens ceiling anyway, because the subjects were fundraisers *reading* someone else's story, not sellers *telling* one, and because the move it supports is pre-call self-motivation — the one place in this layer where the buyer is not involved at all. Do not cite it as evidence that storytelling moves deals.

## Unresolved contradictions inside the layer

These are live, not editorial slips; both sources are in the file and they disagree.

- **Announce the story or not.** `[STORY:HOOK-ONE-LINE]` deletes any opener that flags a story coming; `[STORY:ASK-PERMISSION]` makes the explicit ask the state-change device. Pick by whether the story answers something just said (do not announce) or interrupts an analytical stretch (ask).
- **End on lesson-plus-ask, or lesson-then-nothing.** `[STORY:LESSON-EXIT]` allows the attached recommendation; `[STORY:LEARNED-CLOSE]` and `[STORY:POST-STORY-SILENCE]` forbid it and treat the ask after a climax as manipulation risk.
- **Perform or flatten.** `[STORY:ACT-OUT]` says relive the beats physically; `[STORY:FLAT-DELIVERY]` says the more emotional the material, the more neutral your face and voice.
- **Rehearse in front of a mirror or never.** `[STORY:DRILL-FIRST]` prescribes it; `[STORY:NO-MIRROR]` rejects it and a third expert view holds that mirror-practicers are identifiable by their flatness. `[STORY:REFINE-IN-FIELD]`-style live iteration is the third position, and the canon reports all three without adjudicating.
- **Closed questions.** The canon keeps them for discrete facts (`[STORY:STORYQ]`); the second source treats the entire diagnostic sequence as the thing to abandon (`[STORY:ARC-NOT-PAIN]`).

## What the layer does not contain at all

Pricing logic, negotiation mechanics, competitive displacement, close mechanics, renewal economics, forecasting beyond reading email tone, product-led and self-serve motions, and any digital-first buyer journey. The buyer here is always a person in a room or on a phone. Those layers must be imported from other lenses — this one has no opinion worth weighting on them.
