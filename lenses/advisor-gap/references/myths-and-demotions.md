# advisor-gap — myths and demotions

Two directions at once. **Demoted by the tradition**: what this layer actively rejects
from mainstream sales practice, with the reason. **Demoted inside the tradition**: the
book's own claims, numbers and techniques that must not be carried into a council verdict
at face value. The second list matters more — a lens that only repeats its author's
demotions is a fan, not an advisor.

---

## 1. What this layer demotes in mainstream sales practice

- **B.A.N.T. and single-decision-maker qualification.** Demoted to an output of discovery,
  not a gate before it — [GAP:NOBANT], [GAP:QUAL4]. WHY: budget appears when the gap is
  large enough; a non-signer who sees value routes you to a signer; a stated absence of
  need is a hypothesis. Caveat the tradition does not state: this is only affordable where
  discovery time per lead is not the binding constraint.
- **Closing techniques** — assumptive, option, urgency closes. Rejected as seller-centric;
  "good closers" recast as sellers who lost the many small yeses that make a final one
  possible ([GAP:NEXTYES]). WHY it is a demotion and not a refutation: no comparative data
  is offered, only the staircase-of-effort argument.
- **"People buy from people they like."** Demoted to a tiebreaker; credibility is what
  converts ([GAP:EXPERT]). Named failure mode: sellers who need approval skip the provoking
  questions and never walk away. Support is vendor-published (OMG/Kurlan, SEC) — argument,
  not evidence, and it overreaches in relationship-gated and channel motions.
- **Selling to the stated need.** Same surface symptom, different underlying causes
  ([GAP:NOTNEED]) — the three-headache-buyers argument.
- **Half-discovery, half-demo.** Splitting one hour into 30 minutes of questions and 30 of
  product is called fake discovery ([GAP:NODEMO]). A conditional "if" inside a demo is
  treated as diagnostic of a failed discovery rather than a style flaw ([GAP:NOIF]).
  Demoing everything is treated as irritation, not thoroughness ([GAP:SIXMAX]).
- **Traditional objection handling — asking the buyer to clarify the objection.** Called
  actively harmful: it produces a well-articulated reason you then cannot counter. Replaced
  by making the buyer reconcile the objection with their own stated outcome
  ([GAP:OBJDEFEND]).
- **"Just checking in" cadences.** Named as the desperation pattern that guarantees
  continued silence; replaced by holding the buyer's own words up ([GAP:GHOST]).
- **"Cold calling is dead / email is dead / social selling doesn't work."** The channel is
  demoted as a variable; the message is the variable ([GAP:CHANMIX]). Tooling is explicitly
  denied as a source of success. Also rejected as asks: 30 minutes "to learn about your
  business", 45 minutes to show a product benefit — both score negative on Offer − Ask.
- **Gut-based forecasting and status-theatre pipeline review** ("looks good, waiting on the
  demo") — no verifiable data, control conceded to the buyer ([GAP:PIPE7], [GAP:FACTSONLY]).
  Optimism, not dishonesty, is named as the main distortion; sandbagging is the mirror
  failure; a manager editing a rep's number is said to collapse ownership ([GAP:CHALLACC]).
- **Sales statistics as a hiring signal.** President's Club, quota history, industry tenure
  and big personality declared non-predictive ([GAP:NINETRAIT]). Note the replacement is an
  unvalidated trait model — a demotion without a better-evidenced substitute.
- **Loss excuses as causes.** "Price too high", "went dark", "no decision", "couldn't reach
  the DM" treated as symptoms of shallow diagnosis. **This is the tradition's least
  falsifiable move** — see §3.

## 2. Documented failure modes of the techniques themselves

The book names these; the council should treat them as the expected failure modes of any
advice this lens gives.

- **Rushing to a closing question after a handful of environment questions** — reported as
  appearing in essentially every training roleplay. The default failure of the method is
  not over-questioning, it is stopping too early.
- **Solving the wrong problem.** Leaves the buyer believing their problem was never a big
  deal — worse than not selling, because it burns the diagnosis itself. Direct AVOID
  condition on [GAP:ROOTCAUSE]: a confidently wrong root cause destroys the credibility the
  move exists to build.
- **Assuming you are too expensive**, and therefore never asking the questions that would
  have revealed the value.
- **Treating the four-step presentation as a linear checklist.** Discovery loops and
  overlaps; running it as a sequence produces the deposition feel ([GAP:QTIMING] AVOID).
- **Discovery treated as finished when a new stakeholder invalidates it** ([GAP:REDISCOVER],
  [GAP:GAPFLIP]).
- **Memory used instead of the CRM** — the [GAP:CRMCHAL] failure.
- **Solo adoption is fragile.** The author admits the method decays without managerial
  reinforcement. Convenient for a training vendor; still probably true.
- **[GAP:PIPE7] and [GAP:FACTSONLY] on an untrained or fear-driven team** produce padded CRM
  notes and invented specifics rather than better discovery — the obstacle-course mode.
- **[GAP:NEVERNO] and [GAP:CADENCE12] have no stopping rule** except explicit buyer refusal.
  In transactional or inbound contexts twelve problem-heavy touches are harassment; in
  gift-restricted accounts [GAP:SURPRISE] is disallowed outright.
- **[GAP:WIDEN] degrading into manufactured fear**, and its mirror — a rep using the exit to
  abandon deals that only needed more digging.

## 3. Demoted inside the tradition — what not to carry into a verdict

- **The unfalsifiable core.** Every loss is re-describable as a discovery failure, which
  makes the method immune to disconfirmation — and aligns perfectly with selling more
  training. Any verdict of the form "you lost because your discovery was shallow" must
  carry an alternative explanation alongside it or it is unfalsifiable by construction.
- **The untested conversion step.** The system optimises for a quantified, agreed gap and
  never measures how often that becomes a purchase. Status-quo bias and no-decision losses
  are absent as a category; the only competitor modelled is another vendor. [GAP:GAPMATH],
  [GAP:COMMIT15] and [GAP:HEALTHCHK] all inherit this hole. The counter-canon (PULL /
  Snyder) argues the default outcome of a found pain is no decision at all — the book never
  engages it.
- **No capacity model.** Discovery depth is never priced per opportunity, so the method
  cannot say which pipelines it is economic for. The single largest gap for anything below
  mid-ACV, and the mechanical source of the "допрос ради комиссии" reading.
- **Two rhetorical devices to discount outright.** (a) The "accept the framework or stop
  reading" demand at the end of Ch. 6 — a loyalty test, not an argument. (b) The
  pedestrian-into-traffic analogy, which grants the seller unilateral certainty about the
  buyer's interests. The latter is exactly where "protecting the customer" and "overriding
  the customer" stop being distinguishable, since the seller's certainty rests only on
  their own discovery notes.
- **Thin ethical guardrails.** The book states plainly that a seller can move the perceived
  size of the gap in either direction, then relies on the seller volunteering [GAP:WIDEN]
  and [GAP:GRACEEXIT]. [GAP:FSEMO], [GAP:PROBCHAIN] and [GAP:DEMANDCREATE] have no external
  check against manufactured urgency.
- **Neuro-rationales are REFUTED by design.** The ACC / error-related negativity /
  "oh-shit circuit" framing behind [GAP:GAPSUBJ] carries no citable source; under the lens
  spec this class of justification is refused regardless of whether the technique works.
- **Self-serving structure.** The hiring chapter makes the author's method the criterion of
  a good hire; the conclusion is a discovery call for the training business and the book
  ends on a training offer. Success claims are marketing-adjacent by construction.
- **Blind spots that make whole council questions unanswerable from this lens:** competitive
  dynamics (nothing on countermoves, incumbent defence, displacement economics, or two
  vendors both running diagnostic discovery on the same buyer); pricing/packaging/
  negotiation beyond "price the gap" ([GAP:TERMS] is the only mechanic, no discounting or
  multi-year policy); post-sale entirely absent (no onboarding, adoption, renewal,
  expansion, churn) despite its own impact logic applying directly there; the untrainability
  claim for eight of nine hiring traits; channel-to-segment mapping that is stereotype-level
  and pre-dates current outbound saturation and compliance regimes; and cultural/hierarchical
  variance — [GAP:QTYPES] provoking questions, [GAP:CONFUSED] and public correction of a
  buyer's reasoning are treated as universally safe.

## 4. Numbers: what may and may not be quoted

**Quotable as third-party (with attribution and its own caveats):** CSO Insights quota
attainment (53% in 2016 vs 63% in 2012); Gartner/CEB 2014 — at least 5.4 decision makers
per B2B sale, single decision maker plausible only under roughly $1,000; the Gong.io
call-corpus talk-ratio findings; InsideSales Labs 2017 (151 companies, nine quarters, in
HBR) — 3× more deals closed at end of month but 11× more lost, ~$98M average annual loss
per company; Kanter's ten change threats; the University of Arkansas chocolate/brand-age
study. Flag as **vendor-published**: Sales Executive Council 2009 (~6,000 reps; Challengers
40% of top performers, Relationship Builders 7%), OMG/Kurlan (82% of bottom-quartile reps
report a need to be liked; 89% of the top 5% report none, 1% believe relationships are the
key closing factor), and the *Challenger Sale* 53%-of-loyalty figure, which is itself a
second-hand citation.

**Never quotable as fact — self-reported by the author or his clients:**
- ARMS Reliability: +38% bookings and conversion from 30% to 45% in the first 12 months of
  training; the buyer being 29% off plan. One solicited testimonial from one client.
- The author's unpublished survey: 72% of social-media-using salespeople outperform peers,
  exceeding quota 23% more often. No methodology, no publication.
- ~99% of cold emails get no reply; 80%+ of deals lost; ~half of customers do not know they
  have the problem; 99% of the time the product's process improvement reveals competitive
  value; "hundreds" of roleplays.
- 8–12 touches to earn attention; 1–3% cold-call conversation/appointment rates presented as
  common knowledge; the ~4-second attention verdict.
- **Message copy quoted as if it were data:** "54% of salespeople make quota" and "80% of
  companies on legacy systems rank last in their industry" appear inside sample outreach.
  They are copy, not findings. Citing them as evidence is the sharpest citation trap in this
  digest.
- The neuroscience framing for intrigue — asserted, no source.

**Structural constants (safe to state as the method's shape, not as findings):** 5
current-state elements; 3 problem layers plus root cause; 3 future-state layers; 4 question
types; 4 qualification questions; 9 Truths of Selling; 9 hiring traits; 7 pipeline
verification questions; 10 change threats. Demo: two separate days, 3–4 days between, max 6
features, 45–60 minutes. Review: 2–10 min per opportunity, 15–30 min per rep; close dates
within 30 days; commits within ±15% both ways. Pipeline = quota ÷ close rate.

**Worked examples are the author's arithmetic, never measured results** — the growth-point
and deal-size gap sums, the $20K/month reporting workaround, the hotel's 20%/80%/22%
targets, the $50K/month × 6 = $300K deferral, the theatre subscriber case, the commit-math
scenarios, the $18B-telecom buyer letter figures. Use them to illustrate a calculation
shape; never as a benchmark for the user's own numbers.
