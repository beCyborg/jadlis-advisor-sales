## Posture and framing

### [GAP:DIAGFIRST] Treat every deal as a diagnosis, not a pitch
**Move:** Before any product talk, force yourself to state the buyer's problem, its business impact and its root cause in your own words. If you cannot, you have no deal yet — go back to questioning rather than advancing the stage.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-stage deals; whenever a rep can only describe the opportunity in terms of product fit or "they liked the demo".
**AVOID WHEN:** pure fulfillment / re-order motions, or commodity buys where the buyer's stated spec genuinely is the whole job — diagnosis theatre there reads as stalling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's consulting practice across B2B teams; the causal claim is untested.

### [GAP:NOTNEED] Sell to the problem, never to the stated need
**Move:** Treat the buyer's self-diagnosed need as a hypothesis. Ask what is actually producing the symptom before quoting what they asked for; offer the corrected solution only after they confirm the underlying mechanism (the Palm charger case: the buyer asked for a charger, the real fix was a case that stopped the buttons draining the battery).
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound requests with a specific SKU/spec attached; any "we just need X" opener.
**AVOID WHEN:** buyer is time-boxed and transactional, or already did the diagnosis with an internal expert — re-litigating burns goodwill and reads as an upsell attempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one retail story plus the assertion that need-selling is always wrong.

### [GAP:EXPERT] Be an expert, not a friend
**Move:** Replace rapport-building activity (small talk, hospitality, likeability effort) with visible domain expertise — know their processes, metrics and failure modes well enough to challenge assumptions and say uncomfortable things. Benchmark: you should be able to quit and open a consultancy in the field you sell into. Optimise for respected and useful, not liked.
**APPLY WHEN:** complex, solutions-oriented deals; reps whose pipelines are warm but stalled.
**AVOID WHEN:** relationship-dependent motions (channel/partner, long expansion accounts, high-context regional markets) where the blanket dismissal overreaches; also when "challenging" becomes a licence for rudeness without the expertise behind it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — position propped up by vendor-published third-party figures the author relays (Sales Executive Council via Dixon & Adamson; OMG / Kurlan), not by his own data.

### [GAP:NOPITCHBIO] Kill the company-history opener ("show-me economy")
**Move:** Remove founder story, tenure, award lists, logo slides and rankings from live meetings; let owned content (articles, e-books, video, social) carry credibility beforehand, and spend meeting time on the buyer's situation. Judge every touch by what you give the buyer, not what you tell them about yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** first meetings and demos; any deck whose first five slides are about you.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly asks for vendor credentials, security/compliance history or references — the book concedes longevity signals do help, just not delivered in the room.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the illustrative scenario is invented; the claim is repeated observation of buyer meetings.

## Prep and targeting

### [GAP:PIC] Build a Problem Identification Chart before outreach
**Move:** Pre-build a three-column table for your offer: Problems you can solve / Impacts each problem could have on a customer's business / Root Causes behind each problem. Complete it before the first email or call and use it as the map for question targeting. Expect many impacts but a finite, reusable set of root causes.
**APPLY WHEN:** onboarding a rep, entering a new segment, or before any prospecting sequence.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as a script to recite at the buyer — it is targeting prep, not a talk track; also weak where problems are genuinely idiosyncratic per account.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/channel · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's core training artefact; effectiveness self-reported.

### [GAP:ICPRANK] Build the ICP from the PIC and rank A/B/C/D
**Move:** From the problems, impacts and root causes in your PIC, derive which company types would suffer them — industry, size, affected departments, departmental responsibilities — then identify who inside those departments takes the greatest impact. Rank targets: A = many problems, high impact; B = few problems, high impact; C = many problems, low impact; D = few, low (ignore). Prioritise pain over problem count, so B outranks C.
**APPLY WHEN:** target list construction before any outreach.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no evidence for who feels the pain — the ranking then encodes your assumptions and filters out real buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a named prep routine built on the book's own PIC tool.

### [GAP:PIPEMATH] Size the pipeline off quota and close rate
**Move:** Compute required pipeline as quota ÷ close rate and count only qualified opportunities toward it. Treat unqualified opportunities kept for management optics as a symptom, not coverage.
**APPLY WHEN:** territory planning, quarterly pipeline review.
**AVOID WHEN:** close rate is unstable or unmeasured (new segment, new product) — the ratio then produces false precision.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard pipeline arithmetic restated; the claim that gap selling raises both volume and close rate is asserted.

## Prospecting and outreach

### [GAP:PROBFIRST] Open outreach on the buyer's most likely quantified problem
**Move:** From the PIC pick the problem most likely live for this buyer type, name it in the first two lines with a number attached to what it costs, and make the ask contingent on testing whether that number applies to them; add a secondary impact as a bonus. Cut company, product and partner claims from the opening entirely. Agreement to continue is the qualification event.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outbound into a segment you have charted and hold segment-level impact figures for.
**AVOID WHEN:** no credible segment number exists or the guessed problem is generic — an invented figure is disproved on the first reply, and a vague problem statement is indistinguishable from every other cold email; also weak in saturated segments.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — before/after rewrite of one rep's expense-reporting email plus general prospecting practice; no reply-rate data.

### [GAP:OFFERMINUSASK] Net the offer against the ask
**Move:** Score every outreach as Offer − Ask = Value from the buyer's side, treating the requested minutes as a monetary spend. If the offer cannot plausibly cover the time, either cut the ask (shorter meeting, one question, a reply) or raise the offer (a number, a benchmark, a diagnostic). Make the offer about their named problem, not your product, and sequence small asks before the big one.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing any outreach message, cold-call script, voicemail or meeting request where the buyer owes you nothing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has already engaged and set the agenda — re-pricing an agreed meeting reads as hedging. Note the arithmetic is subjective and self-scored: a discipline, not a measurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a named formula with worked positive and negative examples plus one rewritten sample email.

### [GAP:GAPSUBJ] Subject line as an open knowledge gap
**Move:** Write the subject so it asserts something surprising about the buyer's peer group that they cannot verify without opening — a challenge or an education hook, not a product name or a meeting request.
**APPLY WHEN:** email and social outreach where open rate is the binding constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** the body cannot pay off the hook; clickbait with a pitch behind it destroys the second touch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — the "four seconds to register" and "oh-shit circuit" framing carries no named source; the neuro-rationale is refused by the lens's ethical contour.

### [GAP:EMAILSCORE] Email Response Probability Scorecard
**Move:** Score each outbound message on separate components — reasonableness of the ask, value of the offer, intrigue (challenge or education), relevance, length — and average them. A single weak component drags the composite down even when offer and ask are strong, so fix the weakest element rather than adding strengths.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing or reviewing outbound sequences; coaching a rep's templates.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as a pass/fail send gate — it is a self-scored heuristic with no calibration data behind it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — author-designed rubric, no outcome data.

### [GAP:SURPRISE] Break the pattern with the physically unexpected
**Move:** Make the first touch physically or formally unexpected (the book's example: outreach delivered inside a box of fresh croissants) so it fails the buyer's "same as always" filter.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-value named accounts where per-touch cost is justified.
**AVOID WHEN:** compliance-restricted buyers (public sector, procurement gift rules) or volume outbound where cost per touch matters; a gimmick without a problem-relevant message buys attention and then wastes it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single illustrative suggestion.

### [GAP:MYSTERY] Create anticipation the buyer must resolve
**Move:** Open with a statement that raises an unanswered question in the buyer's mind — e.g. that a named competitor cut a cycle time using your product — so that only engaging with you closes the loop.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outreach where you hold a concrete, checkable fact about their market.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot substantiate the claim, or the mystery is manufactured — a hook that resolves into nothing destroys credibility on the first call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a message pattern the author teaches, shown with sample lines.

### [GAP:KNOWGAP] Open a knowledge gap the buyer cannot close alone
**Move:** Lead with something true about their role, industry or competitors that they probably do not know — ideally that their standard practice is producing the outcome they are paid to prevent. Research failure modes, obscure regulation or comparative data, and ask a question the buyer cannot answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** outbound to specialists whose domain you genuinely know and where you can produce a defensible fact.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are bluffing expertise — a specialist detects it immediately; also when the framing implies the buyer is incompetent at their own job, which triggers defensiveness rather than curiosity.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the reliability-industry example, where the client's prospects were unknowingly creating the failures they were hired to prevent.

### [GAP:BUYERLETTER] Audit outreach against the buyer's own questions
**Move:** Before sending, check the message against what a senior buyer actually asks: do you know my challenges, my three-year strategy, my current vendors and why I buy from them, my competitors, whether my staff can absorb your product, and whether you intend to be a partner (shared risk), a vendor, or a commodity supplier.
**APPLY WHEN:** enterprise outbound to executives.
**AVOID WHEN:** SMB or low-ACV outbound where this depth of research cannot pay back per contact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one unsolicited letter from a single named-role executive at an $18B telecom, reproduced in the book.

### [GAP:CADENCE12] Multi-touch cadence over 10+ business days
**Move:** Run a scripted rhythm — initial email, ~2 business days later a matching voicemail, ~3 days later a fresh email, ~3–4 days later another voicemail, then another email; if still silent wait ~4 days and call, ~4 more and email, continuing to roughly 12 touches. Each pass changes channel, offer, and the impact or problem addressed.
**APPLY WHEN:** outbound to an account worth sustained effort with a known, credible problem hypothesis.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional/inbound motions or low-value accounts, where 12 problem-heavy touches read as harassment; also when the message is simply repeated — repetition without variation is what makes cadence feel like stalking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — prescribed training schedule; the 8–12 touch figure is stated without a cited source.

### [GAP:CHANMIX] Let the buyer segment choose the channel, then mix
**Move:** Build the sequence across email, phone, video, LinkedIn and other social, and physical mail, chosen by how the target population actually communicates (email/social for tech and younger buyers; phone and mail for traditional manufacturing) rather than defending one channel as alive and another dead. Keep every channel problem-centric and avoid gimmicks.
**APPLY WHEN:** designing outbound sequences for a new segment; especially when one channel has gone silent.
**AVOID WHEN:** buyer or industry norms make a channel intrusive (regulated buyers, personal social accounts), or the segmentation is treated as demographic stereotype rather than a tested hypothesis — the split offered is coarse and dated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own unpublished survey numbers, not independent data.

### [GAP:IMPACTROTATE] Rotate the impact, not the pitch
**Move:** On each touch surface a different impact of the same problem set, or a new data point, or a relevant piece of research, so the buyer's pattern-matching cannot pre-classify the message as "that seller again."
**APPLY WHEN:** touches 2 through 12 of a sequence.
**AVOID WHEN:** the seller has only one impact hypothesis — rotating into speculation invents problems the buyer does not have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — training practice; the mechanism claim about buyer expectation is asserted.

### [GAP:NEVERNO] Never say no on the buyer's behalf
**Move:** Treat silence as unanswered, not declined; keep working the cadence until the buyer explicitly says stop, on the reasoning that stopping unilaterally is the seller deciding no for the buyer.
**APPLY WHEN:** the problem hypothesis is quantified and material to the account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has asked to be left alone, or the "problem" is generic — this is the move most likely to degrade into harassment and is the sharpest edge of the discovery-fatigue objection to the method.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — recollection of buyers thanking him for persistence; no base rate for those who did not.

### [GAP:LATECHAL] Late-sequence challenge message
**Move:** Around touch 9–10 with no response, send a message that hands the burden of the decision back to the buyer — explicitly asking them to say where this stands rather than continuing to guess.
**APPLY WHEN:** deep into a cadence, prior touches were value-carrying, and the account still matters.
**AVOID WHEN:** used early as a shortcut, or phrased as guilt/breakup manipulation rather than a genuine request for a decision.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's own practice.

## Discovery — access, structure, delivery

### [GAP:LETHELP] Run the "are they letting me help?" checkpoint
**Move:** At each contact, test whether the buyer is saying yes to sharing — numbers, issues, worries. If they are guarded or you are doing the talking, stop advancing the deal and rebuild credibility instead of scheduling the next step.
**APPLY WHEN:** any stage; especially before booking a demo or proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** early inbound conversations where guardedness is normal procurement behaviour rather than a credibility failure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a heuristic from practice, no measurement.

### [GAP:RFPOUT] Treat discovery-less RFPs as out of scope
**Move:** Classify a traditional RFP with no access for questioning as a checkbox contest, not a sale, and decide participation on that basis — the method explicitly does not apply where you cannot ask why or reframe the buyer's problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound RFP/tender with locked requirements and no discovery access.
**AVOID WHEN:** you shaped the requirements earlier, or the RFP permits a bidder Q&A / discovery phase.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a scope limit of the methodology, with no comparative win-rate data.

### [GAP:NOBANT] Replace B.A.N.T. qualification with discovery
**Move:** Stop gating opportunities on Budget/Authority/Need/Timing up front. Let discovery expose all four: budget appears when the gap is large enough; a non-signer who sees value routes you to the signer; stated absence of need is a hypothesis to test; timing moves when the cost of waiting is quantified.
**APPLY WHEN:** demand-creation motions into accounts unaware of the problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** high-volume, low-ACV pipelines where discovery time per lead is the binding constraint — the book gives no answer for capacity limits, and dropping all gating there inflates cost per deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — argued from principle plus the relayed Gartner/CEB decision-maker count; the anti-B.A.N.T. claim itself is untested.

### [GAP:TALKRATIO] Front-load questions, invert the talk-to-listen ratio
**Move:** In discovery, aim to listen more than half the call and ask materially more questions than a traditional discovery would; treat "the close happens at the beginning" as the operating rule and drop assumptive/option/urgency closing techniques.
**APPLY WHEN:** all consultative first calls; coaching reps off pitch-heavy discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** buyer explicitly wants a demo-first evaluation, or executive slots too short for open exploration.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many relaying external-research — the book cites a Gong.io analysis of recorded calls rather than producing its own dataset; directional correlation, not validation of this method.

### [GAP:QTYPES] Use the four question types deliberately
**Move:** Classify each question before asking it — Probing (open, presses for specific detail), Process (open, asks how work actually gets done), Provoking (open, pushes the buyer to view their current state from a new angle: what happens when…, has there ever been a time when…, if you did X what would happen), Validating (closed, replays what you heard to confirm accuracy).
**APPLY WHEN:** structuring a discovery agenda; reviewing a recorded call for coverage gaps.
**AVOID WHEN:** provoking questions before any credibility exists — the same question that opens a buyer at minute 30 reads as accusatory at minute 3.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's taxonomy, taught in his training.

### [GAP:CMDSTART] Command-statement starters instead of interrogatives
**Move:** Convert questions into invitations — tell me about…, help me understand…, please describe…, walk me through… — so a dense fact-finding pass feels like a conversation the buyer controls rather than a deposition. Credited to Deb Calvert (*Discover Questions Get You Connected*).
**APPLY WHEN:** any block of consecutive fact questions; senior stakeholders sensitive to being quizzed.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes a verbal tic across every turn, or it is used to soften a question volume that is itself the problem — phrasing does not fix an interrogation, only its surface.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — attributed technique, adopted without measurement.

### [GAP:QTIMING] Sequence questions instead of firing them
**Move:** Plan the order of questions so each is asked at a point the buyer is willing to answer it properly. A question that returns a shallow answer is spent — you rarely get to ask it again well.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparing a discovery call; sensitive or numeric questions in the plan.
**AVOID WHEN:** rigid adherence turns the call into a checklist; the book insists discovery loops and overlaps rather than running linearly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — drawn from the author's roleplay coaching practice.

### [GAP:TONECAL] Calibrate tone to who owns the failure
**Move:** When the root cause is something your buyer personally built, approved or runs, shift to humility and deference — drop even confident delivery. Otherwise stay warm and engaged.
**APPLY WHEN:** root cause traces to the person in the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot read the buyer's emotional state; the book's own remedy (improve empathy) is not an in-call fix.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — craft advice with an illustrative example, no data.

### [GAP:VALIDATE] Validating questions as a running checksum
**Move:** Periodically play back your interpretation of what the buyer said and ask whether you got it right, especially before switching topics. Frame it as making sure you are not mis-assessing them.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout every call; mandatory before moving to a new topic or a proposed solution.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as filler after every sentence — turns the call into an echo chamber and burns the buyer's time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — loosely attributed to psychologists' validation technique with no named source, so transfer to sales rests on practice.

## Discovery — current state, impact, root cause

### [GAP:CS5] Excavate all five current-state elements
**Move:** Run discovery until you can write down, for this specific account: literal operational facts, the problems, each problem's impact, the root causes, and how the buyer feels about it. Restrict fact-finding to the parts of the business your product can affect (selling project management software → project count, sizes, cycle length, approvals, resourcing, change management; not their HR department).
**APPLY WHEN:** any consultative deal before proposal or demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** scope creep into areas you cannot influence — exploring unsolvable problems wastes both parties' time; also unsuited to short transactional cycles where five-element depth exceeds the buyer's patience budget.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the asserted correlation between current-state knowledge and win rate is stated without measurement.

### [GAP:PROCESSLAYER] Drill to the process layer, not the summary
**Move:** When a buyer describes a workflow at summary level ("we email them, then mail a flyer"), push for the tactical steps — who exports what, into which tool, which box gets ticked, who prints and labels. Value and differentiation live in those steps.
**APPLY WHEN:** your product replaces or automates manual work.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot influence the process you are mapping — depth there costs buyer patience and returns nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked example from practice.

### [GAP:NOOPEN] Ban open-ended (unquantified) answers
**Move:** Whenever a buyer answers vaguely ("not growing fast enough", "revenue is down a lot", "we need more people"), stop and ask them to define the term numerically before moving on. Convert every stated problem and impact into a figure.
**APPLY WHEN:** always in discovery; especially before writing a business case.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer genuinely lacks the number — the next move is helping them find it, not pressing repeatedly; over-pressing here is a main source of the interrogation complaint.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — two scripted exchanges plus a consistent practice claim.

### [GAP:PROBCHAIN] Chain problems rather than stopping at the first
**Move:** After a buyer admits one gap in capability, keep asking downstream consequence questions until you have a numbered stack (regional data can't be consolidated → no consistency across the sales team → no bird's-eye performance view → goal at risk → quota misses → unpredictability → planning breaks down). Do not pivot to solution at problem #1.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer is open and answering; mid-discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chain is being extended for pressure rather than accuracy — stacking six problems on someone who has one is manufacturing a gap.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one illustrative dialogue.

### [GAP:BASICFACT] Check for the missing basic fact
**Move:** Before concluding discovery, ask the obvious quantity question you may have skipped — headcount, unit count, volume. A single missing base number can unlock problems nobody in the account had connected (the football-coach case: 12 players → forfeits → recruiting failure → enrolment decline).
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery feels complete but the impacts do not add up.
**AVOID WHEN:** effectively never — it is a low-cost check; the only risk is asking it so late it reveals inattention.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single constructed example.

### [GAP:IMPACTQ] Let the buyer state the damage
**Move:** Ask impact questions in command-statement form — tell me how this affects you, describe the impact on your department, what are the consequences each time this happens — then quantify the answer. Never deliver the bad news yourself; the urgency has to be the buyer's own conclusion.
**APPLY WHEN:** after problems are confirmed, before any solution framing.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as staged fear-building; the book rejects scare tactics, and the line between letting them see the damage and steering them to a rehearsed conclusion is where this move goes wrong.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — scripted examples from practice.

### [GAP:LADDER3] Escalate technical → business → existential problem
**Move:** Log the technical problem (broken tool, bad database, antiquated inventory system, weak reporting) as the conversation opener only, then ask what it costs the organisation — extra headcount, wasted inventory, lost revenue — and then what happens if nothing changes over the planning horizon. Record the three layers separately alongside the root cause and sell to the business layer, since technical problems repeat across prospects while business problems are unique per account.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer frames their issue as a feature gap or tooling annoyance; whenever the champion is technical and the budget holder is not.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely low-stakes transactional purchases, or purely technical buys with a delegated budget — inflating a minor annoyance into an existential threat reads as manipulation and invites pushback.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's core diagnostic frame, illustrated with police-department and theatre-company cases plus cost figures.

### [GAP:ROOTCAUSE] Diagnose root cause before proposing
**Move:** For each confirmed problem, ask why the buyer thinks it exists, how their current processes contribute, and what the previously bought tool actually changed; treat the first causal story as provisional, enumerate candidate causes (no leads → can't prospect, no marketing, wrong message, wrong target market, wrong tooling) and narrow to the real one, stated in the buyer's own operational terms, before offering a solution. The differentiator is being the practitioner who identifies why, not the fifth one guessing at symptoms.
**APPLY WHEN:** impact is agreed; buyer offers a plausible-sounding single cause, or has already tried and failed with other vendors or internal fixes.
**AVOID WHEN:** you lack the domain depth to be right — a confidently wrong root cause destroys the credibility the move exists to build; also when the buyer personally built or owns the failing system and is defensive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — argued by medical analogy (heartburn misdiagnosed by five doctors) plus standard consulting-and-training discovery.

### [GAP:DEMANDCREATE] Demand-creation question ladder
**Move:** Open on a process the prospect believes is fine, then walk it step by step with volume and frequency questions until the hidden manual cost surfaces, and only then describe the automated alternative as a question ("would that change your business?"). The ticketing sequence: how do names reach the CRM → how many new buyers → how many tickets → so that's how many manual entries → who is paid to do it → how do they know a buyer is new → does the step ever get missed → how do you market to those buyers.
**APPLY WHEN:** target says "we don't need that"; latent-problem markets where the buyer has normalised a workaround.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has no time budget for a multi-turn ladder, or the arithmetic is being run *at* them to corner them — the method's interrogation risk peaks here, and the counter-hypothesis bites: an exposed problem's default outcome is still "do nothing".
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single scripted dialogue presented as illustration.

### [GAP:THREATSCAN] Scan for the ten change-resistance threats
**Move:** During and after discovery, name which of Kanter's ten threats each stakeholder is exposed to — loss of control, excess uncertainty, surprises, too much change at once, loss of face, insecurity, extra work, ripple effect, past resentments, real danger — and adjust sequencing, pacing and proof to defuse the live ones instead of pushing harder.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal stalls late despite an agreed problem; a champion cools after internal socialisation; incumbent-replacement deals where someone chose the incumbent.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a private label for the buyer rather than a change to your own plan — diagnosing "he's just insecure" and pressing on is the misuse mode.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — borrows Rosabeth Moss Kanter's named list, illustrated with invented vignettes rather than measured outcomes.

## Future state, gap math, motivation, qualification

### [GAP:FSQUANT] Quantify the future state, refuse vague answers
**Move:** Ask what they are trying to accomplish, how the process should work, how long it should take, how many more units/leads/tickets, how much less staff time, how much more revenue — and what solving it would mean to the organisation, the team and to them personally. Push every answer to a number and a timeframe (the hotel case: back to 20% growth, 80% conference-room utilisation, 22% profitability) so the figures become the contrast against the current state.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery, alongside current-state work and before pricing or proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** the current state is still vague — a future state built on unquantified problems produces a fictitious gap that collapses under procurement scrutiny; if the buyer genuinely does not hold the numbers yet, collect them across several interactions rather than forcing invention on call one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the methodology's central data-collection routine, shown with worked examples and no outcome data.

### [GAP:FSEMO] Anchor the buyer emotionally in the future state
**Move:** Ask how they will feel once the problem is gone and what it lets them do, so the future state is imagined concretely rather than described abstractly. The claim: pairing current-state pain with anchored future-state pleasure raises the value the buyer assigns to closing the gap, versus exposing pain alone.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer is engaged but not urgent; change-averse stakeholders.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gap is unverified — this is the method's classic manipulation surface; future-pacing on an unverified or small gap manufactures urgency the deal cannot support.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as "the magic of gap selling", with no support beyond the author's confidence.

### [GAP:GAPMATH] Compute the gap per metric, then net it against price
**Move:** Subtract current from desired on each quantified dimension (growth %, revenue, close rate, average deal size), then ask whether the resulting gap justifies the full cost of change — price plus implementation, process change and risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-discovery, before proposing or forecasting.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's metrics were invented on the spot; arithmetic on made-up numbers produces false confidence in the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's title mechanism, shown with worked examples; the conversion step from "gap agreed" to "gap bought" is never measured.

### [GAP:WIDEN] Widen the gap, or exit on a small one
**Move:** If the distance between current and future state is too small to warrant change, go back for more discovery to surface additional impacts. If it still will not widen, say so, exit, and refer the buyer to someone who fits. Treat the exit as a positive outcome that frees selling time.
**APPLY WHEN:** impacts come back unquantifiable or trivial; net gap is marginal; the deal stalls on value rather than mechanics.
**AVOID WHEN:** "widening" degenerates into manufacturing fear about impacts the buyer does not feel — that is where the method shades into the pressure it claims to avoid; the mirror risk is a rep using the exit to abandon deals that only needed more digging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — routine practice, with the disqualification half framed as both ethics and pipeline hygiene.

### [GAP:GAPDISTANCE] Ask how far they are from their own goal
**Move:** Instead of accepting the buyer's own problem diagnosis, ask the distance between where they are and their stated plan or target, and let the size of that number land on them.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer arrives with a self-diagnosed problem and a preferred solution.
**AVOID WHEN:** no formal plan or target exists to measure against.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the buyer-side account from ARMS Reliability's COO, who reported being 29% off plan and never having framed it that way.

### [GAP:FS3LAYER] Model the future state in three layers
**Move:** Describe the outcome as technical future state (what the tool does), business future state (what that changes operationally and commercially), and core future state (competitive position, stock, board satisfaction). Anchor the sale on the third layer.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the demo narrative or the proposal after discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo buyer with no board/market layer — the third tier collapses to personal stakes and should be framed as such.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a structuring heuristic without supporting cases.

### [GAP:WHYDIG] Dig to intrinsic motivation with repeated "why"
**Move:** Keep asking why the buyer wants the stated outcome until you reach the personal or organisational driver behind it; two buyers with an identical problem statement can have incompatible motivations (his: commute and blood pressure; hers: adopting twins with special needs), and only the motivation tells you whether your solution lands.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery and re-discovery; before configuring a proposal or a custom scope.
**AVOID WHEN:** stacked "why" questions on a buyer already showing discovery fatigue — the sequence is meant to feel conversational, not like an interrogation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted from consulting practice, illustrated with a constructed example.

### [GAP:WHYDRIVE] Ask what is driving the change
**Move:** After summarising current and future state back to the buyer, ask them to describe what is driving this change. Then run the rest of the sale on that intrinsic motivation rather than on features or even on the headline metric.
**APPLY WHEN:** late discovery, once trust has been earned by the earlier work.
**AVOID WHEN:** asked cold or early — the book conditions the honest answer on having gap-sold up to that point.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — credited with winning the ARMS Reliability engagement against three better-known consultants.

### [GAP:REDISCOVER] Never inherit a stakeholder's current state
**Move:** When a new buyer joins, run discovery again from scratch for that person. Do not present the first buyer's problems, impacts and root causes to a peer in the same role.
**APPLY WHEN:** champion introduces a colleague, peer or committee member.
**AVOID WHEN:** the book names no exception — it treats this as a cardinal rule; the practical cost is meeting count, so budget for it rather than skipping it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own lost deal at a change-management training company, where a second sales director rejected a solution built on her peer's discovery.

### [GAP:QUAL4] Four-question qualification instead of B.A.N.T.
**Move:** Declare the opportunity qualified only if all four are yes: there is a problem you can fix; the buyer agrees the problem exists; the buyer wants it fixed; the buyer will work through the fix with you. Budget, authority and timing are deliberately deferred.
**APPLY WHEN:** end of discovery, before any demo, proposal or forecast commit.
**AVOID WHEN:** short-cycle inbound where the buyer's mandate is already fixed and the extra time costs more than it de-risks; also when your org's forecast process contractually requires budget confirmation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's named qualification gate, repeated as the demo precondition.

### [GAP:CRMCHAL] The CRM Challenge (self-audit)
**Move:** Have a manager or peer pick a random open opportunity and read your notes aloud with all identifying data stripped. If you cannot name the account from the problem/impact/root-cause detail alone, your discovery was too high-level and you must go back for specifics.
**APPLY WHEN:** self-audit of pipeline quality; coaching reps whose notes read as generic ("wants to grow more").
**AVOID WHEN:** weaponised as a management compliance test — forced note-quantity audits push reps to pad the CRM rather than run better discovery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a named diagnostic used in the author's training engagements.

## Demo

### [GAP:NODEMO] No discovery, no demo
**Move:** Schedule discovery and demo as two meetings on two different days, ideally with three to four days between them to design the demo around what you learned. If the buyer resists discovery, present the demo as an inherently two-step process. Be willing to decline a demo request outright.
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound or outbound request to "see the product"; any deal where you cannot yet answer the four qualification questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional, low-ACV, self-serve-adjacent purchases where the demo *is* the evaluation and a two-meeting gate loses the deal to a competitor who just shows the product.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — an absolute rule from training practice; no comparative win-rate data.

### [GAP:NOIF] Ban conditional framing in the demo
**Move:** Run the whole presentation without saying "if you have this problem" or "if you ever struggle with." Every feature shown is tied to a problem the buyer already confirmed. Self-test: complete a 45–60 minute demo with zero uses of the word.
**APPLY WHEN:** any post-discovery demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** you genuinely did not get the information — the instruction is to go back to discovery, not to improvise certainty you do not have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as a named "Demo Challenge" used in coaching.

### [GAP:SIXMAX] Cap the demo at six features
**Move:** Show at most six features, each mapped to a serious business problem, and spend the time on business value per feature rather than coverage. Hard-cap the session at roughly one hour.
**APPLY WHEN:** feature-rich product, buyer with a short list of real problems.
**AVOID WHEN:** formal RFP-driven evaluations that require documented coverage of a feature matrix.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — asserted rule of thumb, illustrated by analogy rather than measurement.

### [GAP:ANCHORQ] Affirming questions after every feature
**Move:** After each feature, ask a closed question tying it to a specific metric in their future state (can you see how this affects churn / input time / conversions). Accept yes/no here. A "no" is a stop signal: halt the demo and return to discovery on the spot.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout the demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered as a rhetorical tic — mechanical "can you see how" after every click reads as agreement-farming and trains the buyer to nod without meaning it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the anchoring rationale is a general cognitive-bias claim with no named study.

### [GAP:DEMOMAP] Build the demo map from the PIC
**Move:** Take the solvable problems from the Problem Identification Chart, rank them by impact on the buyer's organisation from greatest to least, and use that list as the demo agenda. Anything not on the list is not mentioned.
**APPLY WHEN:** demo prep, after discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** committee demo where a non-champion attendee's problems were never discovered — the map covers only the buyers you actually interviewed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — direct application of the book's own PIC tool.

## Moving the deal: criteria, process, commitments

### [GAP:DECCRIT] Ask for the decision criteria explicitly
**Move:** Ask how they will decide which solution is best and which factors weigh most. Treat the answer as a cheat sheet and cross-check it against discovery: a criterion you never heard about (e.g. an integration requirement) means you missed something and must go back immediately.
**APPLY WHEN:** after future state and intrinsic motivation are known; before proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** very early — asking before you know the future state gives you a list you cannot evaluate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard step in the author's deal-strategy work.

### [GAP:CRITALIGN] Test criteria against the buyer's own outcome
**Move:** Hold each stated criterion against the stated future state and ask whether satisfying it actually moves them toward the outcome. Where it does not, surface the contradiction and ask them to explain the criterion's importance. The goal is that they own the reasoning, not that you steer them to where you win.
**APPLY WHEN:** criteria list contains items unconnected to the business problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are using it to disqualify a competitor's differentiator rather than to protect the buyer — the book names this misuse and forbids it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — illustrated with the theatre season-ticket seating example.

### [GAP:CONFUSED] The "I'm confused, you said…" challenge opener
**Move:** Open any challenge by stating that you are confused, then restate the buyer's own earlier words, then lay out the inconsistency and ask them to explain it. Variants: "I was under the impression," "I thought." The device puts the possible error on you, so the buyer can correct the record without losing face.
**APPLY WHEN:** any contradiction between stated goals and current behaviour, criteria, objections, or silence.
**AVOID WHEN:** overused within one relationship — repeated feigned confusion becomes transparent and reads as a scripted gotcha; also risky where hierarchy or culture makes public correction costly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's signature phrase, reused across four separate situations.

### [GAP:BUYPROC] Map the buying process, not just the criteria
**Move:** Ask the buyer to walk through the company's actual buying steps — RFP, shortlist, committee presentation, contract negotiation, signature authority, HR or partner sign-off. Combine those steps with the decision criteria to build the deal strategy: pull out early if the process is one you cannot serve, or plan discovery with each newly revealed participant.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-qualification, before forecasting a close date.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo buyer with signature authority — the mapping collapses to one step and the questioning adds friction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many relaying external-research — the multi-stakeholder premise comes from a cited Gartner / *The Challenger Customer* statistic, not the author's data.

### [GAP:NEXTYES] Sell the next yes, not the close
**Move:** Identify the single next commitment the buyer must make (a test drive, a CEO meeting, sending specs) and sell that as a small sale. Track whether the buyer's effort is rising alongside yours — a rising staircase means the deal advances; only your effort rising means it does not. If the buyer resists the next yes, return to discovery and find the missing piece of current state, future state or motivation.
**APPLY WHEN:** every stage transition in the cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to manufacture micro-commitments with no value to the buyer — each yes must serve their process, not your stage-gate hygiene.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter's central model, argued from the staircase-vs-line effort graph, not from measured cycles.

### [GAP:YESMAP] Map every required yes before you need it
**Move:** Enumerate in advance every department and person whose agreement the deal requires, and what each one's current state and objection will be; a single unmapped approver can stall or kill a deal that is otherwise sold. Where a known blocker exists (IT, legal, HR), bring them in proactively once the primary buyer is bought in — it accelerates the next yes and demonstrates research into how their business works.
**APPLY WHEN:** solutions touching integration, data, employment practice, procurement or compliance.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo-buyer transactional deals where dragging in extra departments creates approval requirements that did not previously exist.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's ski-resort platform startup, where ski-school heads bought in, IT was pre-empted, and an HR/FLSA liability concern killed every deal.

### [GAP:GAPFLIP] Watch for the gap flipping negative
**Move:** Re-evaluate the gap whenever a new stakeholder introduces a future-state risk; a large positive gap (more revenue, better experience, more repeat customers) can be erased or inverted by a risk the new party sees (legal liability, fines), at which point there is no compelling reason to change and the deal is unwinnable as configured.
**APPLY WHEN:** a late stakeholder joins, or a deal stalls right after a new department is looped in.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to rationalise losses that were actually discovery failures.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own failed startup, analysed retrospectively.

### [GAP:ROSE] Return on sales effort — learn to say no
**Move:** Judge where to spend time by return on sales effort, not by buyer demands. Self-test for loss of control: quoting without asking why, granting trials without shared results, meeting every request while none of yours are met, reshaping your business model on request. Fear of losing the deal is itself the signal that you stopped gap selling and lack information.
**APPLY WHEN:** a deal where you are absorbing all the work and risk.
**AVOID WHEN:** early prospecting stage — the book concedes the power balance is legitimately asymmetric until you have earned attention.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a positioning doctrine with a checklist, no outcome data.

### [GAP:TERMS] Convert an unreasonable demand into balanced terms
**Move:** When a buyer demands something one-sided (e.g. a 90-day trial from a company that does not do trials), either decline outright or grant it against conditions: an installation fee, written measurable success criteria, and a commitment to purchase if those criteria are met. Frame it as equalising risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** solution stage; buyer states a non-negotiable procedural demand.
**AVOID WHEN:** the demand is a genuine, documented procurement requirement rather than a preference — refusing there just ends the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author claims buyers often accept, self-reported without counts.

### [GAP:NOPRICEYET] Refuse to send pricing before discovery
**Move:** When asked for pricing cold, decline and explain that price cannot be set before you understand their organisation, the problems they want solved and what they want to accomplish — because the value of the same offering varies with how badly they want the change.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold request for a quote with no meeting and no context.
**AVOID WHEN:** published/standard pricing exists and is discoverable anyway, or in procurement-driven markets where refusing a price sheet removes you from the list.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own policy, justified by the tow-truck value argument.

## Troubleshooting, objections, disqualification

### [GAP:GHOST] Re-engage a dark prospect by holding their words up
**Move:** Drop the check-in cadence. Send one direct message that names the contradiction: they stated a problem and a goal, agreed the solution addressed it, and have now gone silent — has something changed, did they solve it another way, or have they abandoned the goal? Ask for the status either way.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer previously committed to next steps and stopped responding despite multiple attempts.
**AVOID WHEN:** no real prior commitment exists — without a stated goal to hold up, the message is just pressure; also poor fit where the silence has an obvious external cause you already know about.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recovery framing with a self-reported illustrative payoff, not a measured recovery rate.

### [GAP:STALL] Restart a stalled deal with the cost of delay
**Move:** Agree to the postponement, then restate their own numbers: each month without a change costs them X, while their stated target requires Y — ask whether the current pace still gets them there and whether that is acceptable. Either they defend the delay or they re-commit; both give you information and a path to the next yes.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer keeps taking calls and restating interest while meetings and deliverables slip.
**AVOID WHEN:** the delay is caused by a known external event (budget freeze, reorg) — replaying cost-of-delay there is tone-deaf.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a worked script pattern; no data on stall-recovery rates.

### [GAP:OBJDEFEND] Make the buyer defend the objection
**Move:** Do not ask them to clarify the objection (that hardens it into a reason you cannot beat). Instead ask them to reconcile it with their stated desired outcome: does resolving this concern actually move them closer to what they said they want? Never defend your product; make them explain why the missing thing matters against the outcome.
**APPLY WHEN:** any late-stage objection where you hold quantified current and future state.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection is a real, disqualifying gap — the book's own test says if resolving it would genuinely advance their outcome, exit rather than argue.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — illustrated with local-business and theatre-seating objections; the claim that most objections dissolve is self-reported.

### [GAP:PRICEFRAME] Reprice the outcome, then go silent
**Move:** On "too expensive," restate the quantified outcome the purchase produces and ask them to justify why that outcome is not worth the price. Then stop talking entirely and let them answer. Respond only after hearing their reasoning.
**APPLY WHEN:** price objection in a deal where the gap has been quantified with the buyer's own numbers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gap was never quantified — without their numbers the move is a bluff; also risky where the objection is a negotiating ritual rather than a value question.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented with three worked examples including the tow-truck case.

### [GAP:BUDGVAFF] Separate "no budget" from "cannot afford"
**Move:** Before responding, establish which one it is. No budget means the money exists but was allocated elsewhere — respond with the arithmetic of waiting (monthly loss × months of delay plus forgone opportunities) and make them defend the delay. Cannot afford means the money does not exist — walk away.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer cites budget as the blocker.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating every affordability statement as a reallocation problem — a genuine affordability wall is not sellable at any level of value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a taught distinction, illustrated with the $4M house parable.

### [GAP:COSTOFWAIT] Convert a budget deferral into cost-of-waiting
**Move:** When a buyer says they want it but must wait for next year's budget, keep asking questions until the buyer computes for themselves that the cost of the delay exceeds the cost of acting now — rather than accepting the timing objection or discounting to overcome it.
**APPLY WHEN:** genuine enthusiasm plus a timing/budget objection, and the problem's impact is already quantified with their numbers.
**AVOID WHEN:** budget is a hard constraint rather than a priority choice, or the impact figures are the seller's estimates rather than the buyer's — then this is pressure dressed as arithmetic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recalled deal, outcome self-reported.

### [GAP:GRACEEXIT] Disqualify out loud and refer
**Move:** When the fit is wrong, summarise what you learned about their current state, restate their goals, say plainly that you do not think you can help, and hand them a recommendation for someone who can.
**APPLY WHEN:** gap cannot be widened; criteria or constraints rule you out.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a manufactured takeaway to trigger loss aversion — that inverts the stated intent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated in both Ch. 9 and Ch. 13 as standard practice.

### [GAP:WALKAWAY] Walk away when you cannot serve the real motivation
**Move:** If the discovered intrinsic motivation is something your product cannot deliver, disqualify and say so rather than selling the partial fix that trades one problem for another.
**APPLY WHEN:** motivation surfaces late and sits outside your capability envelope.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gap is merely smaller than hoped but still real — that is a downsized deal, not a disqualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule, justified by reputational risk in an era of public reviews, no data.

## Management: pipeline, commit, culture

### [GAP:CRMCOACH] Run the CRM Challenge on the rep
**Move:** In review, put the rep through the same self-test taught for individual use — walk the deal's current state, future state, gap, intrinsic motivation, decision criteria, evidence, and next step, out loud.
**APPLY WHEN:** weekly pipeline review in a team that has been taught the framework.
**AVOID WHEN:** the team has not been trained on it — the same questioning without prior teaching reads as interrogation and produces defensive fabrication.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's consulting practice with client sales teams.

### [GAP:PIPE7] Seven-question pipeline verification set
**Move:** Score each reviewed deal against a fixed set — (1) does the rep understand the current state (literal business, critical problems, assessed organisational impact, buyers' emotional state, root cause); (2) the future state and its physical impact; (3) can they identify the gap and explain how it was calculated; (4) name the intrinsic motivations; (5) list the decision criteria; (6) produce evidence for all of it; (7) state what happens next and when. Only all-yes counts as an accurate deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** any structured pipeline review; onboarding a new rep.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional/high-velocity motions where per-deal depth this large is uneconomic — reserve it for deals that materially affect the commit; applied top-down without training it turns discovery into an obstacle course.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard review protocol across client implementations.

### [GAP:FACTSONLY] Demand specific, definitive facts and send the rep back
**Move:** Reject generalities ("the customer wants to grow faster") and press for the number, the date and the current baseline; when a rep answers with what they think rather than what the buyer said, treat it as not-known and assign them to go have the conversation again. Answers the buyer actually gave should exist in the CRM and be repeatable near-verbatim.
**APPLY WHEN:** every review; especially on intrinsic motivation and decision-criteria answers.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied without the accompanying safety culture — pressing for evidence in a fear-driven team produces invented specifics instead of admitted gaps.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated coaching practice.

### [GAP:TALKERTELL] Treat verbosity as a data-deficit signal
**Move:** Listen for brevity and specificity; a rep who needs ten minutes of narrative to justify a deal is flagged as probably lacking data, since people talk most where they know least. Cut the story, ask for the facts.
**APPLY WHEN:** reviews with mixed-tenure teams.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a personality judgment — the heuristic is soft, and complex enterprise deals legitimately need more explanation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a stated pattern observation, no data.

### [GAP:REVIEWBOX] Time-box and target the review
**Move:** Do not walk the whole pipeline; select the deals nearest the close and largest in impact on the rep's commit, budget roughly 2–10 minutes per opportunity and 15–30 minutes per rep, and let the process compress once the team learns the standard. Keep watching un-challenged deals for an updated next yes even when not reviewing them in depth.
**APPLY WHEN:** weekly cadence with a pipeline too large to review exhaustively.
**AVOID WHEN:** small pipelines where skipping deals hides the only trouble; or when "targeting big deals" becomes an excuse never to inspect a weak rep's small ones.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — timing ranges from the author's implementations.

### [GAP:RATEMATH] Sanity-check commits against the rep's own rates
**Move:** Cross-check pipeline value against the rep's average close rate, average deal size, average cycle length and rate of new deals added — e.g. a $60K commit backed by $130K of pipeline at a 50% close rate and a two-month cycle is credible in February; $200K of pipeline committed in full at a 40% close rate is not and triggers a question.
**APPLY WHEN:** reviewing commits and forecast rollups.
**AVOID WHEN:** the rep's historical rates are thin or the mix has changed (new segment, new price point) — the arithmetic then encodes a stale base rate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked examples from the author's management practice.

### [GAP:NEXTYESMGT] Manage to the buyer's next yes
**Move:** For every live deal require the rep to state the buyer's next yes — a commitment from the buyer, not an activity of the seller (an intro to the CEO, sending specs, a written price agreement, agreeing to a site visit) — plus why the buyer values giving it, since every yes costs the buyer time or effort and needs its own value case. In review, ask them to repeat the next yes and its rationale.
**APPLY WHEN:** every deal, every review cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to manufacture artificial micro-commitments as a compliance ladder rather than steps the buyer genuinely benefits from.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — central management practice, illustrated with the author's own deals.

### [GAP:STALLALARM] Same next yes for a month = alarm
**Move:** Track whether the next yes changes between reviews; a deal parked on the same next yes for a month is treated as a signal that something is wrong, prompting intervention rather than another quarter of reassurance without evidence.
**APPLY WHEN:** weekly review cadence with next-yes tracking already in place.
**AVOID WHEN:** cycle length legitimately exceeds the window (long enterprise procurement, budget cycles).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a routine early-warning pattern.

### [GAP:HEALTHCHK] Healthy pipeline checklist
**Move:** Certify a pipeline as healthy only when reps can supply close dates accurate within 30 days, quarterly commits accurate within ±15% either way, a clear next yes, a clear deal strategy, adequate coverage to reach commit and quota, and evidence for all of it.
**APPLY WHEN:** quarterly forecast rollup; assessing an inherited team.
**AVOID WHEN:** early-stage or new-segment teams with no historical rates — the accuracy thresholds are unmeetable and become punitive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard, plus one named client outcome (ARMS Reliability) that is a solicited testimonial.

### [GAP:COMMIT15] Rep-owned commit within ±15%
**Move:** Ask each rep what the data says they will close this quarter, hold them to ±15% in both directions (missing high is also a miss), and lock the number for the quarter. The manager rolls individual commits into a group commit upper management can plan against.
**APPLY WHEN:** teams with enough deal history to compute rates, and a review process that verifies the underlying data.
**AVOID WHEN:** brand-new teams, new products, or lumpy high-ACV pipelines where a single deal exceeds 15% of the number — the tolerance is then arithmetically unreachable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — management practice across client organisations.

### [GAP:ACCEPTLOW] Accept a below-quota commit
**Move:** Take the low number as given, do not order the rep to go find more pipeline and come back with a better figure, and spend the recovered 90 days on new prospecting approaches, marketing support, and coaching the specific reps whose skills caused the shortfall.
**APPLY WHEN:** the commit is defended with data and the rate math supports it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the low commit is chronic and uncoupled from any improvement plan — acceptance without remediation just formalises underperformance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — prescribed stance with a worked scenario ($1.1M quota vs $900K commit).

### [GAP:CHALLACC] Challenge gently, then accept
**Move:** When a commit contradicts the rep's own close rate or deal stages, name the contradiction and ask why they are over-committing — often surfacing missing information — but if the rep holds the number after the challenge, let it stand; forcing a rep who computed $80K to chase $200K reliably yields $80K anyway and destroys ownership.
**APPLY WHEN:** commit-setting conversations at quarter start.
**AVOID WHEN:** the challenge tone slides into doubting and interrogating rather than trusting and verifying — the author draws this line explicitly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — with a worked example (40% close rate, $200K pipeline, $200K commit).

### [GAP:COACHCULT] Lead as coach, reinforce at every touchpoint
**Move:** Reframe the manager role as coaching — teach, train, motivate, be demanding, then step back during execution — and re-anchor the gap-selling objective in every pipeline meeting, coaching session, hiring decision and annual review rather than treating training as a one-off event.
**APPLY WHEN:** rolling out or sustaining the methodology across a team.
**AVOID WHEN:** the organisation's comp and quota pressure contradicts the message — reinforcement language layered over end-of-quarter panic produces cynicism.

## Hiring

### [GAP:NINETRAIT] Screen on the nine traits
**Move:** Rank candidates on curiosity, critical thinking, empathy, problem-solving, leadership, creativity, deliberate learning, coachability and business acumen, letting experience and network be tiebreakers rather than filters. Curiosity is operationalised as compulsive layered questioning (why, why again, then how/who/when/how many) plus the ability to reassemble answers into a picture; critical thinking as gently challenging whether the buyer's stated status quo, future state and understood impacts are actually true.
**APPLY WHEN:** building or reshaping a team around the method.
**AVOID WHEN:** hiring for velocity/transactional motions where the job really is speed and volume — the trait profile is tuned to consultative discovery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the author's own trait model; no validation study offered.

### [GAP:NOKNOWALL] Screen out the know-it-all
**Move:** Deliberately avoid candidates certain of their own prowess, on the grounds that they resist feedback and cannot improve; test instead for people who solicit critique and treat criticism as useful. Coachability is treated as bundled with curiosity and deliberate learning.
**APPLY WHEN:** final-round evaluation, reference calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to filter out confident senior sellers who are simply already competent — the signal is response to feedback, not assertiveness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — invokes the Dunning-Kruger effect by name without citation, as rhetorical support.

### [GAP:HIREQS] Behavioural interview question set
**Move:** Interrogate temperament directly: how much patience, grit and curiosity do they show; how much time do they spend reading and tracking industry news and innovation; do they project credibility; do they get frustrated or relish hard problems; are they interested in selling or in problem-solving; do they care about helping clients or about quota; are they eager to learn; do they lead or isolate others; do they have a useful perspective on almost any topic.
**APPLY WHEN:** interviewing for a discovery-heavy role.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as self-report — these are answerable with the desired answer, so they need behavioural evidence and work samples behind them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — interview heuristics, no hiring-outcome data.
