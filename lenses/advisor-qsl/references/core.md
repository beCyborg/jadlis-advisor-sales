# advisor-qsl — Core principles, ranked by evidence

Layer: qualification / MEDDPICC. Source: *The Qualified Sales Leader* (McMahon, 2021), evidence ceiling `practitioner-n-many`.

**Read the ceiling first.** Nothing in this lens reaches `field-study` or `external-research`. There is no control group anywhere in the source: no losing-deal cohort, no A/B, no baseline, no external dataset. The strongest thing available is a pattern the author reports having run repeatedly across five sales organizations. Anyone quoting this lens as "proven" is over-claiming by two rungs. Everything below is sorted by how much even that weak support actually attaches.

---

## Tier 1 — practitioner-n-many: repeated across many deals and several organizations

These are the components with the most support the source can offer: patterns the author states he ran at multiple companies, usually with more than one worked case attached.

1. **The gate is a customer event, not a rep opinion.** [QSL:GATE] The single load-bearing mechanism of the whole system. Stage data becomes trustworthy only when what closed the previous stage is something the customer did — replied, scheduled, signed, wrote. Everything else in the method (forecast, inspection, coaching) is parasitic on this one idea. Corollaries with the same support: CRM stage is an unverified claim and is gamed in both directions [QSL:CRMSTAGE]; the Discovery gate has a concrete form, the confirming email plus a booked next meeting [QSL:DISCOEMAIL].

2. **Whoever controls the decision criteria wins the evaluation.** [QSL:POV-CRITERIA] [QSL:DC-CONTROL] The most repeated claim in the book, and the one the largest number of scenes are built to demonstrate. Criteria drift is treated as the observable that tells you whether you are gaining or losing — which is also its strength: it is measurable, unlike rapport. Its enforcement arm is the two-condition callback with the EB [QSL:TWOCOND] and the same-criteria-for-all-vendors agreement [QSL:POV-QUIDPRO].

3. **Champion is defined by action, coach by information.** [QSL:CHAMPTEST] [QSL:CHAMPQUAL] The distinction is operationalized rather than asserted — it becomes a list of things you ask a person to *do* (arrange a demo elsewhere, sponsor the EB meeting, co-write criteria, introduce procurement) and a list of things a manager asks the *rep*, not the customer. This is the lens's genuine contribution and the part least duplicated by other layers of the council.

4. **The process is the map, the qualification method is the GPS.** [QSL:MEDD-GPS] MEDDPICC elements are earned at specific stages; a missing element is a stage diagnosis, not a score. Stated as the book's central management claim and repeated throughout.

5. **Control is read from the direction of information flow.** [QSL:CONTROL-3] When the champion starts needing information from you, control has moved to you; when criteria and process arrive fully formed and the rep is merely told them, the competitor holds control. A clean, checkable diagnostic — its weakness is that it can be declared from rapport instead of artifacts.

6. **Inspection follows inspiring and coaching, and inspects the why.** [QSL:ICI] The author's operating model across five organizations, with the honest counter-claim attached: managers who only inspect become scorekeepers, and the book ties that directly to attrition.

7. **Qualification questioning goes down levels, not across items.** [QSL:ROOTQ] [QSL:3WHYS] Drive each answer through second-, third- and fourth-level follow-ups until you reach why the deal is or is not advancing through a named stage; any answer describing activity or warmth counts as unanswered. Reported across many forecast sessions in multiple companies.

8. **Forecast discipline is arithmetic done early, not pressure applied late.** [QSL:PUSH-OFF] [QSL:FCFILLER] [QSL:REDFLAG] [QSL:TAKE-ME-CHAMP] Push unqualified deals off in the first weeks while there is runway; kill deals whose size shrinks while their date rolls; run a standing red-flag list; verify champions in person in month one. Well supported as pattern — note that two specific claimed *effects* of push-off (more information surfaced, the gap forcing new pipeline) are only asserted.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->

9. **The economic buyer meeting is a confirmation stage with a Go/No-Go function.** [QSL:EB-CONFIRM] [QSL:EBASK] [QSL:MSSE] Five things to secure (outcome, budget, authority, priority, timeframe), a standing question set, and a four-bucket taxonomy of what EBs actually ask. The taxonomy is taught as near-universal; the agenda that goes with it [QSL:EBAGENDA] rests on a single meeting and belongs in Tier 2.

10. **Late-stage artifacts are collateral from early-stage work.** [QSL:BIZCASE] [QSL:PAPER-PROC] [QSL:ASISTOBE] [QSL:PROC-CALM] The business case, the paper-process map and the as-is/to-be quantification are all repeat practice. But their power is conditional: [QSL:PROC-CALM] explicitly only works for a rep who actually shaped criteria and holds an EB-acknowledged justification. For anyone who skipped steps, the same posture is a bluff.

11. **Pipeline generation is inspected weekly, separately from forecasted deals.** [QSL:PIPE-WEEKLY] Next quarter's number is built during this one; the threshold given for when this binds is cycles of 90 days or more.

12. **Targeting starts from provable differentiators, not firmographics.** [QSL:ICPBUILD] The chain from differentiator to pain to use case to quantified benefit to persona is the author's standard build across his career.

13. **Vocabulary precedes methodology.** [QSL:VOCAB] Champion, coach, EB, technical buyer, gatekeeper, enemy — publish one definition set before installing anything. Supported by an informal audit that returned four incompatible definitions of "champion", which is thin evidence for a strong claim, but the claim is also cheap to test locally.

## Tier 2 — practitioner-anecdote: one deal, one rep, one remembered scene

Same rhetorical confidence, an order of magnitude less support. Treat these as hypotheses with a story attached, not as rules.

14. **Educating the champion into an internal seller enlarges deals.** [QSL:EDUCHAMP] [QSL:ROLEPLAY] Both rest on the same UPS deal (forecast $500K, closed just over $1M). One deal, told twice, is not two data points — and the counterfactual (would it have closed at $1M anyway?) is absent.

15. **Structural manoeuvres to force EB access.** [QSL:GOODBADEB] rests on the Northwest Insurance deal; [QSL:WALKAWAY] on one rep at a pharmaceutical company where committee members called back within two hours. Both are high-variance plays presented through their successes, and both concede a real chance of losing the deal outright.

16. **Entry altitude and pain ownership.** [QSL:PAIN-OWNER] (the CAD / VP Engineering story), [QSL:BIZLANG] (one mentor's coaching), [QSL:ABOVENOISE] (one CIO/CISO conversation retold as analogy), [QSL:EXECQUAL] (one CIO call at Sysco). The underlying claim — enter above the noise — is the book's most consequential, and it is supported almost entirely by remembered scenes.

17. **Implication questioning converts pain into urgency.** [QSL:IMPLICATE] One remembered inbound call plus the author's own purchase. The source itself says the illustration does not transfer to complex B2B. This is the weakest-supported move in the lens carrying the highest manipulation risk — see myths-and-demotions.md.

18. **Forecast arithmetic with borrowed constants.** [QSL:RECON-4X] The 4x multiplier encodes one organization's assumed ~25% close rate, derived from the author's first shortfall as a new manager. [QSL:KNOWUNKNOWN] rests on one rep forecasting $175K and landing $250K. [QSL:SLIPADD] is one manager character arguing against another, reported approvingly.

19. **Skill-to-complexity matching and coaching allocation.** [QSL:SKILLMATCH] (the "Puck" company), [QSL:FLIP-8020] (one manager's six-rep team, arithmetic done on the spot), [QSL:CALLDEBRIEF] (one ride-along in Atlanta), [QSL:URGCUR] (the Ferrari story), [QSL:PRIOFILTER] and [QSL:EMO-PROP] (single episodes from the author's own career).

20. **Arming the champion with competitive traps.** [QSL:FUD-TRAPS] Classroom dialogue, no outcome data, and the technique inverts into the exact behaviour the book condemns when competitors do it.

## Tier 3 — assertion: stated as a rule, nothing behind it

Present in the lens for completeness; never cite these as evidence for anything.

21. **The Scoping exit gate itself.** [QSL:SCOPEGATE] A checklist produced inside workshop dialogue. This matters more than its position suggests: the "no champion, no advancement" rule is one of the most consequential decisions the method makes, and it is the least evidenced. It is also the rule most likely to block winnable business in short-cycle or single-stakeholder deals.

22. **The Scoping journey structure and its three deliverables.** [QSL:SCOPEJOURNEY] Prescribed structure, no outcome data.

23. **The EB preparation list.** [QSL:EBPREP] Prescriptive, and it carries an unsourced statistic ("eight of ten executives call sales meetings a waste of time") that must not be repeated as fact.

24. **BANT's scope limitation.** [QSL:BANT-SCOPE] The argument that BANT fails as an org-wide method is structural, not measured. It is probably right and still unproven.

25. **Champion personal-win motives.** [QSL:PERSONALWIN] Motive categories listed without a single case attached.

26. **Leadership principles.** [QSL:MIRROR], [QSL:TWO-BUCKETS], [QSL:TERM-MIRROR], [QSL:SELFAUDIT], [QSL:GRANDMA] — normative rules stated in dialogue. [QSL:TWO-BUCKETS] deserves suspicion beyond its evidence tier: it formalizes the book's structural blind spot by ruling product, pricing and market out of the sales conversation entirely.

27. **The listening and intuition claims.** [QSL:DEEPLISTEN] cites a 250 wpm comprehension versus 150 wpm speech gap with no source; [QSL:TRIANGULATE] presents intuition as a leadership faculty and then concedes it fails when it competes with prejudice from prior experience — which is the common case.

28. **The compounding-cost arithmetic.** [QSL:STAGECOMP] Illustrative multiplication, no observed dataset. Useful for persuasion, worthless as measurement.

## The causal chain, and where it is load-bearing

The entire lens runs on one chain [QSL:PAINCHAIN]: pain found → pain quantified (which justifies a price point) → pain implicated (which creates urgency) → pain plus urgency attracts a champion → the champion opens the economic buyer → the EB holds discretionary funds → large value-based deal. Inverted, the same chain explains small deals, low validation win rates and churn.

Two things must be said about it honestly:

- The chain is **asserted, not measured** — practitioner-n-many at best, and the inversion (no pain → small deal) is unfalsifiable as stated, because any small deal can be attributed retroactively to insufficient pain.
- The chain has a **hard applicability floor**. It assumes an economic buyer distinct from the working contact, a champion who carries rooms you are not in, and a technical validation stage. Below roughly $25K ACV or with a single signer, the chain and everything hanging off it ([QSL:SCOPEGATE], [QSL:EB-CONFIRM], [QSL:PAPER-PROC], [QSL:POWERCHART], [QSL:DP-MAP]) adds cycle time and friction with nothing to gain.

## What this method deliberately does not model

Also structural, also worth naming next to the principles: this is qualification and inspection, not a conversation methodology. It says how to read and gate a deal; it standardizes almost nothing about questioning style, objection handling, rapport or listening technique. The source concedes this itself, calling that layer the art rather than the science — which means every principle above is silent on how the words in the room should actually go.
