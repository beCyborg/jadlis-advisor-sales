# The Qualified Sales Leader — digest

## What this book is for the lens

This is the lens's reference for **qualifying and inspecting a committee deal**, not for how to talk to a buyer. Its mechanism: a stage process supplies the map, verifiable customer events supply the gates, and MEDDPICC supplies the position read — so a forecast becomes an aggregation of evidence instead of a negotiation between managers. It beats other sources on three things: the champion-vs-coach distinction operationalized as tests a manager can run on a rep, the economic-buyer meeting as a Go/No-Go stage rather than a courtesy call, and forecast hygiene (push-off, reconciliation, slip arithmetic) written as a manager routine. It is weakest exactly where it is most often cited: it is not a conversation methodology, and the last third (the MEDDPICC walk-through) is largely a restatement of the first two thirds in checklist form.

## Core model

Six-stage reference process: **Discovery → Scoping → Economic Buyer Meeting → Validation Event → Business Case & Final Proposal → Negotiate and Close**. Each stage exits on a gate evidenced by something the *customer* did; gate KPIs are stage-to-stage conversion and time-in-stage.

Causal chain the whole book runs on: pain found → pain quantified (justifies a price point) → pain implicated (creates urgency) → pain + urgency attracts a **Champion** → the Champion opens the **Economic Buyer** (defined narrowly as discretionary reallocation of funds plus final say) → the EB has real money → value-based large deal. Inverted, it explains small deals, low validation win rates, and churn: no pain → no champion → no EB → feature-priced departmental purchase that gets cut at budget time.

Management layer on top: **Inspire → Coach & Develop → Inspect**, activity KPIs replaced by advancement indicators, and MEDDPICC as the GPS over the process map (the book explicitly corrects the belief that MEDDPICC is itself a process). Org model for altitude: the account as a tree — every level has some money, only the top controls big money.

## Moves

### Stage 0 — Org readiness: vocabulary, process, stage integrity

### [QSL:VOCAB] Vocabulary audit before any methodology
**Move:** Ask reps and managers separately to define champion, coach, economic buyer, technical buyer, gatekeeper, enemy; count the divergent answers, publish one definition set, and enforce it in every deal review before installing a qualification framework.
**APPLY WHEN:** First intervention in any org; before rolling out qualification.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as the fix rather than the prerequisite — shared words with no process behind them only make the theater more fluent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — informal audit over coffee breaks returned four different definitions of "champion".

### [QSL:6STAGE] Six-stage B2B enterprise reference process
**Move:** Use Discovery → Scoping → EB Meeting → Validation Event → Business Case and Final Proposal → Negotiate and Close as the skeleton, then adjust stages and in-stage steps to the customer's actual buying process.
**APPLY WHEN:** Installing a first common process in a multi-stakeholder enterprise SaaS motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** Single-signer or PLG motions — the book itself calls these stages typical for most but not all B2B SaaS forces; no EB stage or validation event is needed there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's reference process across the companies he ran.

### [QSL:GATE] Exit criteria as verifiable customer events
**Move:** Give every stage tangible action steps plus a gate evidenced by something the customer did, not by rep opinion; attach stage-to-stage conversion rate and time-in-stage as gate KPIs. A deal without gate evidence stays in the earlier stage.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any process rollout; this is the mechanism that makes CRM stage data trustworthy.
**AVOID WHEN:** Gates get defined as internal artifacts (demo delivered, deck sent) — that recreates activity theater one level up.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the core control mechanism of the whole system.

### [QSL:CRMSTAGE] Treat CRM stage as an unverified claim
**Move:** Never read stage from CRM as fact — require the verifiable customer event that closed the prior stage, and assume gaming in both directions: weak reps push deals forward to inflate coverage, strong reps park their best deals early to avoid weekly pestering. Account for update latency (a Friday-update policy makes mid-week views days stale).
**APPLY WHEN:** Any pipeline inspection or roll-up built on CRM stage fields.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never — but the fix is process gates, not more CRM enforcement; mandatory fields without gates only shift where reps lie.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — both gaming directions reported as normal team behaviour by the manager characters.

### [QSL:KPISHIFT] Replace activity KPIs with advancement indicators
**Move:** Stop scoring calls, emails, demos and POC counts as ends. Score the tangible result of each process stage and the why behind it: which stage the deal is in, what verifiable customer event closed the previous stage, conversion rate between stages, time spent in stage.
**APPLY WHEN:** Activity volume is high but win rate, deal size or forecast accuracy are flat or falling.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely transactional high-velocity motions where raw activity is still the binding constraint, and for brand-new reps whose problem is coverage, not qualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated diagnosis across advised sales forces; no measured before/after.

### [QSL:STAGECOMP] Show the compounding cost of one misplaced deal
**Move:** Make stage discipline concrete by doing the arithmetic upward: one misrepresented $100K deal per rep across five reps is $500K of phantom forecast at first-line; across three managers and fifteen reps, $1.5M; at CRO/CEO level it distorts hiring and burn decisions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Selling the process change to managers who read gates as bureaucracy, or to a CEO/board.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a shaming device in a review — it argues for the system, it is not a verdict on a rep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — illustrative arithmetic, no observed dataset.

### Stage 0b — Manager craft: the inspire–coach–inspect operating system

### [QSL:ICI] Inspire → Coach & Develop → Inspect loop
**Move:** Run the manager–rep relationship in that order: learn the rep's strengths, goals, frustrations, insecurities and fears; assess and build their process knowledge and execution skill; only then inspect — and inspect the why of deal movement, not the activity count. Treat the right to inspect as earned per rep, continuously.
**APPLY WHEN:** Managing a quota-carrying team, especially with attrition above ~20–25%.
**AVOID WHEN:** An active performance-management case where documented inspection must legally precede coaching.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's operating model across five sales organizations.

### [QSL:BADNEWS] Transformational contract: bad news early, coaching for truth
**Move:** State the exchange explicitly — the rep gives an honest, unflattering account assessment; the manager returns coaching, not penalty. Install the norm that bad news travels immediately, while there is still time to fix the deal. Judge yourself by whether reps volunteer problems before you find them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any managed team, especially after a quarter where deals fell out in the final two weeks.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually withhold penalty (comp or headcount pressure forces punishment) — announcing the contract and breaking it destroys the channel permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated leadership rule, repeated across companies.

### [QSL:GOALDOC] Document each rep's annual, financial and career goals
**Move:** Sit with each rep, ask why they joined and what they expected to achieve, write down their annual, financial and career goals, then align every ask to those goals so the required behaviour visibly serves their own outcome. Endpoint: the rep is self-sufficient and could take your job.
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding a rep, taking over a team, or after an attrition spike.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as a one-time HR form — undocumented follow-through reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — management practice; the attainment and attrition outcomes are asserted, not measured.

### [QSL:INTENT] Desired outcome before every rep meeting
**Move:** Before any meeting with a rep or the team, write the intended outcome for the rep and for the team and the tangible value you will add; enter with that intention rather than an agenda of status collection.
**APPLY WHEN:** 1:1s, deal reviews, forecast calls, ride-alongs.
**AVOID WHEN:** The intended outcome is really your own forecast number — the meeting reverts to extraction and reps read it instantly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing practice.

### [QSL:SELFAUDIT] Trait audit against team behaviour
**Move:** Write down which of your traits motivate your people and which ones stop them bringing you honest account information or accepting coaching in front of customers. Assume the damaging trait is invisible to you until you look.
**APPLY WHEN:** Reps go quiet, or bad news consistently arrives late.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used instead of changing behaviour — self-diagnosis with no changed practice ("this is just who I am") is the failure the book stages through the Andy character.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a leadership requirement.

### [QSL:DEEPLISTEN] Listen to understand, and place yourself in the account
**Move:** In review, suppress the next question while the rep is talking and listen for what was left out and what the statement actually meant; reconstruct the account — which stage, which persona level, coach or champion or enemy, what they were trying to achieve, what caused the failure, what the account dynamics are. Devices off, laptop closed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every deal review and call debrief.
**AVOID WHEN:** Time-boxed roll-ups with sixty people in the room — depth listening does not scale to a full-day parade; do it in small sessions instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the 250 wpm comprehension vs 150 wpm speech gap is stated with no named source.

### [QSL:TRIANGULATE] Use intuition as a second processor
**Move:** Triangulate what the rep says, what the facts support, and your gut read; where they disagree, ask the probing question that resolves the gap. Precondition: a clear mind and no competing anxiety about your own number.
**APPLY WHEN:** The deal story is internally consistent but something feels wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The "intuition" is pattern-matched from your own past deals — the book warns intuition fails when it competes with prejudice from prior experience, and that is the common case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a leadership faculty with no supporting data.

### [QSL:GRANDMA] Simplicity test on any communication
**Move:** Strip presentations and internal communication until a non-specialist would follow them; the leader's job is to make complex things easy for reps and customers, not to display sophistication.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building enablement, exec-level decks, or value messaging.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deeply technical validation content aimed at the technical buyer, where precision beats simplification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule.

### [QSL:PRIOFILTER] Five-question filter on internal requests
**Move:** For every inbound internal ask (survey, report, meeting, form) ask: why is it important, is it urgent, who says it is important, why must it be done now, will it help my team sell more. Decline what fails and protect the time for field calls with reps.
**APPLY WHEN:** A first- or second-line leader's calendar is filling with internal process work.
**AVOID WHEN:** The request is compliance, security, revenue-recognition or legal — those fail "helps my team sell" and must still be done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's first management job, files binned and inbox cleared nightly.

### [QSL:URGCUR] Urgent curiosity — get the answer today
**Move:** When a call ends with open questions and the people who can answer are still in the building, go back in now instead of scheduling follow-ups. Debrief each call on what we learned and what is still unknown, and treat a customer belittling your product as an opening to understand why.
**APPLY WHEN:** On-site or multi-stakeholder days; any cycle where elapsed calendar time is the main risk.
**AVOID WHEN:** The unanswered question needs a stakeholder you have no permission to approach — going around the champion to grab answers is how the book elsewhere says deals get lost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Carlo Carpanelli / Ferrari story, one rep, one day.

### [QSL:MIRROR] Be the mirror of reality
**Move:** When someone brings an account issue, drive to the core of what is actually happening — listen, strip your own emotional bias, probe — and act on the reality rather than the version presented.
**APPLY WHEN:** Reports arrive emotional, incomplete or stretched; bad news is being softened.
**AVOID WHEN:** No stated contraindication; the named failure mode is adopting someone else's framing instead of establishing facts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — leadership principle stated without support.

### [QSL:NO-RUSH] Don't be in a hurry to make a mistake
**Move:** On non-urgent, non-high-stakes questions, say plainly that you understand the situation intellectually but cannot yet feel it, decline to give a date for the answer, and decide only when instinct and analysis agree.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep or manager pushes you to be the answer-man at the end of a long briefing and the decision is low-clock-pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The matter is urgent and high-stakes — carved out explicitly; also when waiting is a way to dodge an unpopular call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's personal decision habit.

### [QSL:TWO-BUCKETS] Sort issues into control / no-control
**Move:** Split every complaint the team raises into what you control (recruiting, onboarding, training, skill development, leading) and what you do not (product roadmap, lead quality, company issues); refuse performance excuses from the second bucket and pour the effort into making the first world-class.
**APPLY WHEN:** Forecast reviews keep sliding into blame on other functions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The no-control item is real and fixable by escalation — dismissing it suppresses signal the company needs.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle.

### [QSL:EMO-PROP] Build emotional proprioception
**Move:** Deliberately track the effect your words and actions have on your people the way a trail runner calibrates foot pressure to terrain, and vary the pressure instead of applying one setting everywhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager is a one-trick pony who tells people what to do and gets angry when it fails.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes preoccupation with being liked — the book separately insists leadership is not a popularity contest.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's private coaching of one manager.

### [QSL:MAKE-IT-RAIN] Manufacture productive discomfort through early questioning
**Move:** Ask every rep the hard deal-qualification and process questions early in the quarter and after every unaccompanied call, consistently, until arriving without answers is the more unpleasant option than facing the customer's hard questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** The team is slow to adopt a new process; forecast conversations are vague; the quarter is young.
**AVOID WHEN:** It degrades into yelling or public humiliation — flagged as creating an intolerable environment that stops producing information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing management practice across teams.

### [QSL:COMP-VS-COMMIT] Diagnose competence versus commitment before acting
**Move:** For any performance problem, first decide whether the moment calls for managing or leading, then run two question sets. Competence (to the head, fact-finding): which step and stage they fail at and why, what knowledge is missing, whether training exists, whether they can comprehend it, whether it is a skill gap, who could develop that skill, whether you are competent in it. Commitment (to the heart, feeling-finding): why they think performance is low, what changed, whether something changed personally, whether you are motivating them, whether they are bored, whether they are adapting to the new selling environment, whether someone is demotivating them, whether they want to learn and are coachable.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep is missing quota, running a thin pipeline or arriving unprepared; before any termination decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** You bring emotion or a pre-formed verdict — that collapses the diagnosis into "not trying hard enough".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard coaching framework, illustrated with one rep case.

### [QSL:ONESKILL] Coach one skill at a time until it is automatic
**Move:** Identify a single fundamental skill gap per rep, coach only that until performance is consistent, then add the next. Observe live calls in person — CRM, email and KPI dashboards cannot show you skill level.
**APPLY WHEN:** New managers, ramping reps, or any rep whose failure cause is unknown.
**AVOID WHEN:** The gap is knowledge (product, competition) rather than skill — that needs training, not repetition; also insufficient when the deal is on fire and needs direct intervention now.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — golf-coach analogy plus the author's stated routine.

### [QSL:CALLDEBRIEF] Debrief every joint call immediately
**Move:** Right after a call, before phones come out, walk through what happened, what went right, what went wrong and the next logical step, and agree it with the rep. A manager who rides along without debriefing wasted the ride.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every manager ride-along and every joint executive meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** No stated exception; the practical limit is time-boxing so it does not become a critique session that suppresses the rep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's frustration on a ride-along in Atlanta.

### [QSL:FLIP-8020] Invert coaching time toward performers
**Move:** Measure how your coaching hours split between performers and non-performers, compute the yield of a 20% lift on each group, and reallocate accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager reports spending most of their time on chronic under-performers who look coachable but never adapt.
**AVOID WHEN:** The under-performer is new, or the gap is a fixable competence gap you have not addressed; also when top-heavy coaching becomes an excuse never to manage the bottom tier out.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one first-line manager's team, arithmetic done on the spot.

### [QSL:NON-NEG] Publish non-negotiable minimum operating standards
**Move:** Agree a short list the team cannot operate without and enforce it: on time, always prepared, respect for teammates, consistent full effort, CRM forecast updated every Friday 5pm; required knowledge (your top differentiators, competitors' top differentiators, buyer pains and business issues, three customer success stories with dollar metrics, top three value drivers); required delivery skills (corporate overview, 30-second pitch, flawless whiteboard, answers to the top ten objections, top ten Discovery and top ten Scoping questions).
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding, or when meeting discipline and account-plan quality have decayed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The list is published but never enforced — unenforced standards are worse than none.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — one manager's working list inside the narrative.

### [QSL:THREE-TIERS] Define exceptional / acceptable / unacceptable, then act on the bottom tier
**Move:** For every standard, draw explicit lines between exceptional, acceptable and unacceptable performance, celebrate the top tier, and impose a visible consequence on sustained non-performance such as removing territory or accounts.
**APPLY WHEN:** Top performers are visibly disengaging; non-performers face no cost.
**AVOID WHEN:** The standard is poorly defined, or the rep has not been given the development you owe them (see QSL:TERM-MIRROR).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated leadership claim, supported by the unprepared-new-rep story.

### [QSL:TERM-MIRROR] Treat every termination as a leadership post-mortem
**Move:** Before removing a rep, establish that you hired them against your own position description — therefore either the hire or the development was your failure; name which one and change that practice.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep is being managed out after a full year of coaching.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to delay a necessary decision indefinitely — self-blame is a diagnostic step, not a stay of execution.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — normative rule stated in dialogue.

### Stage 0c — Hiring and account assignment

### [QSL:POSDESC] Hire against a written position description, not a resume
**Move:** Derive required knowledge and skills stage by stage from your own sales process, write them into a position description together with required characteristics and execution experience, and interview against that document. Treat the resume as uncertified self-marketing that reveals neither specific skills (cold calling, pipeline generation, objection handling, listening) nor character.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any rep or manager hire, especially where your process differs from the candidate's prior motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** The process itself is undefined — you would codify a fantasy; also risky if written so tightly it screens out coachable candidates from adjacent motions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated hiring practice across the author's companies.

### [QSL:SKILLMATCH] Match rep skillset to account complexity
**Move:** Assign accounts by the complexity a rep has actually operated in — single-threaded single-department selling is a different skill from multi-stakeholder, multi-level, multi-department selling with conflicting requirements, multiple champions, cross-department budgets and per-department cost justifications. Do not assume transfer between them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Territory design, account reassignment, or after acquiring reps from a different selling motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a permanent ceiling on a coachable rep — matching should not freeze development.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "Puck" company, single-threaded HR-solution reps hired for a multi-stakeholder BI sale; company eventually sold for assets.

### Targeting and pipeline generation

### [QSL:ICPBUILD] Build the ICP from differentiators, not from firmographics
**Move:** Run the chain as a written artifact: list unique and defensible (provable head-to-head) differentiators → the pains they uniquely solve → use cases → quantified business benefit per use case → negative consequences of not solving → companies and industries with those use cases → the personas owning them → priority by customer value delivered. Extend with buyer detail (job measures, priorities, power, typical budget, vocabulary), environmental prerequisites expressed as thresholds, industry and size cuts, and an honest check that your company can support that segment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before scaling outbound or adding headcount; whenever average deal size is small and territory design is arbitrary.
**AVOID WHEN:** No repeatable wins yet to generalize from — the differentiator list becomes wishful; over-narrow ICPs also starve early pipeline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard build, echoed across his career.

### [QSL:PROPENSITY] Score propensity to buy on top of ICP fit
**Move:** Re-rank the ICP list by observable buying behaviour rather than fit alone: short product life cycles, cloud-only posture, engineering-talent quality, habit of adopting leading-edge technology, pending regulation, impending critical events such as audits and deadlines. Fit without propensity goes down the list.
**APPLY WHEN:** More targets than capacity; territory or ABM list construction.
**AVOID WHEN:** Signals are inferred at parent-company level for large conglomerates — divisional pockets can invert the score.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — example criteria offered without outcome data.

### [QSL:COMPLEXFILTER] Deprioritize by sales complexity
**Move:** Rate each target on multiple stakeholders, multiple levels, multiple departments, multiple locations, C-level accessibility, required POV length, cost-justification hurdles and legal/procurement duration; with limited headcount, push high-complexity/low-propensity segments down the list.
**APPLY WHEN:** Small sales force, quarterly pressure, long procurement drag observed in past deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** Complexity is where your differentiation actually lives, or the segment is strategically required — then staff for it rather than skip it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — argued through the Forego workshop dialogue (financial services and federal agencies deprioritized, with divisional exceptions noted).

### [QSL:PIPEMATH] Reverse-engineer the activity load from quota
**Move:** Take quota ÷ average deal size to get required won deals, then walk backwards through every conversion ratio (marketing-sourced share, MQL→close, cold outreach→close, validation win rate) until you have the implied per-rep weekly load; put that next to actual capacity and let the impossibility argue for itself. For validation-gated motions run the same arithmetic specifically on evaluations: orders needed minus installed-base orders, new-logo orders ÷ win rate = POVs per year, converted to weekly, monthly and quarterly load against POV duration and technical-resource capacity. Then rank which single ratio, improved, collapses the most required activity.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any review where leadership's answer to a miss is "more activity"; before raising headcount or MQL targets; whenever evaluation win rate is below roughly half.
**AVOID WHEN:** The conversion ratios are guesses — the exercise manufactures false precision; useless if the binding constraint is product or market rather than process; and irrelevant where evaluations are short and self-serve so capacity does not bind.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the diagnostic pattern is standard; the worked numbers come from one company's self-reported metrics.

### [QSL:PIPE-WEEKLY] Inspect pipeline generation weekly
**Move:** Hold reps accountable every week for pipeline-creation activity — cold calls, email, partner networking, social connection, lead follow-up, list and MQL qualification — rather than inspecting only forecasted deals, because next quarter's number is built this quarter.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sales cycles of 90 days or more; any team entering a quarter thin.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing stated; the acknowledged obstacle is that it is unglamorous work for both manager and rep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated across the author's teams, with one supporting rep case.

### Stage 1 — Discovery

### [QSL:DISCOQ] Discovery question bank
**Move:** Work an open question set: why is this a problem, why does it happen, why fix it now, when and how often does it occur, what has been tried before and how, which company measure it affects, who is personally accountable for that measure, how it affects people and other processes, who else it touches, what it depends on, walk me through the current process, who else is in that process, what is the desired business outcome. Withhold product discussion until pain is confirmed.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings, and any deal where the rep cannot state why the customer must buy.
**AVOID WHEN:** Fired as uninterrupted interrogation (pair it with QSL:OPENICE); also wrong where the buyer arrived with a fully specified requirement and no time budget.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard discovery set, assembled in-scene from the managers.

### [QSL:OPENICE] Quid pro quo pacing ("skate to the open ice")
**Move:** Take questioning as far as the customer's patience allows, sense when they are out of space, then hand back value — a summary and confirmation of what you heard, how your differentiators map to their pain, why you differ from the alternatives, or a before/after customer story with the same pain — then resume questioning.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any discovery conversation past a few minutes; senior stakeholders with low tolerance for interview format.
**AVOID WHEN:** The give turns into a product pitch — that ends discovery and hands control back to the buyer's checklist.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative

### [QSL:COLDPREP] Cold proficiency before the call, then run the immersion loop
**Move:** Before the meeting know without thinking: the customer's use case, its typical pains, your open-ended discovery questions, your unique differentiators, how they solve that pain, typical quantified value, and customer stories with before/after states. In the meeting cycle question → listen to understand → confirm understanding → sense the response.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every customer-facing call; the manager's checklist when coaching call prep.
**AVOID WHEN:** The prepared use case is assumed rather than verified — cold proficiency in the wrong use case produces confident, irrelevant questions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative

### [QSL:LEADWITNESS] Prepare precision questions whose answers you can predict
**Move:** Work like a trial lawyer: research persona and account (10-K risk section, analyst calls, executive panels) until you can draft questions whose likely answer range you already know, then sequence them in three tiers — discovery facts, quantification, implication — and follow the openings with secondary and tertiary questions instead of moving to the next scripted item.
**APPLY WHEN:** Executive or single-shot meetings where you get one pass, and accounts with public material to research.
**AVOID WHEN:** The homework is not done — predicting answers you have not researched turns into leading questions the buyer resents; closed-ended predicted questions are explicitly wrong for complex software sales.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the lawyer analogy plus the insurance-call story.

### [QSL:PAINCHAIN] Pain → champion → economic buyer → funds
**Move:** Diagnose deal size by walking the chain: find and quantify mission-critical pain; pain is what makes someone want to be your champion; the champion is what gets you to the economic buyer; the EB holds discretionary funds; that is where large deals come from. Invert it to explain a small deal — no pain found, no champion, no EB access, small or no deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deal-size diagnosis, and when a rep is stuck at a single friendly contact.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer genuinely holds their own budget (true single-signer, low ACV) — the chain adds cycle time and friction with nothing to gain.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's core causal model, asserted repeatedly rather than measured.

### [QSL:ABOVENOISE] Qualify pain against the "forest ranger" bar
**Move:** Before investing cycles, force the pain up to revenue, profitability or risk and ask whether the company could keep operating comfortably without solving it. If it is a fire they can let burn, find the bigger fire or expect a small deal. Search grid of common pain families: regulatory non-compliance (PCI, HIPAA, GDPR), competitive or process modernization, security exposure, productivity–cost–quality, reputational risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deals stuck low in the org; small average deal size; reps reporting enthusiasm but no funding.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small-ticket transactional sales where a departmental annoyance genuinely funds itself — forcing a C-level narrative there only adds cycle time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one CIO/CISO conversation at First Data, retold as analogy.

### [QSL:DISCOEMAIL] Confirming email as the Discovery gate
**Move:** Close Discovery with an email to the customer restating the discovered pain, the players, the desired business outcome and the next process step, plus the booked next meeting; the rep forwards it to the manager and the calendared meeting is the evidence the stage closed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every deal entering the pipeline; the concrete answer to what a verifiable exit criterion looks like.
**AVOID WHEN:** The customer never replies or corrects it — an unacknowledged summary is a sent artifact, not customer verification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as the author's example gate.

### [QSL:TREETOP] Enter at the top of the tree
**Move:** Make the first meaningful call at the level that controls discretionary money and owns the business problem, before engaging technical evaluators; treat any downward delegation as a route you chose rather than one you were assigned.
**APPLY WHEN:** New account or replacement of an incumbent; competitors still low in the org; the pain you sell against is above the noise.
**AVOID WHEN:** The product solves only a departmental/technical problem with no executive measure attached, or the top and the user are the same person — the climb is then theater.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise

### [QSL:BIZLANG] Speak the language of the branch you are on
**Move:** Frame executive-level conversation in revenue, cost, margin, productivity, risk, time-to-market and market share, with the homework done beforehand so the framing is theirs rather than generic.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any call above director level; a champion is arranging access upward.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot yet quantify the problem in their measures — generic "productivity" language invites relegation back down the tree.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to one mentor's coaching; no measurement offered.

### [QSL:REPLACE-BLOCK] Treat the incumbent's buyer as a hostile gatekeeper
**Move:** In a replacement sale, assume the person who originally bought the incumbent will block you; route around them by finding pain above the noise owned by someone higher and building a champion at that level rather than negotiating for their permission.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitor installed; the technical owner controls access and keeps deferring upward introductions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The incumbent owner is genuinely dissatisfied and has the influence to drive replacement themselves — routing around them burns your best asset.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a repeated pattern in replacement deals.

### [QSL:PAIN-OWNER] Target the pain owner, not the functional title
**Move:** Identify who is personally measured on the problem and whose position is jeopardized by it, rather than the departmental owner of the current system — the incumbent owner has no personal pain and a rehearsed "my vendor will leapfrog you in six months" brush-off.
**APPLY WHEN:** Replacement or displacement sales; feature-led pitches stalling at a technical director.
**AVOID WHEN:** The functional owner genuinely carries the measure, as in small orgs where roles collapse into one person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the CAD / VP Engineering story from the author's early career.

### [QSL:EXECQUAL] The exec-meeting qualification sequence
**Move:** Once an executive signals interest, do not close on the offer — ask for more, in sequence: who is personally accountable for this initiative, who else is involved in the decision, may I say I spoke with you, are you working with other vendors, have specific capabilities been specified, is there a definitive timeframe, is it budgeted and who controls the budget, may I bring you a summary of my findings after meeting your people, and would you speak with a comparable customer of ours afterwards. Precede it with real homework (annual report, 10-K risks, public statements by the CEO and the target executive) and anchor with a named peer customer in the same city or industry.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meeting with a senior sponsor who has just opened the door; deals where you need names, budget owner and timeline before committing resources.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rapport is not earned or the homework is not done — it then reads as interrogation; note the book's own critique that this sequence still misses the negative business consequences, which must be gathered from operational stakeholders next.

**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one observed CIO call at Sysco, under 90 days to a December audit.

### Stage 2 — Scoping: quantify, implicate, shape the criteria

### [QSL:ASISTOBE] Quantify the as-is and to-be process
**Move:** Map the customer's current process step by step with numbers attached — elapsed time, headcount, cost, error/rework rate — then build the proposed future-state numbers alongside, and feed both into a cost justification whose gain visibly dwarfs the pain. Keep that document as the anchor for later price negotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early stage, before you present; any deal where you expect a price objection or a discount fight.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer will not expose operational metrics (late stage, procurement-led, or no trust yet) — the justification then becomes fiction; also disproportionate for small transactional purchases.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard scoping requirement across companies.

### [QSL:IMPLICATE] Implication questioning to convert pain into urgency
**Move:** After discovery facts and quantification, ask a chained sequence of consequence questions about the unsolved state — what breaks next, who absorbs it, what the second-order effects on people and measures are — and stop before presenting anything. Do not state the consequence yourself; ask the question whose answer the buyer must supply.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pain is real and quantified but the buyer treats it as tolerable; the deal slips quarter to quarter with no compelling event.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pain is unverified or trivial — the sequence reads as manufactured fear; risky with a buyer who has no personal stake, and manipulative if the consequences you imply are not ones your product actually prevents. The book flags that its own illustration (an insurance cold call using closed-ended questions on a solo consumer) does not transfer: complex B2B requires open questions and real listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one remembered inbound call plus the author's own purchase.

### [QSL:SCOPEJOURNEY] Share the Scoping journey and its three deliverables
**Move:** Tell the customer up front what the next stage looks like — a scoping exercise to quantify pain and outline a potential POV, a Go/No-Go meeting with the EB to confirm the pain is a funded priority, then POV criteria and timeframe — and commit to three artifacts for joint review: a cost justification with as-is/to-be metrics, a POV plan tied to the criteria, and a preliminary pricing proposal derived from the justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** Real pain is found and you need to earn access upward; when you want the evaluation criteria written before a competitor writes them.
**AVOID WHEN:** No pain of consequence is established — publishing a process before value looks procedural and invites the buyer to skip to price; fits badly against a rigid RFP timetable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — prescribed structure with no outcome data attached.

### [QSL:DC-CONTROL] Co-author the decision criteria and monitor its drift
**Move:** Have the rep help the champion write the criteria — the buyer's shopping list of required capabilities defining the future state — with your unique differentiators documented in the final version, then track every change: shifts in your favour mean you are gaining control, shifts toward a competitor's differentiators mean you are losing it. Inspect with: which of your differentiators appear, have the criteria changed since agreement, which competitor influenced the change, when, and who has the power to change them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scoping stage while criteria are still being documented; any mid-cycle inspection.
**AVOID WHEN:** No champion exists — a rep "writing criteria" without an internal author is writing a wish list.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated as the book's control thesis.

### [QSL:METRICS-ASIS] Inspect the Metrics element before pricing exists
**Move:** Require the rep to state the use case and its owner, the quantified pain, explicit as-is and to-be scenarios, quantified business value, whether gain far outweighs pain, the desired business outcome and how success is measured, existing KPIs, a preliminary cost justification, champion sign-off on every element of it, re-confirmation of why your differentiators are needed in the to-be state, and the price point to be set with the EB.
**APPLY WHEN:** A deal claims to be past Scoping; before any EB meeting or pricing conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** No access to operational data or no champion — invented metrics produce an anchor that collapses under procurement scrutiny.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the standard Scoping exit test.

### [QSL:SCOPEGATE] Set a customer-verifiable exit gate for Scoping
**Move:** Define exit from Scoping as verifiable events rather than rep opinion: full understanding of pains and capability alignment, confirmation the customer is buying what you sell, articulated negative consequences, a quantified cost justification built on as-is/to-be, and an identified champion. No champion, no advancement.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stage-gating a pipeline for inspection; deciding what may enter forecast.
**AVOID WHEN:** Short-cycle or single-stakeholder deals where a formal champion never exists — the gate then blocks winnable business. This is the method's known bias in its sharpest form.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a checklist produced in workshop dialogue.

### Stage 2b — Champion development

### [QSL:POWERCHART] Build the power chart before working the org chart
**Move:** Plot influence (x) against authority (y) and place every known contact: influence+authority are candidate business champions, influence without authority are candidate technical champions, the remaining quadrants can be coaches at best. Hunt the influential with diagnostics — who is on critical projects, who was recently promoted, who owns new initiatives, who is personally accountable for key decisions, who made the last major departmental purchase, whom C-levels consult, whose statements carry the room, who asks the most perceptive questions, whom others name as influential. Mine the operations staff around each C-level (sales ops, enablement, HR, finance ops).
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-stakeholder accounts; any deal where you cannot name who reaches the EB.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo-buyer or small deals where mapping cost exceeds the deal; also unreliable when built entirely from one contact's account of the politics.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing framework, illustrated by the Fujitsu account where a champion surfaced only after four months.

### [QSL:PERSONALWIN] Sell the champion's personal win
**Move:** Identify the differentiating outcome this person needs internally — recognition as the problem solver, control over the implemented solution, departmental productivity, a promotion case, status from a visible business win — tie it to one of their job measures or a major company measure, and make the internal narrative theirs rather than yours.
**APPLY WHEN:** A candidate influencer is engaged and the business pain is already above the noise.
**AVOID WHEN:** Business value is not yet established — appealing to personal ambition without a defensible business case is precisely what a shrewd champion refuses; inappropriate where the personal incentive conflicts with the buyer's fiduciary duty.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — motive categories listed without cases attached.

### [QSL:AMCLOOP] Ask–Message–Confirm to build trust
**Move:** Cycle prepared questions, then a message aligning the potential solution to the pain and to company fit, then explicit confirmation of your understanding — and re-ask when the confirmation misses. Run it from the customer's vantage point, not your own.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every discovery and scoping interaction with a candidate champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** There is no real curiosity behind it — without genuine interest the loop degenerates into interrogation-then-pitch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as the author's standing method.

### [QSL:EDUCHAMP] Educate the champion into an internal seller
**Move:** Stop selling and start arming: brief them on known product limitations, the competitor's product and its traps, common objections, and how to justify the purchase internally; connect them to champions in your installed base, arrange meetings with executives at your company, and hand them the cost justification in a form they can defend, so they are never caught flat-footed in a room you are not in.
**APPLY WHEN:** A champion is identified and an EB or committee meeting is coming.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person is actually a coach — you are then educating someone with no influence, and disclosing limitations and competitive positioning carries obvious leak risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the UPS deal, forecast $500K, closed slightly over $1M.

### [QSL:ROLEPLAY] Role-play the champion's EB meeting the day before
**Move:** Book a session with the champion ahead of their meeting with the EB and rehearse it: business pain, cost justification, desired business outcome, anticipated EB objections, competitive counters. Say plainly that you are worried and why, given the work invested. Their performance in rehearsal doubles as verification that the champion is real.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large deals where the champion, not you, will carry the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trust is thin — asking a lightly committed contact to rehearse reads as distrust and can burn them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal at UPS.

### [QSL:CHAMPTEST] Test the champion with knowledge questions and action asks
**Move:** Test on two levels. Knowledge: how purchases are made here, the typical evaluation process, who made the last major purchases, can they map the as-is process, can they explain why the pain is major and what inaction implies, how do they articulate your business value and the competitor's, what happened in the competitor's meeting. Action: ask them to arrange a demo for another department, introduce another stakeholder, share evaluation documents, call a reference, sponsor the EB meeting, help write the POV criteria, introduce legal and procurement, or co-build the cost justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** After thorough discovery, established above-the-noise pain, earned trust and completed education.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any of those preconditions is missing — premature asks burn a potential champion who has not yet seen you do your own diligence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing test list taught with worked cases.

### [QSL:BOSSTEST] The "my boss says walk away" test
**Move:** Tell the candidate champion that your manager thinks you are spending too much time on the account and that there is no deal, then ask what advice they would give you and what you should tell your boss; their answer tells you whether to stay or leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ambiguous engagement where you cannot tell coach from champion and want a low-cost read.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early in the relationship, or with a buyer who reads pressure tactics — the boss is a manufactured pretext and costs credibility if seen through.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as a routine, reusable test.

### [QSL:WALKAWAY] The professional walk-away to surface stealth champions
**Move:** In a committee-run RFP where no champion has surfaced and the criteria exclude your differentiators, send a respectful written withdrawal stating your reasons. Nobody calls back — there was no stealth champion and no perceived value, so you stop burning time. People call back — return and re-educate the committee on which capabilities must be in the criteria; if they still refuse, disengage for real.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long RFP cycles, criteria written against you, months invested with no champion identified.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not actually built trust or educated the committee — the withdrawal then just ends the deal; also inappropriate where the account is strategically mandatory regardless of this cycle.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep at a large pharmaceutical company; committee members called back within two hours and amended criteria in writing.

### [QSL:GOODBADEB] Good guy / bad guy to break an EB block
**Move:** When a sponsor claims to want the deal but refuses access to the EB, treat the block as an unqualified deal and force the test: the manager schedules the EB meeting, discloses honestly to the sponsor that they did so and instructed the rep to present the larger option, then in the meeting credits the sponsor's professionalism and presents the larger purchase framed on the EB's business drivers. A sponsor whose real constraint was signature authority is relieved to have you play the bad guy.
**APPLY WHEN:** Forecasted deal, sponsor with prior purchase history, discovery shows the business goal exceeds what the sponsor can sign, and you cannot otherwise verify control.
**AVOID WHEN:** No discovery basis for the larger number, no manager willing to own the blowup, or the block is legitimate policy — the source concedes the sponsor may be brokering a discount or playing the same game with a competitor, and the deal can be lost outright.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Northwest Insurance, one deal, $500K most-likely plus $500K best-case pursued as $1M.

### [QSL:CHAMPQUAL] Manager-side champion qualification
**Move:** Inspect the rep, not the customer: were criteria and process told to us or developed by us; did the champion help build the before/after scenarios, cost justification, criteria containing our differentiator, decision process and EB-meeting agenda; where do they sit on the power chart; if they hold authority how do we know, and if not, why do they have influence; are they in fact a non-influencer; has their influence been confirmed by others; what is their personal win; how have they been educated, developed and tested; what have they actually *done* to prove they are a champion; whom do they name as EB; have they scheduled the EB meeting. Add a prep call with manager, rep and champion before that meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal claimed to be past Scoping; before booking an EB meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** Turned into an interrogation of the customer rather than of your own rep — these are manager-to-rep questions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's most-used inspection block.

### Stage 3 — The Economic Buyer meeting (Go/No-Go)

### [QSL:EBPREP] Four preparations before an EB meeting
**Move:** (1) Do the homework — company site, annual report, 10-K, shareholder letters, analyst presentations — so you never spend the EB's time on questions you should have answered. (2) Act as a business partner: do not restate issues they already know; surface one they had not considered. (3) Position against both corporate measures (revenue, cost, risk) and the EB's own job measures (time to market, employee productivity, application quality). (4) Speak in the EB's business terms, which may differ from those that resonated with the champion.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every first meeting with an economic buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing exempts it; the practical failure is substituting research for the champion's briefing — both are required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — prescriptive list; the "eight of ten executives call sales meetings a waste of time" figure is cited with no named source.

### [QSL:EBAGENDA] Run the standard EB agenda
**Move:** Substantiate in order: high-level summary of discovery and scoping findings; the as-is state; the negative consequences of that state; the proposed to-be state; the benefits of it; the capabilities required to get there (your unique and comparable differentiators); the decision criteria for the validation event built from those capabilities; a preliminary ROI grounded in the customer's own internal numbers; customer success stories with quantified before/after metrics; confirmation of the remaining decision-process steps.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scoping complete, champion aligned, before the POV.
**AVOID WHEN:** Discovery is thin — you cannot run discovery questions at an EB; also mismatched if this person is really an approver and the priority decision sits elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the ABB Transformers meeting in Zürich, one deal.

### [QSL:EBASK] Questions to ask the Economic Buyer
**Move:** Ask where this problem ranks on their priority list; which specific business measure it most affects; when they would prefer it solved; a conditional budget commitment (if validation proves we save $X for an investment of $Y, would you allocate $Y); whether anyone besides them must approve a purchase of this size; and what the remaining decision-process steps are after a successful POV.
**APPLY WHEN:** Inside the EB meeting, after substantiating the case.
**AVOID WHEN:** No quantified case exists to attach the conditional close to — the $X/$Y question is empty without a cost justification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing question set.

### [QSL:EB-CONFIRM] Run the EB meeting as a confirmation, not a discovery
**Move:** Use the meeting to verify Discovery and Scoping findings and secure five things — desired business outcome, budget, authority, priority of the pain, purchase timeframe — plus agreement that all vendors test to the same validation criteria, permission to call the EB if criteria change, review of the preliminary cost justification, and a follow-up meeting if validation results materially change it. Beforehand: confirm who the champion says the EB is, validate that with multiple people, know the date and what will be presented, and confirm the champion briefed the EB in advance. Preference stated: the sales leader attends every EB meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** The champion has secured access and Discovery/Scoping findings are documented.
**AVOID WHEN:** Findings are thin — walking in to discover in front of the EB burns the one meeting that sets the price anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing rule with a stated personal preference for leader attendance.

### [QSL:MSSE] Prepare for Much-ness, Soon-ness, Sure-ness, Easy-ness
**Move:** Pre-build answers in four buckets, because EB questions almost always land there. Much-ness: purchase cost, implementation cost, customer savings. Soon-ness: time to implement, time to payback. Sure-ness: confidence it works in their environment, confidence in the cost justification, which reference they can call. Easy-ness: ease of implementation, ease of learning for their people.
**APPLY WHEN:** Rehearsal before any EB or executive committee meeting; also as a coaching checklist for the champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication given; it is a preparation grid, not a script to recite.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — taxonomy taught as near-universal for EB questions.

### [QSL:TWOCOND] The two-condition callback agreement
**Move:** As the meeting closes, ask permission to call the EB directly under exactly two conditions: if the final business case differs materially from the preliminary justification after validation, and if anyone attempts to significantly change the agreed criteria. State it deliberately when the competitor's champion is in the room — it functions as a warning against criteria tampering.
**APPLY WHEN:** End of a successful EB meeting in a competitive deal with a defined validation event.
**AVOID WHEN:** No agreed criteria yet, or the EB is not the criteria owner; it also loses force if you never actually use the channel.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as the author's habitual closing question.

### [QSL:BANT-SCOPE] Use BANT only where it works
**Move:** Keep BANT as the EB-meeting confirmation set — pain ranked as a priority (Need), a specific timeframe (Time), authority to buy (Authority), budget allocated on successful validation (Budget) — and do not use it as the deal-inspection layer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Preparing or debriefing an EB meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** Adopted as the organization's qualification method — it does not map to process steps, does not identify tangible advancement, does not diagnose where reps struggle, and ignores champion, EB, competition and their champion, pain identification, as-is/to-be metrics, criteria framing, process control and the paper process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — comparative claim argued from structure, not from measured outcomes.

### Stage 4 — Validation Event (POV)

### [QSL:POVRENAME] Ban "POC", require "POV"
**Move:** Change the vocabulary from Proof of Concept to Proof of Value across forecast, CRM stages and manager language, on the rationale that nobody doubts the product functions — what must be proven is that specific capabilities produce quantified business value for this customer's use case, so every evaluation carries success criteria derived from the customer's own as-is metrics.
**APPLY WHEN:** Teams run technical bake-offs with no agreed business criteria; evaluation win rates are low and evaluation length is drifting.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely unproven technology or a first-of-kind integration, where technical feasibility really is the open question; also pointless as pure renaming if criteria-setting does not change with it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing rule imposed at the companies the author ran.

### [QSL:POV-CRITERIA] Write and weight the test criteria before agreeing to compete
**Move:** Before the event, agree in writing on the criteria, the weighting of each element and the scoring method, with your differentiators represented and the weighting favourable to your solution. Test it by asking whether you would wager on these rules.
**APPLY WHEN:** A validation event is being scheduled and a champion exists who can co-author the criteria.
**AVOID WHEN:** The criteria were fixed before you arrived and reflect a competitor's differentiators — the guidance is to fix control first or decline, not to enter and hope.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the core repeated claim that whoever controls the test criteria wins.

### [QSL:POV-GATE] Run the POV only after four preconditions
**Move:** Refuse to start until: a confirmed champion exists; that champion helped frame the criteria and scoring rules; a cost justification built from Discovery and Scoping shows gain far exceeding pain; and priority, budget, authority, timing and remaining process steps are verified with the EB.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any resource-heavy technical validation late in the cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deliberately used as a lead-generation or education device — listed as exactly what a POV is not.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing gate, echoed by the managers' confessions of prior failures.

### [QSL:POV-QUIDPRO] Secure the quid pro quo with the Economic Buyer
**Move:** Before the event, get the EB to agree that the winner receives a purchase order subject only to legal review and pricing negotiation, that all vendors test against the same criteria, and that you may call the EB if anyone tries to change agreed criteria mid-test.
**APPLY WHEN:** EB access exists via the champion; competitive evaluation with two or more vendors.
**AVOID WHEN:** No EB access has been obtained — asking for the commitment through a proxy produces an unenforceable promise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as standard practice in the author's process.

### [QSL:POV-RUN] Work the event, don't just supervise it
**Move:** During the event, reconfirm the as-is and to-be metrics from the cost justification, adjust them as new information appears, capture material evidence that the solution resolves the customer's pain, and keep aligning to the champion's personal win.
**APPLY WHEN:** The event is underway and technical resources are engaged.
**AVOID WHEN:** Using the event to start Discovery, hunt for a champion or build relationships — all listed as disqualifying misuse.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — checklist offered as standard rep behaviour.

### [QSL:POV-DEBRIEF] Close the loop face-to-face with the champion, then re-anchor with the EB
**Move:** Build a summary report of findings and walk the champion through it in person until they agree with every element of the business case; where results materially change the original cost justification, rebuild it and take the new version to the EB to justify price and drive urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the event, before pricing discussions begin.
**AVOID WHEN:** The champion has doubts you paper over — the purpose is removing doubt, not producing a document.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — articulated by the trainees and endorsed by the author.

### Stage 5 — Business case, negotiation, close

### [QSL:BIZCASE] Write the Business Case as the customer's internal document
**Move:** Present face-to-face to the EB a document written from their perspective: their corporate objectives; their strategic initiatives; the business problem or opportunity; how it maps to those initiatives; before/after use-case scenarios showing how your solution uniquely solves it; how the outcome is delivered (people, process, timeframes); and a final cost justification in their own measures with quantified values, never bare phrases like "increased productivity". It doubles as the post-implementation baseline for expansion and cross-sell.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-validation, pre-negotiation, with EB access and agreed metrics.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot quantify — an unquantified business case hands price-setting to the customer's perception.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the canonical late-stage artifact.

### [QSL:RISK-BRIDGE] Carry the buyer across peak risk
**Move:** When buyer fear spikes after validation, use five levers: replay the validation results proving the product solved their use case; name the negative consequences of not solving the pain; share comparable results from customers with the same pain; arrange a peer conversation with a champion at another company; remind them of their personal win.
**APPLY WHEN:** Right after the POV, when the champion is exposed to organizational risk for advocating you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The proof points are borrowed rather than earned in this account — this is where fabricated evidence collapses. The consequence-framing lever is a pressure technique and stays legitimate only while tied to pains the customer already quantified.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as repeated late-stage practice.

### [QSL:IMPL-PLAN] Back-date an implementation plan to create urgency
**Move:** Present a resourced implementation plan and derive the contract deadline backwards from the EB's own target date — project start date, stakeholder training date, internal resource reservation date, all inside the EB's stated timeframe, therefore contract finalized by a specific date.
**APPLY WHEN:** The EB has already stated a required outcome date and you are entering negotiation.
**AVOID WHEN:** No timeframe was established by the EB — the urgency then reads as vendor-invented deadline pressure and is easily dismissed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — template produced as standard practice.

### [QSL:PROC-PLAYBOOK] Name the procurement gambit instead of reacting to it
**Move:** Recognize the standard repertoire as tactics rather than information: manufactured hostility on first contact; take-it-or-leave-it anchored to an unrelated enterprise deal (demanding 70% because another vendor gave 50%); "you need our logo more than we need you"; "we love you but not at this price"; a "no budget" claim contradicting what the EB told you; good-guy/bad-guy staging around an unseen VP; the friendly contact who goes dark until the last hours of the quarter; the shame play about software margins; the one-more-thing add-on at the finish; the appeal to hard times; and the "it's over — PO attached, just agree to terms" close.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement has taken over the conversation post-validation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as licence to disbelieve everything — some constraints (real budget ceilings, real approval thresholds) are genuine and testable via the paper process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — enumerated from repeated experience.

### [QSL:PROC-CALM] Hold the anchor; never decide on price in the room
**Move:** Stay calm, keep the conversation anchored to the EB-acknowledged cost justification and the Business Case, and always retain the option to think about it or consult your manager rather than answering a price demand immediately; their pain has a clock and your quarter-end is not their concern to know about.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement applies pressure and the sales process was actually completed.
**AVOID WHEN:** You skipped steps — with no champion and no agreed justification, patience is a bluff and the outcome is a deep discount or a loss. This is pressure symmetry, legitimate only because urgency came from the customer's own quantified consequences rather than being manufactured.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated negotiation posture across deals.

### [QSL:PAPER-PROC] Map the signature process step, person and duration
**Move:** Document each step from purchase request to PO — named person, function, elapsed time — and check whether the champion has ever purchased at this dollar level, since thresholds change the path; where they are a first-timer, find someone who has bought at that amount and map it together. Get legal and procurement contacts early, introduce your counsel to theirs, and introduce yourself to procurement. Inspect: does the champion know the process, walk me through the steps, who are the stakeholders at each, what is the timeframe per step, who is the legal contact, have documents been redlined, have you spoken to procurement.
**APPLY WHEN:** A deal is being forecast for a specific quarter.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small-dollar, no-committee purchases where the paper process is a credit card.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented with a worked example and forecast arithmetic.

### Cross-stage — MEDDPICC as the deal-analysis layer

### [QSL:MEDD-GPS] Bind the qualification method to process stages, not to the acronym
**Move:** Map each MEDDPICC element to the stage where it is earned (Identify→Discovery; Metrics, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Champion→Scoping; Economic Buyer→EB meeting; Competition→continuous; Paper Process→Negotiate & Close), then read missing elements as a stage diagnosis rather than a checklist score. The process is the map; the method is the GPS.
**APPLY WHEN:** Forecast reviews devolve into unstructured question barrages; managers disagree on what stage a deal is in.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as a form to fill in — box-ticking without understanding is the documented degeneration of this method.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's central management claim.

### [QSL:3WHYS] Qualify Identify with the Three WHYs
**Move:** Ask the rep, per deal: why do they have to buy (pain level, specific pain points, is it above the noise, technical or business terms, what use case); why do *they* have to buy (who is most impacted, their title, how the pain hits their job measures); why buy from us (do our differentiators tie to their pains, which capabilities are uniquely differentiated); why buy now (what happens with no action, which company measure suffers, what alternatives exist, what is the desired business outcome). If the rep cannot answer, the deal is still in Discovery regardless of what the forecast says.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any forecasted deal review; coaching a rep after early-stage calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used on transactional single-owner deals where the answers are trivially "the buyer" — the questioning adds cycle time without adding information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's core inspection routine.

### [QSL:DP-MAP] Map and re-validate the decision process
**Move:** Document the specific people, events and timeframes the customer will use to evaluate and select — overview presentations, demos, POV, cost justifications, selection and negotiation, each with a date — know the stakeholders per event and validate the process with all of them. Inspect: who else is involved, whom the rep has met one-on-one, whether new people entered, whether the process changed and who changed it, whether the champion was involved, whether a competitor's champion controls it and their title, whether the POV has start and end dates, and whether the EB will confirm the process.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scoping through validation; whenever dates slip without explanation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Single-decision-maker deals where the process is one person's calendar.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard inspection list built with the trainees.

### [QSL:CONTROL-3] Diagnose which of three parties controls the deal
**Move:** Ask who holds control — the customer, the competition, or you — and decide it by the direction of information flow. Early the customer controls, because you need information from them. When the champion starts needing information from you (how to evaluate, how to handle competitors, references, internal justification), control has shifted to the rep. If criteria and process were defined without your rep and the rep is merely being told them, the competition controls. Three levels: no control, partial control, full control.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-stage forecast review; deciding whether a deal is forecastable.
**AVOID WHEN:** Control is declared from rapport rather than from the concrete artifacts — criteria, process, EB meeting — required as evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's routine forecast diagnostic.

### [QSL:COMP-WATCH] Track the competitor's champion, not just their product
**Move:** Inspect who the competitor is, when they entered and whether before you, who their champion is and at what influence level, whether that champion outranks yours, whether additional competitive champions exist, who else the competitor is meeting, whether they have met the EB, whether criteria were already set when you arrived, whether criteria changed to include their differentiators and when, how their champion differentiates and strategizes, and whether the decision process changed after they entered. Then ask explicitly who wins if the two champions collide in an internal meeting and whom the room sides with; if you are outmatched, arm your champion harder against competitive traps and recruit additional champions wider and higher.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive committee evaluations, especially late stage or after any unexplained change to criteria or process.
**AVOID WHEN:** Paranoia substitutes for customer work — criteria drift is the observable, not gossip; and there is nothing to weigh where no competitor is identified or the decision is single-threaded.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated coaching pattern; the head-to-head champion comparison itself is offered as an inspection question without cases.

### [QSL:FUD-TRAPS] Arm the champion with competitive traps (use with care)
**Move:** Educate the champion on how competitors sell against you, how to handle their typical objections and traps, and — as stated in the source — supply traps, objections and fear/uncertainty/doubt about the competitor's capabilities, grounded in known competitor weaknesses.
**APPLY WHEN:** A competitor with a real, verifiable capability gap is actively reshaping criteria in their favour.
**AVOID WHEN:** The weakness is not verifiable, or the material is anything other than accurate capability comparison — planting FUD is the same tactic the book condemns when done to you, so it inverts into deception easily; also unusable where the champion's credibility with peers is your only asset.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted in classroom dialogue, no outcome data.

### Cross-stage — Forecast management and the QBR

### [QSL:ROOTQ] Multi-level inspection questioning
**Move:** In deal review, drive each answer down through second-, third- and fourth-level follow-ups until you reach why the deal is or is not advancing through a named process stage, then close by naming the next logical step. Treat any answer describing activity or relationship warmth as an unanswered question and re-ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any forecasted deal in a committee sale; reps answering with feelings, features or third-party assurances.
**AVOID WHEN:** No shared vocabulary or process exists yet — the questions land as interrogation and reps learn to perform answers; fix vocabulary and stages first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated pattern across many forecast sessions in multiple companies.

### [QSL:REDFLAG] Standing red-flag list for forecast review
**Move:** Flag and stop on: champion not spoken to in days or weeks; no competition claimed on a large first deal into a new logo; stakeholders unnamed beyond one contact; buying criteria written before the rep arrived; criteria shifting toward a competitor's features; a large discount demand explained only as budget cuts; a POC the customer wants re-run plus a price-sharpening request; a loss recorded as "no decision"; legal or procurement status unchecked on a deal expected to close this quarter.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal on the current-quarter forecast, especially a first deal into a large regulated account.
**AVOID WHEN:** True single-signer, low-ACV transactional purchases — missing stakeholders and procurement silence are normal there and firing on them adds noise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — each flag drawn from a rep scenario and matched against the quarter's actual result.

### [QSL:KNOWUNKNOWN] Forecast what you can defend; name what you don't know
**Move:** Commit only to the number you can defend with pain, quantified pain, consequence of inaction and evidence you shaped the buying criteria, and explicitly name the unknowns (such as the signature path) that keep an upside deal out of commit. Hold the number under manager and peer pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any commit-level forecast conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as cover for chronic under-forecasting with no supporting deal detail — the same posture without the detail is sandbagging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep forecasting $175K against a $300K quota and landing $250K.

### [QSL:SLIPADD] Carry the slip, don't reset it
**Move:** When a deal slips out of a quarter, the rep's next-quarter accountability is quota plus the slipped amount and the slipped deal does not count as new coverage; say the arithmetic out loud in the review.
**APPLY WHEN:** A committed deal falls out in the last weeks and reappears in next quarter's forecast.
**AVOID WHEN:** The slip came from a verifiable external event (acquisition, budget freeze) — reloading quota then punishes the rep for reality and drives hiding of bad news.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — argued by one manager character against another's forecast; presented approvingly, not measured.

### [QSL:FCFILLER] Kill the shrinking recurring deal
**Move:** Track each deal's forecasted amount across quarters; a deal whose size steps down quarter over quarter while its close date rolls forward is a forecast filler — remove it rather than re-qualify it, and audit why it was accepted three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** The same account appears on three consecutive forecasts at decreasing value.
**AVOID WHEN:** The shrink is a deliberate land-and-expand rescope with a signed first phase — that is a scope change, not decay.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — named as a common pattern, illustrated by one nine-month deal.

### [QSL:PUSH-OFF] Push unqualified deals off the forecast early
**Move:** When qualifying questions are exhausted and the rep still cannot articulate the deal's exact status, instruct them to remove it from the forecast — burden of proof on the rep, preponderance of evidence required to stay in — and coach them on why it failed to qualify.
**APPLY WHEN:** First weeks of the quarter, while there is runway to replace it.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**AVOID WHEN:** Done late in the quarter (too late to reconcile) or without the coaching half, which turns it into arbitrary punishment; acknowledged as unpopular with reps.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard forecast discipline; the two claimed effects (more information surfaced, gap forces new pipeline) are asserted.

### [QSL:RECON-4X] Reconcile every shortfall at 4x the gap
**Move:** For each rep compute quota minus closed-and-committed to get the shortfall, set a reconciliation target of at least four times that gap (an assumed ~25% close rate), and have them write the specific activity per account that produces it, broken out both by pipeline stage and by account.
**APPLY WHEN:** First weeks of the quarter, after push-off, when a gap exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your actual stage-to-close rate differs materially from 25% — the multiplier is a rule of thumb from one org, not a measured conversion; also when it becomes a coercion ritual rather than planning, since intimidation produces commitments that do not close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — derived from the author's first forecast shortfall as a new manager.

### [QSL:TAKE-ME-CHAMP] "Take me to your champion"
**Move:** After the forecast session, ask each rep to bring you to the champion of every remaining forecasted deal and meet them inside the first month of the quarter, to verify a champion exists and the rep is in control; use the rest of the quarter for the newly generated reconciliation deals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-push-off, month one, with 11–12 weeks of runway left.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**AVOID WHEN:** Deal volume per manager makes it impossible, or in transactional/solo-buyer motions where there is no champion construct to verify.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the surest forecast-verification method.

## Where this book stops working

- **Below roughly $25K ACV, or with a single signer.** The whole apparatus assumes an economic buyer distinct from the working contact, a champion who carries rooms you are not in, and a technical validation stage. Where the buyer holds their own budget, the pain→champion→EB chain (QSL:PAINCHAIN), the scoping gate (QSL:SCOPEGATE), the EB meeting (QSL:EB-CONFIRM) and the paper-process map (QSL:PAPER-PROC) add cycle time and friction with nothing to gain — they lengthen the cycle and can block winnable business outright.
- **Fewer than three stakeholders.** Power charts, champion-versus-competitor's-champion analysis and decision-process mapping (QSL:POWERCHART, QSL:COMP-WATCH, QSL:DP-MAP) are mapping costs that exceed the deal when the room is two people; QSL:DP-MAP degenerates into one person's calendar.
- **Self-serve, PLG and high-velocity transactional motions.** The six-stage skeleton is explicitly described as typical for most but not all B2B enterprise SaaS, and activity really is the binding constraint in high-velocity motions — QSL:KPISHIFT misfires there, and QSL:PIPEMATH's validation arithmetic does not bind when evaluations are short and self-serve.
- **This is qualification and inspection, not conversation methodology.** It tells a manager how to read and gate a deal; it says almost nothing standardized about questioning style, objection handling, rapport or listening technique — the book concedes this itself, calling those the art rather than the science.
- **The last third is the first two thirds in checklist form.** The MEDDPICC walk-through restates Discovery/Scoping/Champion/EB material as inspection lists. Read as a source of new moves, it adds little; read as a form to fill in, it degenerates into checklist theater — the failure mode the book itself names and that practitioners report as the normal outcome of MEDDIC rollouts.
- **The forecast arithmetic carries borrowed constants.** The 4x reconciliation multiplier (QSL:RECON-4X) encodes one organization's ~25% close rate; applied to a different conversion profile it manufactures either a phantom crisis or false comfort.
- **Everything late-stage is collateral from earlier stages.** QSL:PROC-CALM and QSL:RISK-BRIDGE work only for a rep who actually shaped criteria and has an EB-acknowledged justification; used by a rep who skipped steps, patience is a bluff and the outcome is a deep discount or a loss.

## What the book debunks

- **Forecast confidence is not deal knowledge.** A manager team rolled up to plan and finished far short; a high self-forecast bought the softest questioning, teaching everyone to over-forecast. Weigh a forecast against the rep's history of actually closing above quota.
- **Bullying up the number backfires mechanically.** The rep concedes in the room and drops back in the final week, leaving no time to react. Cajoling and intimidation produce temporary relief and late-quarter collapse.
- **A CRM is not a qualification system** — it is a repository and cannot analyze a selling situation. Adding mandatory fields without gates only shifts where reps lie.
- **Activity is not progress.** Activity KPIs unlinked to deal advancement are hollow: they cannot forecast and they do not teach. Managers who only inspect become scorekeepers, which the book ties directly to attrition.
- **"Customers only buy after a POC"** is treated as half-true and operationally wrong when three of four evaluations are lost. The POV is not an early-process event, not customer education, not a place to start Discovery, not a champion hunt, and not relationship-building.
- **A POV cannot rescue a deal whose criteria a competitor wrote.** Educating the customer inside someone else's criteria improves their ability to score you down; entering such an event is a pre-committed loss.
- **Late executive-to-executive escalation does not reverse a decision.** The EB asks whether the rep agreed to the evaluation knowing the criteria; if yes, the verdict is that you were outsold. Entry altitude decides the deal, not late heroics.
- **Confusing a coach with a champion** is named as the number-one salesperson mistake: coaches supply information and never take action, champions take action; a coach cannot become a champion. Reps keep a friendly coach through a whole cycle while a competitor works a real one.
- **Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence** — failing to find a champion in four months does not prove there is none.
- **Title does not equal economic buyer.** The EB is defined by discretionary reallocation of funds plus final say, not by being the CFO or "whoever holds budget"; an assumed EB can turn out to be the champion to the real one once deal size grows past their lens.
- **MEDDPICC is not a sales process** — an explicit correction. BANT is useful only at EB level and fails as an org-wide qualification method.
- **Skipping early steps causes five downstream pathologies**, not one: small deals, relegation low in the org, churn (no perceived business value means the subscription is cut first at budget time), forecast noise, and rep attrition.
- **Selling small deals low in the org is a churn cause**, not merely a deal-size problem.
- **Knowledge was not the gap.** Reps and managers could correctly describe good discovery while doing none of it; process, accountability and time pressure were the binding constraints.
- **Caring is not perks.** The company with hug-opening meetings had the highest startup attrition the author had seen because nobody was trained; caring is defined as making people competent.
- **Setting standards without enforcing them is actively harmful** — non-performers learn mediocrity is free, and A-players read the inaction as a penalty on performance.
- **A very large qualified pipeline masks all selling and qualification problems** — a consistently over-quota leader can be hiding real defects behind volume. (The book's own honest counter-observation.)
- **Procurement's warmth means nothing.** A competitor invited to quote is not a neutral bidder: they are structurally motivated to delay, plant doubt and lowball.
- **Cleverness loses to fundamentals** (fox vs hedgehog): constantly changing strategy without integrating a few guiding principles loses to instinctive execution of basics. And telling a struggling rep to perform better is useless — the image is shouting "swim" at a drowning person.

Author's first shortfall as a manager: 7 reps × $300K = $2.1M quota against a $1.1M roll-up, a $1M gap with 85 days left. Accuracy scale: one quarter is a point, two a line, three a trend. Pipeline-inspection threshold: sales cycles of 90 days or more.
- **Paper process worked example:** 3 days financial review + 4 days budgeting + 2 weeks legal + 2 days procurement — a six-week paper process inside four remaining weeks guarantees a miss. Metrics example: a task taking 12 months with 5 people at $300K burdened cost each, reduced to 2 people over 3 months.
- **Estimates stated without data:** ~80% of boxed-in reps capitulate and run a POV hoping to find a champion mid-test; "eight of ten executives" call sales meetings a waste of time (no source); listening comprehension ~250 wpm against ~150 wpm speech (no source). ICP threshold examples: at least 500 servers, at least 50 sales reps, at least 2% of revenue on marketing. Large organizations commonly require 2–3 competitive quotations on major purchases.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No counterfactual, ever.** Every mechanism is evidenced by a deal the author remembers winning. There is no losing-deal cohort, no A/B, no baseline — the ceiling is practitioner-n-many and much of it is practitioner-anecdote wearing a framework's clothes. Deals that closed *without* a champion, or that the process would have killed correctly, are absent.
- **The narrative is the author's own fictionalized client**, so the reported improvements (win rate 25%→35%, deal size $50K→$95K) are self-graded homework with no control group and no attribution analysis.
- **One motion only.** Enterprise, committee, outbound, high-ACV, on-premise-inflected software. PLG, self-serve, marketplace, channel/partner-led, procurement-first public sector, and founder-led selling appear only as things to deprioritize, never as motions with their own mechanics.
- **Buyer psychology is thin.** The buyer is modeled as a rational actor with pains, measures and a personal win. Group dynamics, status anxiety, risk-aversion of committees and post-purchase regret get named but never mechanized — the book's own admission that questioning, objection handling, rapport and intuition are "the art" leaves the entire conversational layer unbuilt.
- **Ethics are handled by assumption.** Implication questioning, FUD supplied to a champion, the fabricated-boss test and the good-guy/bad-guy manoeuvre are presented as craft; the guardrail is the author's assumption that the pain is real. There is no treatment of what these do when the value story is weak, and no acknowledgment that the same techniques are what the book condemns in competitors.
- **Marketing, product and pricing are exogenous.** Everything wrong is a sales-process defect (see QSL:TWO-BUCKETS, which formalizes this). A product that does not differentiate, a price that is wrong for the segment, or a market that has moved are outside the frame.
- **Time and cost of the method are never priced.** Power charts, rehearsals, champion education, EB meetings, business cases and paper-process mapping are treated as free. Where they exceed the deal's margin, the book has no answer beyond "deprioritize the segment".
- **No mechanism for a champion who is wrong.** The champion is a conduit to be armed and tested, never a source of error; the case where a well-intentioned, influential champion sells the wrong internal narrative is absent.
- **Remote, async and modern buying committees are absent** (2021, but written from a walk-the-halls era): the moves assume you can go back into the building, ride along, and sit face-to-face with the EB.
