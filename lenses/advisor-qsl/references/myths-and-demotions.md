# advisor-qsl — Myths and demotions

What this tradition itself debunks, what it demotes, where its own techniques are documented to fail, and which numbers must never leave this lens as fact.

---

## 1. Debunked inside the tradition

- **Forecast confidence is not deal knowledge.** A manager team rolled up to plan and finished far short. The mechanism named: a high self-forecast bought the softest questioning, which taught the whole team to over-forecast. Weigh a forecast against the rep's history of actually closing above quota, not against their conviction. → [QSL:KNOWUNKNOWN], [QSL:ROOTQ]
- **Bullying the number up backfires mechanically, not just morally.** The rep concedes in the room and drops back out in the final week, leaving no time to react. Cajoling and intimidation buy temporary relief and pay for it with late-quarter collapse. → constrains [QSL:MAKE-IT-RAIN], [QSL:RECON-4X]
- **A CRM is not a qualification system.** It is a repository; it cannot analyze a selling situation. Adding mandatory fields without gates only relocates where reps shade the truth. → [QSL:CRMSTAGE], [QSL:GATE]
- **Activity is not progress.** Activity KPIs unlinked to deal advancement can neither forecast nor teach. Managers who only inspect become scorekeepers, which the source ties directly to attrition. → [QSL:KPISHIFT], [QSL:ICI]
- **"Customers only buy after a POC" is half-true and operationally wrong** when three of four evaluations are lost. A POV is not an early-process event, not customer education, not a place to begin Discovery, not a champion hunt, and not relationship-building. → [QSL:POV-GATE], [QSL:POV-RUN]
- **A POV cannot rescue a deal whose criteria a competitor wrote.** Educating the customer inside somebody else's criteria improves their ability to score you down. Entering such an event is a pre-committed loss. → [QSL:POV-CRITERIA]
- **Late executive-to-executive escalation does not reverse a decision.** The EB's question is whether the rep agreed to the evaluation knowing the criteria; if yes, the verdict is that you were outsold. Entry altitude decides the deal, not late heroics. → [QSL:TREETOP]
- **Confusing a coach with a champion** is named as the number-one salesperson mistake. Coaches supply information and never take action; champions take action; a coach does not become a champion. Reps routinely carry a friendly coach through an entire cycle while a competitor works a real one. → [QSL:CHAMPTEST]
- **Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.** Failing to find a champion in four months does not prove there is none. → [QSL:POWERCHART]
- **Title does not equal economic buyer.** The EB is defined by discretionary reallocation of funds plus final say — not by being the CFO or "whoever holds budget". An assumed EB frequently turns out to be the champion to the real one once deal size outgrows their lens. → [QSL:EB-CONFIRM]
- **MEDDPICC is not a sales process.** This is an explicit correction inside the tradition, not an outside critique. It is a diagnostic layer over a process; without a process underneath, there is nothing for it to diagnose. → [QSL:MEDD-GPS]
- **BANT fails as an org-wide qualification method.** It maps to no process step, identifies no tangible advancement, diagnoses nothing about where reps struggle, and ignores champion, EB, competition, pain identification, as-is/to-be metrics, criteria framing, process control and the paper process. Demoted to a single use: the EB-meeting confirmation set. → [QSL:BANT-SCOPE]
- **Skipping early steps causes five downstream pathologies, not one:** small deals, relegation low in the org, churn, forecast noise, and rep attrition.
- **Selling small deals low in the org is a churn cause,** not merely a deal-size problem — with no perceived business value, the subscription is the first line cut at budget time.
- **Knowledge was not the gap.** Reps and managers could describe good discovery accurately while doing none of it. The binding constraints were process, accountability and time pressure — which demotes training-as-content in favour of enforced gates.
- **Caring is not perks.** The company running hug-opening meetings had the highest startup attrition the author had seen, because nobody was trained. Caring is redefined as making people competent.
- **Setting standards without enforcing them is actively harmful.** Non-performers learn that mediocrity is free and A-players read the inaction as a penalty on performance. → [QSL:NON-NEG], [QSL:THREE-TIERS]
- **A very large qualified pipeline masks all selling and qualification problems.** The source's own honest counter-observation: a consistently over-quota leader can be hiding real defects behind volume — which also means over-attainment is not evidence that this method works.
- **Procurement's warmth means nothing,** and a competitor invited to quote is not a neutral bidder — they are structurally motivated to delay, plant doubt and lowball. → [QSL:PROC-PLAYBOOK]
- **Cleverness loses to fundamentals.** Constantly changing strategy without integrating a few guiding principles loses to instinctive execution of basics. And telling a struggling rep to perform better is useless — the image used is shouting "swim" at a drowning person. → [QSL:COMP-VS-COMMIT], [QSL:ONESKILL]

## 2. Anti-recommendations — do not do these, and why

- **Do not run MEDDPICC as a form.** Box-ticking without understanding is the documented degeneration of this method, named by the source itself. The last third of the book — the MEDDPICC walk-through — restates the earlier Discovery/Scoping/Champion/EB material as inspection lists; read as a form to fill in, it produces checklist theater, which practitioners report as the normal outcome of MEDDIC rollouts. → [QSL:MEDD-GPS]
- **Do not define gates as internal artifacts.** "Demo delivered", "deck sent", "proposal issued" recreate activity theater one level up and leave the forecast exactly as unreliable as before. → [QSL:GATE]
- **Do not enter a validation event whose criteria you did not shape.** Fix control first or decline. Entering and hoping is the documented pre-committed loss. → [QSL:POV-CRITERIA], [QSL:POV-GATE]
- **Do not use a POV to find a champion.** Roughly 80% of boxed-in reps are said to capitulate and run one anyway, hoping to find a champion mid-test. That figure is an estimate with no data behind it, but the prohibition stands on its own logic. → [QSL:POV-GATE]
- **Do not apply the qualification apparatus below its floor.** Under roughly $25K ACV, or with a single signer, or with fewer than three stakeholders, the pain→champion→EB chain, the scoping gate, the EB meeting and the paper-process map lengthen the cycle, add friction and can block winnable business outright. [QSL:DP-MAP] degenerates into one person's calendar; [QSL:POWERCHART] costs more than the deal. → the hard gate, restated in role-bias.md
- **Do not push deals off the forecast late in the quarter,** and never without the coaching half — late push-off leaves no time to reconcile, and coaching-free push-off is arbitrary punishment that teaches reps to hide deals rather than surface them. → [QSL:PUSH-OFF]
- **Do not reload quota on a slip caused by a verifiable external event** (acquisition, budget freeze). Doing so punishes the rep for reality and drives exactly the concealment [QSL:BADNEWS] exists to prevent. → [QSL:SLIPADD]
- **Do not announce the bad-news-for-coaching contract if you cannot honour it.** Announcing it and then penalizing destroys the information channel permanently — an irreversible failure, unlike most others here. → [QSL:BADNEWS]
- **Do not go around the champion to harvest answers.** The same source that recommends urgent curiosity says going around the champion is how deals get lost. → [QSL:URGCUR] against [QSL:CHAMPTEST]
- **Do not read intuition that is really pattern-matching from your own past deals.** The source concedes intuition fails when it competes with prejudice from prior experience — and that is the common case, not the exception. → [QSL:TRIANGULATE]

## 3. Documented failure modes of this lens's own techniques

Named inside the tradition, not imported criticism:

- **[QSL:IMPLICATE] — implication questioning.** Reads as manufactured fear when pain is unverified or trivial; risky with a buyer who has no personal stake; and outright manipulative when the consequences implied are not ones the product actually prevents. The source's own illustration (an insurance cold call using closed-ended questions on a solo consumer) is explicitly disclaimed as non-transferable to complex B2B. This is the lowest-evidence, highest-manipulation-risk move in the lens.
- **[QSL:FUD-TRAPS] — supplying fear/uncertainty/doubt to a champion.** Inverts into deception as soon as the competitor weakness is not verifiable, and it is the same tactic the book condemns when a competitor does it to you. Unusable where the champion's credibility with peers is your only asset.
- **[QSL:BOSSTEST] — the "my boss says walk away" test.** The boss is a manufactured pretext. Costs credibility outright if seen through, and misfires early in a relationship or with a buyer who recognizes pressure tactics.
- **[QSL:GOODBADEB] — good guy / bad guy to break an EB block.** The source concedes the sponsor may be brokering a discount or running the same play with a competitor, and that the deal can be lost outright. It also requires a manager willing to own the blowup.
- **[QSL:WALKAWAY] — the professional withdrawal.** Without prior trust and committee education, the withdrawal simply ends the deal. Inappropriate where the account is strategically mandatory.
- **[QSL:PROC-CALM] and [QSL:RISK-BRIDGE] — late-stage posture.** Both are collateral from earlier stages. For a rep who skipped steps, patience is a bluff and the outcome is a deep discount or a loss; borrowed rather than earned proof points collapse at exactly this moment.
- **[QSL:EDUCHAMP] — arming the champion.** Disclosing product limitations and competitive positioning to someone who turns out to be a coach carries obvious leak risk.
- **[QSL:MAKE-IT-RAIN] — productive discomfort.** Degrades into shouting or public humiliation, producing an environment that stops yielding information at all.
- **[QSL:RECON-4X] — 4x reconciliation.** Becomes a coercion ritual rather than planning; intimidation produces commitments that never close.
- **[QSL:SCOPEGATE] — no champion, no advancement.** In short-cycle or single-stakeholder deals this blocks winnable business. The source names this as the method's known bias in its sharpest form — and it is an assertion-tier rule doing consequential work.
- **[QSL:PERSONALWIN] — selling the champion's personal win.** Appealing to personal ambition before business value is established is precisely what a shrewd champion refuses; inappropriate wherever the personal incentive conflicts with the buyer's fiduciary duty.

## 4. Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

Every figure in this tradition is self-reported inside the narrative or asserted by the author. There is no external dataset anywhere. The narrative itself is the author's own fictionalized client, so the reported improvements are self-graded homework with no control group and no attribution analysis.

**Never cite as evidence of the method working:**
- Post-training results: number beaten by 15%, average deal size up to $95K, productivity per rep $1.4M, forecast within 10% of the finish; interim POV win rate 25%→35% in one quarter and team average deal size $50K→$55K. Self-graded, no control, no attribution.
- Author framing: five public software companies (PTC, Geo-Tel, Ariba, BladeLogic, BMC); 33+ BladeLogic sellers later becoming CROs; PTC at $0→$1.1B in nine years at ~40% operating margins. Biographical, not causal.
- Named deal outcomes: UPS forecast $500K closing just over $1M; Northwest Insurance $500K + $500K pursued at $1M; T-Mobile $500K slipping two quarters; ABB Transformers with a $30.6M justification at $30M deal scale and a reference customer citing a 20% reduction in time to market. Single deals, survivor-selected.

**Never cite as constants — they are one org's numbers:**
- The **4x reconciliation multiplier** and its implied ~25% close rate [QSL:RECON-4X]. Applied to a different conversion profile it manufactures either a phantom crisis or false comfort.
- The **POV economics chain** (25% win rate, 4-week duration, ~$50K average deal, $1.2M quota → 72 POCs/year ≈ 1.5/week; across 50 reps, 850 evaluations/quarter → 212 wins, 638 losses) [QSL:PIPEMATH]. The *diagnostic shape* transfers; the numbers do not.
- Activity load derivations: 10% MQL→close implying 360 MQLs per rep, 10% cold-outreach→close implying another 360 self-generated leads, ~50% of qualified leads from marketing.
- Org-health reference points: 35% rep attrition against a stated 20–25% "typical for SaaS startups"; one customer lost per three new deals per quarter; ~6-month enterprise rep ramp; 61 selling days per quarter.
- ICP thresholds offered as examples only: at least 500 servers, at least 50 sales reps, at least 2% of revenue on marketing [QSL:ICPBUILD].

**Explicitly sourceless — do not repeat at all:**
- "Eight of ten executives call sales meetings a waste of time" [QSL:EBPREP].
- Listening comprehension ~250 wpm against ~150 wpm speech [QSL:DEEPLISTEN].
- ~80% of boxed-in reps capitulate and run a POV hoping to find a champion mid-test.
- "Large organizations commonly require 2–3 competitive quotations on major purchases."
- The stage-misrepresentation compounding arithmetic (5 reps × $100K = $500K; 15 reps = $1.5M) [QSL:STAGECOMP] — illustrative multiplication, useful for persuasion, worthless as measurement.

## 5. Structural blind spots that demote the whole lens in certain deals

- **No counterfactual, ever.** Every mechanism is evidenced by a deal the author remembers winning. Deals that closed *without* a champion, and deals the process would have killed correctly, are absent from the record.
- **One motion only:** enterprise, committee, outbound, high-ACV, on-premise-inflected software. PLG, self-serve, marketplace, channel/partner-led, procurement-first public sector and founder-led selling appear only as things to deprioritize, never as motions with their own mechanics.
- **Buyer psychology is thin.** The buyer is modeled as a rational actor with pains, measures and a personal win. Group dynamics, status anxiety, committee risk-aversion and post-purchase regret are named but never mechanized.
- **Ethics are handled by assumption.** Implication questioning, FUD, the fabricated-boss test and good-guy/bad-guy are presented as craft, with the author's assumption that the pain is real as the only guardrail. There is no treatment of what these techniques do when the value story is weak.
- **Marketing, product and pricing are exogenous.** [QSL:TWO-BUCKETS] formalizes this: a product that does not differentiate, a price wrong for the segment, or a market that has moved sit outside the frame — so this lens will diagnose a sales-process defect even when the defect is elsewhere.
- **No mechanism for a champion who is wrong.** The champion is a conduit to be armed and tested, never a source of error. A well-intentioned, influential champion selling the wrong internal narrative has no place in this model.
- **The method's own cost is never priced.** Power charts, rehearsals, champion education, EB meetings, business cases and paper-process mapping are treated as free. Where they exceed the deal's margin, the only answer offered is "deprioritize the segment".
- **Remote and async buying committees are absent.** Written from a walk-the-halls posture: the moves assume you can re-enter the building, ride along, and sit face-to-face with the EB.
