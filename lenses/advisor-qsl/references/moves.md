# advisor-qsl — Moves

Layer: qualification / MEDDPICC. Citation prefix `[QSL:]`.
Single canonical source: *The Qualified Sales Leader* (McMahon, 2021). Evidence ceiling for this lens: `practitioner-n-many` — no move here may claim `field-study` or `external-research`, because the book contains neither.

Grouped by deal stage. Every code below is unique inside this file.

## Stage 0 — Org readiness: vocabulary, process, stage integrity

### [QSL:VOCAB] Vocabulary audit before any methodology
**Move:** Poll reps and managers separately for their definitions of champion, coach, economic buyer, technical buyer, gatekeeper and enemy; count how far the answers diverge, publish one authoritative definition set, and require it in every deal review before any qualification framework is installed.
**APPLY WHEN:** First intervention into an unfamiliar org; ahead of a qualification rollout.
**AVOID WHEN:** Mistaken for the cure rather than the precondition — common words with no process behind them just make the theater more articulate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — an informal hallway audit that produced four incompatible definitions of "champion".

### [QSL:6STAGE] Six-stage B2B enterprise reference process
**Move:** Adopt Discovery → Scoping → EB Meeting → Validation Event → Business Case and Final Proposal → Negotiate and Close as the skeleton, then bend the stages and in-stage steps to match how this customer actually buys.
**APPLY WHEN:** Installing a first shared process across a multi-stakeholder enterprise SaaS motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** Single-signer or PLG motions — the source itself calls this typical of most, not all, B2B SaaS forces; neither an EB stage nor a validation event belongs there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's reference process across the organizations he ran.

### [QSL:GATE] Exit criteria as verifiable customer events
**Move:** Attach to each stage both tangible action steps and an exit gate evidenced by something the customer did rather than by rep opinion; measure stage-to-stage conversion and time-in-stage as the gate KPIs; a deal lacking gate evidence stays in the previous stage.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any process rollout — this is the mechanism that makes CRM stage data worth reading.
**AVOID WHEN:** Gates get written as internal artifacts (demo delivered, deck sent) — that just relocates activity theater one level up.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the central control mechanism of the entire system.

### [QSL:CRMSTAGE] Treat CRM stage as an unverified claim
**Move:** Never accept a CRM stage field as fact; demand the verifiable customer event that closed the prior stage and assume gaming runs both ways — weaker reps advance deals to inflate coverage, stronger reps park their best deals early to dodge weekly pestering. Factor in update latency: a Friday-update policy leaves mid-week views days out of date.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any pipeline inspection or roll-up built on stage fields.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never skip it — but the remedy is process gates, not tighter CRM enforcement; mandatory fields without gates only move where reps shade the truth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — both gaming directions reported as ordinary team behaviour by the manager characters.

### [QSL:KPISHIFT] Replace activity KPIs with advancement indicators
**Move:** Stop scoring calls, emails, demos and POC counts as ends in themselves. Score the tangible outcome of each process stage plus its reason: current stage, the verifiable customer event that closed the previous one, inter-stage conversion, and time spent in stage.
**APPLY WHEN:** Activity volume is high while win rate, deal size or forecast accuracy stay flat or decline.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely transactional high-velocity motions where raw activity is still the binding constraint, and for brand-new reps whose real problem is coverage rather than qualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a diagnosis repeated across advised sales forces, with no measured before/after.

### [QSL:STAGECOMP] Show the compounding cost of one misplaced deal
**Move:** Make stage discipline tangible by doing the arithmetic upward: one misrepresented $100K deal per rep across five reps is $500K of phantom first-line forecast; across three managers and fifteen reps, $1.5M; at CRO or CEO altitude it corrupts hiring and burn decisions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Selling the process change to managers who read gates as bureaucracy, or to a CEO/board.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deployed as a shaming device inside a review — the arithmetic argues for the system, it is not a verdict on any individual rep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — illustrative arithmetic with no observed dataset behind it.

## Stage 0b — Manager craft: the inspire–coach–inspect operating system

### [QSL:ICI] Inspire → Coach & Develop → Inspect loop
**Move:** Run the manager–rep relationship strictly in that sequence: first learn the rep's strengths, goals, frustrations, insecurities and fears; then assess and build process knowledge and execution skill; only then inspect — and inspect the reason a deal moved, not the activity tally. Treat the right to inspect as something earned per rep, continuously.
**APPLY WHEN:** Running a quota-carrying team, particularly with attrition above roughly 20–25%.
**AVOID WHEN:** An active performance-management case where documented inspection must legally precede coaching.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's operating model across five sales organizations.

### [QSL:BADNEWS] Transformational contract: bad news early, coaching for truth
**Move:** Name the exchange out loud — the rep supplies an honest, unflattering account read; the manager returns coaching instead of penalty. Install the norm that bad news travels immediately, while the deal can still be repaired, and judge yourself by whether reps raise problems before you find them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any managed team, especially following a quarter where deals collapsed in the last fortnight.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually suppress the penalty (comp or headcount pressure forces punishment) — announcing the contract and then breaking it destroys the channel permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a stated leadership rule, repeated across companies.

### [QSL:GOALDOC] Document each rep's annual, financial and career goals
**Move:** Sit with every rep, ask why they joined and what they came to achieve, write down their annual, financial and career goals, then frame each request so the required behaviour visibly serves their own outcome. The endpoint is a rep self-sufficient enough to take your job.
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding a rep, inheriting a team, or reacting to an attrition spike.
**AVOID WHEN:** Handled as a one-off HR form — documenting goals and then ignoring them reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a management practice; the attainment and attrition outcomes are asserted, not measured.

### [QSL:INTENT] Desired outcome before every rep meeting
**Move:** Ahead of any meeting with a rep or the team, write down the intended outcome for the rep, the intended outcome for the team, and the concrete value you will add; walk in carrying that intention rather than a status-collection agenda.
**APPLY WHEN:** 1:1s, deal reviews, forecast calls, ride-alongs.
**AVOID WHEN:** The real intended outcome is your own forecast number — the meeting reverts to extraction and reps detect it immediately.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing practice.

### [QSL:SELFAUDIT] Trait audit against team behaviour
**Move:** Write out which of your own traits actually motivate your people and which ones stop them from bringing you honest account information or accepting coaching in front of a customer; assume the damaging trait stays invisible until you deliberately look for it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Reps have gone quiet, or bad news keeps arriving late.
**AVOID WHEN:** Substituted for changed behaviour — self-diagnosis followed by "that's just who I am" is the exact failure the book dramatizes through the Andy character.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a leadership requirement without support.

### [QSL:DEEPLISTEN] Listen to understand, and place yourself inside the account
**Move:** During review, hold back your next question while the rep talks and listen for what was omitted and what the words actually meant; then reconstruct the account — stage, persona level, coach versus champion versus enemy, what they were trying to achieve, what caused the failure, what the internal dynamics are. Devices away, laptop shut.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every deal review and call debrief.
**AVOID WHEN:** Time-boxed roll-ups with sixty people in the room — depth listening does not survive a full-day parade; run it in small sessions instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the 250 wpm comprehension versus 150 wpm speech gap is stated with no named source.

### [QSL:TRIANGULATE] Use intuition as a second processor
**Move:** Cross-check three inputs — what the rep says, what the facts support, and your own gut read — and wherever they disagree, ask the probing question that closes the gap. Precondition: a clear head, with no competing anxiety about your own number.
**APPLY WHEN:** The deal story is internally consistent yet something reads wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The "intuition" is really pattern-matching from your own past deals — the source warns that intuition fails once it competes with prejudice from prior experience, which is the usual case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as a leadership faculty with no supporting data.

### [QSL:GRANDMA] Simplicity test on any communication
**Move:** Strip presentations and internal communication down until a non-specialist could follow them; the leader's job is making complex things easy for reps and customers, not demonstrating sophistication.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building enablement, executive decks, or value messaging.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deeply technical validation material aimed at a technical buyer, where precision outranks simplification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule.

### [QSL:PRIOFILTER] Five-question filter on internal requests
**Move:** For each inbound internal ask — survey, report, meeting, form — ask why it matters, whether it is urgent, who declared it important, why it must happen now, and whether it helps the team sell more; decline what fails and reinvest the time in field calls with reps.
**APPLY WHEN:** A first- or second-line leader's calendar is silting up with internal process work.
**AVOID WHEN:** The request concerns compliance, security, revenue recognition or legal — those fail the "helps my team sell" test and must still be done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's first management job, files binned and inbox emptied nightly.

### [QSL:URGCUR] Urgent curiosity — get the answer today
**Move:** When a call ends with open questions and the people who could answer them are still in the building, walk back in now instead of booking a follow-up. Debrief every call around what was learned and what remains unknown, and treat a customer disparaging your product as an invitation to understand why.
**APPLY WHEN:** On-site or multi-stakeholder days; any cycle where elapsed calendar time is the dominant risk.
**AVOID WHEN:** The open question requires a stakeholder you have no permission to approach — going around the champion to harvest answers is how the same book says deals get lost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Carlo Carpanelli / Ferrari story, one rep, one day.

### [QSL:MIRROR] Be the mirror of reality
**Move:** When someone brings you an account problem, drive to what is actually happening — listen, subtract your own emotional bias, probe — and act on that reality rather than on the version you were handed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Reports arrive emotional, partial or stretched; bad news is being softened.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication is stated; the named failure mode is adopting somebody else's framing instead of establishing the facts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — leadership principle stated without support.

### [QSL:NO-RUSH] Don't be in a hurry to make a mistake
**Move:** On non-urgent, low-stakes questions, say plainly that you grasp the situation intellectually but cannot yet feel it, refuse to commit to a date for your answer, and decide only once instinct and analysis line up.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep or manager pushes you to be the answer-man at the end of a long briefing and clock pressure is low.
**AVOID WHEN:** The matter is urgent and high-stakes — carved out explicitly — and also when waiting is really a way to dodge an unpopular call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's personal decision habit.

### [QSL:TWO-BUCKETS] Sort issues into control / no-control
**Move:** Split every complaint the team raises into things you control (recruiting, onboarding, training, skill development, leading) and things you do not (roadmap, lead quality, company issues); refuse performance excuses drawn from the second bucket and pour the energy into making the first world-class.
**APPLY WHEN:** Forecast reviews keep degenerating into blame aimed at other functions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The no-control item is real and fixable by escalation — dismissing it suppresses a signal the company needs.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a principle.

### [QSL:EMO-PROP] Build emotional proprioception
**Move:** Deliberately track how your words and actions land on each person, the way a trail runner calibrates foot pressure to terrain, and vary the pressure per person instead of applying one setting everywhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager is a one-trick pony who instructs people and then gets angry when the instruction fails.
**AVOID WHEN:** It slides into a preoccupation with being liked — the same source insists leadership is not a popularity contest.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's private coaching of a single manager.

### [QSL:MAKE-IT-RAIN] Manufacture productive discomfort through early questioning
**Move:** Put the hard qualification and process questions to every rep early in the quarter and after every unaccompanied call, consistently, until showing up without answers becomes more unpleasant than facing the customer's hard questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** The team is slow to adopt a new process; forecast conversations are vague; the quarter is still young.
**AVOID WHEN:** It decays into shouting or public humiliation — flagged as producing an intolerable environment that stops yielding information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing management practice across teams.

### [QSL:COMP-VS-COMMIT] Diagnose competence versus commitment before acting
**Move:** For any performance problem, first decide whether the moment calls for managing or for leading, then run two distinct question sets. Competence (fact-finding, aimed at the head): which step and stage they fail at and why, what knowledge is missing, whether training exists, whether they can absorb it, whether it is a skill gap, who could develop that skill, whether you yourself are competent in it. Commitment (feeling-finding, aimed at the heart): why they think performance dropped, what changed, whether something changed personally, whether you are motivating them, whether they are bored, whether they are adapting to the new selling environment, whether someone is demotivating them, whether they still want to learn and are coachable.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep is missing quota, carrying a thin pipeline or arriving unprepared; before any termination decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** You arrive with emotion or a pre-formed verdict — that collapses the whole diagnosis into "not trying hard enough".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standard coaching framework, illustrated through one rep case.

### [QSL:ONESKILL] Coach one skill at a time until it is automatic
**Move:** Isolate a single fundamental skill gap per rep, coach only that until performance is consistent, then move to the next. Observe live calls in person, because CRM records, email and KPI dashboards cannot reveal skill level.
**APPLY WHEN:** New managers, ramping reps, or any rep whose cause of failure is still unknown.
**AVOID WHEN:** The gap is knowledge (product, competition) rather than skill — that requires training, not repetition; also insufficient when a deal is on fire and needs direct intervention now.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — golf-coach analogy plus the author's stated routine.

### [QSL:CALLDEBRIEF] Debrief every joint call immediately
**Move:** Immediately after a call, before phones come back out, walk through what happened, what went well, what went badly and the next logical step, and agree that step with the rep. A manager who rides along and skips the debrief wasted the ride.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every manager ride-along and every joint executive meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** No exception is stated; the practical limit is time-boxing so the debrief does not turn into a critique session that silences the rep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's frustration on a ride-along in Atlanta.

### [QSL:FLIP-8020] Invert coaching time toward performers
**Move:** Measure how your coaching hours actually split between performers and non-performers, compute the yield of a 20% lift applied to each group, and reallocate the hours accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager reports spending most of their time on chronic under-performers who look coachable but never change.
**AVOID WHEN:** The under-performer is new, or the gap is a fixable competence gap you have not addressed yet; also when top-heavy coaching becomes a pretext for never managing the bottom tier out.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one first-line manager's team, with the arithmetic done on the spot.

### [QSL:NON-NEG] Publish non-negotiable minimum operating standards
**Move:** Agree a short list the team cannot operate without and enforce it: punctuality, always prepared, respect for teammates, consistent full effort, CRM forecast updated every Friday at 5pm. Add required knowledge — your top differentiators, competitors' top differentiators, buyer pains and business issues, three customer success stories carrying dollar metrics, the top three value drivers — and required delivery skills: corporate overview, 30-second pitch, flawless whiteboard, answers to the top ten objections, the top ten Discovery and top ten Scoping questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding, or when meeting discipline and account-plan quality have visibly decayed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The list is published and never enforced — unenforced standards are worse than none at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — one manager's working list reproduced inside the narrative.

### [QSL:THREE-TIERS] Define exceptional / acceptable / unacceptable, then act on the bottom tier
**Move:** For each standard, draw explicit boundaries between exceptional, acceptable and unacceptable performance, celebrate the top tier publicly, and attach a visible consequence to sustained non-performance, such as removing territory or accounts.
**APPLY WHEN:** Top performers are visibly disengaging while non-performers face no cost.
**AVOID WHEN:** The standard itself is poorly defined, or the rep has not yet received the development you owe them (see QSL:TERM-MIRROR).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeated leadership claim, supported by the unprepared-new-rep story.

### [QSL:TERM-MIRROR] Treat every termination as a leadership post-mortem
**Move:** Before removing a rep, establish that you hired them against your own position description — so either the hire or the development was your failure; name which one it was and change that practice.
**APPLY WHEN:** A rep is being managed out after a full year of coaching.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to postpone a necessary decision indefinitely — the self-blame is a diagnostic step, not a stay of execution.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a normative rule stated in dialogue.

## Stage 0c — Hiring and account assignment

### [QSL:POSDESC] Hire against a written position description, not a resume
**Move:** Derive the required knowledge and skills stage by stage from your own sales process, write them into a position description alongside required characteristics and execution experience, and interview against that document. Treat the resume as uncertified self-marketing that reveals neither specific skills (cold calling, pipeline generation, objection handling, listening) nor character.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any rep or manager hire, especially where your process differs from the candidate's previous motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your process is itself undefined — you would be codifying a fantasy; also risky if written so tightly that coachable candidates from adjacent motions get screened out.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated hiring practice across the author's companies.

### [QSL:SKILLMATCH] Match rep skillset to account complexity
**Move:** Assign accounts by the complexity the rep has actually operated in: single-threaded, single-department selling is a different skill from multi-stakeholder, multi-level, multi-department selling with conflicting requirements, several champions, cross-department budgets and per-department cost justifications. Do not assume the skills transfer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Territory design, account reassignment, or after inheriting reps from a different selling motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a permanent ceiling on a coachable rep — matching should not freeze development.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "Puck" company, single-threaded HR-solution reps hired into a multi-stakeholder BI sale; the company was eventually sold for assets.

## Targeting and pipeline generation

### [QSL:ICPBUILD] Build the ICP from differentiators, not from firmographics
**Move:** Run the chain as a written artifact: unique and defensible (head-to-head provable) differentiators → the pains they uniquely solve → use cases → quantified business benefit per use case → negative consequences of leaving it unsolved → companies and industries carrying those use cases → the personas that own them → priority by value delivered. Then extend with buyer detail (job measures, priorities, power, typical budget, vocabulary), environmental prerequisites expressed as thresholds, industry and size cuts, and an honest check that your company can actually support that segment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before scaling outbound or adding headcount; whenever average deal size is small and territory design looks arbitrary.
**AVOID WHEN:** There are no repeatable wins yet to generalize from — the differentiator list turns wishful; over-narrow ICPs also starve early pipeline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard build, echoed across his career.

### [QSL:PROPENSITY] Score propensity to buy on top of ICP fit
**Move:** Re-rank the fit list by observable buying behaviour rather than fit alone: short product life cycles, cloud-only posture, engineering-talent quality, a habit of adopting leading-edge technology, pending regulation, impending critical events such as audits and deadlines. Fit without propensity drops down the list.
**APPLY WHEN:** There are more targets than capacity; building a territory or ABM list.
**AVOID WHEN:** The signals are inferred at parent-company level for a large conglomerate — divisional pockets can invert the score.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — example criteria offered without outcome data.

### [QSL:COMPLEXFILTER] Deprioritize by sales complexity
**Move:** Rate each target on stakeholder count, levels involved, departments, locations, C-level accessibility, required POV length, cost-justification hurdles and legal/procurement duration; with limited headcount, push high-complexity, low-propensity segments down the list.
**APPLY WHEN:** Small sales force, quarterly pressure, and long procurement drag observed in past deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** Complexity is exactly where your differentiation lives, or the segment is strategically required — then staff for it rather than skip it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — argued through the Forego workshop dialogue, where financial services and federal agencies were deprioritized with divisional exceptions noted.

### [QSL:PIPEMATH] Reverse-engineer the activity load from quota
**Move:** Divide quota by average deal size for the required won deals, then walk backwards through every conversion ratio — marketing-sourced share, MQL-to-close, cold-outreach-to-close, validation win rate — until you hold the implied per-rep weekly load; set that beside real capacity and let the impossibility make the argument. For validation-gated motions, run the same arithmetic on evaluations specifically: orders needed minus installed-base orders, new-logo orders ÷ win rate = POVs per year, converted to weekly, monthly and quarterly load against POV duration and technical-resource capacity. Finally rank which single ratio, if improved, collapses the most required activity.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any review where leadership answers a miss with "more activity"; before raising headcount or MQL targets; whenever evaluation win rate sits below roughly half.
**AVOID WHEN:** The conversion ratios are guesses — the exercise then manufactures false precision; useless when the binding constraint is product or market rather than process; irrelevant where evaluations are short and self-serve so capacity never binds.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the diagnostic pattern is standard, but the worked numbers come from one company's self-reported metrics.

### [QSL:PIPE-WEEKLY] Inspect pipeline generation weekly
**Move:** Hold reps accountable every week for pipeline-creation work — cold calls, email, partner networking, social connection, lead follow-up, list and MQL qualification — rather than inspecting only the forecasted deals, because next quarter's number is built during this one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sales cycles of 90 days or longer; any team entering a quarter thin.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing is stated as an exception; the acknowledged obstacle is that the work is unglamorous for manager and rep alike.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated across the author's teams, with one supporting rep case.

## Stage 1 — Discovery

### [QSL:DISCOQ] Discovery question bank
**Move:** Work an open question set: why this is a problem, why it happens, why fix it now, when and how often it occurs, what has been tried before and how, which company measure it affects, who is personally accountable for that measure, how it hits people and adjacent processes, who else it touches, what it depends on, a walk-through of the current process, who else sits in that process, and what business outcome is wanted. Hold back all product discussion until pain is confirmed.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings, and any deal where the rep cannot state why the customer must buy.
**AVOID WHEN:** Fired as uninterrupted interrogation — pair it with QSL:OPENICE; also wrong where the buyer arrived with a fully specified requirement and no time budget.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard discovery set, assembled in-scene with the managers.

### [QSL:OPENICE] Quid pro quo pacing ("skate to the open ice")
**Move:** Push questioning as far as the customer's patience allows, sense the moment they run out of room, then hand value back — a summary and confirmation of what you heard, a mapping of your differentiators onto their pain, why you differ from the alternatives, or a before/after customer story with the same pain — and then resume questioning.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any discovery conversation past the first few minutes; senior stakeholders with low tolerance for an interview format.
**AVOID WHEN:** The give turns into a product pitch — that ends discovery and returns control to the buyer's checklist.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a hockey analogy, presented as standard practice.

### [QSL:COLDPREP] Cold proficiency before the call, then run the immersion loop
**Move:** Before the meeting, know without thinking: the customer's use case, its typical pains, your open-ended discovery questions, your unique differentiators, how they resolve that pain, typical quantified value, and customer stories with before/after states. Inside the meeting, cycle question → listen to understand → confirm understanding → sense the response.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every customer-facing call; also the manager's checklist when coaching call prep.
**AVOID WHEN:** The prepared use case is assumed rather than verified — cold proficiency in the wrong use case yields confident, irrelevant questions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as what separates a fluid call from a mechanical one.

### [QSL:LEADWITNESS] Prepare precision questions whose answers you can predict
**Move:** Work like a trial lawyer: research the persona and account — 10-K risk section, analyst calls, executive panels — until you can draft questions whose likely answer range you already know, sequence them in three tiers (discovery facts, quantification, implication), and chase each opening with secondary and tertiary questions rather than jumping to the next scripted item.
**APPLY WHEN:** Executive or single-shot meetings where you get exactly one pass, and accounts with public material to research.
**AVOID WHEN:** The homework is not done — predicting answers you never researched turns into leading questions the buyer resents; closed-ended predicted questions are explicitly wrong for complex software sales.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the lawyer analogy plus the insurance-call story.

### [QSL:PAINCHAIN] Pain → champion → economic buyer → funds
**Move:** Diagnose deal size by walking the chain: find and quantify mission-critical pain; pain is what makes someone want to be your champion; the champion is what gets you to the economic buyer; the EB controls discretionary funds; that is where large deals originate. Invert the chain to explain a small deal — no pain found, no champion, no EB access, small deal or none.
**APPLY WHEN:** Diagnosing deal size, and whenever a rep is stuck at a single friendly contact.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer genuinely owns their own budget — a true single signer at low ACV — where the chain only adds cycle time and friction for no gain.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's core causal model, asserted repeatedly rather than measured.

### [QSL:ABOVENOISE] Qualify pain against the "forest ranger" bar
**Move:** Before investing cycles, force the pain up to revenue, profitability or risk and ask whether the company could go on operating comfortably without solving it. If it is a fire they can let burn, find the larger fire or expect a small deal. Search grid of pain families: regulatory non-compliance (PCI, HIPAA, GDPR), competitive or process modernization, security exposure, productivity–cost–quality, reputational risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deals are stuck low in the org; average deal size is small; reps report enthusiasm but no funding.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small-ticket transactional sales where a departmental annoyance genuinely funds itself — forcing a C-level narrative there only adds cycle time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single CIO/CISO conversation at First Data, retold as an analogy.

### [QSL:DISCOEMAIL] Confirming email as the Discovery gate
**Move:** Close Discovery with an email to the customer restating the pain found, the players, the desired business outcome and the next process step, together with the booked next meeting; the rep forwards it to the manager, and the calendared meeting is the evidence that the stage closed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every deal entering pipeline — this is the concrete answer to "what does a verifiable exit criterion look like".
**AVOID WHEN:** The customer never replies or corrects it — an unacknowledged summary is a sent artifact, not customer verification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as the author's example gate.

### [QSL:TREETOP] Enter at the top of the tree
**Move:** Make the first substantive call at the level that controls discretionary money and owns the business problem, before engaging technical evaluators, and treat any downward delegation as a route you chose rather than one you were handed.
**APPLY WHEN:** A new account or an incumbent replacement; competitors still positioned low in the org; the pain you sell against sits above the noise.
**AVOID WHEN:** The product solves only a departmental or technical problem with no executive measure attached, or the top of the tree and the user are the same person — the climb is then theater.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a recurring pattern claimed across companies, told through the Emilia-Romagna accounts.

### [QSL:BIZLANG] Speak the language of the branch you are on
**Move:** Frame executive conversation in revenue, cost, margin, productivity, risk, time-to-market and market share, with the homework done in advance so the framing is theirs rather than generic.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any call above director level; a champion is arranging access upward.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot yet quantify the problem in their measures — generic "productivity" language invites relegation back down the tree.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to one mentor's coaching, with no measurement offered.

### [QSL:REPLACE-BLOCK] Treat the incumbent's buyer as a hostile gatekeeper
**Move:** In a replacement sale, assume the person who originally bought the incumbent will block you; route around them by locating pain above the noise owned by someone higher and building a champion at that level, instead of negotiating for their permission.
**APPLY WHEN:** A competitor is installed and the technical owner controls access while deferring upward introductions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The incumbent's owner is genuinely dissatisfied and has the influence to drive the replacement themselves — routing around them burns your best asset.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a repeated pattern in replacement deals.

### [QSL:PAIN-OWNER] Target the pain owner, not the functional title
**Move:** Identify who is personally measured on the problem and whose position is jeopardized by it, rather than whoever owns the current system departmentally — the incumbent owner carries no personal pain and has a rehearsed brush-off about their vendor leapfrogging you within six months.
**APPLY WHEN:** Replacement or displacement sales; feature-led pitches stalling at a technical director.
**AVOID WHEN:** The functional owner genuinely carries the measure, as in small orgs where the roles collapse into one person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the CAD / VP Engineering story from the author's early career.

### [QSL:EXECQUAL] The exec-meeting qualification sequence
**Move:** Once an executive signals interest, do not close on the offer — ask for more, in order: who is personally accountable for this initiative, who else is in the decision, may I say I spoke with you, are you working with other vendors, have specific capabilities been specified, is there a definitive timeframe, is it budgeted and who controls the budget, may I bring you a summary of my findings after meeting your people, and would you speak with a comparable customer of ours afterwards. Precede it with genuine homework — annual report, 10-K risks, public statements by the CEO and the target executive — and anchor with a named peer customer in the same city or industry.
**APPLY WHEN:** A first meeting with a senior sponsor who has just opened the door; deals where you need names, budget owner and timeline before committing resources.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rapport is unearned or the homework is missing — it then reads as interrogation; note also the book's own critique that this sequence still misses the negative business consequences, which must be gathered from operational stakeholders next.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one observed CIO call at Sysco, under 90 days ahead of a December audit.

## Stage 2 — Scoping: quantify, implicate, shape the criteria

### [QSL:ASISTOBE] Quantify the as-is and to-be process
**Move:** Map the customer's current process step by step with numbers attached — elapsed time, headcount, cost, error and rework rate — then build the proposed future-state numbers alongside, feed both into a cost justification whose gain visibly dwarfs the pain, and keep that document as the anchor for the later price negotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early stage, before you present; any deal where you expect a price objection or a discount fight.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer will not expose operational metrics — late stage, procurement-led, or trust not yet established — because the justification then becomes fiction; also disproportionate for small transactional purchases.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard scoping requirement across companies.

### [QSL:IMPLICATE] Implication questioning to convert pain into urgency
**Move:** After discovery facts and quantification, ask a chained sequence of consequence questions about the unsolved state — what breaks next, who absorbs it, what the second-order effects on people and measures are — and stop before presenting anything. Do not voice the consequence yourself; ask the question whose answer the buyer has to supply.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pain is real and quantified but the buyer treats it as tolerable; the deal slips quarter to quarter with no compelling event.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pain is unverified or trivial — the sequence then reads as manufactured fear; risky with a buyer who has no personal stake, and manipulative if the consequences you imply are not ones your product actually prevents. The source itself flags that its own illustration — an insurance cold call using closed-ended questions on a solo consumer — does not transfer: complex B2B needs open questions and real listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one remembered inbound call plus the author's own purchase.

### [QSL:SCOPEJOURNEY] Share the Scoping journey and its three deliverables
**Move:** Tell the customer in advance what the next stage looks like — a scoping exercise to quantify pain and outline a potential POV, a Go/No-Go meeting with the EB to confirm the pain is a funded priority, then POV criteria and timeframe — and commit to three artifacts for joint review: a cost justification with as-is/to-be metrics, a POV plan tied to the criteria, and a preliminary pricing proposal derived from that justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** Real pain is established and you need to earn access upward; when you want the evaluation criteria written before a competitor writes them.
**AVOID WHEN:** No pain of consequence has been established — publishing a process ahead of value looks procedural and invites the buyer to skip straight to price; also fits badly against a rigid RFP timetable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a prescribed structure with no outcome data attached.

### [QSL:DC-CONTROL] Co-author the decision criteria and monitor its drift
**Move:** Have the rep help the champion author the criteria — the buyer's shopping list of required capabilities defining the future state — with your unique differentiators documented in the final version, then track every change: shifts your way mean control is increasing, shifts toward a competitor's differentiators mean it is slipping. Inspect with: which of your differentiators appear, have the criteria changed since agreement, which competitor drove the change, when, and who holds the power to change them.
**APPLY WHEN:** The Scoping stage, while criteria are still being written; any mid-cycle inspection.
**AVOID WHEN:** No champion exists — a rep "writing criteria" with no internal author is writing a wish list.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated as the book's control thesis.

### [QSL:METRICS-ASIS] Inspect the Metrics element before pricing exists
**Move:** Require the rep to state the use case and its owner, the quantified pain, explicit as-is and to-be scenarios, quantified business value, whether gain far outweighs pain, the desired business outcome and how success will be measured, existing KPIs, a preliminary cost justification, champion sign-off on every element of it, re-confirmation of why your differentiators are needed in the to-be state, and the price point still to be set with the EB.
**APPLY WHEN:** A deal claims to be past Scoping; before any EB meeting or pricing conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** There is no access to operational data or no champion — invented metrics produce an anchor that collapses under procurement scrutiny.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the standard Scoping exit test.

### [QSL:SCOPEGATE] Set a customer-verifiable exit gate for Scoping
**Move:** Define exit from Scoping through verifiable events rather than rep opinion: full understanding of the pains and capability alignment, confirmation that the customer is buying what you sell, articulated negative consequences, a quantified cost justification built on as-is/to-be numbers, and an identified champion. No champion, no advancement.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stage-gating a pipeline for inspection; deciding what is allowed into forecast.
**AVOID WHEN:** Short-cycle or single-stakeholder deals where a formal champion never exists — the gate then blocks winnable business. This is the method's known bias in its sharpest form.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a checklist produced inside workshop dialogue.

## Stage 2b — Champion development

### [QSL:POWERCHART] Build the power chart before working the org chart
**Move:** Plot influence on one axis and authority on the other and place every known contact: influence plus authority are candidate business champions, influence without authority are candidate technical champions, the remaining quadrants can be coaches at best. Hunt influence with diagnostics — who sits on critical projects, who was recently promoted, who owns new initiatives, who is personally accountable for key decisions, who made the last major departmental purchase, whom C-levels consult, whose statements carry a room, who asks the most perceptive questions, whom others name as influential — and mine the operations staff around each C-level (sales ops, enablement, HR, finance ops).
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-stakeholder accounts; any deal where you cannot name who reaches the EB.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo-buyer or small deals where the mapping cost exceeds the deal; also unreliable when the whole chart is built from one contact's account of the politics.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing framework, illustrated by the Fujitsu account where a champion surfaced only after four months.

### [QSL:PERSONALWIN] Sell the champion's personal win
**Move:** Identify the differentiating outcome this person needs internally — recognition as the problem solver, control over the implemented solution, departmental productivity, a promotion case, status from a visible business win — tie it to one of their job measures or to a major company measure, and make the internal narrative theirs rather than yours.
**APPLY WHEN:** A candidate influencer is engaged and the business pain is already above the noise.
**AVOID WHEN:** Business value is not yet established — appealing to personal ambition with no defensible business case is precisely what a shrewd champion refuses; also inappropriate where the personal incentive conflicts with the buyer's fiduciary duty.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — motive categories listed with no cases attached.

### [QSL:AMCLOOP] Ask–Message–Confirm to build trust
**Move:** Cycle prepared questions, then a message aligning the potential solution to their pain and to company fit, then explicit confirmation of your understanding — and re-ask whenever the confirmation misses. Run the whole loop from the customer's vantage point rather than your own.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every discovery and scoping interaction with a candidate champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** There is no real curiosity driving it — without genuine interest the loop degenerates into interrogation followed by a pitch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as the author's standing method.

### [QSL:EDUCHAMP] Educate the champion into an internal seller
**Move:** Stop selling and start arming: brief them on your product's known limitations, the competitor's product and its traps, common objections, and how to justify the purchase internally; connect them to champions in your installed base, arrange meetings with executives at your company, and hand them the cost justification in a form they can defend, so they are never caught flat-footed in a room you are not in.
**APPLY WHEN:** A champion is identified and an EB or committee meeting is approaching.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person is actually a coach — you would be educating someone with no influence, and disclosing limitations plus competitive positioning carries obvious leak risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the UPS deal, forecast at $500K, closed slightly above $1M.

### [QSL:ROLEPLAY] Role-play the champion's EB meeting the day before
**Move:** Book a session with the champion ahead of their meeting with the EB and rehearse it end to end: business pain, cost justification, desired business outcome, anticipated EB objections, competitive counters. Say plainly that you are worried and why, given the work invested. Their rehearsal performance doubles as verification that the champion is real.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large deals where the champion, not you, will carry the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trust is thin — asking a lightly committed contact to rehearse reads as distrust and can burn them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal at UPS.

### [QSL:CHAMPTEST] Test the champion with knowledge questions and action asks
**Move:** Test on two levels. Knowledge: how purchases get made here, the typical evaluation process, who made the last major purchases, whether they can map the as-is process, whether they can explain why the pain is major and what inaction implies, how they articulate your business value and the competitor's, what happened inside the competitor's meeting. Action: ask them to arrange a demo for another department, introduce another stakeholder, share evaluation documents, call a reference, sponsor the EB meeting, help write the POV criteria, introduce legal and procurement, or co-build the cost justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** After thorough discovery, established above-the-noise pain, earned trust and completed education.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any of those preconditions is missing — premature asks burn a potential champion who has not yet watched you do your own diligence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing test list taught with worked cases.

### [QSL:BOSSTEST] The "my boss says walk away" test
**Move:** Tell the candidate champion that your manager believes you are overinvesting in the account and that there is no deal here, then ask what advice they would give you and what you should tell your boss; their answer tells you whether to stay or leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ambiguous engagement where you cannot separate coach from champion and want a low-cost read.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early in the relationship, or with a buyer who reads pressure tactics — the boss is a manufactured pretext and costs credibility if seen through.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as a routine, reusable test.

### [QSL:WALKAWAY] The professional walk-away to surface stealth champions
**Move:** In a committee-run RFP where no champion has surfaced and the criteria exclude your differentiators, send a respectful written withdrawal stating your reasons. If nobody calls back, there was no stealth champion and no perceived value, so you stop burning time. If people do call back, return and re-educate the committee on which capabilities belong in the criteria; if they still refuse, disengage for real.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long RFP cycles, criteria written against you, months invested with no champion identified.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not actually built trust or educated the committee — the withdrawal then simply ends the deal; also inappropriate where the account is strategically mandatory regardless of this cycle.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep at a large pharmaceutical company; committee members called back within two hours and amended the criteria in writing.

### [QSL:GOODBADEB] Good guy / bad guy to break an EB block
**Move:** When a sponsor claims to want the deal but refuses access to the EB, treat the block as an unqualified deal and force the test: the manager schedules the EB meeting, tells the sponsor honestly that they did so and that the rep was instructed to present the larger option, then in the meeting credits the sponsor's professionalism and presents the larger purchase framed on the EB's own business drivers. A sponsor whose real constraint was signature authority is relieved that you played the bad guy.
**APPLY WHEN:** A forecasted deal with a sponsor who has purchase history, where discovery shows the business goal exceeds what the sponsor can sign and you cannot otherwise verify control.
**AVOID WHEN:** There is no discovery basis for the larger number, no manager willing to own the blowup, or the block is legitimate policy — the source concedes the sponsor may be brokering a discount or running the same play with a competitor, and the deal can be lost outright.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Northwest Insurance, one deal, $500K most-likely plus $500K best-case pursued as $1M.

### [QSL:CHAMPQUAL] Manager-side champion qualification
**Move:** Inspect the rep rather than the customer: were the criteria and process told to us or developed by us; did the champion help build the before/after scenarios, the cost justification, the criteria containing our differentiator, the decision process and the EB-meeting agenda; where do they sit on the power chart; if they hold authority how do we know, and if they do not, why do they have influence; are they in fact a non-influencer; has their influence been confirmed by anyone else; what is their personal win; how have they been educated, developed and tested; what have they actually done to prove they are a champion; whom do they name as EB; have they scheduled the EB meeting. Add a prep call with manager, rep and champion before that meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal claimed to be past Scoping; before an EB meeting is booked.
**AVOID WHEN:** Turned into an interrogation of the customer instead of your own rep — these are manager-to-rep questions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's most heavily used inspection block.

## Stage 3 — The Economic Buyer meeting (Go/No-Go)

### [QSL:EBPREP] Four preparations before an EB meeting
**Move:** (1) Do the homework — company site, annual report, 10-K, shareholder letters, analyst presentations — so no EB time is spent on questions you should have answered yourself. (2) Act as a business partner: skip issues they already know and surface one they had not considered. (3) Position against both corporate measures (revenue, cost, risk) and the EB's own job measures (time to market, employee productivity, application quality). (4) Speak in the EB's business terms, which may differ from the terms that landed with the champion.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every first meeting with an economic buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing exempts it; the practical failure is substituting your research for the champion's briefing — both are required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a prescriptive list; the "eight of ten executives call sales meetings a waste of time" figure is cited with no named source.

### [QSL:EBAGENDA] Run the standard EB agenda
**Move:** Substantiate in this order: high-level summary of discovery and scoping findings; the as-is state; the negative consequences of that state; the proposed to-be state; its benefits; the capabilities required to reach it (your unique and comparable differentiators); the decision criteria for the validation event built from those capabilities; a preliminary ROI grounded in the customer's own internal numbers; customer success stories with quantified before/after metrics; and confirmation of the remaining decision-process steps.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scoping is complete, the champion is aligned, and the POV has not yet started.
**AVOID WHEN:** Discovery is thin — you cannot run discovery questions at an EB; also mismatched if this person is really an approver while the priority decision sits elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the ABB Transformers meeting in Zürich, one deal.

### [QSL:EBASK] Questions to ask the Economic Buyer
**Move:** Ask where this problem ranks on their priority list; which specific business measure it most affects; when they would prefer it solved; a conditional budget commitment (if validation proves we save $X for an investment of $Y, would you allocate $Y); whether anyone besides them must approve a purchase of this size; and what decision-process steps remain after a successful POV.
**APPLY WHEN:** Inside the EB meeting, after the case has been substantiated.
**AVOID WHEN:** No quantified case exists to attach the conditional close to — the $X/$Y question is empty without a cost justification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing question set.

### [QSL:EB-CONFIRM] Run the EB meeting as a confirmation, not a discovery
**Move:** Use the meeting to verify Discovery and Scoping findings and to secure five things — desired business outcome, budget, authority, priority of the pain, purchase timeframe — plus agreement that all vendors test to identical validation criteria, permission to call the EB if criteria change, a review of the preliminary cost justification, and a follow-up meeting if validation results materially move it. Beforehand: confirm who the champion says the EB is, validate that with several people, know the date and what will be presented, and confirm the champion briefed the EB in advance. Stated preference: the sales leader attends every EB meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** The champion has secured access and Discovery/Scoping findings are documented.
**AVOID WHEN:** The findings are thin — walking in to discover in front of the EB burns the single meeting that sets the price anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing rule with a stated personal preference for leader attendance.

### [QSL:MSSE] Prepare for Much-ness, Soon-ness, Sure-ness, Easy-ness
**Move:** Pre-build answers in four buckets, because EB questions nearly always land in them. Much-ness: purchase cost, implementation cost, customer savings. Soon-ness: time to implement, time to payback. Sure-ness: confidence it works in their environment, confidence in the cost justification, which reference they may call. Easy-ness: ease of implementation, ease of learning for their people.
**APPLY WHEN:** Rehearsal ahead of any EB or executive committee meeting; also as a coaching checklist for the champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication is given; it is a preparation grid, not a script to recite.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a taxonomy taught as near-universal for EB questions.

### [QSL:TWOCOND] The two-condition callback agreement
**Move:** As the meeting closes, ask permission to call the EB directly under exactly two conditions: if the final business case differs materially from the preliminary justification after validation, and if anyone attempts to significantly change the agreed criteria. State it deliberately while the competitor's champion is in the room — it functions as a warning against criteria tampering.
**APPLY WHEN:** The end of a successful EB meeting in a competitive deal with a defined validation event.
**AVOID WHEN:** No criteria are agreed yet, or the EB does not own the criteria; it also loses all force if you never actually use the channel.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as the author's habitual closing question.

### [QSL:BANT-SCOPE] Use BANT only where it works
**Move:** Keep BANT as the EB-meeting confirmation set — pain ranked as a priority (Need), a specific timeframe (Time), authority to buy (Authority), budget allocated on successful validation (Budget) — and refuse to use it as the deal-inspection layer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Preparing or debriefing an EB meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** Adopted as the organization's qualification method — it maps to no process step, identifies no tangible advancement, diagnoses nothing about where reps struggle, and ignores champion, EB, competition and their champion, pain identification, as-is/to-be metrics, criteria framing, process control and the paper process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a comparative claim argued from structure rather than from measured outcomes.

## Stage 4 — Validation Event (POV)

### [QSL:POVRENAME] Ban "POC", require "POV"
**Move:** Replace Proof of Concept with Proof of Value across forecast, CRM stages and manager vocabulary, on the reasoning that nobody doubts the product functions — what must be proven is that specific capabilities produce quantified business value for this customer's use case, so every evaluation carries success criteria derived from the customer's own as-is metrics.
**APPLY WHEN:** Teams run technical bake-offs with no agreed business criteria; evaluation win rates are low and evaluation length is drifting.
**AVOID WHEN:** The technology is genuinely unproven or the integration is first-of-kind, where technical feasibility really is the open question; also pointless as pure renaming if criteria-setting does not change alongside it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing rule imposed at the companies the author ran.

### [QSL:POV-CRITERIA] Write and weight the test criteria before agreeing to compete
**Move:** Before the event, agree in writing on the criteria, the weighting of every element and the scoring method, with your differentiators represented and the weighting favourable to your solution. Test the result by asking whether you would place a wager on these rules.
**APPLY WHEN:** A validation event is being scheduled and a champion exists who can co-author the criteria.
**AVOID WHEN:** The criteria were fixed before you arrived and reflect a competitor's differentiators — the guidance is to fix control first or decline, not to enter and hope.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the core repeated claim that whoever controls the test criteria wins.

### [QSL:POV-GATE] Run the POV only after four preconditions
**Move:** Refuse to start until: a confirmed champion exists; that champion helped frame the criteria and scoring rules; a cost justification built from Discovery and Scoping shows gain far exceeding pain; and priority, budget, authority, timing and remaining process steps have been verified with the EB.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any resource-heavy technical validation late in the cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** Deliberately used as a lead-generation or education device — listed as precisely what a POV is not.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing gate, echoed by the managers' confessions of prior failures.

### [QSL:POV-QUIDPRO] Secure the quid pro quo with the Economic Buyer
**Move:** Before the event, get the EB to agree that the winner receives a purchase order subject only to legal review and pricing negotiation, that every vendor tests against the same criteria, and that you may call the EB if anyone tries to change agreed criteria mid-test.
**APPLY WHEN:** EB access exists through the champion and the evaluation is competitive with two or more vendors.
**AVOID WHEN:** No EB access has been obtained — asking for the commitment through a proxy produces an unenforceable promise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as standard practice in the author's process.

### [QSL:POV-RUN] Work the event, don't just supervise it
**Move:** During the event, reconfirm the as-is and to-be metrics from the cost justification, adjust them as new information appears, capture material evidence that the solution resolves the customer's pain, and keep aligning back to the champion's personal win.
**APPLY WHEN:** The event is running and technical resources are engaged.
**AVOID WHEN:** The event is being used to start Discovery, hunt for a champion or build relationships — all listed as disqualifying misuse.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a checklist offered as standard rep behaviour.

### [QSL:POV-DEBRIEF] Close the loop face-to-face with the champion, then re-anchor with the EB
**Move:** Build a summary report of findings and walk the champion through it in person until they agree with every element of the business case; where results materially change the original cost justification, rebuild it and take the new version to the EB to justify price and drive urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the event, before pricing discussions open.
**AVOID WHEN:** The champion holds doubts you paper over — the purpose is removing doubt, not producing a document.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — articulated by the trainees and endorsed by the author.

## Stage 5 — Business case, negotiation, close

### [QSL:BIZCASE] Write the Business Case as the customer's internal document
**Move:** Present face-to-face to the EB a document written from their perspective: their corporate objectives; their strategic initiatives; the business problem or opportunity; how it maps to those initiatives; before/after use-case scenarios showing how your solution uniquely resolves it; how the outcome gets delivered (people, process, timeframes); and a final cost justification in their own measures with quantified values, never bare phrases such as "increased productivity". It doubles as the post-implementation baseline for expansion and cross-sell.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-validation, pre-negotiation, with EB access and agreed metrics in hand.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot quantify — an unquantified business case hands price-setting over to the customer's perception.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the canonical late-stage artifact.

### [QSL:RISK-BRIDGE] Carry the buyer across peak risk
**Move:** When buyer fear spikes after validation, use five levers: replay the validation results proving the product solved their use case; name the negative consequences of leaving the pain unsolved; share comparable results from customers with the same pain; arrange a peer conversation with a champion at another company; and remind them of their personal win.
**APPLY WHEN:** Right after the POV, when the champion is personally exposed for advocating you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The proof points are borrowed rather than earned inside this account — that is where fabricated evidence collapses. The consequence-framing lever is a pressure technique and stays legitimate only while tied to pains the customer already quantified.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as repeated late-stage practice.

### [QSL:IMPL-PLAN] Back-date an implementation plan to create urgency
**Move:** Present a resourced implementation plan and derive the contract deadline backwards from the EB's own target date — project start date, stakeholder training date, internal resource reservation date, all inside the EB's stated timeframe, therefore contract finalized by a specific date.
**APPLY WHEN:** The EB has already stated a required outcome date and you are entering negotiation.
**AVOID WHEN:** No timeframe was ever established by the EB — the urgency then reads as vendor-invented deadline pressure and is easily dismissed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a template produced as standard practice.

### [QSL:PROC-PLAYBOOK] Name the procurement gambit instead of reacting to it
**Move:** Recognize the standard repertoire as tactics rather than information: manufactured hostility on first contact; take-it-or-leave-it anchored to an unrelated enterprise deal (demanding 70% because another vendor gave 50%); "you need our logo more than we need you"; "we love you but not at this price"; a no-budget claim that contradicts what the EB told you; good-guy/bad-guy staging around an unseen VP; the friendly contact who goes dark until the final hours of the quarter; the shame play about software margins; the one-more-thing add-on at the finish line; the appeal to hard times; and the "it's over — PO attached, just agree to terms" close.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement has taken over the conversation after validation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as licence to disbelieve everything — some constraints, such as real budget ceilings and real approval thresholds, are genuine and testable through the paper process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — enumerated from repeated experience.

### [QSL:PROC-CALM] Hold the anchor; never decide on price in the room
**Move:** Stay calm, keep the conversation anchored to the EB-acknowledged cost justification and the Business Case, and always retain the option to think about it or consult your manager rather than answering a price demand on the spot; their pain has a clock, and your quarter-end is not their concern to know about.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement applies pressure and the sales process was genuinely completed.
**AVOID WHEN:** You skipped steps — with no champion and no agreed justification, patience is a bluff and the outcome is a deep discount or a loss. This is pressure symmetry, legitimate only because the urgency came from the customer's own quantified consequences rather than being manufactured.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a stated negotiation posture across deals.

### [QSL:PAPER-PROC] Map the signature process step, person and duration
**Move:** Document every step from purchase request to PO — named person, function, elapsed time — and check whether the champion has ever purchased at this dollar level, since thresholds change the path; where they are a first-timer, find someone who has bought at that amount and map it jointly. Get legal and procurement contacts early, introduce your counsel to theirs, and introduce yourself to procurement. Inspect: does the champion know the process, walk me through the steps, who are the stakeholders at each one, what is the timeframe per step, who is the legal contact, have documents been redlined, have you spoken to procurement.
**APPLY WHEN:** A deal is being forecast for a specific quarter.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small-dollar, no-committee purchases where the paper process is a credit card.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented with a worked example and forecast arithmetic.

## Cross-stage — MEDDPICC as the deal-analysis layer

### [QSL:MEDD-GPS] Bind the qualification method to process stages, not to the acronym
**Move:** Map each MEDDPICC element to the stage where it is earned (Identify → Discovery; Metrics, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Champion → Scoping; Economic Buyer → EB meeting; Competition → continuous; Paper Process → Negotiate & Close), then read a missing element as a stage diagnosis rather than a checklist score. The process is the map; the method is the GPS.
**APPLY WHEN:** Forecast reviews degenerate into unstructured barrages of questions; managers disagree about what stage a deal is in.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as a form to fill in — box-ticking without understanding is the documented degeneration of this method.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's central management claim.

### [QSL:3WHYS] Qualify Identify with the Three WHYs
**Move:** Ask the rep, per deal: why do they have to buy (pain level, specific pain points, is it above the noise, technical or business terms, which use case); why do *they* have to buy (who is most impacted, their title, how the pain hits their job measures); why buy from us (do our differentiators tie to their pains, which capabilities are uniquely differentiated); and why buy now (what happens with no action, which company measure suffers, what alternatives exist, what business outcome is wanted). If the rep cannot answer, the deal is still in Discovery no matter what the forecast says.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any forecasted deal review; coaching a rep after early-stage calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied to transactional single-owner deals where every answer is trivially "the buyer" — the questioning adds cycle time without adding information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's core inspection routine.

### [QSL:DP-MAP] Map and re-validate the decision process
**Move:** Document the specific people, events and timeframes the customer will use to evaluate and select — overview presentations, demos, POV, cost justifications, selection and negotiation, each carrying a date — know the stakeholders per event, and validate the process with all of them. Inspect: who else is involved, whom the rep has met one-on-one, whether new people entered, whether the process changed and who changed it, whether the champion was involved, whether a competitor's champion controls it and what their title is, whether the POV has start and end dates, and whether the EB will confirm the process.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scoping through validation; whenever dates slip with no explanation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Single-decision-maker deals where the "process" is one person's calendar.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standard inspection list built with the trainees.

### [QSL:CONTROL-3] Diagnose which of three parties controls the deal
**Move:** Ask who holds control — the customer, the competition, or you — and settle it by the direction of information flow. Early on the customer controls, because you need information from them. Once the champion starts needing information from you (how to evaluate, how to handle competitors, references, internal justification), control has moved to the rep. If criteria and process were defined without your rep and the rep is merely being informed of them, the competition controls. Score it at three levels: no control, partial control, full control.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-stage forecast review; deciding whether a deal is forecastable at all.
**AVOID WHEN:** Control is declared from rapport rather than from the concrete artifacts — criteria, process, EB meeting — that the diagnosis requires as evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's routine forecast diagnostic.

### [QSL:COMP-WATCH] Track the competitor's champion, not just their product
**Move:** Inspect who the competitor is, when they entered and whether before you, who their champion is and at what influence level, whether that champion outranks yours, whether additional competitive champions exist, who else the competitor is meeting, whether they have met the EB, whether criteria were already set when you arrived, whether criteria changed to include their differentiators and when, how their champion differentiates and strategizes, and whether the decision process changed after they entered. Then ask explicitly who wins if the two champions collide in an internal meeting and whom the room sides with; if you are outmatched, arm your champion harder against competitive traps and recruit more champions wider and higher.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive committee evaluations, especially late stage or after any unexplained change to criteria or process.
**AVOID WHEN:** Paranoia substitutes for customer work — criteria drift is the observable, not gossip; and there is nothing to weigh where no competitor is identified or the decision is single-threaded.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeated coaching pattern; the head-to-head champion comparison itself is offered as an inspection question with no cases attached.

### [QSL:FUD-TRAPS] Arm the champion with competitive traps (use with care)
**Move:** Educate the champion on how competitors sell against you, how to handle their typical objections and traps, and — as the source states it — supply traps, objections and fear/uncertainty/doubt about the competitor's capabilities, grounded in known competitor weaknesses.
**APPLY WHEN:** A competitor with a real, verifiable capability gap is actively reshaping criteria in their own favour.
**AVOID WHEN:** The weakness is not verifiable, or the material is anything other than accurate capability comparison — planting FUD is the same tactic the book condemns when it is done to you, so it inverts into deception easily; also unusable where the champion's credibility with peers is your only asset.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted in classroom dialogue with no outcome data.

## Cross-stage — Forecast management and the QBR

### [QSL:ROOTQ] Multi-level inspection questioning
**Move:** In deal review, drive each answer down through second-, third- and fourth-level follow-ups until you reach why the deal is or is not advancing through a named process stage, then close by naming the next logical step. Treat any answer describing activity or relationship warmth as an unanswered question and re-ask it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any forecasted deal in a committee sale; reps answering with feelings, features or third-party assurances.
**AVOID WHEN:** No shared vocabulary or process exists yet — the questions land as interrogation and reps learn to perform answers; fix vocabulary and stages first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeated pattern across many forecast sessions in multiple companies.

### [QSL:REDFLAG] Standing red-flag list for forecast review
**Move:** Flag and stop on: a champion not spoken to in days or weeks; no competition claimed on a large first deal into a new logo; stakeholders unnamed beyond one contact; buying criteria written before the rep arrived; criteria drifting toward a competitor's features; a large discount demand explained only as budget cuts; a POC the customer wants re-run together with a price-sharpening request; a loss recorded as "no decision"; and legal or procurement status unchecked on a deal expected to close this quarter.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal on the current-quarter forecast, especially a first deal into a large regulated account.
**AVOID WHEN:** True single-signer, low-ACV transactional purchases — missing stakeholders and procurement silence are normal there and firing on them only adds noise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — each flag drawn from a rep scenario and matched against the quarter's actual result.

### [QSL:KNOWUNKNOWN] Forecast what you can defend; name what you don't know
**Move:** Commit only to the number you can defend with pain, quantified pain, consequence of inaction and evidence that you shaped the buying criteria, and explicitly name the unknowns — such as the signature path — that keep an upside deal out of commit. Then hold the number under manager and peer pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any commit-level forecast conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as cover for chronic under-forecasting with no supporting deal detail — the same posture without the detail is sandbagging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep forecasting $175K against a $300K quota and landing $250K.

### [QSL:SLIPADD] Carry the slip, don't reset it
**Move:** When a deal slips out of a quarter, the rep's next-quarter accountability becomes quota plus the slipped amount, and the slipped deal does not count as new coverage; say the arithmetic out loud in the review.
**APPLY WHEN:** A committed deal falls out in the final weeks and reappears on next quarter's forecast.
**AVOID WHEN:** The slip came from a verifiable external event such as an acquisition or budget freeze — reloading quota then punishes the rep for reality and teaches them to hide bad news.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — argued by one manager character against another's forecast, presented approvingly rather than measured.

### [QSL:FCFILLER] Kill the shrinking recurring deal
**Move:** Track each deal's forecasted amount across quarters; a deal whose size steps down quarter over quarter while its close date rolls forward is forecast filler — remove it rather than re-qualify it, and audit why it was accepted three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** The same account shows up on three consecutive forecasts at decreasing value.
**AVOID WHEN:** The shrink is a deliberate land-and-expand rescope with a signed first phase — that is a scope change, not decay.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — named as a common pattern, illustrated by one nine-month deal.

### [QSL:PUSH-OFF] Push unqualified deals off the forecast early
**Move:** When the qualifying questions are exhausted and the rep still cannot articulate the deal's exact status, instruct them to remove it from forecast — burden of proof sits with the rep, preponderance of evidence is required to stay in — and coach them on why it failed to qualify.
**APPLY WHEN:** The first weeks of the quarter, while there is still runway to replace it.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**AVOID WHEN:** Done late in the quarter, when it is too late to reconcile, or without the coaching half, which turns it into arbitrary punishment; acknowledged as unpopular with reps.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard forecast discipline; the two claimed effects (more information surfaced, the gap forcing new pipeline) are asserted.

### [QSL:RECON-4X] Reconcile every shortfall at 4x the gap
**Move:** For each rep compute quota minus closed-and-committed to get the shortfall, set a reconciliation target of at least four times that gap (an assumed ~25% close rate), and have them write out the specific per-account activity that produces it, broken out both by pipeline stage and by account.
**APPLY WHEN:** The first weeks of the quarter, after push-off, when a gap exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your actual stage-to-close rate differs materially from 25% — the multiplier is a rule of thumb from one org, not a measured conversion; also when it turns into a coercion ritual rather than planning, since intimidation produces commitments that never close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — derived from the author's first forecast shortfall as a new manager.

### [QSL:TAKE-ME-CHAMP] "Take me to your champion"
**Move:** After the forecast session, ask each rep to take you to the champion of every remaining forecasted deal and meet them within the first month of the quarter, to verify that a champion exists and that the rep holds control; spend the rest of the quarter on the newly generated reconciliation deals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-push-off, in month one, with 11–12 weeks of runway remaining.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**AVOID WHEN:** Deal volume per manager makes it physically impossible, or in transactional/solo-buyer motions where there is no champion construct to verify.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the surest method of forecast verification.
