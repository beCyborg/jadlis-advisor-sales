# advisor-blount-objections — myths and demotions

What this tradition debunks *inside itself*, what must be demoted despite the tradition asserting it, and which numbers may never be quoted as fact. Read alongside `core.md` when a user quotes this school back at you, or when a proposed move needs its failure mode stated.

---

## 1. Myths the tradition itself refuses

- **"Overcome / combat / rebut the objection."** Rejected as a category, not as a wording preference. Argument triggers reactance and hardens the buyer regardless of how good the data is. You cannot argue someone out of a feeling.
- **Silver bullets — the perfect script, magic words, an AI that closes for you.** Named and rejected up front. Generic "50 ways to overcome objections" lists are dismissed outside narrow transactional contexts. Boiler-room one-liners are described as winning largely by accident, with intermittent reinforcement keeping reps believing in them and buyer's remorse cleaning up after.
- **"Objections happen at the close."** The tradition's central correction. Training that only drills closing rebuttals leaves the prospecting, red-herring, and next-step layers entirely untreated — three of the four objection types.
- **"Being less pushy reduces objections."** Claimed inverse: passive, hedged asks manufacture resistance, because there is nothing concrete to say yes to and insecurity is transmitted.
- **"Toughen up / don't take it personally."** Rejected as a non-technique — it intellectualizes pain the rep actually feels, decays with the motivational high, and leaves no skill behind. Related: fight-or-flight cannot be suppressed; only the response after it is trainable, so programs promising fearlessness sell the wrong product.
- **"Are you the decision maker?"** Asking directly is claimed to *create* the late-stage "I need to run this by my boss" objection, because the status admission conflicts with self-image. `[OBJ:DMINDIRECT]` is the replacement.
- **Pump and pounce.** Answering the first objection before knowing whether it is the only, real, or biggest one. A product of impatience that produces an endless objection queue — the anti-pattern `[OBJ:ISOLATE]` exists to prevent.
- **Arguing a buyer out of a bad past experience with satisfaction statistics.** The scripted bad example ends with the buyer hardened and asking to be emailed prices. Same mechanism: confronting a buyer with proof their current vendor is failing makes them defend their original decision and recast you as the adversary.
- **"Never take no for an answer."** Split verdict: useful for top-of-funnel persistence, actively harmful deeper in the process, where it becomes arguing a buyer into submission.
- **Assuming logic wins.** Anchored on the author's own $500,000 loss to a worse incumbent despite the highest pilot scores the client had recorded — lost to sunk cost plus status quo.
- **Comforting readings of a stalled deal** ("he told me to call back in a month, so he's interested"). Seller-side confirmation bias meeting buyer-side conflict aversion; accepting a brush-off feels like progress and is not.
- **Single-ratio optimization.** One client doubled proposal-to-close and still missed revenue because inbound lead volume had collapsed underneath — the improved ratio would have had to roughly quadruple to compensate.
- **"Scripts sound fake."** Counter-claim: the real problem is *unrehearsed* scripts. Paired myth: "there are infinite ways to be told no" — against which three to five recurring RBOs cover most prospecting volume.
- **"Toughness transfers across domains."** Military recruiters who preferred live fire to cold calls are the example; rejection immunity has to be drilled in the domain that actually scares you.
- **"Control comes from talking."** Counter-claim: whoever asks the questions holds control.
- **Winging it as authenticity, and the lottery model of success.** Both named as low-performer rationalizations. Reading a no as a verdict on personal worth is rejected, as is post-rejection rumination — the rep invents a story about what the prospect thought while the prospect has already forgotten the interaction.
- **Sleep deprivation as a badge of honor.** Named directly as a performance cost.

## 2. Demotions — asserted by the tradition, downgraded here

- **Power posing as a hormonal mechanism** `[OBJ:POSTURE-SHIFT]`. The book cites Cuddy by name, which is why the move carries an `external-research` token at all — but the testosterone/cortisol effect failed replication after the book's sources. Only the felt-confidence component survives. Never present the hormonal version.
- **All in-book behavioral-science citations except three.** Kahneman on loss aversion, cognitive dissonance, commitment/consistency, illusory truth, frequency illusion, availability, ambiguity bias, negativity bias — invoked by name or concept, none measured in a sales context, none sourced to a specific study in the text. These are second-hand and belong at `assertion`, not at `external-research`. Only Damasio `[OBJ:EMOTION-FIRST]`, Mitchell & Tamir `[OBJ:SELFDISC]`, and Cuddy `[OBJ:POSTURE-SHIFT]` name actual research, and all three support background premises rather than any sales move.
- **Neuro-narration generally.** Amygdala/novelty-detection under `[OBJ:PATTERN-PAINT]`, the "magic quarter second" under `[OBJ:LEDGE]`, the emotional-contagion premise under `[OBJ:NONVERB-CONF]` — all popular-press framing. Per the lens spec, brain-based rationalizations are refused by design; the behaviors may stand, the explanations may not.
- **Priming and scarcity claimed for the meeting objective** `[OBJ:AGENDA-OBJECTIVE]`. Illustrated with generic word-cue examples and nothing else. Lowest-ranked move in the layer.
- **Same-author and same-school corroboration.** `[OBJ:BRIDGE-STATES]` is cross-referenced to the author's own earlier book; `[OBJ:ASSUM-ASK]` quotes Gitomer. Neither is independent confirmation, and neither may be presented as a second source.
- **The persistence chapter.** Survivorship-biased exemplars (Harland Sanders told no 1,000+ times, *Harry Potter* rejected 12 times, Stephen King's rejection nail, UCF going 0–12 then 12–0) plus two unresolvable footnoted claims — rejection as a creativity catalyst, rejection as a signal you are onto something. It directly contradicts the qualification and walk-away discipline elsewhere in the same book, and the book never adjudicates. Treat the persistence material as motivational, never as strategy.
- **"Step-skipping is an emotional-control gap, not a training gap."** Convenient for a trainer whose product is discipline coaching; unfalsifiable as stated.
- **"Objection = process defect"** `[OBJ:PROCESSDISCIPLINE]`. Directionally the best thing in the layer, but it leaves no room for the case where the buyer is right and the product is the wrong fit. Disqualification is framed as timing or targeting, almost never as the offer being weaker than the incumbent's. When a council answer uses this principle, it must keep "the product is losing on merit" on the list of causes.

## 3. Documented failure modes of these techniques

- **Chasing the red herring.** The canonical backfire: an AE pushed into itemizing a professional-services fee before a demo. Defending the fee turned one question into a group pile-on — company stability, references, missing same-size logos — and a later offer to waive the fee could not recover the meeting.
- **Minimize before isolate.** The named ordering error in `[OBJ:5STEP-FRAME]`; you resolve a decoy and the real blocker arrives untouched.
- **The yes-ledger read as a gotcha** `[OBJ:YES-LEDGER]`. Replaying agreements converts consistency pressure into reactance the moment it sounds like a ledger being presented against them. Tie-down trap phrasing ("so if I fix this, you'll sign") is explicitly rejected as gotcha closing.
- **Risk-reversal you cannot honor** `[OBJ:BURDEN-SHIFT]`. On long-term, non-cancelable, high-switching-cost commitments it becomes false reassurance and produces churn and remorse instead of a close — a point the book concedes about heavy-handed risk reversal.
- **Takeaway and calendar scarcity when the scarcity is fabricated** `[OBJ:TAKEAWAY]`. Checkable and permanently trust-destroying; also punishes a genuine slow champion for someone else's delay. Same failure shape as padded timeline math in `[OBJ:DEADLINE-MATH]`.
- **Pattern-painting into gimmick.** Breaks the buyer's script and your credibility together; weakest exactly where predictability signals safety — conservative enterprise and procurement settings.
- **Hunch-testing that invents the concern** `[OBJ:DEEPLISTEN]`, and leading paraphrase in `[OBJ:PRIORITY-ECHO]`. Both manufacture an objection that did not exist — the very self-inflicted-objection failure `[OBJ:NO-SELF-INFLICT]` warns about.
- **Rehearsal hardening into a script** `[OBJ:REHEARSE]`, and murder boarding run alone `[OBJ:MURDER-BOARD]` — the latter defeats its own stated purpose, since a confirmation-bias countermeasure needs an outside challenger.
- **Rejection exposure on live accounts or against a broken list** `[OBJ:REJECT-EXPOSURE]`. Multiplies wasted rejection and burns real targets; also the wrong tool where the avoidance is clinical anxiety.
- **The "reasons 5–7" branch as a face-saving exit** `[OBJ:BRUSHOFFDIAG]`. Disqualification needs behavioral evidence, not the rep's mood.

## 4. Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

All of the following are self-reported by the author or his firm, with no published methodology. Cite them as claims of this school, never as findings.

- **50–70% yes rate for confident asks vs. 10–30% for hedged asks** — the load-bearing number under `[OBJ:ASSUM-ASK]`, from claimed tracking of "thousands" of interactions. No methodology. Related: "nine times out of ten" not getting what you want traces to not asking directly.
- **~90% of stakeholders say yes to "are you the decision maker?"** and **~90% answer "I'm good" to the agenda check** — both explicitly field observation, not measured samples.
- **80%+ of stalled pipeline attributed to missing next steps**; **~20% of confidently asked next-step requests draw an objection** — the author's estimates.
- **Rule of thirds** (one-third yes / no / objection, resolved in 15–20 seconds) and **~50% of turnarounds draw a second RBO** — personal observation and rule of thumb.
- **Three to five RBOs cover 80%+ of prospecting objections** — from boot-camp exercises, an artifact of the exercise as much as of the field.
- **37% of a targeted call list attended the conference anyway** — one campaign.
- **"IQ drops when preoccupied with rejection"** — asserted as proven, no study named. **"~50% of rejection's damage is self-inflicted"** — a secondhand citation of Guy Winch via a magazine piece.
- **The worked-example arithmetic** — 15 hours/month saved, 20% vendor-consolidation savings, $63,600/year status-quo cost vs. $34,800/year savings and a ~$10,000 buyout, ~$25,000 first-year ROI. Illustrative dialogue math, not benchmarks; do not reuse the ratios as planning inputs.
- **The 96% customer-satisfaction figure** appears only *inside a negative example*, as an illustration of what not to argue with. Quoting it as a supporting statistic inverts the book's point.
- **80%-objection vs. 80%-yes doors** — a thought experiment about qualification, not measured rates.
- **Commercial framing that colors everything above:** a training catalog of 17 programs, a membership bundled with the book, and unreconciled bio numbers (250+ speaking days/year vs. 300+ nights on the road). The book doubles as a funnel for the methodology it evaluates.

## 5. Where the layer stops working

- **Enterprise committee deals with formal procurement.** Assumptive unilateral closing outruns legal review and vendor onboarding; `[OBJ:ASSUM-LANG]`, `[OBJ:5STEP-FRAME]`, `[OBJ:BURDEN-SHIFT]`, `[OBJ:ISOLATE]` are gated to solo/few stakeholders and micro/low/mid ACV for exactly this reason.
- **Transactional one-call motions.** Micro-commitment ladders, confirmation calls, agenda frameworks, and relate laps add cycle time where the sale closes in one conversation — and this is the only context where the book concedes canned rebuttal scripts are defensible.
- **Expansion, account management, and channel motions.** `[OBJ:PIPE-DETACH]` assumes you can go make more pipeline; with a fixed book of accounts that detachment is unavailable, and takeaway/scarcity risks a customer relationship rather than a prospect's.
- **Deals where the objection is a real constraint, not a feeling.** Frozen budget, signed contract, absent authority, compliance block: the stop rules are the correct output, not a better rebuttal.
- **Where discovery was skipped or is structurally impossible** — RFP-only processes, no stakeholder access, procurement-mediated bids. The minimize step has no banked yeses to replay and the book's honest position is that the close was already lost upstream.
- **Modern buying reality outside a live conversation.** Product-led and self-serve motions, trial-driven purchase, committees that never take a call, asynchronous email-only evaluation. The model assumes a rep in a room.
- **Buyer-side economics and non-US norms.** Pricing strategy, packaging, contract structure, and procurement mechanics barely appear — price is handled as emotion and framing rather than as a pricing problem. Assumptive asking, takeaways, and pattern-breaking directness are US-normed B2B behaviors offered without cultural adjustment.
- **The ethics boundary is left undefined.** Takeaway, scarcity, deadline math, and consistency replay all operate on discomfort. The book warns against gotcha closing and fabricated scarcity in passing but supplies no test for when a legitimate move has become manipulation. This lens applies the council's own contour instead: a move the user is not willing to show the buyer is not proposed.
