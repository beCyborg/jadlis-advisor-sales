# advisor-founding — core

What this layer claims, ranked by how well each claim is actually supported. The ladder used is `field-study → external-research → practitioner-n-many → practitioner-anecdote → assertion`.

**Ceiling notice.** Neither source can exceed `practitioner-n-many`. Kazanjy's evidence base is one company arc plus companies he advised; Fitzpatrick's is his own two startups plus founders he coached. There is no control group anywhere in either book, and the strongest-looking external citations (lead-response contact-rate figures behind [FOUND:FIVEMINUTE] and [FOUND:SIXTRIES]) arrive without verifiable references, so they are treated as relayed practitioner claims, not `external-research`.

**School-echo notice.** Kazanjy and Fitzpatrick belong to the same founder-led / lean-startup tradition. Where they agree — discovery before pitching ([FOUND:PITCHFORMAT] / [FOUND:NOBLINDPITCH] / [FOUND:DISCOFRAME]), in-person first ([FOUND:INPERSONFIRST] / [FOUND:INPERSON]), buying-process questions ([FOUND:AUTHCOMMERCIALS] / [FOUND:MONEYPATH]), commitment as the unit of progress ([FOUND:MICROCONTRACT] / [FOUND:CURRENCY]) — that is one voice repeated, not two independent confirmations.

## Tier 1 — practitioner-n-many (repeated across many deals or many coached founders)

These rest on a practitioner running the same move dozens to hundreds of times and reporting a consistent pattern. Still uncontrolled, still self-reported, but the strongest thing this layer has.

1. **Founder-led selling is product management wearing a sales costume.** The person carrying the message into customer conversations must own or sit inside what gets built next, and the loop from hearing an objection to shipping the artifact that answers it should be hours, not quarters. [FOUND:NO-ABSTRACTION], [FOUND:APPENDIX-2X], [FOUND:PITCH-AS-PRODUCT], [FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH]

2. **Epochs, not techniques.** What is correct pre-repeatability is destructive post-repeatability and vice versa, so every tactic ships with a stage gate; the characteristic disaster is applying scaling-era methods (hiring a sales leader to discover the motion) during the figuring-it-out era. [FOUND:STAGE-GATE], [FOUND:DEFER-AE-HIRE], [FOUND:MATURITY-LADDER], [FOUND:LEADER-EXIT-CRITERIA]

3. **Progress is measured in commitments surrendered, not in sentiment expressed.** A meeting that produced warmth and no defined next step produced nothing; the counterparty must give up time, reputation or cash, and every stage transition is bracketed by an explicit agreement. [FOUND:CURRENCY], [FOUND:MICROCONTRACT], [FOUND:ZOMBIE], [FOUND:EXPLICIT-NEXT-STEP-CALENDARED], [FOUND:BOOKLIVE]

4. **Past behaviour is evidence; opinions, hypotheticals and compliments are not.** Anchor every claim to the last concrete instance, and expect the anchored version to contradict the self-image version. This is the single best-supported operating rule in the layer, resting on many coached founders plus repeated worked dialogues. [FOUND:MOMTEST3], [FOUND:LASTTIME], [FOUND:ANCHORFLUFF], [FOUND:DEFLECT], [FOUND:WALKTHRU]

5. **The narrative comes before every artifact, and it is inductive.** Problem, who owns it, what it costs, how it is solved today and why that fails, what changed, how yours works, what proof exists — presented problem-first so disagreement surfaces early and cheaply, with pricing as the conclusion rather than the opening. [FOUND:PSS-NARRATIVE], [FOUND:INDUCTIVE-EXIT], [FOUND:MVP-DECK], [FOUND:PITCHFORMAT]

6. **Cost of the problem is the load-bearing number.** Per-unit maths scaled by account size converts an acknowledged problem into urgency, sets the price floor, defends against discounting, and prices the status quo when the buyer is content. [FOUND:COST-MODEL], [FOUND:PRICEFRAME], [FOUND:STATUSQUOCOST], [FOUND:ROIDEFENSE], [FOUND:IMPLICATIONS]

7. **Qualification is about the account's pain, not the contact's title or budget line.** In a category the buyer did not know existed, strict budget-and-timeline qualification disqualifies exactly the right prospects; substitute magnitude and urgency, and separate "the account cannot buy" from "this person cannot decide". [FOUND:URGENCYQUAL], [FOUND:QUALACCOUNT], [FOUND:BUDGET-OR-HOURS-TARGET], [FOUND:WRONGPERSON]

8. **Segmentation defects masquerade as research defects.** When the feedback is incoherent despite good technique, the segment is wrong, not the questions; and a segment is not finished until it names both a group and a place to find them. [FOUND:SEGMIX], [FOUND:SEGSPEC], [FOUND:WHOWHERE], [FOUND:ICP-METADATA]

9. **Objections run one uniform loop back to the ask.** Catch, convert to the question under the question, answer with quantitative and qualitative proof, validate it landed, resume, and never mistake a handled objection for a close. [FOUND:OBJLOOP], [FOUND:SOLEBLOCKER], [FOUND:BLOCKERQ], [FOUND:PRICEDIAGNOSE]

10. **Unattended trials fail by default.** A trial request is usually a request for proof, and the reliable answers are existing evidence or a guided session — never credential handoff. [FOUND:TRIALDIAGNOSE], [FOUND:RIDEALONG], [FOUND:REFUSEUNGUIDED], [FOUND:STRUCTPILOT], [FOUND:TRIALCOST]

11. **Pipeline decays through missing next steps, and the fix is calendar mechanics, not willpower.** Booked meetings, written recaps in one continuous thread, timed backstops, and a standing block to clean the pipe. [FOUND:CALENDARIT], [FOUND:POSTMEET-SUMMARY-THREAD], [FOUND:PIPELINE-REVIEW-BLOCK], [FOUND:RUTHLESS-PIPE-CLEANING]

12. **The sale does not end at signature; unreported value erodes into churn.** Implementation is a sales motion with its own micro-contracts, and proof of value must be instrumented and delivered back to the sponsors who authorised the spend. [FOUND:IMPL-REDISCOVERY], [FOUND:BACK-CHECK-ON-CALENDAR], [FOUND:INSTRUMENT-OUTCOMES], [FOUND:QUARTERLY-SUCCESS-REVIEW]

13. **Screening beats interviewing when hiring sellers.** Artifact-based written screens and full mock passes authenticate ability; resumes and quota claims are the candidate's own marketing. [FOUND:WRITTEN-SCREEN-FIRST], [FOUND:MOCK-DEMO-FULL-PASS], [FOUND:PROOF-OVER-SELF-REPORT], [FOUND:SCREEN-BEFORE-ONSITE]

14. **Learning cannot be outsourced before a working business model exists.** A hired interviewer softens bad news, and founders assimilate disconfirming evidence poorly at second hand — so the founder runs the conversations and the team reviews the notes. [FOUND:TEAMPREP], [FOUND:LISTOF3], [FOUND:MOTIONS], [FOUND:MISTAKELIST]

## Tier 2 — practitioner-anecdote (one case, one company, one memorable outcome)

Real but singular. Use as illustration and hypothesis, never as an effect size.

15. **The price ladder finds willingness to pay by climbing until resistance appears.** The supporting evidence is one company's monthly ladder, and the author states the experiment was confounded by simultaneous product improvement. [FOUND:PRICE-LADDER], [FOUND:CHARGE-NOW], [FOUND:GRANDFATHER-CLOSE]

16. **Feature requests are inputs, not instructions.** Grounded in one case where obeying a request wasted about three months and produced a dashboard reported to be roughly ninety percent irrelevant. Directionally strong, statistically nothing. [FOUND:DIGREQUEST], [FOUND:WHYBOTHER], [FOUND:DREAMSET]

17. **Access is manufactured, not found.** Convening, teaching, publishing and immersion convert you from suspicious approacher to credible host — each supported by a single story, including one where the immersion worked and the segment was then abandoned. [FOUND:HOSTMEETUP], [FOUND:CONVENEELITE], [FOUND:IMMERSE], [FOUND:BLOGCRED], [FOUND:ASKTHEROOM]

18. **Prospect-specific artifacts outperform generic ones.** Custom screenshots, micro-videos and demoing against the prospect's own public context; the response-rate deltas behind this are vendor-reported and not independently verifiable. [FOUND:CUSTOMASSET], [FOUND:CONTEXT-DEMO], [FOUND:ONDEMAND-CUSTOM-CLIP], [FOUND:PROSPECT-IN-THE-SLIDE]

19. **The unasked scary question is the expensive one.** One founder's estimate of a large loss from a single unasked ownership question carries this principle; the estimate is self-reported and unverifiable. [FOUND:SCARYQ], [FOUND:FAILPOINTS], [FOUND:PREMORTQ]

20. **Data-preloaded demos convert.** One company's process, with a self-reported close rate; the named failure mode — an analysis that returns nothing, live — is the reason this stays in Tier 2. [FOUND:PRELOAD-DATA-DEMO], [FOUND:REALDATADEMO]

## Tier 3 — assertion (stated as principle, no case attached)

Reason about these on mechanism only. Several are also the ethically loaded ones, which is not a coincidence: the moves with the least evidence tend to be the ones that substitute pressure for proof.

21. **Inevitability framing moves stalled buyers.** Asserted, never measured, and unfalsifiable at the moment it is made. [FOUND:INEVITABILITY], [FOUND:INEVITABLE], [FOUND:SOFT-NO-DEFLECT]

22. **Manufactured urgency converts drift into signature.** Offered as handy tricks with no outcome data, and explicitly poisonous if the scarcity claim is checkable and false. [FOUND:URGENCY-LEVERS], [FOUND:BUYNOWSTARTLATER]

23. **Implied leverage moves an intermediary.** The most coercive item in the layer, recommended repeatedly but supported only by the mechanism argument; on an unqualified account it is pressure with nothing behind it. [FOUND:CREDTHREAT], [FOUND:GATEHERO], [FOUND:SENDINFO]

24. **Consent shortcuts are treated as normal early-stage practice.** Publicity rights taken through unread terms, auto-renewal as the default state, inferred personal email addresses — each asserted, each jurisdiction- and buyer-dependent. [FOUND:LOGOS-MSA], [FOUND:AUTO-RENEW-DEFAULT], [FOUND:EMAIL-PATTERN-FORM]

25. **Style rules: plain text, peer tone, deliberate send windows, standing calls, energy management.** All asserted from personal habit. Cheap to adopt, but do not let them displace a real diagnosis. [FOUND:PLAINTEXT-ONLY], [FOUND:PEERTONE], [FOUND:SENDWINDOW], [FOUND:PRECALL-ENERGY], [FOUND:PACING]

26. **Scaling arithmetic (win-rate bands, cost-of-sales ceilings, span of control).** Rules of thumb with worked arithmetic and no external benchmark; useful as sanity bounds, invalid as forecasts. [FOUND:WIN-RATE-SCALE-GATE], [FOUND:COST-OF-SALES-MATH], [FOUND:TEAM-LEAD-THEN-LAYER], [FOUND:HIRE-THE-SILVER-MEDALISTS]

27. **Framework claims about the pitch itself.** The three-term model of organisational persuasion is the author's own construct, offered without empirical support; it is a thinking aid, not a measurement. [FOUND:PVCB]

## Where the layer's principles collide with each other

- **[FOUND:BROADFIRST] versus [FOUND:LEADRESEARCH].** One says open on their priorities and zoom only when they volunteer the topic; the other says open by asserting the researched fact and attaching a frustration question. Both are Tier 1. The reconciliation the sources imply: research-led opening is safe only where the signifier is externally verifiable and you are right about it; where you are guessing which problem matters, broad-first wins.
- **[FOUND:LOVEMEH] versus [FOUND:SOFT-NO-DEFLECT].** Accept the lukewarm no and leave, or assert relevance and re-ask. The scope divider is the stage: learning conversations versus a live appointment-setting call. Note the asymmetry of evidence — the accept-and-leave rule is Tier 1, the push-through rule is Tier 3.
- **[FOUND:MOMTEST3] versus [FOUND:DIRECT-ASK].** Do not talk about your idea, versus put the three commercial questions on the table unsoftened. Reconciled by phase: questions govern before a product exists, commitments govern after.
- **[FOUND:GOOD-TIME-GOOD-OPPS] versus enterprise reality.** Truncating marginal opportunities assumes a large addressable set; with a few dozen named accounts it is self-harm.

## The loop this layer actually runs

Diagnose the epoch ([FOUND:STAGE-GATE]) → cut a findable segment ([FOUND:WHOWHERE], [FOUND:ICP-METADATA]) → write the narrative and cost model ([FOUND:PSS-NARRATIVE], [FOUND:COST-MODEL]) → render it into rough collateral ([FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH]) → build the list by hand ([FOUND:NO-BOUGHT-LISTS]) → run the cadence ([FOUND:CADENCE]) → book live ([FOUND:BOOKLIVE]) → discovery before demo ([FOUND:PITCHFORMAT]) → prove in the buyer's own metrics ([FOUND:SAME-METRIC-COMPARE]) → ask ([FOUND:ASKSALE]) → run objections through one loop ([FOUND:OBJLOOP]) → capture the commitment and the next date ([FOUND:MICROCONTRACT]) → implement and report value ([FOUND:IMPL-REDISCOVERY], [FOUND:INSTRUMENT-OUTCOMES]) → only then instrument ratios and hire ([FOUND:FUNNEL-MATH], [FOUND:MATURITY-LADDER]).

Every arrow in that loop is Tier 1 or Tier 2 evidence from one or two practitioners. Treat the loop as a well-worn default, not as a validated causal chain.
