# advisor-founding — moves

Layer: **свои продукты, founder-led** — the founder personally carrying an unknown product from first list to first repeatable revenue. Citation prefix `[FOUND:]`.

Sources: Kazanjy, *Founding Sales* (canon) and Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (second-source). Both are single-practitioner books of the same founder/lean-startup school; where they agree, that is **school echo, not independent confirmation**, and any Evidence line saying "both sources" must be read that way. Evidence ceiling for every move here is `practitioner-n-many`.

Code disambiguation: `STAKEMAP` named two different moves in the two digests; they are kept apart as `STAKEMAP-KAZ` (speak each stakeholder's value language in a live deal) and `STAKEMAP-MOM` (enumerate all affected parties before a discovery round). No other code collided.

## A. Operating stance before any deal exists

### [FOUND:STAGE-GATE] Read and act only to your current stage
**Move:** Diagnose which epoch the go-to-market is in — figuring it out versus scaling it — and refuse work belonging to the later epoch (hiring, specialization, quota design) until narrative, collateral and outreach exist.
**APPLY WHEN:** assembling a sales system from scratch, or being pushed toward org-building before repeatability exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** the motion is already repeatable and the real bottleneck is headcount.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his own build plus patterns from startups he advises.

### [FOUND:CARGO-CULT-CHECK] Refuse borrowed practice you cannot explain
**Move:** Before copying a practice from a mature sales org, state the mechanism by which it produces the outcome in your market at your stage; if you cannot, adapt it or build anew.
**APPLY WHEN:** someone proposes a process because a large company runs it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the practice is cheap and reversible and deriving the theory costs more than trying it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated as principle, no cases attached.

### [FOUND:NO-ABSTRACTION] Keep message-crafting and product decisions in one head
**Move:** The person carrying the value proposition into customer conversations also owns what gets built next; if that is two people, couple them tightly and pull the product founder onto calls.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF, product hypotheses still moving, feature bets placed monthly.
**AVOID WHEN:** post-PMF and scaling, where the founder becomes the bottleneck.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his own pairing at TalentBin, described as workable but imperfect.

### [FOUND:UNSCALABLE-INVEST] Deliberately do things that do not scale
**Move:** Accept per-deal economics that would never survive at scale — travelling to a small account, hand-implementing, hand-sourcing lists — and book the cost as learning rather than as customer acquisition cost.
**APPLY WHEN:** the quarter's goal is information about pain, willingness to pay and fit, not efficiency.
**AVOID WHEN:** scaling against a payback bar, or when the unscalable work yields no new information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: own practice, framed as an extension of Paul Graham's consumer-side argument.

### [FOUND:DEFER-AE-HIRE] Postpone the first AE or VP Sales hire until repeatability
**Move:** Do not hire a sales professional — especially one poached from the incumbent — to establish the motion; keep that résumé for the scaling act and stay the rep yourself until then.
**APPLY WHEN:** narrative still shifting, win/loss reasons ambiguous, no repeatable persona.
**AVOID WHEN:** persona, pitch and objection set are stable and founder calendar capacity is the binding constraint.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: own counterfactual plus the widely repeated first-VP-fired pattern; cites Steve Blank without data.

### [FOUND:GOOD-TIME-GOOD-OPPS] Truncate marginal opportunities on purpose
**Move:** On weak fit, absent budget or a stalling buyer, close the account out and move on, recording the conditions under which it would become viable later; treat time, not opportunity count, as the scarce resource.
**APPLY WHEN:** greenfield territory with many untouched accounts.
**AVOID WHEN:** the addressable set is a few large named accounts, or the marginal account is a lighthouse logo.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: attributed to TalentBin's first AE and used as team doctrine.

### [FOUND:JUMP-PREPARE-MIDAIR] Default to action; cap preparation
**Move:** Set the default state to being on the phone or sending email and treat idleness as the anomaly — one proofread per email, template everything repeated, stop at diminishing returns on any single action.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-volume pipelines with dozens of concurrent conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a single meeting carries the deal — the same book prescribes real pre-call planning for pitches.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: repeated team practice; the supporting maxim is offered as apocryphal.

### [FOUND:DIRECT-ASK] Ask the commercial questions plainly, and ask for the sale
**Move:** Put the three questions on the table unsoftened — do you have this problem, does it need solving, will you spend money on it — then state that the fit is good and name the quantity to buy.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect already spent time with you and that time is treated as evidence of intent.
**AVOID WHEN:** trust must precede commercial framing — notably consulting and services, where the buyer is buying the person; also on learning-purpose evangelical calls.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: assertion plus a film reference, no data.

### [FOUND:SHALLOW-MANY] Run breadth of relationships on a records system, not memory
**Move:** Accept dozens of net-new contacts weekly and a live pipeline of a few dozen to a hundred-plus concurrent conversations; externalise org, pain and rapport details into the CRM and keep low-cost rapport openers ready.
**APPLY WHEN:** volume-driven pipelines.
**AVOID WHEN:** a handful of deep enterprise or consulting relationships is the entire business.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: standing practice on his teams.

### [FOUND:INEVITABILITY] Frame the purchase as "when", not "if"
**Move:** After qualifying fit, run the conversation from the premise that adoption happens — with you or a competitor — and move to timing and implementation rather than justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-PMF, qualified account with confirmed pain.
**AVOID WHEN:** fit is unverified or the buyer is early in problem awareness; it then reads as arrogance and burns credibility. Ethically it is manufactured certainty, so do not deploy it where you could not defend the claim to the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: mechanism argued without outcome data.

### [FOUND:LOSS-AUTOPSY-THEN-DROP] Record the loss cause honestly, then discard the feeling
**Move:** On every loss log the actual reason — timing, budget, competitor, feature gap, your own follow-up failure — share it so teammates do not relearn it, then resume expecting to win the next one.
**APPLY WHEN:** any pipeline where most opportunities close lost.
**AVOID WHEN:** the honest reasons degrade into a picklist that hides the truth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: team practice tied to win-rate reporting.

### [FOUND:FUTURE-YOU-NOTES] Record for the version of you that returns next quarter
**Move:** Take call notes by hand, transfer only the salient points into the CRM, ask what future-you will wish existed when the account resurfaces, then automate capture so it stops depending on discipline.
**APPLY WHEN:** long cycles, recycled closed-lost accounts, many concurrent deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** verbatim transcription — explicitly rejected as waste; capture is selective.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: standing practice on his teams.

### [FOUND:STUDENT-OF-GAME] Build vertical expertise deliberately, competitors' strengths included
**Move:** Absorb the vertical's processes, roles and incumbent tools until you know the domain better than the buyer, credit competitors where they are genuinely good, and drill conversational fearlessness on strangers.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, and especially before you feel ready — "enough" expertise is the threshold for acting.
**AVOID WHEN:** chasing exhaustive knowledge of every alternative; the common few suffice.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: mechanism asserted from his own transition into the vertical.

### [FOUND:GLASS-HOUSE] Instrument every customer-facing interaction and make it visible
**Move:** Record emails, presentations and calls; publish activity levels, win/loss notes, close ratios and error reports; socialise failures alongside wins so both read as real.
**APPLY WHEN:** building a team culture from scratch where the norm can still be set.
**AVOID WHEN:** jurisdictions requiring recording consent, or where transparency arrives as surveillance without the shared-learning half.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: implemented and load-bearing at TalentBin.

### [FOUND:FUNNEL-MATH] Instrument the ratios before you make org decisions
**Move:** Build reporting for emails per demo booked, opportunities per closed deal, value per opportunity, win rate by rep and average contract value, and use ACV to decide whether a sales development hire is affordable.
**APPLY WHEN:** any decision about headcount, channel investment or rep performance.
**AVOID WHEN:** sample sizes are tiny — the ratios are noise on the first handful of deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: operating practice, no dataset shown.

### [FOUND:ADVISORFLIP] Go looking for advisors, not customers
**Move:** Reframe the meeting internally as assessing whether this person would make a good industry advisor; you need not say so aloud, and the switch removes neediness and puts you in the evaluating seat.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-product conversations where the temptation to sell keeps leaking in.
**AVOID WHEN:** real sales meetings with a product to move — the flip is then avoidance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Mom Test: argued from a willpower-as-finite-resource premise, no named source.

## B. Segment, ICP and choosing whom to talk to

### [FOUND:SEGSPEC] Narrow to one buyer before serving all
**Move:** Pick the single sub-group most likely to buy today and commit to it, dropping features and messaging aimed at the others until that group is served; broaden back out later and deliberately.
**APPLY WHEN:** feedback across discovery calls contradicts itself and every feature is somebody's favourite.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already have consistent problem and goal signal — further slicing just shrinks reachable volume; not a substitute for a real ICP once revenue data exists.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: the author's own advertising startup, where a broad segment made every experiment unfalsifiable.

### [FOUND:SEGMIX] Mixed-feedback diagnostic
**Move:** When interviews produce inconsistent must-have features and must-solve problems, treat it as a segmentation defect rather than a research defect — stop iterating on question quality and re-cut the segment.
**APPLY WHEN:** question technique is already sound yet the picture stays incoherent.
**AVOID WHEN:** the inconsistency traces to talking to non-buyers or wrong stakeholders — fix who you are talking to first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: recurring pattern across founders the author coached.

### [FOUND:TAXEXP] Borrow an industry taxonomy from an expert
**Move:** When you cannot tell which sub-segments exist, spend a conversation with an industry insider purely to get the category map, then choose a starting cell from that map.
**APPLY WHEN:** entering an unfamiliar vertical where your segment names are outsider labels.
**AVOID WHEN:** no such access exists — then guess and slice; and never let the expert's taxonomy substitute for talking to actual buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — Mom Test: recommended without a supporting case.

### [FOUND:SLICEQ] Customer-slicing question ladder
**Move:** Run a fixed sequence over the segment — who inside wants it most, does everyone or only some buy, what problem or goal drives them, is that motivation shared, what other motivations exist, who else has them — then repeat on whichever outputs are still generic.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF, choosing whom to interview next, or re-cutting a segment after mixed feedback.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes an all-day theorising exercise instead of contacting people.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: the author's standard workshop drill with a worked example.

### [FOUND:WHOWHERE] Who-where pairs as the segment definition
**Move:** Refuse to call a segment finished until each entry names both a group and a concrete place to find them, and keep slicing until the "where" is answerable.
**APPLY WHEN:** planning outreach for discovery, especially when the plan amounts to walking around and seeing who you meet.
**AVOID WHEN:** findability is being optimised at the expense of buying power — an easy-to-reach group with no budget still fails.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: stated as a rule of thumb and demonstrated across several segment examples.

### [FOUND:WORKARND] Target existing coping behaviour, not just demographics
**Move:** List what the group already does to survive the problem — searching, buying books, workshops, on-the-job training — and reach them at those behaviours: their search terms, their podcast, their meetup.
**APPLY WHEN:** the demographic exists but is diffuse and you need warm, self-selected conversation volume.
**AVOID WHEN:** people who ignore the problem entirely are in scope; behaviour-based targeting deliberately excludes them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas/info-product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: the author's own choice of a public-speaking meetup as an entry point.

### [FOUND:SEG3CRIT] Three-criteria pick among candidate segments
**Move:** Rank surviving who-where pairs on profitable, easy to reach and personally rewarding to serve, and start with the top one; treat the rewarding criterion as real, since discovery is a grind if you dislike the industry.
**APPLY WHEN:** multiple viable slices and no data to separate them.
**AVOID WHEN:** revenue data already exists — let actual buying behaviour choose instead of preference.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: the author's standing selection heuristic.

### [FOUND:SHELFTEST] Commitment-shaped segment test with a channel partner
**Move:** Instead of asking a channel partner for opinions, ask for a small concrete resource inside the narrowest viable slice of that channel, return a week later to debrief what actually happened, and use the outcome to open access to end customers.
**APPLY WHEN:** physical or channel-mediated products where the partner is a gatekeeper and no product data exists yet.
**AVOID WHEN:** direct-sale motions with no gatekeeper, or when the partner's incentives make a yes meaningless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel/founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=few · offer=product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a single condiment-founder case.

### [FOUND:STAKEMAP-MOM] Enumerate every affected party before interviewing
**Move:** Before a discovery round, write out all sides of the transaction — end user, payer, approver, blocker, dependency partner — and check each has been talked to; treat an unmapped party as a known gap rather than an unknown.
**APPLY WHEN:** B2B with a real buying process, marketplaces, or products bought by one party and used by another.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuinely single-actor consumer purchase, where mapping adds no information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: presented as a recurring failure category with several structural examples. Distinct from STAKEMAP-KAZ, which is about register, not coverage.

### [FOUND:USERNOTVIP] Interview the representative user, not the impressive title
**Move:** Deliberately allocate discovery time to the actual end user even when that population is unglamorous, and resist substituting senior contacts because they read better in reporting.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer and user differ, or your interview log is dominated by executives.
**AVOID WHEN:** the decision genuinely sits with the senior party and usage is irrelevant — user interviews then inform product but not the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: the author's ad product, where he interviewed many buyers and none of the assumed end users.

### [FOUND:ICP-METADATA] Compress the narrative into queryable characteristics
**Move:** Restate the ICP as a sentence of concrete searchable metadata, separating hard prerequisites from demand-magnitude signals and ability-to-pay signals such as existing spend on an incumbent.
**APPLY WHEN:** before any outbound, and as a qualification filter for inbound and referrals.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have too few real customers to know which characteristics matter — expect to revise after the first cohort.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: two worked company examples plus iteration.

### [FOUND:NO-RELATIONSHIP-TARGETING] Refuse relationship-based targeting
**Move:** Do not prioritise prospects because they are reachable; when an acquaintance says they could use it, run the qualifying questions and be willing to decline.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, and especially when fear of cold rejection is steering the list.
**AVOID WHEN:** a known contact genuinely matches the ICP — the rule is about need, not about excluding acquaintances.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his framing plus an illustrative cost calculation.

### [FOUND:DEMAND-SCORE] Score accounts by demand magnitude, not just fit
**Move:** Beyond minimum viable criteria, build an attractiveness score from combined signals — potential seats, volume of the painful activity, sophistication, ability to pay, recent funding — and rank the list so the highest-pain accounts get your hours.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have more qualified accounts than selling hours.
**AVOID WHEN:** the score's inputs are unverified guesses; it is then false precision.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: TalentBin's scoring dimensions and minimum criteria.

### [FOUND:HUNT-DEER] Target mid-sized accounts, skewing to the bigger ones
**Move:** Prefer accounts large enough to carry real pain and absorb new tooling but small enough to decide without heavy change management, and within that band favour the ones with most pain per unit of size.
**APPLY WHEN:** choosing the initial 50–100 target list.
**AVOID WHEN:** your product only works at enterprise scale, or is inherently self-serve for very small accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: the rabbits/deer/elephants framing with two company examples.

### [FOUND:GEO-LOCAL-FIRST] Start in your own geography
**Move:** Build the first list locally even if deal size implies inside sales; same time zone plus the option of an on-site visit is worth a lot early, and failing to find 50–100 matching accounts locally is itself a signal.
**APPLY WHEN:** first cohort of prospects.
**AVOID WHEN:** the ICP is highly specialised and geographically dispersed by nature.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated preference with a heuristic attached.

### [FOUND:SOURCE-ENTRY-CHOICE] Enter sourcing through your hardest gate
**Move:** Start from whichever characteristic is the absolute prerequisite — a required role means title search rolled up to employer, a required tech stack means technology detection then sizing by title — then flip to the other data type to append demand signifiers.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting up the sourcing workflow.
**AVOID WHEN:** neither characteristic is externally visible; discovery then carries the qualification load.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: two contrasting company sourcing setups.

### [FOUND:PROXY-SIGNALS] Use correlated proxies and paid-tier tells
**Move:** Where the true demand signal is invisible, source on correlates — technology detected on the site, a premium marketplace profile, current or historical job postings — and treat payment for an adjacent service as evidence of willingness to pay.
**APPLY WHEN:** the definitive characteristic is private.
**AVOID WHEN:** the correlation is weak — you then fill the funnel with plausible non-buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: multiple worked sourcing examples; named tools date fast.

### [FOUND:HIDDEN-CHAR-ASK] Keep unobservable characteristics in the ICP and ask for them
**Move:** Do not drop a decisive characteristic because it is not public; source on its correlates, then confirm it explicitly through a discovery question or the inbound capture form.
**APPLY WHEN:** the characteristic strongly predicts success but is not published.
**AVOID WHEN:** asking early would read as interrogation before you earned the conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: TalentBin's incumbent-seat characteristic.

### [FOUND:ONE-SOURCE-DISCIPLINE] Source from one channel; multi-source only to enrich
**Move:** If one source reliably yields enough good accounts, stay on it; use several sources to enrich an already-found account, not to find accounts.
**APPLY WHEN:** a working source exists with sufficient volume.
**AVOID WHEN:** the source is genuinely exhausted or systematically biased against part of your market.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated as discipline, no data.

### [FOUND:NO-BOUGHT-LISTS] Build the first list manually
**Move:** Assemble the first hundred targets by hand; bought lists are stale and thinly modelled, and the manual work teaches you which characteristics actually matter.
**APPLY WHEN:** first cohort, and whenever you are re-tuning the ICP.
**AVOID WHEN:** later scale, where enrichment vendors and refresh services earn their keep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his stated dim view plus the learning-value argument.

### [FOUND:SHEET-SCHEMA] Structure prospect metadata into merge-ready columns
**Move:** Keep prospects in a sheet with a distinct column for every fact you might later query or merge on — the rating, the profile link, the specific damning review — stored as fields rather than prose so outreach can carry account-specific evidence.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the initial 50–100 list and wanting automation leverage without generic-blast tone.
**AVOID WHEN:** the signifier data is stale or unverified — a wrong merged number destroys the researched effect entirely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: a mail-merge example plus the practice behind his cited response-rate deltas.

### [FOUND:CASCADE-CONTACTS] Capture the full cascade of decision-maker titles
**Move:** Rather than betting on one title, collect every plausible decision-maker in the hierarchy with a title search scoped to the account, since which title owns the problem varies with company stage; capture users later for drip.
**APPLY WHEN:** you do not yet know which title owns the budget in this segment.
**AVOID WHEN:** blasting all of them simultaneously — that is what makes you look like spam internally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: stated preference plus a sample query.

### [FOUND:COMPLEMENTARY-DM] Work the complementary decision-maker
**Move:** Identify the internal customer of your primary decision-maker — the one carrying the downstream pain — and either get referred inward through them or convince them and make the case jointly.
**APPLY WHEN:** the primary contact is unreachable or unmotivated and the downstream pain is acute.
**AVOID WHEN:** going around a champion would embarrass them internally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: reasoning without cited outcomes.

### [FOUND:CEO-TOPDOWN-CAUTION] Top-down CEO outreach, used with eyes open
**Move:** The received pattern emails the CEO a crisp dollars-and-cents case and asks to be pointed at the right delegate; prefer instead to identify and appeal to the likely decision-maker directly, cascading upward only if that fails.
**APPLY WHEN:** you cannot determine the owner, or the org is small enough that the CEO is that owner.
**AVOID WHEN:** senior executives in your market run assistant-mediated inbox triage; the tactic's advantage was largely an artifact of an earlier era.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: attributed to Ross and Tyler with his counterargument; no data either way.

### [FOUND:BOTTOM-UP-DEFER] Bottom-up user-led entry, deferred
**Move:** Convince individual users the product improves their working life and enlist them to argue upward to budget holders, with free-tier signups as the account signal that triggers a sale into the organisation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product is usable standalone by an individual or small team and later expands.
**AVOID WHEN:** early founder-led selling — the book explicitly defers this as a later, scaled motion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: well-known companies cited as patterns, not measured.

### [FOUND:WARM-PATH-AFTER-QUALIFY] Check for an intro path only after the account qualifies
**Move:** Once the account passes ICP and the right person is identified, then look for a shared connection or an internal forwarder, recorded as a potential-intros column — the deliberate inverse of choosing accounts because you know someone.
**APPLY WHEN:** the account qualifies and you are deciding the first touch.
**AVOID WHEN:** the connection is a nominal social-graph link with no real relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his stated sequencing.

### [FOUND:EMAIL-PATTERN-FORM] Find or form the email address yourself
**Move:** Search first, then infer the company's address pattern from a known employee address, permute candidates and validate which resolves; do it manually early, because unobtainable decision-maker emails are a go-to-market problem you want to discover now.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the first outreach list yourself.
**AVOID WHEN:** local privacy and anti-spam law makes unsolicited mail to inferred or personal addresses a legal or reputational risk — the book waves this off, which is jurisdiction-dependent, not settled.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his method; named validation tools are dated.

### [FOUND:PHONE-FOR-LOCAL] Phone-first for local businesses
**Move:** Default to email before phone, but invert it for local and SMB go-to-markets where decision-maker email is hard to obtain — capture direct dials and the switchboard and reach out by phone.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling into restaurants, clinics, trades and similar local businesses.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a corporate knowledge worker reachable by email.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional

### [FOUND:EARLYCRM] Minimum viable system of record

## C. Narrative, value model and pricing design

### [FOUND:PSS-NARRATIVE] Build the seven-part narrative before any collateral
**Move:** Write answers to what the problem is, who has it, what it costs hard and soft, how it is solved today and why that fails, what changed, how your solution works, and how you know it is better quantitatively and qualitatively; treat it as a living hypothesis the market rewrites.
**APPLY WHEN:** a new or disruptive offering entering a market for the first time.
**AVOID WHEN:** commodity replacement where the problem is universally acknowledged — the "what changed" beat then adds nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his framework, illustrated with his own and reconstructed public examples.

### [FOUND:INDUCTIVE-EXIT] Use narrative order as a qualification gate
**Move:** Present the problem first, in conversation and as the deck's opening section, and check agreement before advancing; if the prospect denies the problem, dig into the disagreement or end the meeting, treating an early mismatch as a win.
**APPLY WHEN:** early calls and first presentations; volume pipelines where time is the constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already declared the pain and expects depth, or low problem awareness makes a premature exit kill an educable deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: the operating logic of both his deck and his call.

### [FOUND:PROBLEM-STATEMENT-TEST] Validate the problem statement on a live insider
**Move:** Say the problem statement to someone inside the target industry and ask whether they have hit it; passing means not just yes, but that they can immediately continue in detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** drafting or revising the narrative.
**AVOID WHEN:** the insider is a friend with an incentive to agree — the test's value is their willingness to say no.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: heuristic without cases. Compare MOMTEST3, which supplies the question hygiene this test assumes.

### [FOUND:BUDGET-OR-HOURS-TARGET] Target the owner of the budget or of the hours
**Move:** Identify the person functionally responsible — controls the budget for the pain, or personally spends daily hours on it; if they can say it is not really their job, it is the wrong person, and expect the right title to shift as the org grows.
**APPLY WHEN:** building an ICP, choosing outreach titles, or checking whether a champion can act.
**AVOID WHEN:** very small orgs where roles collapse into the founder — such accounts are often unqualified anyway.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: worked through for several buyer profiles.

### [FOUND:COST-MODEL] Quantify the cost of the problem in per-unit maths, then scale it
**Move:** Compute hard ROI and soft or opportunity ROI, attach a metric the prospect can apply to themselves, and roll it into a running monthly cost of doing nothing; treat vague agility claims as the weakest tier.
**APPLY WHEN:** pricing, qualification, deck-building, and whenever the buyer accepts the problem but shows no urgency.
**AVOID WHEN:** the causal chain is long and hypothetical or you cannot defend the inputs — invented multipliers torch credibility for the rest of the deck.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: illustrated across storage, support, CRM and sales-automation categories.

### [FOUND:INCUMBENT-LADDER] Classify how the account solves it today
**Move:** Place each account on the ladder of nothing, manual process, service provider, competing product, and pick the attack: prove the problem is worth solving, attack time cost and process frailty, attack margin and fee, or prepare for direct comparison.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery, qualification, and deciding which accounts to prioritise.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot credibly name the incumbent's real strengths — credibility requires it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: derived from one competitive landscape and generalised.

### [FOUND:WHAT-CHANGED] Name the enabling change and check the trend direction
**Move:** Identify the specific shift that made your solution newly possible or necessary and check whether it is accelerating; if it is, it reinforces the narrative over time.
**APPLY WHEN:** pitching innovative categories where the buyer needs to believe the "why now".
**AVOID WHEN:** the trend is reversing or contested — it then undermines the argument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: several industry examples at mechanism level.

### [FOUND:SAME-METRIC-COMPARE] Prove superiority in the metrics the buyer already uses
**Move:** Beat existing solutions in the buyer's own language, demonstrated live where possible; break the top-level metric into workflow components and prove each, attaching a countable proxy where the comparison is qualitative.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive deals and demos where the buyer has an incumbent tool.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would lose the head-to-head on that metric — reframe instead of competing on it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: standing demo practice against two named incumbent categories.

### [FOUND:VANITY-METRIC-REFRAME] Redirect from the competitor's flattering metric
**Move:** When a rival cites scale that does not map to the buyer's job, reframe to the metric that matters for this buyer's narrow need.
**APPLY WHEN:** the incumbent's headline number is bigger than yours but less relevant.
**AVOID WHEN:** the metric genuinely is decision-relevant — reframing then reads as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one worked example from talent acquisition.

### [FOUND:THIRD-PARTY-PROOF] Stack external validation as a separate "who agrees" layer
**Move:** Assemble customer counts, testimonials restating your own metric claims, case studies, press and analyst coverage, deployed as supplement to the argument and never as the argument.
**APPLY WHEN:** there is a credibility gap between a small vendor and an established incumbent.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no narrative yet — social proof over an incoherent story is called worthless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: listed as options without outcome evidence.

### [FOUND:CHARGE-NOW] Charge from the first conversations, even at a token price
**Move:** Set an initial price and ask for it rather than deferring monetisation; free pilots are allowed for a few lighthouse accounts paying in structured feedback, but the default is a real price.
**APPLY WHEN:** any early B2B motion — unpaid users do not evaluate seriously and often do not even use the product.
**AVOID WHEN:** the named exception of a small lighthouse set whose engaged feedback is the consideration.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his practice; the diagnosis that founders defer pricing to avoid hearing no is asserted.

### [FOUND:PRICE-LADDER] Start under-priced and ratchet up until resistance appears
**Move:** Deliberately give away more value than you capture at first to protect early close rates, raise the ask on each successive batch of conversations, and stop climbing when price visibly drags on close rates.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF and early PMF, where willingness-to-pay information is the objective.
**AVOID WHEN:** contracts are long and reference-priced, or the buyer set is small enough that early prices become permanent market anchors.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: the $99→$499 monthly ladder, confounded by simultaneous product improvement, as he notes himself.

### [FOUND:GRANDFATHER-CLOSE] Grandfather early customers and use it as a buy-now lever
**Move:** When raising prices, leave early customers at their original rate and tell active prospects that today's price locks in if they commit now.
**APPLY WHEN:** an announced or genuinely expected price increase, or deals stalled on timing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the increase is not real — a fabricated deadline is manipulation the book's own consultative framing does not support.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: described as his company's practice.

### [FOUND:COMPARATOR-PRICE] Anchor price and terms on the incumbent the buyer already buys
**Move:** Find products the buyer already pays for in this budget line, copy their pricing unit and commercial terms because the market is trained on them, then position your price against that anchor with a computable value gap.
**APPLY WHEN:** an established comparator exists and the buyer has a mental price for the category.
**AVOID WHEN:** no comparator exists, or the incumbent's model actively misprices your value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: per-seat annual pricing mirroring two named incumbents. Compare CURRENTSPEND, the discovery-side version of the same anchor.

### [FOUND:ONE-INNOVATION] Do not innovate on pricing model and product at once
**Move:** If the product is the novel thing, adopt the category's conventional pricing structure and assume the incumbent's model encodes reasons you have not discovered.
**APPLY WHEN:** a technically differentiated product inside an existing budget category.
**AVOID WHEN:** the pricing model is the wedge — then hold product novelty constant instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated as preference, no supporting cases.

### [FOUND:ROI-PRICE] Price off a defensible ROI model, not off realised value
**Move:** Estimate value delivered per unit of usage from your own data and set a fixed price capturing a fraction of it; do not charge a percentage of value actually recovered, since instrumenting it is hard and it rewards hiding captured value.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have enough data to model value per unit, or can run experiments to establish it.
**AVOID WHEN:** too early for a real ROI case — blend with comparator pricing instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: one company's model described from its own submission dataset.

### [FOUND:THRESHOLD-ENTRY] Price under a purchasing threshold so someone can just buy it
**Move:** Check what an individual can expense, a manager can put on a card, or a department can cover discretionarily, set entry pricing under one of those lines, and pair it with value-aligned metering.
**APPLY WHEN:** land-and-expand, trial or freemium motions, self-service-adjacent SaaS.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product is inherently org-wide and cannot be bought by one team.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: several well-known companies cited as observed patterns.

### [FOUND:ELEVATOR-DRILL] Rehearse the narrative as an elevator pitch, then expand on demand
**Move:** Compress the whole narrative into a version for someone who knows the space, deliver it, and let follow-up questions dictate which section you zoom into; write the long form once to check every component exists.
**APPLY WHEN:** the narrative is drafted and needs stress-testing before collateral is built.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating the written version as a script to recite verbatim.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: offered as an exercise.

## D. Collateral construction

### [FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH] Ship rough collateral immediately
**Move:** Prefer ten five-minute laptop screencasts with verbal stumbles over one polished explainer, build the objection slide the day you hear the objection, and never let design or approval cycles gate a new asset.
**APPLY WHEN:** early stage with product and messaging still moving.
**AVOID WHEN:** regulated categories with genuine legal review; note that polish over an empty narrative is called worse than nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: standing practice; the feedback-loop mechanism is argued, not measured.

### [FOUND:MVP-DECK] Start with one slide per narrative step, then zoom in
**Move:** Map the deck directly onto the narrative — problem, who has it, costs, existing solutions and failures, what changed, how it works, proof, price — one bulleted slide each, splitting sections later while keeping the summary versions.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the first deck, or extending one under time pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** deleting the coarse versions as you elaborate — you lose the ability to present at the right altitude.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his deck evolution described concretely.

### [FOUND:MASTER-DECK] Keep one canonical master deck and fork presentation copies
**Move:** Maintain a single large master file holding every slide ever built, refactored continuously, and fork a copy for each actual use so nothing is destroyed and the canonical source cannot be hacked by individual sellers.
**APPLY WHEN:** collateral volume is growing and more than one person presents.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication stated; the author calls it an inelegant stopgap in the absence of version control for presentations.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his own file, maintained from company inception.

### [FOUND:TEMPLATE-THEN-DESIGNER] Reach production value through templates, hire design after MVP
**Move:** Reuse the fundraising deck's theme, define reusable slide templates and clone them, add cheap polish, and only then pay a freelance designer to glaze the templates so future slides inherit it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deck exists and is in use and polish is now a marginal improvement.
**AVOID WHEN:** design becomes a gate on producing a new slide — explicitly forbidden.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his build sequence.

### [FOUND:PROSPECT-IN-THE-SLIDE] Drop the prospect's own data into the deck
**Move:** Template a few slides so prospect-specific material pastes in before the call — your product overlaid on their site, third-party evidence of their pain, a live search for their company.
**APPLY WHEN:** prospect-specific evidence is cheap to gather in pre-call planning.
**AVOID WHEN:** the customisation surfaces something embarrassing you cannot handle, or per-deal prep is uneconomic at your ACV.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one named startup he worked with.

### [FOUND:PROBLEM-VALIDATORS] Third-party validation of the problem
**Move:** Alongside your own problem statement, show outside corroboration — analyst statistics, press, market data — so the prospect sees that others already agree the problem is real; anecdotal slides are an acceptable substitute when hard data is missing.
**APPLY WHEN:** first presentation to a buyer who has not named the pain, or a new vertical where your credibility is thin.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already opened with the pain in their own words.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one vertical deck.

### [FOUND:ALT-BUCKET] Group the incumbents into buckets on one slide
**Move:** Put the pervasive market-standard alternatives into a few labelled buckets with a one-line summary of each bucket's shortcoming, so you control the framing of how you differ.
**APPLY WHEN:** a small set of near-universal solutions dominates and the prospect almost certainly runs one.
**AVOID WHEN:** discovery shows they do not use those standards — skip the slides and do not raise competitors the prospect has not raised.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

### [FOUND:ALT-ROOTCAUSE] Attack the dominant incumbent by value bucket, with existence proofs
**Move:** For the alternative most buyers own, build a chain: name the root cause of its weakness, show a concrete failure instance, extend it to scale, and tie each weakness to metrics the buyer tracks.
**APPLY WHEN:** a large share of prospects hold the incumbent and you can demonstrate the failure live.
**AVOID WHEN:** the incumbent is also a channel or partner, or the weakness is generic rather than tied to their metrics.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: repeated across three of his segments.

### [FOUND:WHATCHANGED-CHECK] "What changed" slide as a comprehension checkpoint
**Move:** Include a short market-timeline section and use it to verify the prospect actually follows — if they know the history, acknowledge and move fast; if not, slow down and level them before continuing.
**APPLY WHEN:** your category is new enough that a buyer can silently lose the thread and then politely disappear.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is an expert practitioner — a history lesson insults them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise

### [FOUND:CONCEPT-DIAGRAM] Conceptual "how it works" diagram before any feature
**Move:** Show one visual of the underlying mechanism — how data is aggregated, how money flows between parties — before pain-by-pain solutions, anchored against a product they already understand.
**APPLY WHEN:** your value depends on a non-obvious mechanism, or multiple parties sit in the flow.
**AVOID WHEN:** the mechanism is trivial — the diagram then reads as padding.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise

### [FOUND:QUANT-PROOF] Superiority proof bucketed by value proposition

**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer has agreed the problem is costly and is deciding where to spend attention.
**AVOID WHEN:** your numbers come from a rigged internal benchmark you cannot reproduce live in the demo.

### [FOUND:QUAL-PROOF] Ghostwrite customer testimonials for early customers

### [FOUND:LOGOS-MSA] Logo slide, with publicity rights taken through the MSA

## E. Access, warm paths and lead multiplication

### [FOUND:COLDSTART] Bootstrap with cold outreach and ignore the rejection rate
**Move:** Hustle the first one or two conversations from any channel available, treat mass non-response as meaningless, and convert each accepted conversation into introductions by being respectful of their time and genuinely useful.
**APPLY WHEN:** no network in the target industry; starting from zero.
**AVOID WHEN:** judging demand by cold-response rate — it measures channel tolerance, not problem severity.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a team reaching C-level executives at major retailers after near-universal silence.

### [FOUND:GOODEXCUSE] Manufacture a legitimate excuse to talk
**Move:** Find a pretext that is true and flattering to them — praising the business and asking about its backstory, or genuine academic research — and use it to reach staff, then the manager, then the owner, without mentioning your idea.
**APPLY WHEN:** local and SMB segments where owners are physically reachable and gatekeepers refuse abstract requests.
**AVOID WHEN:** the pretext is false — the technique depends on genuinely caring about the topic, and a fabricated one is deception.

### [FOUND:CASHFAVOURS] Call in the empty offers of help

### [FOUND:WHOELSE] Close every conversation with a referral ask

**AVOID WHEN:** stakeholder time is scarcer than pipeline — use the lighter profile-by-profile method instead.

## F. Outbound email and sequencing

### [FOUND:COLD-PAIN-SPECIFIC] Pain-specific cold email with an explicit ask
**Move:** Write from the recipient's point of view: name the specific pain you know they have, give two or three concrete superiority claims with numbers, and close with a direct ask for a 20–30 minute slot plus alternate reply paths.
**APPLY WHEN:** first-touch outbound to a prospect you actually pre-qualified.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot evidence the pain — the specificity then reads as fake personalisation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: templates run at company scale.

### [FOUND:SUBJ-QUALIFY] Subject line that proves you did the research
**Move:** Customise the subject so it carries qualification evidence — a signal that the message was built for this recipient rather than blasted.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outbound where the open is the bottleneck.
**AVOID WHEN:** the evidence is a merge field that will render wrong or stale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: sample subject lines from two companies.

### [FOUND:CLICK-TARGETS] Plant instrumented click targets inside the email
**Move:** Deliberately embed one or two things worth clicking — a hyperlinked video thumbnail, an infographic, a custom screenshot — both to persuade and to generate the interest signal you will act on.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have tracking in place and collateral that actually explains the value.
**AVOID WHEN:** heavy link use trips spam filters, or the recipient's environment strips tracking and makes the signal meaningless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: stated preference carried over from his materials chapter.

### [FOUND:PLAINTEXT-ONLY] Keep templates plain text
**Move:** Strip marketing design — no logos or HTML chrome, text only, with screenshots or slides as the sole permitted visuals — because designed email signals a robot blast.
**APPLY WHEN:** all founder-led outbound.
**AVOID WHEN:** a brand-driven marketing nurture stream where design is expected.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated as a rule without data.

### [FOUND:DRIP-SPLIT] Chop the narrative into a one-thought drip series
**Move:** Design templates from the start as a cadence of short standalone messages, each carrying a single idea plus the same call to action: headline ROI number, then one email per value bucket, then a customer-success email.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outbound to busy decision-makers where a single touch rarely converts and you have enough distinct content to fill the slots.
**AVOID WHEN:** one thin idea stretched across seven emails — it reads as nagging; or when the buyer has explicitly declined.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his bucket-by-bucket series plus vendor campaign data he cites.

### [FOUND:CUSTOMASSET] Prospect-specific screenshot or micro-video as the click target
**Move:** Record a short screen capture or annotate a screenshot built around the prospect's own site or data, narrate it as if speaking to them, and embed it as the click target in the first email.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-value named accounts where five minutes of investment is affordable, at first touch.
**AVOID WHEN:** long-tail lists where per-prospect minutes do not pay back, or on every message in the sequence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: tooling preference tied to personalisation response data.

### [FOUND:FOUNDERFRAME] Founder-as-researcher framing
**Move:** Ask for the meeting as a founder trying to understand how they handle the problem area, using the title as the door-opener, while keeping an explicit commercial thread so they know a solution and a price sit behind the question.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF, first customer conversations, unknown brand.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect starts treating it as free advice or pure user research; also weaker once a sales team exists and "just learning" is no longer credible.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: presented as the founder's structural advantage over professional appointment setters. Compare VFWPA, the Mom Test's version, which drops the commercial thread deliberately.

### [FOUND:PEERTONE] Peer-to-peer plain-text tone
**Move:** Write as one executive to another — calm, text-based, no exclamation stacking, no bombastic claims — with the focus on their situation.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outreach to functional leaders.
**AVOID WHEN:** the recipient's category genuinely expects marketing-formatted email.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: style rule without supporting data.

### [FOUND:PIXELREAD] Read the open and click telemetry
**Move:** Instrument every outbound message and interpret the pattern — repeat opens as continued thinking, opens from new locations as internal forwarding, clicks on video links as real interest — and use those as the trigger list for the next touch.
**APPLY WHEN:** any outbound sequence where you control the sending tool.
**AVOID WHEN:** recipients block images by default, which makes silence unreadable; never treat an open as intent on its own. Pixel-blocking has degraded this signal since the book was written.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: mechanism described from repeated use of tracking tooling.

### [FOUND:SINGLESERVE] Single-serving drip: hand-built first touch, automated leverage
**Move:** Invest roughly five minutes hand-personalising only the first email, then let the drip tool reply into that same thread so each follow-up pulls the prospect back to the personalised message.
**APPLY WHEN:** named-account outbound pre-qualified by demand signifiers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the tool starts new threads instead of replying, or the first email's personalisation is fake-personal and will not survive scrutiny.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: vendor-reported campaign comparisons he cites, not independently verifiable.

### [FOUND:SOCIALHOOK] Social-context opener
**Move:** Before the first touch, pull one non-business hook — shared employer, shared connection, alma mater, a recent post — and use it to open on top of the business-pain argument.
**APPLY WHEN:** the hook is genuine, recent and light.
**AVOID WHEN:** politics, religion, or anything readable as surveillance; also when the hook substitutes for a pain-based reason to talk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: standard prospecting hygiene with profile links captured per contact.

### [FOUND:SENDWINDOW] Send-time selection
**Move:** Fire campaigns into low-traffic inbox windows — mid-morning after triage, very early, or evening — and avoid the Monday-morning inbox purge.
**APPLY WHEN:** sending to office-based decision-makers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's day cycle differs — shift work, hospitality, field roles — in which case derive the window from their rhythm.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: heuristic timing advice without data.

### [FOUND:BREAKUP] Terminal "I will stop emailing" message
**Move:** End the sequence with a message stating you will stop emailing, paired with a plain statement of conviction that this is relevant to them, removing the assumption that they can always respond later.
**APPLY WHEN:** after roughly five touches, when sequence response rates decay.
**AVOID WHEN:** you intend to keep emailing anyway — the move only works if it is true; also weak on accounts you must nurture long-term.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: tied to the sequence-position response curve he cites.

### [FOUND:CADENCE] Interleaved multi-channel cadence
**Move:** Run a fixed alternating rhythm — email plus call, call, call plus voicemail, pause, email, breakup — by hand or through cadence tooling, letting the system force the activity rather than memory.
**APPLY WHEN:** any cold list, especially when you personally dislike follow-up.
**AVOID WHEN:** content runs out — cadence length should track how much genuinely new material you have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: the operational core of his appointment setting.

## G. Calling, appointment setting and first-touch objections

### [FOUND:CALL-BULLETS] Guidepost bullets, not a read-aloud script
**Move:** Build a 30–90 second bulleted reformatting of the core narrative that a founder can improvise around, with more content than any one call will use, distinct from the rigid script needed once you have ten-plus reps.
**APPLY WHEN:** founder or first market-development person making calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have a large rep team needing consistency and QA — that is a different artifact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: two company scripts reproduced as examples.

### [FOUND:NUMBER-OPENER] Open with a concrete found-money number
**Move:** State in the first breath what you do and a hard result across a known customer base, then ask for the meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** your product recovers or protects measurable money and you have real aggregate numbers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the number is unaudited or not comparable to this prospect's situation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: one company's figure spanning 120 customers, self-reported.

### [FOUND:CATEGORY-QUESTION] Diagnostic question that qualifies and educates
**Move:** After naming the problem you solve, ask whether they are familiar with it or have experienced it; the answer both qualifies them and bridges to proposing the demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** the problem has a name the buyer may or may not know.
**AVOID WHEN:** the question is rhetorical bait rather than a real qualification check.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one cold-call script.

### [FOUND:DEFER-TO-DEMO] Defer detail questions, then re-ask for the slot
**Move:** On a feature or price question during appointment setting, acknowledge it, note it is best covered in the short prepared session, add a one-line partial answer where fair, then immediately offer two named days.
**APPLY WHEN:** the caller's job is booking, not closing, and the question would consume the call.
**AVOID WHEN:** a single blocking question genuinely decides fit — answer it honestly; repeated deflection of a direct pricing question reads as evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: call-branch script for a recurring pattern.

### [FOUND:SOFT-NO-DEFLECT] Handling a reflexive "not interested"
**Move:** Answer a reflexive refusal by asserting you would not be calling if you did not believe it would help them on a named metric, argue the technology is becoming standard, then re-ask for the demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** the refusal is reflexive and arrives before the prospect has heard any value.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect has understood and declined. The inevitability framing is manufactured urgency — unfalsifiable when made, and pushing it after a considered no is manipulation, not persistence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: script text with no effectiveness data; the supporting essay is his own blog.

### [FOUND:REJECTMAIL] Templated post-rejection email into nurture
**Move:** After a rejected call, accept that the timing is wrong and send a templated follow-up blending the value argument with metrics and your retreat language, promise materials, still request a slot, and hand the prospect to the drip for a check-back in about a month.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after a rejected live call where the objection is timing, not fit.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect asked not to be contacted, or fails your ICP — it is then noise and in some jurisdictions a compliance problem.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: reports frequent reversals from this follow-up.

### [FOUND:INCUMBENT] Competitor familiarity as self-qualification
**Move:** Treat a named competitor as partial qualification — they own the problem and pay to solve it — acknowledge the incumbent's standing genuinely, position on a differentiating axis with one or two quantified differentiators, and use their knowledge as the reason a demo completes the picture.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect names or volunteers a competitor on a cold call.
**AVOID WHEN:** your differentiation is marketing superlatives you cannot demonstrate — a knowledgeable buyer will call it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one worked head-to-head response.

### [FOUND:VM-AS-AUDIO-EMAIL] Voicemail as audio email, sent in pairs
**Move:** Leave a short templated message — research signal, size of pain, why a conversation helps, that you are the founder, number repeated twice — and send the matching email simultaneously, expecting the reply by email rather than callback.
**APPLY WHEN:** every non-connect on a targeted account, given low connect rates.
**AVOID WHEN:** you expect callbacks to be the primary response; and avoid trick voicemails implying a returned call, which the book itself calls unnecessary and shabby.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: the default mechanic observed across his appointment-setting practice.

### [FOUND:DAYCYCLE] Call inside the buyer's day cycle
**Move:** Derive call windows from how the target actually works — early and late for office workers, the mid-afternoon lull for restaurants — and follow the productive hour across time zones.
**APPLY WHEN:** any volume dialling against a homogeneous profile.
**AVOID WHEN:** calling into operational peak hours, which actively damages the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: repeated field practice across two motion types.

### [FOUND:AFTERHOURS] Late dial to bypass the gatekeeper
**Move:** Call senior executives after normal hours, when assistants have left and the executive may pick up their own line.
**APPLY WHEN:** the target is a senior executive behind a screening layer.
**AVOID WHEN:** the culture reads an after-hours call as intrusive, or the target is not desk-bound.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: offered as a personal trick.

### [FOUND:SIGNALCALL] Call on the engagement signal
**Move:** Prioritise the day's dials by who opened or clicked yesterday's drip, and where possible call within minutes of a live open so your message is on screen when the phone rings.
**APPLY WHEN:** instrumented email runs alongside calling.
**AVOID WHEN:** the immediacy is obvious enough to read as surveillance, or open data is noisy from image proxies and forwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: described as a repeatable connect-rate lift.

### [FOUND:GATEHERO] Gatekeeper hero framing
**Move:** Give the gatekeeper a one-sentence quantified value statement they can evaluate without authority, frame passing you along as what makes them look good, and ask for the decision-maker's name, hours, direct line and email even when they put you through.
**APPLY WHEN:** switchboard entry, SMB accounts, or when the decision-maker was not discoverable in research.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would have to lie about being expected, or press unpleasantly — both are self-defeating. The implied "blocking me looks bad" half is pressure on a person with no stake; drop it on unqualified accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: recurring pattern illustrated with a one-line value statement.

### [FOUND:NOLIVEMSG] Decline the "I will pass a message" offer
**Move:** When a gatekeeper offers to take a written message, redirect politely — say the content is involved, ask for the contact's email so you can send it directly, or ask to be patched to voicemail while they note only name, number and headline benefit.
**APPLY WHEN:** switchboard interaction where the gatekeeper is cooperative.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already hold the contact's email — then just ask for the voicemail transfer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: scripted pattern from repeated switchboard calling.

### [FOUND:THIRTYSEC] The thirty-second appointment pitch
**Move:** On an unexpected live connect, state the pain you believe they have, say the goal is a short scheduled conversation to understand it and show them something useful, and ask for ten to twenty minutes — explicitly not selling the product on this call.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live pickup, whether or not they recall your emails.
**AVOID WHEN:** you drift into demoing on the spot without agreeing to a real meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: the core scripted motion of the chapter.

### [FOUND:BOOKLIVE] Book the calendar while still on the phone
**Move:** On a yes, ask whether they are in front of their calendar, propose two named slots, state the duration, say you will send an invite and ask them to accept it, capturing email and numbers in the same breath; keep the meeting a couple of days out, never more than a week.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect agrees to a follow-up conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** never — promising to email some times is named as the rookie error that loses the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: a repeatedly observed failure point among new sellers.

### [FOUND:PULLUSERS] Pull users and influencers onto the booked call
**Move:** While booking, ask the enthusiastic decision-maker whether operational users or adjacent leads should join, and collect their emails for the invite.
**APPLY WHEN:** the decision-maker is enthusiastic and the account has multiple potential users.
**AVOID WHEN:** it dilutes an already-qualified single-owner deal or delays the meeting; never as a substitute for reaching the real decision-maker.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: routine multithreading step at appointment-setting stage.

### [FOUND:HOTLITE] Hot transfer as a demo preview
**Move:** When the prospect wants the demo right now, take it but frame it as a preview — do discovery, show enough to be useful, name what a full prepared session would add, and use that to sell the scheduled demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** unexpected live availability and interest.
**AVOID WHEN:** you compensate by pre-planning every cold call, which is explicitly rejected since most dials never connect.

### [FOUND:SPLITDISCO] Split discovery from the demo, or deliberately do not

## J. Discovery and question craft

### [FOUND:MOMTEST3] The three rules
**Move:** Run every question through three filters before asking — is it about their life rather than my idea, is it about a specific past event rather than a generic or hypothetical, and am I listening more than talking — and rewrite failures toward their existing behaviour.
**APPLY WHEN:** any pre-product or pre-commitment conversation, and whenever you catch yourself hoping for a particular answer.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already have a product and need a purchase decision — commitments, not questions, are the instrument; also weak with deep industry experts, whose informed opinion has real content.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: his own failed company plus repeated work with founders; no controlled comparison.

### [FOUND:LASTTIME] Anchor to the last concrete instance
**Move:** Replace what they usually do with what they last did — when it last happened, walk me through it — and when an answer comes back generic, ask for the most recent actual example and step through it.
**APPLY WHEN:** early discovery; any answer containing usually, always or never.
**AVOID WHEN:** the behaviour genuinely has no precedent in a brand-new category — you are then testing product risk, not market risk.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: the book's central operating rule, illustrated by many dialogues.

### [FOUND:WHYBOTHER] Ask why they do it at all
**Move:** When someone describes a task or a requested feature, ask why they do it at all, follow the chain until you hit the underlying goal, then design against the goal rather than the stated task.
**APPLY WHEN:** the customer states a perceived problem or a workaround they treat as normal.
**AVOID WHEN:** repeated too often it reads as interrogation; drop it once you already know their goal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a finance team asking for messaging tools whose real need was version certainty.

### [FOUND:IMPLICATIONS] Ask what actually happens because of the problem
**Move:** After a stated problem, ask what it costs in money, delay, headcount or lost revenue, and use the answer to separate pay-to-fix problems from tolerable annoyances and as a first pricing signal.
**APPLY WHEN:** someone describes a problem with heat and emotion, before you decide to build anything against it.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skip it because the emotion looked convincing — loud framing frequently sits on top of a working workaround.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a respondent who called a workflow a disaster, then revealed interns had made it fine.

### [FOUND:WALKTHRU] Be shown the workflow, not told about it
**Move:** Ask to watch the task, or failing that ask them to narrate the last occurrence end to end — tools, people, constraints, hand-offs, integrations — preferring observed behaviour over stated preference.
**APPLY WHEN:** you need to know where the product must fit and what it must integrate with; B2B discovery especially.
**AVOID WHEN:** you need the reason behind a preference — observation shows what, not why.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: repeated claim that observation reveals inefficiencies the customer cannot self-report.

### [FOUND:WHATELSE] The search test
**Move:** After any enthusiastic problem statement, ask what they have already tried, whether they searched for a solution, and what it cost them; if they never even searched, treat the enthusiasm as noise.
**APPLY WHEN:** you hear future-tense enthusiasm about definitely paying for something.
**AVOID WHEN:** the category is genuinely unsearchable or unknown to them — absence of search is then weaker evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a respondent who would definitely pay but had never searched once.

### [FOUND:CURRENTSPEND] Ask how they deal with it now, as a price anchor
**Move:** Ask what the current solution is and what it costs per month or year, and use the spread between cheap workaround and expensive incumbent to choose which one you position against.
**APPLY WHEN:** you need pricing orientation and a positioning decision, at mid to late discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating the number as their willingness to pay for you — it bounds the ballpark, not the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise

### [FOUND:MONEYPATH] Ask where the money comes from
**Move:** In B2B, ask whose budget the purchase would come from and who else can kill it, accumulating these answers across conversations into a repeatable map of the buying process.
**APPLY WHEN:** B2B discovery, especially when your contact is clearly not the budget owner.
**AVOID WHEN:** consumer purchases where the buyer is the user.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise

### [FOUND:WHATSHOULDIASK] Invite them to repair your questioning

**APPLY WHEN:** you are new to the industry and suspect you are missing the central issue.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already know the domain — it then signals unpreparedness.

### [FOUND:DREAMSET] The dream-product question as a set-up only
**Move:** Use the dream-product question purely as a springboard: immediately follow with why they want each element, what constraint it removes, and what they do today instead — never collecting the answer as a feature list.

**AVOID WHEN:** you have no time or intention to run the follow-ups — alone it produces build-by-committee noise.

### [FOUND:SCARYQ] Plant one terrifying question per conversation

**APPLY WHEN:** every conversation, at any stage.
**AVOID WHEN:** never legitimately skipped, but scale it to the relationship — a scary question can end a fragile first contact early, which is itself information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any

### [FOUND:BROADFIRST] Start broad, zoom only on a volunteered signal
**Move:** Open with the person's actual priorities — big goals, biggest annoyances, costs, what they are trying to fix right now — rather than with your problem area, and zoom in only after they raise the topic themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** a new segment or geography, or any segment whose economics differ from your proven one.
**AVOID WHEN:** established must-solve categories for that buyer, where starting broad wastes the meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any

### [FOUND:MATTERQ] Does-this-problem-matter battery
**Move:** When it is unclear whether you are selling a painkiller or a vitamin, probe seriousness rather than pain — do they make money from it, have they tried to make more, how much time per week, what do they already pay for, what three things are they trying to fix right now, what are they already doing about this.
**APPLY WHEN:** ambiguous segments, hobby-versus-business boundary cases, before committing roadmap.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a survey — these are anchors to dig around, not a scored questionnaire.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: his own blog used as the worked negative example.

### [FOUND:FAILPOINTS] Enumerate every failure point, including the boring one
**Move:** List all conditions that must simultaneously hold — need exists, buyer has budget, you can reach them, you can build it, you can grow supply and demand — deliberately test the least interesting ones, and classify the business as market-risk or product-risk to know how much conversations can prove.
**APPLY WHEN:** before investing weeks in discovery, or whenever a customer's enthusiasm is conditional on your capability.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating conditional enthusiasm as validation — "if you build it I will buy" is near-tautological in ad, affiliate, marketplace and game businesses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any

### [FOUND:EARLYVANGELIST] Hunt the emotionally over-committed first buyer
**Move:** Filter for people who are disproportionately upset or excited rather than merely agreeable; in B2B look for the four markers — has the problem, knows it, has budget, has already built a makeshift solution — and keep those people close.
**APPLY WHEN:** choosing who to build with and who to sell to first.
**AVOID WHEN:** generalising from them to the mainstream market — their willingness is by definition irrationally early.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: attributed to Steve Blank's earlyvangelist concept plus his own experience.

### [FOUND:DISCOFRAME] Frame discovery as a service to the prospect
**Move:** Open the discovery block by saying you like to start with questions so you can understand their day-to-day and focus the rest of the time on what will be valuable to them, allocating the first five to ten minutes of a presentation slot.
**APPLY WHEN:** the start of any presentation or demo, especially when combined and the prospect expects a walkthrough.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already ran a separate discovery call — cite what you learned rather than re-interrogating.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative

### [FOUND:URGENCYQUAL] Qualify on pain and urgency, not on budget and timeline
**Move:** Run the checklist — problem exists, org has a buying process, decision-makers identified, urgency — but do not disqualify a new-category prospect for lacking a pre-existing budget line or timeline; substitute magnitude of problem and urgency as the primary gates.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling a category the buyer did not know existed.
**AVOID WHEN:** the organisation has no mechanism at all for spending money on tools — that is a disqualifier, not an objection.

### [FOUND:LEADRESEARCH] Lead discovery from pre-call research
**Move:** Instead of asking whether a known condition exists, state the researched fact and attach a frustration question to it, validating the fact aloud so they know you know it.
**APPLY WHEN:** qualifying signals are publicly observable and you did the research.

### [FOUND:PAINCASCADE] Cascade down the pain
**Move:** Start with volume and scope of the relevant activity, ask a leading question about whether that is easy, then step down — what do you use today, have you tried the obvious alternative, how well does it work, what does it cost in time — normalising each step so admitting pain carries no status risk.

### [FOUND:DOWNSTREAMSTAKES] Surface the downstream consequence of not solving

**APPLY WHEN:** pain is confirmed but urgency is unclear.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is too new for a personal-consequence question to land as anything but pressure.

**AVOID WHEN:** the feature is not one you will demo for this persona — you are spending discovery minutes on nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

## K. Repairing bad data in flight

### [FOUND:DEFLECT] Deflect the compliment, return to facts
**Move:** When praised, do not bank it — acknowledge briefly, name your own slip if you pitched, and immediately redirect to how they currently handle the thing, then follow their workflow; watch for compliments hidden at the end of otherwise factual answers.
**APPLY WHEN:** any meeting drifting toward warmth without facts.
**AVOID WHEN:** the praise comes with a commitment attached — it is then backed by currency and worth more.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: multiple worked dialogues including a good negative outcome.

### [FOUND:ANCHORFLUFF] Anchor generic and future-tense claims
**Move:** On hearing always, never, would or might, convert it to a specific — what is the state right now, when did it last break down, walk me through that — and expect the anchored version to contradict the self-image version.
**APPLY WHEN:** anywhere, especially after a fluff-inducing question you asked yourself.
**AVOID WHEN:** never treat the fluffy answer itself as data — the error is valuing the answer, not asking the question.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: two worked dialogues plus a large loss he attributes to trusting a future-tense purchase promise.

### [FOUND:ACTNOW] Put the flake to an immediate small decision
**Move:** When someone claims a problem matters, invite them to act on it right now — download it, try it, look it up — and treat deferral to next time as classifying them a complainer rather than a customer.
**APPLY WHEN:** consumer or low-friction contexts where acting takes seconds.
**AVOID WHEN:** enterprise contexts where immediate action is procedurally impossible — non-action there is not evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas/info-product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: one queue conversation about a loyalty app.

### [FOUND:DIGREQUEST] Dig beneath a feature request
**Move:** Never log a requested feature and move on — ask what it would let them do, how they cope without it, whether it should delay launch, and how it fits their day, then build against the motivation, which is usually cheaper than the request.
**APPLY WHEN:** any feature request, from prospects or from paying enterprise customers.
**AVOID WHEN:** obeying the request instead of understanding it — this is the documented failure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: a media customer asked for analytics; the real need was a weekly emailed report, and about ninety percent of the built dashboard proved irrelevant (self-reported).

### [FOUND:DIGEMOTION] Dig into any strong emotion
**Move:** Treat anger, embarrassment, excitement or resignation as a flag and stop to explore it — tell me more, what makes it so bad, why has this not been fixed — giving permission for a brain dump rather than moving to the next scripted question.
**APPLY WHEN:** any emotional spike, positive or negative.
**AVOID WHEN:** the emotion is one you induced by leading them there — that is manufactured, not observed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Mom Test: an overheard founder who wrote down a stated worst-part-of-the-day and moved on.

### [FOUND:PITCHABORT] Abort your own pitch
**Move:** The moment you notice a monologue, stop, name the slip into pitch mode, and hand the floor back with a callback to what they were saying; if they insist on hearing about the product, promise it for the end of the meeting, explaining you do not want to bias them yet.
**APPLY WHEN:** learning-oriented meetings where you have been talking for minutes and they are nodding.
**AVOID WHEN:** an actual sales meeting where the product presentation is the point.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: a scripted recovery line he reports using repeatedly.

### [FOUND:SHUTUP] Do not correct their mental model mid-answer
**Move:** When they start comparing you to something else or raise an objection you have already solved, let the sentence finish rather than interrupting to fix them — the correction costs you the view into their model.
**APPLY WHEN:** after your idea has entered the conversation, deliberately or accidentally.
**AVOID WHEN:** a factual misunderstanding is about to derail a live commitment decision late in a sales meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Mom Test: stated as a rule with reasoning, no case.

### [FOUND:LOVEMEH] Treat lukewarm as reliable and stop selling into it
**Move:** Read hedged and mildly positive reactions as clean negative data and accept them; do not escalate the pitch until they say something nice, and if they are still engaged ask a few follow-ups to classify the apathy, then thank them and leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** learning-stage conversations, before you depend on this deal for revenue.
**AVOID WHEN:** late-stage transactional selling where the goal genuinely is the close, not the learning — the book flags this asymmetry itself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Mom Test: presented as a repeatedly earned lesson, no dataset. Directly opposed to SOFT-NO-DEFLECT from the other source.

## L. Presentation and demo delivery

### [FOUND:USECASE-RANK] Order the demo by importance, not by product architecture
**Move:** Pick the use cases that are simultaneously most common, most important and most impressive, rank them, lead with the top one, then let the sequence follow the user's natural chronology through their job.
**APPLY WHEN:** every demo, especially short slots or calendars that run over.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect has stated a specific priority — theirs outranks your ranking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: a four-step demo sequence run repeatedly.

### [FOUND:DONT-ASK-HARDEST] Research the pain instead of asking what is hardest
**Move:** Choose the demo scenario yourself from research, because asked cold the buyer names their single hardest edge case, which is the wrong demo; pick the highest-volume version of their pain, where the money is.
**APPLY WHEN:** public data lets you infer volume and discovery is possible before the demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly states a priority use case, or your research is thin enough that guessing misfires.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one worked example from talent acquisition.

### [FOUND:PRELOAD-DATA-DEMO] Run the demo on data the prospect sends ahead
**Move:** A week ahead, ask the prospect for their own operational data, load it into a dedicated instance, run your analysis, open with a light framing of problem and value, then turn to the findings from their data.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product produces a verifiable finding from customer data and the buyer will share pre-sale.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot handle their data securely or compliantly, or the analysis might return nothing — an empty result kills the deal live. Advanced motion; its absence is no reason to delay selling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: one company's described process; the close rate is self-reported.

### [FOUND:DEMO-FEATURES-AS-REVENUE] Ask product for demo-enabling features
**Move:** When feeding requirements to product management, include features whose value is making the demo customisable — embedding the prospect's name or logo, importing their data — and argue for them on revenue grounds even with no post-purchase value.
**APPLY WHEN:** you influence the roadmap and demo conversion is a bottleneck.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would displace features that fix real churn drivers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: reasoning offered without data.

### [FOUND:WORKFLOW-NARRATIVE] Narrate the workflow with built-in pauses
**Move:** Walk the buyer through their real sequence of work using one realistic example that matches their needs, tying each step back to earlier ones, and break the script into segments that leave room for discussion.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product sits inside a repeatable daily workflow.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product is a one-off event, or the buyer is not the user.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: one script run across many demos.

### [FOUND:REPEAT] Deliberate repetition of narrative pillars
**Move:** Return to the same handful of messaging buckets at every stage — pain slides, solution slides, proof slides, demoed features — explicitly connecting each back to its pillar, accepting a level of repetition that would feel excessive in writing.
**APPLY WHEN:** any first presentation to a prospect new to the category.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect is a category veteran who has demonstrated command of the concepts — curate instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: a learned adjustment from writing instincts, illustrated by his demo script.

### [FOUND:PERMITNOTKNOW] Give permission not to know
**Move:** When checking whether the prospect knows a referenced concept, pre-absolve them — it is fine if they have not heard of it, these are new or obscure, you did not know them yourself before starting the company.
**APPLY WHEN:** referencing niche vocabulary, tools or market context the buyer may not know.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would read as condescending to a domain expert.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Kazanjy: his habit on one slide.

### [FOUND:SPECIFICCHECK] Replace "does that make sense?" with a specific question
**Move:** After each point ask something requiring a specific answer tied to that pain, feature or proof, which tests attention and comprehension while eliciting agreement; on phone calls also check literal attention and periodically open the floor without judgement.
**APPLY WHEN:** every substantive point, especially on remote pitches where you cannot see the prospect.
**AVOID WHEN:** never use the bare comprehension question as your only check — it invites a face-saving yes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: repeated technique with multiple worked variants.

### [FOUND:ROLLDISCO] Rolling discovery through the presentation
**Move:** Distribute discovery questions across the presentation, asking each next to the slide or feature it relates to rather than stacking an interrogation at the front, and when a question was already answered cite their prior answer back in their own words.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have more discovery questions than the front block can hold, or a comprehension check reveals a gap.
**AVOID WHEN:** re-asking something already answered — that signals you were not listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: examples used across his pitches.

### [FOUND:AGREESCORE] Score agreement continuously
**Move:** At every point where agreement is plausible, use a leading question to make the prospect voice it, welcoming the disagreements this surfaces, so that by the close the purchase reads as the obvious conclusion of a chain they already assented to.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout presentation and demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** the agreement is manufactured on points they have not actually accepted — it collapses at the close as a late objection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his stated organising principle for the pitch.

### [FOUND:PACING] Deliberate pacing and planned pauses
**Move:** Consciously slow delivery once you know the pitch well, since familiarity and nerves both accelerate speech, enunciate, and build in pauses for questions and comprehension tests.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, doubly on phone pitches where the prospect has no facial information.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: stated from experience without supporting data.

### [FOUND:CURATE] Curate slides and features to the account
**Move:** Drop whole slides and skip demo features that will not resonate with this account and persona, double down on those that will, and move non-linearly — backtrack when a comprehension check fails, jump to appendix on questions, skip ahead when they are already convinced.
**APPLY WHEN:** prospecting and discovery gave you real account context, or the prospect already knows the category and wants differentiators.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no account context — which itself indicates the prospecting and discovery work was not done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

### [FOUND:NOBLINDPITCH] Open even a sales meeting with learning questions
**Move:** Before presenting, spend a few minutes on open-ended questions about this specific buyer's goals, problems and budget, then sell — hard pitching first yields only binary feedback, useful for tweaks and useless for the big questions.

**AVOID WHEN:** late-cycle meetings where the buyer expects a decision, not discovery.

## N. Objection handling

### [FOUND:OBJLOOP] The objection loop
**Move:** Use one uniform pattern for every objection — catch it, convert it to the question under the question, answer with quantitative and qualitative proof backed by visual materials, validate that the answer landed, resume where you left off, then keep probing until objections are exhausted or they proceed with some still open.
**APPLY WHEN:** any objection, generic or solution-specific.
**AVOID WHEN:** never treat a handled objection as a close — return to the ask each time.

### [FOUND:BLOCKERQ] Force a specific objection out of a vague one
**Move:** When the refusal is content-free, ask what specifically is blocking progress right now, putting the burden on them to name concrete blockers.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect goes vague or abstract instead of silent.
**AVOID WHEN:** a specific objection is already on the table — handle that one.

### [FOUND:CURRENCY] Price the commitment in currency terms

## P. Negotiation

### [FOUND:PAD-THE-OPENING-PRICE] Build discount room into sticker and proposal
**Move:** Set list and initial proposal above your true floor so every negotiated step still lands on a viable deal, assuming procurement will demand a further cut on top of what the decision-maker agreed.

**AVOID WHEN:** mid-market accounts with no procurement and price-sensitive founders — padding can price you out of a simple, fast deal.

### [FOUND:ONE-INCREMENT-RETREAT] Concede one step of one lever, and trade
**Move:** Name what you must preserve — cash up front, term length — and what is cheap for you; when asked for a concession, move exactly one increment and simultaneously pull a different lever your way.
**APPLY WHEN:** all stakeholders are bought in and only price and terms remain.
**AVOID WHEN:** value is not yet established — it is then a pricing objection, not a negotiation, and discounting papers over unproven value.

## V. Scaling gates and sales management

### [FOUND:WIN-RATE-SCALE-GATE] Gate the first rep hire on win rate
**Move:** Measure the share of first meetings that eventually close won: reliably in the 15–30 percent band, hire one or two reps and test whether they match you; substantially above, consider raising price; below, diagnose messaging, product or pricing before adding headcount.
**APPLY WHEN:** the founder has run enough first meetings for the rate to be meaningful.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is a handful of deals, or wins came from network relationships a hired rep cannot reproduce.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/product · market=stage
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: the band stated as typical without a cited dataset.

### [FOUND:COST-OF-SALES-MATH] Size the hire against a cost-of-sales ceiling
**Move:** Before hiring, compute whether the fully loaded cost of AEs, SDRs and sales engineers stays at or under roughly 20–30 percent of the revenue they close, modelling the rep's week explicitly and checking resulting annual bookings against the ceiling.
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding to add a rep, a pod or a sales-operations headcount.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as a precise forecast rather than a sanity bound; the inputs are estimates.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** assertion — Kazanjy: rule of thumb with worked arithmetic, no external benchmark.

### [FOUND:JACUZZI-INCREMENT] Scale by validated increments, not a step change
**Move:** Add a small increment of headcount, verify the KPIs hold, then add the next; never jump from founder-selling to a fully specialised org in one move.
**APPLY WHEN:** the win-rate and cost-of-sales gates pass and you are choosing hire volume.
**AVOID WHEN:** capital-driven speed pressure tempts a large simultaneous hire — that is the premature-scaling path.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/product · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his stated approach, backed by cautionary cases rather than measurement.

### [FOUND:MATURITY-LADDER] Advance through staged exit criteria

**APPLY WHEN:** planning hiring sequence, or deciding whether you earned the next stage.
**AVOID WHEN:** stages get skipped, or a stage is declared complete on customer count alone without success and KPI evidence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/product · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Kazanjy: his framework from his own build and observed orgs.

### [FOUND:FIRST-LEVERAGE-HIRE-CHOICE] Pick SDR versus success by where your time actually leaks

**APPLY WHEN:** founder or first-time manager has reps to develop.

### [FOUND:CULTURE-SPEC-DOC-ARTICULATE] Specify, document, articulate the culture
**Move:** Answer what kind of org you want and what market you sell into, plus who you must recruit and what has already accreted from founders; bullet the valued and not-valued behaviours into a shared doc, then push it from interview stage through onboarding and all-hands, demonstrating it from the top.
