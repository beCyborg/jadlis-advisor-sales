# advisor-founding — myths and demotions

What this tradition itself debunks, what it demotes, where its own techniques are documented to fail, and which numbers must never leave this lens as fact.

---

## 1. Myths this layer refuses

**"Sprinkle some sales professionals on it."** Hiring a rep or a VP of Sales to discover the message, materials and product fit that the founder never proved. Named as the characteristic startup sales disaster: the failure then gets misattributed to the departing first sales leader rather than to the founders who hired for the wrong epoch. Counter-moves: [FOUND:DEFER-AE-HIRE], [FOUND:STAGE-GATE], [FOUND:LEADER-EXIT-CRITERIA].

**The rep-less go-to-market.** The story that certain famous companies grew without salespeople; the counter offered is simply that their own staff directories were full of sellers. Do not let a PLG anecdote justify skipping founder-led selling. See [FOUND:BOTTOM-UP-DEFER], which is deferred, not recommended.

**Sales as innate talent, or as purely right-brain work immune to metrics** — and its mirror image, "I do not care about inputs, only outputs", named as the signature of a manager afraid of both management and metrics. Counter: [FOUND:ACTIVITY-INSTRUMENTATION], [FOUND:QUANTITY-FIRST-THEN-QUALITY].

**Asking whether the idea is good.** Self-indulgent noise except with experts who have built similar businesses. Attaching a number to a hypothetical ("would you pay X") feels rigorous while remaining a hypothetical — the more dangerous version, because it manufactures the appearance of validation. Counter: [FOUND:MOMTEST3], [FOUND:WHATELSE].

**"I would definitely buy that."** Named as carrying the highest false-positive danger of any phrase; it is future-tense fluff, not a pre-order. Same class: "looks great, let me know when it launches", "the meeting went really well", "everyone loves it", "we're getting a lot of positive feedback". Counter: [FOUND:ANCHORFLUFF], [FOUND:ZOMBIE].

**Doing discovery badly beats not doing it.** False. False positives feel like rigour and justify over-investing cash, time and team. Both extremes are rejected: skipping discovery, and turning discovery into months of full-time interviewing that substitutes for shipping.

**Soliciting criticism fixes the bias.** Inviting brutal honesty works for people nobody fears hurting; it does not reproduce for an ordinary founder sitting across the table. Being pitchy manufactures agreement — anyone will call an idea great if you push hard enough. Counter: [FOUND:PITCHABORT], [FOUND:MISTAKELIST].

**Non-biasing questions are useful questions.** Demographic and trivia questions are safe and worthless. A "top problem" elicited about a category the person does not care about is a false positive. Ranking exercises and numeric satisfaction scales do not reveal whether the person cares at all — reaching for a scale is a symptom of the wrong mode. Counter: [FOUND:MATTERQ], [FOUND:BROADFIRST].

**"Our market is practically infinite."** A warning sign, not an asset: serving several segments at once yields a mediocre product for many, and lets internal feature debates be won by invoking a hypothetical audience. Twenty interviews across twenty customer types is one data point each on twenty different businesses. Counter: [FOUND:SEGSPEC], [FOUND:SEG3CRIT].

**Seniority equals representativeness.** Impressive interviewees make good status reports and bad evidence; the self-deception is optimising for who you can say you met. Counter: [FOUND:USERNOTVIP].

**Pricing to perfection.** Charging so much that every star must align; the harms named are lower win rates and higher renewal churn. The inverse tell is a buyer who says yes too easily — you left money on the table. Also demoted: premature three-tier segmentation, and deferring pricing entirely because you do not want to hear no. Counter: [FOUND:CHARGE-NOW], [FOUND:PRICE-LADDER].

**Collateral perfectionism** — that assets must be polished, legally reviewed and universally signed off before showing — and its opposite, the technical founder's pure-demo-without-slides, which damages the value argument. Counter: [FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH], [FOUND:PITCHFORMAT].

**"Drip marketing is spam."** Rejected conditionally: spam is a function of bad targeting and self-centred messaging, not of automation. But mass merge without real signifiers is called counterproductive, and over-personalising every message in a sequence is wasteful overkill. Counter: [FOUND:SINGLESERVE], [FOUND:DRIP-SPLIT].

**The polite meeting is the safe meeting.** The buyer who never understood the pitch, nodded along and vanished is the dangerous outcome — which is why comprehension checkpoints exist and why the bare "does that make sense?" invites a face-saving lie. Counter: [FOUND:SPECIFICCHECK], [FOUND:WHATCHANGED-CHECK].

**The meeting anti-pattern.** Routing every opportunity into a calendar block wastes founder time and blinds you to serendipitous learning; a one-hour meeting costs roughly four hours fully loaded. A meeting with no stated purpose defaults to a sales meeting, which is triply bad: the buyer closes up about pricing, attention shifts to you, and you are unprepared to sell. Failing framings named explicitly: asking to interview them, asking for their opinion on what you are doing, and asking for a quick coffee. Counter: [FOUND:CASUALCHAT], [FOUND:VFWPA].

**Strict-letter BANT.** Disqualifies exactly the innovative-category prospects an early founder wants, because a buyer who did not know the category existed cannot have budget or timeline for it. Counter: [FOUND:URGENCYQUAL], [FOUND:NOBUDGET].

**Unstructured trials as a sales strategy.** Predictably produce non-usage, week-by-week extensions and a seed of doubt about post-purchase adoption; handing over credentials is not a strategy. Counter: [FOUND:REFUSEUNGUIDED], [FOUND:STRUCTPILOT].

**Winning every deal is good news.** It indicates too few or over-cherry-picked opportunities. Typical new-solution win rates cited are 10–30 percent. Conversely, selling to anyone with a heartbeat destroys the business: unqualified demos burn runway, and a badly fit customer churns, badmouths you, consumes support capacity and steers your roadmap wrong. Counter: [FOUND:QUALACCOUNT], [FOUND:TRACK-CLOSED-UNQUALIFIED].  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->

**Customers notice their own success.** They do not. Unreported ROI erodes into churn, lost upsell and an opening for competitors — and a good year does not auto-convert into a renewal without a renewal process. Counter: [FOUND:INSTRUMENT-OUTCOMES], [FOUND:RENEWAL-ASK-UPSELL].

**Making support hard, blaming the user, closing tickets on silence.** All three named as active damage; hard support paths push complaints onto public channels. Counter: [FOUND:FRICTIONLESS-COMPLAINT-CAPTURE], [FOUND:ABSORB-BLAME-FIRST], [FOUND:TICKET-HYGIENE-AFFIRMATIVE-CLOSE].

**Hiring myths, as a set.** "A sales person is a sales person"; ignoring the role slice; "industry" as a coarse match; hire-the-contact-list; pedigree as a decision rather than one weighted term; self-reported quota taken at face value; the old-school class of five to ten AEs with an expected fifty percent washout; "sell me this pen"; video interviews as narrative rather than ability authentication. Also: "experienced hires do not need onboarding" and "get them in front of customers immediately", both named false economies, along with the belief that information transfer plus a quiz equals readiness. Counter: [FOUND:EXPERIENCE-MATCH-BUYER-TEMPO], [FOUND:NO-ROLODEX-HIRE], [FOUND:MOCK-DEMO-FULL-PASS], [FOUND:COHORT-BOOTCAMP].

**"Meetings are for big companies."** Skip the venue and the need reasserts itself as back-channel morale erosion and work chosen by preference rather than priority. Manager teaming as the default audit tool is separately named as managerial laziness dressed as productivity. Counter: [FOUND:WEEKLY-TEAM-AND-PIPELINE], [FOUND:ZOOM-IN-INSPECTION].

**The "VP of Sales" title as a target.** Noise — hire against stage-appropriate scope, and do not expect a fine-tuned machine, because repeatability rather than smoothness is the bar. Counter: [FOUND:TACTICAL-LEADER-PROFILE].

## 2. Documented failure modes of this layer's own techniques

- **[FOUND:CREDTHREAT], [FOUND:GATEHERO], [FOUND:WRONGPERSON], [FOUND:SENDINFO], [FOUND:SOFT-NO-DEFLECT], [FOUND:URGENCY-LEVERS], [FOUND:LOGOS-MSA]** all run on implied leverage, guilt or consent shortcuts. The source pairs them with a genuine-relevance precondition and does forbid lying to gatekeepers, but it offers no principle for the boundary between implied pressure and coercion. On an unqualified account they are pure pressure with no value behind them. Operational rule for this lens: a move you would not be willing to describe out loud to the buyer does not get recommended.
- **[FOUND:PRICE-LADDER]** — the supporting ladder is confounded by simultaneous product improvement, which the author states himself. It cannot be read as a pricing-elasticity finding.
- **[FOUND:CUSTOM-DEAL-STAGES]** — the author self-critiques his own stage set: a vague catch-all stage became a catchment basin, which is exactly what the move is supposed to prevent.
- **[FOUND:PIXELREAD], [FOUND:SIGNALCALL], [FOUND:CLICK-TARGETS], [FOUND:EMAIL-TOOL-CONCEPTS]** — the telemetry these depend on has degraded since publication under image proxying and pixel-blocking. Over-reading silence as disinterest is now a live error, not a hypothetical one.
- **[FOUND:REMINDPLUS]** — carries its own named failure: early or repeated calendar reminders make prospects arrive hours early and annoyed.
- **[FOUND:RECASK]** — the recording objection given (audio is unsortable) predates searchable transcription and no longer holds in the form stated; the near-universal-consent claim has no count behind it.
- **[FOUND:ACTNOW], [FOUND:CUTKNOT], [FOUND:CASUALCHAT], [FOUND:CAPTUREANY]** — scoped to micro/low ACV and solo buyers. In enterprise procurement, immediate action is procedurally impossible, so non-action is not evidence, and one booked trial is one data point, not validation.
- **[FOUND:IMMERSE]** — the worked case ends with the author abandoning the segment he immersed in. Access is not fit.
- **[FOUND:DIGREQUEST]** — the illustrating case is a documented failure of obeying a request, not a success of the move; roughly three months and most of a built dashboard were lost before the lesson.
- **[FOUND:GOOD-TIME-GOOD-OPPS], [FOUND:JUMP-PREPARE-MIDAIR], [FOUND:SHALLOW-MANY], [FOUND:RUTHLESS-PIPE-CLEANING]** — all assume a large addressable set and many at-bats. Against a few dozen named accounts on multi-year cycles they invert into self-harm, since silence there is structural rather than terminal.
- **[FOUND:EQUIP-FOR-EXPECT], [FOUND:INBOX-ZERO-AUDIT], [FOUND:AMBIENT-REVIEW], [FOUND:SALES-STANDUP], [FOUND:BEER-INTERVIEW]** — all assume physical co-location, which the source never problematises. Remote-first orgs have no direct analogue.
- **[FOUND:TACTICAL-LEADER-PROFILE]** — the author explicitly declines authority here and flags his own limited repetitions. Treat as the weakest material in the canon source.

## 3. Where this layer stops working entirely

- **Consulting, services and expertise offers.** The spine assumes a demoable product whose superiority can be shown numerically in a live screen share. In consulting, trust precedes any product-benefit argument, so [FOUND:DIRECT-ASK], [FOUND:INEVITABILITY], [FOUND:PRICEPARRY] and pain-signifier-first outreach invert the correct order. There is no demo instance, no demo-data problem, no seat-tier ladder; [FOUND:PRELOAD-DATA-DEMO] becomes a paid diagnostic rather than a free pre-sale asset. What does transfer: [FOUND:COST-MODEL], [FOUND:COMPARATOR-PRICE], [FOUND:STATUSQUOCOST], [FOUND:MICROCONTRACT], pipeline hygiene.
- **Micro-ACV, self-serve, freemium and product-led growth.** Explicitly out of scope in the canon source's own introduction. The customer-success material assumes roughly a thousand dollars or more per user per year; below that the implementation labour model collapses.
- **Very high ACV committee enterprise.** Truncation and volume tactics lose to patient nursing and deep multithreading.
- **Product-risk businesses.** The second source states its own limit: conversations validate market risk (do they want it, will they pay, are there enough) and cannot validate product risk (can you build it, grow supply and demand, retain). Ad networks, affiliate models, marketplaces and games return near-tautological "if you build it I will buy" answers.
- **Regulated and privacy-sensitive markets.** [FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH], [FOUND:LOGOS-MSA] and [FOUND:EMAIL-PATTERN-FORM] collide with legal review, MSA scrutiny and anti-spam law; the dismissal of personal-address concerns is jurisdiction-dependent, not settled.
- **Non-US, non-Anglophone contexts.** All examples in both sources are US and UK. Norms around directness, cold approach, refusal and hospitality-driven agreement vary and are never considered.
- **Deep industry experts** are the acknowledged exception to the no-opinions rule: their informed judgement has content, and rigidly Mom-Testing them wastes the access.
- **Channel and partner motions** get one move in the whole layer ([FOUND:SHELFTEST], single anecdote). Partner-mediated distribution, marketing-led demand and international expansion are out of frame entirely.

## 4. Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

Everything numeric in this layer is self-reported by the author's own companies, by companies he advised, or relayed second-hand. Quote them as **shape**, always attributed, never as effect sizes or benchmarks.

- Company arc: roughly $6M annual run rate at acquisition with about twenty sales and success staff. One company, one market, one era.
- Win rates: 20–30 percent called solid for a new innovative solution; 10–20 percent typical; 15–30 percent as the band gating a first rep hire. No dataset; the band is asserted.
- Response rates: roughly 12 percent for human-customised first outreach against roughly 5 percent unpersonalised; a personalised first email yielding about 30 percent more responses than heavy auto-personalisation; response peaking at the second email in a sequence. **Vendor-reported**, not independently verifiable.
- Inbound: an approximately hundredfold drop in contact rates between a five-minute and a thirty-minute response, and about ninety percent cumulative contact by the sixth attempt. Attributed to third-party research but reproduced without a verifiable citation — treat as relayed practitioner claim.
- Pricing: the $99→$499 monthly ladder (confounded, per the author); seat prices appearing inconsistently across examples in the same book; a competitor comparator around $8–9K per year; an ROI multiple range of roughly 6x–15x at one company. Illustrative, not a price book.
- Economics: cost of sales at 20–30 percent; a $100K rep booking roughly $500K; implementation at roughly two hours and about $70 of labour per customer; implementation fee at roughly three times labour cost; each demo internally valued at $5,000. All rules of thumb with worked arithmetic and no external benchmark.
- Mom Test figures: about $10M lost by an early employer attributed to trusting future-tense purchase promises; at least $500K attributed to one unasked question; about 90 percent of one built dashboard irrelevant and about three months lost; about 100 cold contacts yielding 2 conversations; 5 advisors at roughly half a percent equity producing roughly weekly introductions; a dozen prototypes sold pre-launch. Every one self-reported, several second-hand.
- Stop conditions and cadences: 3–5 conversations for a simple tight segment, more than 10 with scattered results meaning the segment is too fuzzy, 1–2 weeks of concentrated conversation, a 30-day untouched-opportunity threshold, a 60-day re-engagement interval, a success-specialist trigger at about a dozen onboarded customers. Operating conventions, not findings.
- Churn illustration over 58 months (0 percent versus 2.5 percent versus 5 percent monthly) is a spreadsheet model, not observed data.

## 5. Structural blind spots to declare when this lens speaks

- **One practitioner per source, no counterfactual.** We see what worked in these authors' deals, never what a control group did, and never the conversations that passed every test and still misled.
- **Survivorship in the scaling material.** The canon source concedes most premature-scaling examples are unrecognisable because those companies died, so the anti-patterns are reconstructed from famous failures rather than sampled.
- **Buyer psychology is thin.** Persuasion is modelled as comprehension plus proof, which under-explains deals lost for reasons the buyer never states — status, risk aversion, internal politics beyond stakeholder mapping.
- **No base rates.** Neither source quantifies how often a good conversation converts to a commitment, what a normal referral rate looks like, or how often an anchored past-behaviour answer still turns out wrong — so moves cannot be prioritised by expected value.
- **Disclosed but unexamined interest.** The canon author discloses having co-founded a sales analytics company while arguing for metrics-first management, and self-grades his own metrics harness as merely adequate.
- **Tooling is load-bearing and dated.** Mechanisms survive; every named vendor should be read as a category slot, and some mechanisms depend on tooling that has since degraded.
