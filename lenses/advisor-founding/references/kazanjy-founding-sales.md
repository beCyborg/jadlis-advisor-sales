# Founding Sales: The Early Stage Go-to-Market Handbook — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The only end-to-end operating manual for the founder who must personally sell a product nobody has heard of, from first cold list to the hire of a sales leader. Its mechanism-level thesis: early-stage selling is product management and product marketing wearing a sales costume, so message-crafting and product decisions must sit in one head, and the loop from hearing an objection to shipping the slide that answers it must be hours, not quarters. It beats other sources on the unglamorous connective tissue — collateral construction, list building, appointment setting, CRM hygiene, implementation, and the arithmetic that says when to hire — where most sales books jump straight to technique. Its second contribution is the epoch split: what is correct pre-repeatability is destructive post-repeatability, and vice versa, so every tactic ships with a stage gate.

## Core model

Two epochs. **"Figuring it out"** (pre-PMF, evangelical, deliberately unscalable) versus **"scaling it"** (post-PMF, specialized, instrumented). Applying epoch-2 methods in epoch 1 — hiring a VP Sales to discover the motion — is the characteristic startup sales disaster.

Inside epoch 1 the build order is fixed: **narrative → collateral → prospecting → appointment setting → pitching → closing → customer success → scaling**. The narrative is a seven-beat problem–solution–specifics formation (what the problem is, who owns it, what it costs, how it is solved today and why that fails, what changed, how yours works, what proof exists) presented **inductively**, so disagreement surfaces early and cheaply; pricing is its conclusion. Collateral is that narrative rendered into artifacts — deck, emails, call bullets, demo script, video — governed by speed over polish and a master-deck-plus-forks content model.

The pitch is organizational persuasion, modeled as **Potential Value × Value Comprehension × Belief**, each term with its own lever (targeting, materials, proof/pilots). Sandler-style **micro-contracts** bracket every stage transition; every objection runs one uniform loop back to the ask. Revenue identity throughout: **opportunities × win rate × average deal size**, with rep hours as the binding constraint — which is why scaling means replicating a proven unit (a rep, or an SDR+2 AE+CSM pod), not squeezing existing reps.

## Moves

### Stage 0 — Mindset and operating stance

### [FOUND:STAGE-GATE] Read and act only to your current stage
**Move:** Diagnose which epoch the go-to-market is in before adopting any tactic; refuse work belonging to the later epoch (hiring, specialization, quota design) until narrative, collateral and outreach exist.
**APPLY WHEN:** assembling a sales system from scratch, or being pushed toward org-building before repeatability exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** the motion is already repeatable and the real bottleneck is headcount.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's own build plus patterns reported from startups he advises.

### [FOUND:CARGO-CULT-CHECK] Refuse borrowed practice you can't explain
**Move:** Before copying a practice from a mature sales org, state the mechanism by which it produces the outcome in your market at your stage. If you can't, adapt or build anew.
**APPLY WHEN:** someone proposes a process because "it worked at [big company]".
**AVOID WHEN:** the practice is cheap and reversible and deriving the theory costs more than trying it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle, no cases attached.

### [FOUND:NO-ABSTRACTION] Keep message-crafting and product decisions in one head
**Move:** The person carrying the value proposition into customer conversations also owns (or sits inside) what gets built next; if two people, couple them tightly and pull the product founder onto calls.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF, product hypotheses still moving, feature bets placed monthly.
**AVOID WHEN:** post-PMF and scaling — the founder becomes the bottleneck.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author + Jason Heidema arrangement at TalentBin, described as workable-but-imperfect.

### [FOUND:UNSCALABLE-INVEST] Deliberately do things that don't scale
**Move:** Accept per-deal economics that would never survive at scale — travel to a small account, hand-implement, hand-source lists — and book the cost as learning, not CAC.
**APPLY WHEN:** the quarter's goal is information about pain, willingness to pay and fit, not efficiency.
**AVOID WHEN:** scaling with a payback bar; or when the unscalable work yields no new information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's practice, framed as an extension of Paul Graham's consumer-side argument.

### [FOUND:DEFER-AE-HIRE] Postpone the first AE/VP Sales hire until repeatability
**Move:** Do not hire a sales professional — especially one poached from the incumbent — to establish the motion. Keep the résumé for the scaling act; until then the founder is the rep.
**APPLY WHEN:** narrative still shifting, win/loss reasons ambiguous, no repeatable persona.
**AVOID WHEN:** persona, pitch and objection set are stable and founder calendar capacity is the constraint.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's counterfactual plus the widely repeated "fire the first VP of Sales" pattern; cites Steve Blank without data.

### [FOUND:GOOD-TIME-GOOD-OPPS] Truncate marginal opportunities on purpose
**Move:** On weak fit, absent budget or a stalling buyer, close the account out and move on; record the conditions under which it would become viable later. The scarce resource is time, not opportunities.
**APPLY WHEN:** greenfield territory with many untouched accounts.
**AVOID WHEN:** the addressable set is a few large named accounts, or the "marginal" account is a lighthouse logo.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — attributed to TalentBin's first AE Brad Snider, used as team doctrine.

### [FOUND:JUMP-PREPARE-MIDAIR] Default to action; cap preparation
**Move:** Set the default state to "on the phone / sending email" and treat idleness as the anomaly. One proofread per email, template everything repeated, stop at the point of diminishing returns on any single action.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-volume pipelines with dozens of concurrent conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a single meeting carries the deal — the book itself prescribes pre-call planning for pitches.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated team practice; supporting maxim offered as apocryphal.

### [FOUND:DIRECT-ASK] Ask the commercial questions plainly, and ask for the sale
**Move:** Put the three questions on the table unsoftened — do you have this problem, does it need solving, will you spend money on it — then state that the fit is good and name the quantity to buy.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect already spent time with you; that time is treated as evidence of intent.
**AVOID WHEN:** trust must precede commercial framing — notably consulting/services, where the buyer is buying the person; also on learning-purpose evangelical calls.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — assertion plus a Glengarry Glen Ross reference, no data.

### [FOUND:SHALLOW-MANY] Run breadth of relationships on a records system, not memory
**Move:** Accept dozens of net-new contacts weekly and a live pipeline of a few dozen to 100+ concurrent conversations; externalize org, pain and rapport details into CRM; keep low-cost rapport openers ready.
**APPLY WHEN:** volume-driven pipelines.
**AVOID WHEN:** a handful of deep enterprise or consulting relationships is the whole business.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing practice on the author's teams.

### [FOUND:INEVITABILITY] Frame the purchase as "when", not "if"
**Move:** After qualifying fit, run the conversation from the premise that adoption happens — with you or a competitor — and move to timing and implementation rather than justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-PMF, qualified account with confirmed pain; flagged as weaker pre-PMF.
**AVOID WHEN:** fit is unverified or the buyer is early in problem awareness — it reads as arrogance and burns credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — mechanism argued without outcome data.

### [FOUND:LOSS-AUTOPSY-THEN-DROP] Record the loss cause honestly, then discard the feeling
**Move:** On every loss log the actual reason — timing, budget, competitor, feature gap, your own follow-up failure — share it so teammates don't relearn it, then resume expecting to win the next one.
**APPLY WHEN:** any pipeline where most opportunities close lost.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication given; the risk is honest reasons degrading into a picklist that hides truth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — team practice tied to win-rate reporting.

### [FOUND:FUTURE-YOU-NOTES] Record for the version of you that returns next quarter
**Move:** Take call notes in a physical lab notebook, transfer only salient points to CRM, and ask what future-you will wish existed when the account resurfaces. Then automate capture so it stops depending on discipline.
**APPLY WHEN:** long cycles, recycled closed-lost accounts, many concurrent deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** verbatim transcription — explicitly rejected as waste; capture is selective.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing practice on the author's teams.

### [FOUND:STUDENT-OF-GAME] Build vertical expertise deliberately, including competitors' strengths
**Move:** Absorb the vertical's processes, roles and incumbent tools; aim to know the domain better than the buyer; credit competitors where genuinely good, because credibility depends on it. Drill conversational fearlessness on strangers.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, and especially before you feel ready — "enough" expertise is the threshold for acting.
**AVOID WHEN:** chasing exhaustive knowledge of every alternative; the common few suffice.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — mechanism asserted from the author's own transition.

### [FOUND:GLASS-HOUSE] Instrument every customer-facing interaction and make it visible to all
**Move:** Record emails, presentations and calls; publish activity levels, win/loss notes, close ratios, leaderboards and error reports; socialize failures alongside wins so both read as real.
**APPLY WHEN:** building a team culture from scratch, where the norm can still be set.
**AVOID WHEN:** jurisdictions requiring recording consent; or where transparency arrives as surveillance without the shared-learning half.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — implemented and load-bearing at TalentBin.

### [FOUND:FUNNEL-MATH] Instrument the ratios before you make org decisions
**Move:** Build reporting for emails per demo booked, opportunities per closed deal, value per opportunity, win rate by rep and average contract value; use ACV to decide whether a sales development hire is affordable.
**APPLY WHEN:** any decision about headcount, channel investment or rep performance.
**AVOID WHEN:** sample sizes are tiny — the ratios are noise on the first handful of deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — operating practice, no dataset shown.

### Stage 1 — Narrative, value model and pricing

### [FOUND:PSS-NARRATIVE] Build the seven-part narrative before any collateral
**Move:** Write answers to: what is the problem; who has it; what it costs hard and soft; how it's solved today and why that falls down; what changed; how your solution works; how you know it's better quantitatively and qualitatively. Treat it as a living hypothesis the market rewrites.
**APPLY WHEN:** a new or disruptive offering entering a market for the first time.
**AVOID WHEN:** commodity replacement where the problem is universally acknowledged — "what changed" adds nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's framework, illustrated with TalentBin, Salesforce, Groupon, HubSpot, Zendesk reconstructions (his renderings).

### [FOUND:INDUCTIVE-EXIT] Use narrative order — and the opening problem slide — as a qualification gate
**Move:** Present the problem first, in conversation and as the deck's opening section, and check agreement before advancing. If the prospect denies the problem or says it isn't theirs, dig into the disagreement or end the meeting; treat an early mismatch as a win, while noting prospecting should have caught it.
**APPLY WHEN:** early calls and first presentations; volume pipelines where time is the constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already declared the pain and expects depth, not restatement; or where low problem awareness makes a premature exit kill an educable deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the operating logic of both the deck and the call.

### [FOUND:PROBLEM-STATEMENT-TEST] Validate the problem statement on a live insider
**Move:** Say the problem statement to someone inside the target industry and ask whether they've hit it. Passing means not just "yes" but that they can immediately continue in detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** drafting or revising the narrative.
**AVOID WHEN:** the insider is a friend with an incentive to agree — the test's value is their willingness to say no.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — heuristic without cases.

### [FOUND:BUDGET-OR-HOURS-TARGET] Target the owner of budget or of the hours
**Move:** Identify the person functionally responsible — controls the budget for the pain, or personally spends daily hours on it. Disqualifier: if they can say "that's not really my job", it's the wrong person. Expect the right title to shift as the org grows.
**APPLY WHEN:** building an ICP, choosing outreach titles, checking whether a champion can act.
**AVOID WHEN:** very small orgs where roles collapse into the founder — and such accounts are often unqualified anyway.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked through for TalentBin, Zendesk and CRM buyer profiles.

### [FOUND:COST-MODEL] Quantify the cost of the problem in per-unit math, then scale it by account size
**Move:** Compute hard ROI (headcount, storage, agency fees displaced) and soft/opportunity ROI (churned customers, deals missed), attach a metric the prospect can apply to themselves — cost per rep per month, deals lost × ACV × headcount — and roll it into a running monthly cost of doing nothing. Treat vague "increased agility" as the weakest tier.
**APPLY WHEN:** pricing, qualification, deck-building; and whenever the buyer accepts the problem but shows no urgency.
**AVOID WHEN:** the causal chain is long and hypothetical, or you cannot defend the inputs — invented multipliers torch credibility for the rest of the deck.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — illustrated across storage, support, CRM and sales-automation categories.

### [FOUND:INCUMBENT-LADDER] Classify how the account solves it today: nothing / process / service / product
**Move:** Place each account on the ladder. Nothing → prove the problem is worth solving via cost of non-solution. Process → attack time cost and process frailty. Service provider → attack margin and fee. Product → positive qualification signal (budget conviction exists); prepare for comparison.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery, qualification, and deciding which accounts to prioritize.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot credibly name the incumbent's real strengths — credibility requires it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — derived from TalentBin's competitive landscape and generalized.

### [FOUND:WHAT-CHANGED] Name the enabling change and check whether the trend is strengthening
**Move:** Identify the specific shift that made your solution newly possible or necessary (ubiquitous browsers, professional networks, falling flash prices, mobile shopping) and check whether it is accelerating — if so it reinforces the narrative over time.
**APPLY WHEN:** pitching innovative categories where the buyer needs to believe "why now".
**AVOID WHEN:** the trend is reversing or contested — then it undermines the argument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — several industry examples, mechanism-level.

### [FOUND:SAME-METRIC-COMPARE] Prove superiority in the metrics the buyer already uses
**Move:** Beat existing solutions in the buyer's own language ("more X" / "less Y"), demonstrated live where possible; break the top-level metric into workflow components and prove each; attach a countable proxy where a comparison is qualitative.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive deals; demos where the buyer has an incumbent tool.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would lose the head-to-head on that metric — reframe instead of competing on it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin's standing demo practice against LinkedIn Recruiter and resume databases.

### [FOUND:VANITY-METRIC-REFRAME] Redirect from the competitor's flattering metric to the one that pays
**Move:** When a rival cites scale that doesn't map to the buyer's job ("200 million profiles"), reframe to the metric that matters for this buyer's narrow need — how many candidates exist for their role in their geography.
**APPLY WHEN:** the incumbent's headline number is bigger than yours but less relevant.
**AVOID WHEN:** the metric genuinely is decision-relevant — reframing then reads as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one worked example from talent acquisition.

### [FOUND:THIRD-PARTY-PROOF] Stack external validation as a separate "who agrees" layer
**Move:** Assemble customer counts, testimonials restating your own metric claims, case studies, press and analyst coverage — deployed as supplement to the argument, never as the argument.
**APPLY WHEN:** there is a credibility gap between a small vendor and an established incumbent.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no narrative yet — social proof over an incoherent story is called worthless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — listed as options without outcome evidence.

### [FOUND:CHARGE-NOW] Charge from the first conversations, even at a token price
**Move:** Set an initial price and ask for it rather than deferring monetization; free pilots are allowed (initial free period, freemium volume, a few lighthouse customers paying in structured feedback) but the default is a real price.
**APPLY WHEN:** any early B2B motion — unpaid users don't evaluate seriously and often don't even use the product.
**AVOID WHEN:** the named exception — a small set of lighthouse accounts whose engaged feedback is the consideration.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's practice; the diagnosis (founders defer pricing to avoid hearing "no") is asserted.

### [FOUND:PRICE-LADDER] Start under-priced and ratchet up until resistance appears
**Move:** Deliberately give away more value than you capture at first to protect early close rates, raise the ask on each successive batch of conversations, and stop climbing when price visibly drags on close rates.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF and early PMF, where willingness-to-pay information is the objective.
**AVOID WHEN:** contracts are long and reference-priced, or the buyer set is small enough that early prices become permanent market anchors.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin's $99→$199→$299→$399→$499/month ladder, confounded by simultaneous product improvement (author says so).

### [FOUND:GRANDFATHER-CLOSE] Grandfather early customers — and use it as a buy-now lever
**Move:** When raising prices, leave early customers at their original rate, and tell active prospects that today's price locks in if they commit now.
**APPLY WHEN:** an announced or expected price increase; deals stalled on timing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the increase isn't real — a fabricated deadline is manipulation the book's own consultative framing doesn't support.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as TalentBin practice.

### [FOUND:COMPARATOR-PRICE] Anchor price and payment terms on the incumbent the buyer already buys
**Move:** Find products the buyer already pays for in this budget line, copy their pricing unit (per seat, per volume) and commercial terms (annual, up front) because the market is trained on them, then position your price against that anchor with a computable value gap.
**APPLY WHEN:** an established comparator exists and the buyer has a mental price for the category.
**AVOID WHEN:** no comparator exists, or the incumbent's model actively misprices your value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin priced per seat, annual up front, mirroring LinkedIn Recruiter and Dice.

### [FOUND:ONE-INNOVATION] Don't innovate on pricing model and product at once
**Move:** If the product is the novel thing, adopt the category's conventional pricing structure; assume the incumbent's model encodes reasons you haven't discovered.
**APPLY WHEN:** technically differentiated product inside an existing budget category.
**AVOID WHEN:** the pricing model is the wedge — then hold product novelty constant instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as preference, no supporting cases.

### [FOUND:ROI-PRICE] Price off a defensible ROI model, not off realized value
**Move:** Estimate value delivered per unit of usage from your own data, then set a fixed price capturing a fraction of it. Do not charge a percentage of value actually recovered — instrumenting it is hard, it removes the usage incentive, and it rewards hiding captured value.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have enough data to model value per unit, or can run experiments to establish it.
**AVOID WHEN:** too early for a real ROI case — blend with comparator pricing instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — HIRABL's model described from their submission dataset.

### [FOUND:THRESHOLD-ENTRY] Price under a purchasing threshold so someone can just buy it
**Move:** Check what an individual can expense, a manager can put on a card, or a department can cover discretionarily, and set entry pricing under one of those lines; pair with value-aligned metering so price grows with realized value.
**APPLY WHEN:** land-and-expand, trial/freemium motions, self-service-adjacent SaaS.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product is inherently org-wide (an HRIS) and cannot be bought by one team.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Yesware, LinkedIn, Box, Slack, Dropbox, Marketo/Eloqua/Act-On/HubSpot cited as observed patterns.

### [FOUND:ELEVATOR-DRILL] Rehearse the narrative as an elevator pitch, then expand on demand
**Move:** Compress the whole narrative into a cocktail-party version for someone who knows the space, deliver it, and let follow-up questions dictate which section you zoom into; write the long form once to check every component exists.
**APPLY WHEN:** the narrative is drafted and needs stress-testing before collateral is built.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating the written version as a script to recite verbatim.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as an exercise.

### Stage 2 — Collateral construction

### [FOUND:SPEED-OVER-POLISH] Ship rough collateral immediately
**Move:** Prefer ten five-minute laptop screencasts with verbal stumbles over one polished explainer; build the objection slide the day you hear the objection; never let design or approval cycles gate a new asset.
**APPLY WHEN:** early stage, product and messaging still moving.
**AVOID WHEN:** regulated categories with genuine legal review; note polish over an empty narrative is called worse than nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing practice; mechanism (feedback-loop length) argued, not measured.

### [FOUND:MVP-DECK] Start with one slide per narrative step, then zoom in
**Move:** Map the deck directly onto the narrative — problem, who has it, costs, existing solutions and failures, what changed, how it works, proof, price — one bulleted slide each; later split any section into sub-chapters while keeping the summary versions.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the first deck, or extending one under time pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** deleting the coarse versions as you elaborate — you lose the ability to present at the right altitude.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin's deck evolution described concretely.

### [FOUND:MASTER-DECK] Keep one canonical master deck and fork presentation copies
**Move:** Maintain a single large master file holding every slide ever built, refactored continuously; fork a copy to the sharing tool for each actual use. Nothing is destroyed, retired slides stay recoverable, reps can't hack the canonical source.
**APPLY WHEN:** collateral volume is growing and more than one person presents.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication stated; the author calls it inelegant, a stopgap absent version control for presentations.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's own file, maintained from company inception.

### [FOUND:TEMPLATE-THEN-DESIGNER] Reach production value through templates; hire design only after MVP
**Move:** Reuse the fundraising deck's theme; define reusable slide templates and clone them; add cheap polish (logo bullets, drop shadows, GIFs, deliberate hierarchy). Only then pay a freelance designer to glaze the templates so future slides inherit it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deck exists and is in use; polish is now a marginal improvement.
**AVOID WHEN:** design becomes a gate on producing a new slide — explicitly forbidden.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's build sequence.

### [FOUND:PROSPECT-IN-THE-SLIDE] Drop the prospect's own data into the deck
**Move:** Template a few slides so prospect-specific material pastes in before the call — your product overlaid on their site, third-party evidence of their pain, a live search for their company.
**APPLY WHEN:** prospect-specific evidence is cheap to gather in pre-call planning.
**AVOID WHEN:** the customization surfaces something embarrassing you can't handle, or per-deal prep is uneconomic at your ACV.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one named startup (LifeGuides) the author worked with.

### [FOUND:PROBLEM-VALIDATORS] Third-party validation of the problem
**Move:** Alongside your own problem statement, show outside corroboration — analyst statistics, press clippings, market data — so the prospect sees that many others already agree the problem is real. Anecdata slides are an acceptable substitute when hard data is missing.
**APPLY WHEN:** first presentation to a buyer who has not named the pain; new vertical where your credibility is thin.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already opened with the pain in their own words.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin healthcare-market deck.

### [FOUND:ALT-BUCKET] Group the incumbents into buckets on one slide
**Move:** Put the pervasive market-standard alternatives into a few labelled buckets with a one-line summary of each bucket's shortcoming, so you control the framing of how you differ.
**APPLY WHEN:** a small set of "everyone uses this" solutions dominates and the prospect almost certainly runs one.
**AVOID WHEN:** discovery shows they don't use those standards — skip the slides; don't raise competitors the prospect hasn't raised.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin slides on job boards, resume databases, LinkedIn, staffing agencies.

### [FOUND:QUANT-PROOF] Superiority proof bucketed by value proposition, granular plus rolled-up

### Stage 3 — Prospecting, ICP and list building

### [FOUND:ICP-METADATA] Compress the narrative into queryable characteristics
**Move:** Restate the ICP as a sentence of concrete searchable metadata, separating hard prerequisites (without which the product cannot work) from demand-magnitude signals and ability-to-pay signals (existing spend on an incumbent tells you the budget you can take a bite of).
**APPLY WHEN:** before any outbound; also as a qualification filter for inbound and referrals.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have too few real customers to know which characteristics matter — expect to revise after the first cohort.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin and Immediately worked examples plus iteration.

### [FOUND:NO-RELATIONSHIP-TARGETING] Refuse relationship-based targeting
**Move:** Do not prioritize prospects because they're reachable. Broken-window test: a stranger who can fix a problem you have is welcome; a friend fixing a problem you don't have is not a commercial conversation. When a friend says "I could use that," run the qualifying questions and be willing to decline.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, and especially when fear of cold rejection is steering the list.
**AVOID WHEN:** a known contact genuinely matches the ICP — the rule is about need, not about excluding acquaintances.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's framing plus an illustrative cost calculation.

### [FOUND:DEMAND-SCORE] Score accounts by demand magnitude, not just fit
**Move:** Beyond minimum viable criteria, build an attractiveness score from combined signals (potential seats, volume of the painful activity, sophistication, ability to pay, recent funding) and rank the list so the highest-pain accounts get your hours.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have more qualified accounts than selling hours.
**AVOID WHEN:** the score's inputs are unverified guesses — then it is false precision.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin's scoring dimensions and minimum criteria (≥1 recruiter, ≥3 open engineering roles).

### [FOUND:HUNT-DEER] Target "deer," skewing to bigger deer
**Move:** Prefer mid-sized accounts — large enough to carry real pain and absorb new tooling, small enough to decide without heavy change management — and within that band favour the ones with most pain per unit of size.
**APPLY WHEN:** choosing the initial 50–100 target list.
**AVOID WHEN:** your product only works at enterprise scale, or is inherently self-serve for very small accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — rabbits/deer/elephants framing with TalentBin and Immediately examples.

### [FOUND:GEO-LOCAL-FIRST] Start in your own geography
**Move:** Build the first list locally even if deal size implies inside sales — same time zone plus the option of an on-site visit is worth a lot early. Failing to find 50–100 matching accounts locally is itself a signal about your market or your location.
**APPLY WHEN:** first cohort of prospects.
**AVOID WHEN:** the ICP is highly specialized and geographically dispersed by nature.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated preference with a heuristic attached.

### [FOUND:SOURCE-ENTRY-CHOICE] Choose people-first or company-first by your hardest gate
**Move:** Enter sourcing through whichever characteristic is the absolute prerequisite — a required role means start from title search and roll up to employer; a required tech stack means start from technology detection then size the user base by title. Then flip to the other data type to append demand signifiers.
**APPLY WHEN:** setting up the sourcing workflow.
**AVOID WHEN:** neither characteristic is externally visible — then discovery carries the qualification load.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin (people-first) vs Immediately (technology-first).

### [FOUND:PROXY-SIGNALS] Use correlated proxies and paid-tier tells
**Move:** Where the true demand signal is invisible, source on correlates: technology detected on the site, a premium profile on a marketplace, current or historical job postings, competitor indexes. Paying for one adjacent service is itself evidence of willingness to pay.
**APPLY WHEN:** the definitive characteristic is private.
**AVOID WHEN:** the correlation is weak — you fill the funnel with plausible non-buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — multiple worked sourcing examples; named tools date fast.

### [FOUND:HIDDEN-CHAR-ASK] Keep unobservable characteristics in the ICP and ask for them
**Move:** Don't drop a decisive characteristic just because it isn't public; source on its correlates, then confirm it explicitly via discovery questions or the inbound lead-capture form.
**APPLY WHEN:** the characteristic strongly predicts success but isn't published.
**AVOID WHEN:** asking early would feel like an interrogation before you've earned the conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin's incumbent-seat characteristic.

### [FOUND:ONE-SOURCE-DISCIPLINE] Source from one channel; multi-source only for enrichment
**Move:** If one source reliably yields enough good accounts, stay on it. Several sources to enrich an already-found account is fine; several to find accounts is usually prospecting ADHD dressed as thoroughness.
**APPLY WHEN:** a working source exists with sufficient volume.
**AVOID WHEN:** the source is genuinely exhausted or systematically biased against part of your market.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as discipline, no data.

### [FOUND:NO-BOUGHT-LISTS] Build the list manually; avoid purchased marketing lists
**Move:** For the first hundred targets, do it by hand. Bought lists are stale and thinly modelled, so targeting suffers and you burn time on dead addresses; manual work teaches you which characteristics actually matter.
**APPLY WHEN:** first cohort, and whenever you're re-tuning the ICP.
**AVOID WHEN:** later scale, where enrichment vendors and refresh services earn their keep.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's stated dim view plus the learning-value argument.

### [FOUND:SHEET-SCHEMA] Structure prospect metadata into merge-ready columns
**Move:** Keep prospects in a spreadsheet with a distinct column for every fact you might later query or merge on — the rating, the profile link, a link to a specific damning review — stored as data fields, not prose, so outreach carries account-specific evidence in subject line and body.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the initial 50–100 list; wanting automation leverage without generic-blast tone.
**AVOID WHEN:** the signifier data is stale or unverified — a wrong merged number destroys the "I researched you" effect entirely; sensitive fields need care.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — LifeGuides rating mail-merge example; standard practice behind the cited response-rate deltas.

### [FOUND:CASCADE-CONTACTS] Capture the full cascade of decision-maker titles
**Move:** Rather than betting on one title, collect every plausible decision-maker in the hierarchy with a Boolean title search scoped to the account, since which title owns the problem varies with company stage; later capture users too, for drip.
**APPLY WHEN:** you don't yet know which title owns the budget in this segment.
**AVOID WHEN:** blasting all of them simultaneously — that is what makes you look like spam internally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated preference plus a sample Boolean query.

### [FOUND:COMPLEMENTARY-DM] Work the complementary decision-maker
**Move:** Identify the internal customer of your primary decision-maker — the one with the downstream pain — and either get referred inward through them or convince them and jointly make the case.
**APPLY WHEN:** the primary contact is unreachable or unmotivated and the downstream pain is acute.
**AVOID WHEN:** going around a champion would embarrass them internally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning without cited outcomes.

### [FOUND:CEO-TOPDOWN-CAUTION] Top-down CEO outreach — use with eyes open
**Move:** The Predictable Revenue "Cold-Calling 2.0" pattern emails the CEO a crisp dollars-and-cents case and asks to be pointed at the right delegate. Kazanjy's counsel: prefer identifying and appealing to the likely decision-maker directly, and cascade upward only if that fails.
**APPLY WHEN:** you cannot determine the owner, or the org is small enough that the CEO is that owner.
**AVOID WHEN:** senior executives in your market run assistant-mediated inbox triage; the tactic's advantage was largely a pre-LinkedIn artifact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to Ross & Tyler with the author's counterargument; no data either way.

### [FOUND:BOTTOM-UP-DEFER] Bottom-up user-led entry, deferred
**Move:** Convince individual users the product makes their working life better and enlist them to argue upward to budget holders; free tiers function as lead generation, with signups as the account signal that triggers a sale into the organization.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product is usable standalone by an individual or small team and later expands.
**AVOID WHEN:** early founder-led selling — the book explicitly defers this as a later, scaled motion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — Box, Yammer, Slack, Yesware cited as patterns, not measured.

### [FOUND:WARM-PATH-AFTER-QUALIFY] Check for an intro path only after the account qualifies
**Move:** Once the account is qualified and the right person identified, then look for a shared connection — a mutual contact, or anyone inside who could forward with a favourable comment — recorded as a "potential intros" column. The deliberate inverse of choosing accounts because you know someone.
**APPLY WHEN:** the account passes ICP and you're deciding the first touch.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "connection" is a nominal social-graph link with no real relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's stated sequencing.

### [FOUND:EMAIL-PATTERN-FORM] Find or form the email address yourself
**Move:** Search first (name plus "email address", personal sites, profiles). Failing that, infer the company's address pattern from a known employee address, permute combinations, and validate by seeing which resolves to a real profile. Do it manually early: unobtainable decision-maker emails are a go-to-market problem you want to discover now.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the first outreach list yourself.
**AVOID WHEN:** market norms or privacy/anti-spam law make unsolicited mail to inferred or personal addresses a legal or reputational risk — the book waves this off as overblown, which is a jurisdiction-dependent claim, not a settled one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's method; named validation tools are dated.

### [FOUND:PHONE-FOR-LOCAL] Phone-first for local businesses
**Move:** Default to email-before-phone, but invert it for local/SMB go-to-markets where decision-maker email is hard to obtain — capture direct dials and the switchboard and reach out by phone.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling into restaurants, clinics, trades and similar local businesses.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a corporate knowledge worker reachable by email.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — pattern attributed to local-business go-to-markets (Yelp, Groupon, GrubHub, Redbeacon).

### [FOUND:EARLYCRM] Minimum viable system of record
**Move:** Track every prospect as a row with deal-state metadata columns — a spreadsheet is fine at first-dozen scale, then a beginner CRM for structure on who is in what state. Assume eventual migration to a full CRM; do not over-configure early.
**APPLY WHEN:** solo founder, first tens of prospects, more conversations in flight than memory holds.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already need activity reporting, win-rate or financial roll-ups, or a team shares the pipeline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated setup pattern across early-stage teams; tool list ages fast.

### Stage 4 — Outbound email, sequencing and warm paths

### [FOUND:COLD-PAIN-SPECIFIC] Pain-specific cold email with an explicit ask
**Move:** Write from the recipient's point of view: name the specific pain you know they have (the exact roles on their careers page, not the abstract category), give two or three concrete superiority claims with numbers, and close with a direct ask for a 20–30 minute slot plus alternate reply paths.
**APPLY WHEN:** first-touch outbound to a prospect you actually pre-qualified.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot evidence the pain — the specificity then reads as fake personalization.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — templates run at TalentBin/Monster scale.

### [FOUND:SUBJ-QUALIFY] Subject line that proves you did the research
**Move:** Customize the subject so it carries qualification evidence — a signal the message was built for this recipient (their hiring stack, their public rating) rather than blasted.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outbound where the open is the bottleneck.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "evidence" is a merge field that will render wrong or stale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — sample subject lines from TalentBin and LifeGuides.

### [FOUND:CLICK-TARGETS] Plant instrumented click targets inside the email
**Move:** Deliberately embed one or two things worth clicking — a hyperlinked video thumbnail, an infographic, a custom screenshot, your website — both to persuade and to generate the interest signal you will act on.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have tracking in place and collateral that actually explains the value.
**AVOID WHEN:** heavy link use trips spam filters or pushes the mail into a promotional look; or the recipient's environment strips tracking, making the signal meaningless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — author's stated preference for video thumbnails, carried over from the materials chapter.

### [FOUND:PLAINTEXT-ONLY] Keep templates plain text
**Move:** Strip marketing design — no logos or HTML chrome; text only, with screenshots or slides as the sole permitted visuals. Designed email signals a robot blast, the opposite of the qualified consultative outreach you're claiming to send.
**APPLY WHEN:** all founder-led outbound.
**AVOID WHEN:** a brand-driven marketing nurture stream where design is expected.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule without data.

### [FOUND:DRIP-SPLIT] Chop the narrative into a one-thought drip series
**Move:** Design templates from the start as a cadence of short standalone messages, each carrying a single idea plus the same call to action: open with a headline ROI number, then the main value buckets, then one email per bucket, then a customer-success email.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold outbound to busy decision-makers where a single touch rarely converts and you have enough distinct content to fill the slots.
**AVOID WHEN:** one thin idea stretched across seven emails — it reads as nagging; or when the buyer has explicitly declined.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin bucket-by-bucket series plus vendor campaign data cited by the author.

### [FOUND:WARM-FWD-PACKET] Warm intro via a forwardable packet, not a direct intro
**Move:** Two steps. Ask the intermediary whether they'd forward something — explaining the target, the verified connection, why you want to engage and why it benefits the target, with social upside for the introducer. Then hand them a ready-to-forward message carrying your pitch and a call to action for the target to reply to you directly, so the referrer is only a reputation layer. Track the forward with a pixel; if CC'd in, move the referrer to BCC and propose times.
**APPLY WHEN:** a shared connection exists at or near a qualified target account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the connection is nominal — burning a weak tie costs the tie and looks presumptuous; also don't expect the referrer to compose the message themselves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's full described sequence, given as standing practice.

### [FOUND:CUSTOMASSET] Prospect-specific screenshot or micro-video as the click target
**Move:** Record a short screen capture or annotate a screenshot built around the prospect's own site or data, narrate it as if speaking to them, and embed it as the click target in the first email — a "video voice mail."
**APPLY WHEN:** high-value named accounts where five minutes of investment is affordable; first touch in a sequence.
**AVOID WHEN:** long-tail lists where per-prospect minutes don't pay back; or on every message in the sequence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

### Stage 5 — Calling, appointment setting and first-touch objections

### [FOUND:CALL-BULLETS] Guidepost bullets, not a read-aloud script
**Move:** Build a 30–90 second bulleted reformatting of the core narrative that a founder can improvise around, distinct from the rigid script needed once you have ten-plus reps; include more content than any one call will use.
**APPLY WHEN:** founder or first market-development person making calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have a large rep team needing consistency and QA — that is a different artifact.

### [FOUND:NUMBER-OPENER] Open with a concrete found-money number
**Move:** State in the first breath what you do and a hard result across a known customer base ("over 4,200 missed fees found across 120 customers last year"), then ask for the meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** your product recovers or protects measurable money and you have real aggregate numbers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the number is unaudited or not comparable to this prospect's situation.

### [FOUND:CATEGORY-QUESTION] Diagnostic question that qualifies and educates
**Move:** After naming the problem you solve, ask whether they're familiar with it or have experienced it; the answer both qualifies them and bridges to proposing the demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** the problem has a name the buyer may or may not know.
**AVOID WHEN:** the question is rhetorical bait rather than a real qualification check.

### [FOUND:DEFER-TO-DEMO] Defer detail and feature questions, then re-ask for the slot
**Move:** On a feature or price question during appointment setting, acknowledge it, compliment their command of the space, explain it's best covered in the short prepared session, add a one-line partial answer where fair, then immediately offer two named days — people get more out of a scheduled block than piecemeal Q&A.
**APPLY WHEN:** the caller's job is booking, not closing; the question would consume the call ("demo by a thousand cuts").
**AVOID WHEN:** a single blocking question genuinely decides fit — answer it honestly, then close; repeated deflection of a direct pricing question reads as evasive.

### [FOUND:SOFT-NO-DEFLECT] Handling a soft "not interested"
**Move:** Answer a reflexive "not interested" by asserting you wouldn't be calling if you didn't believe it would help them hire faster or make money, argue the technology is becoming standard, then re-ask for the demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** the refusal is reflexive and arrives before the prospect has heard the value.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect has understood and declined. The inevitability framing is manufactured FOMO — unfalsifiable when made, and pushing it after a considered no is manipulation, not persistence.

### [FOUND:REJECTMAIL] Templated post-rejection email into nurture
**Move:** After a rejected call or a firm no, accept that the timing is wrong and send a templated follow-up blending the original value argument with metrics and the conciliatory language from your retreat; promise materials, still request a slot, and hand the prospect to the drip for a check-back in about a month.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after any rejected live call where the objection is timing, not fit.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect asked not to be contacted, or fails your ICP — then it is churn-inducing noise and in some jurisdictions a compliance problem.

### [FOUND:INCUMBENT] "We already use / is this like <competitor>?" → self-qualification

**APPLY WHEN:** every non-connect on a targeted account; cold calling where connect rates are low.

### [FOUND:DAYCYCLE] Call inside the buyer's day cycle
**Move:** Derive call windows from how the target actually works — early morning and end of day for office workers, mid-afternoon lull for restaurants — and follow the productive hour west across time zones.
**APPLY WHEN:** any volume dialing, especially against a homogeneous profile.
**AVOID WHEN:** calling into operational peak hours — it actively damages the relationship.

### [FOUND:AFTERHOURS] Late dial to bypass the gatekeeper
**Move:** Call senior executives after normal hours, when assistants have left and the executive may pick up their own line.
**APPLY WHEN:** the target is a senior exec behind a screening layer.
**AVOID WHEN:** the culture reads an after-hours call as intrusive, or the target is not desk-bound.

### [FOUND:SIGNALCALL] Call on the engagement signal
**Move:** Prioritize the day's dials by who opened or clicked yesterday's drip; better, call within minutes of a live open alert so your message is on screen when the phone rings.

### [FOUND:GATEHERO] Gatekeeper hero/doghouse pitch
**Move:** Give the gatekeeper a one-sentence quantified value statement they can evaluate without authority, and frame passing you along as what makes them look good — with the unstated flip side that blocking a valuable solution is a bad look. **AVOID WHEN:** you would have to lie ("is he expecting your call?") or press unpleasantly — both are called self-defeating.

### [FOUND:NOLIVEMSG] Decline the "I'll pass a message" offer
**Move:** When a gatekeeper offers to take a written message, redirect politely — say the content is involved, ask for the contact's email so you can send it directly, or ask to be patched to voicemail while they note only name, number and headline benefit.
**APPLY WHEN:** switchboard interaction where the gatekeeper is cooperative.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already hold the contact's email — then just ask for the voicemail transfer.

### [FOUND:THIRTYSEC] The thirty-second appointment pitch
**Move:** On an unexpected live connect, state the pain you believe they have, that the goal is a short scheduled conversation to understand it and show them something useful, and ask for ten to twenty minutes — explicitly not selling the product on this call.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live pickup, whether or not they recall your emails.
**AVOID WHEN:** you drift into demoing on the spot without agreeing to a real meeting.

### [FOUND:BOOKLIVE] Book the calendar while still on the phone
**Move:** On a yes, ask whether they're in front of their calendar, propose two named slots, state the duration, say you'll send an invite and ask them to accept it. Capture email, direct and mobile numbers in the same breath. Keep the meeting a couple of days out, never more than a week.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect agrees to a follow-up conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** never — "I'll email you some times" is named as the rookie error that loses the meeting.

### [FOUND:PULLUSERS] Pull users and influencers onto the booked call

**APPLY WHEN:** the decision-maker is enthusiastic and the account has multiple potential users.
**AVOID WHEN:** it dilutes an already-qualified single-owner deal or delays the meeting; never as a substitute for reaching the real decision-maker.

### [FOUND:HOTLITE] Hot transfer as "demo lite"
**Move:** When the prospect wants the demo right now, take it but frame it as a preview: do discovery, show enough to be useful, name what a full prepared session would add, and use that to sell the scheduled demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** unexpected live availability and interest.
**AVOID WHEN:** you compensate by pre-planning every cold call — explicitly rejected, since most dials never connect.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

### [FOUND:PREEMPT-CATEGORY] Pre-empt the pattern rejection

**APPLY WHEN:** you can hear the same rejection recurring across calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** the rejection is idiosyncratic rather than patterned — then it's an objection to handle, not a script change.

### [FOUND:GRACERETREAT] Graceful retreat with implied inevitability
**Move:** On hard rejection, restate that your aim was to understand a specific named pain, ask once more for a scheduled fifteen minutes at a better time; if refused, withdraw on your own terms, stating conviction and that you'll take responsibility for reconnecting. Never editorialize about their current approach.
**APPLY WHEN:** aggressive or flat rejection on a well-qualified account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the account is genuinely disqualified — persistence there is waste, not conviction.

### [FOUND:LATERTRADE] "Call me later" → thirty-second trade

**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot make a fast evidence-backed relevance case — the trade only pays if the next thirty seconds land.

### [FOUND:NOBUDGET] "No budget" at first touch → defer the money question

**AVOID WHEN:** the organization genuinely cannot buy at all — pitching a non-buyer is waste.

### [FOUND:INVITEHYGIENE] Meeting invite construction

### [FOUND:DEMOCTA] Obvious demo call-to-action plus structured form
**Move:** Replace a bare mailto with a prominent "Request Demo" button, ideally persistent as the page scrolls, pointing at a simple structured form verified working on mobile; also link the form in outreach email footers.

### [FOUND:SIXTRIES] Six attempts, then move on
**Move:** Work each inbound lead with repeated calls, voicemails and templated emails, logging every attempt; stop after roughly six and reinvest the time.
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound leads that didn't answer the first callback.
**AVOID WHEN:** giving up after one or two attempts, or grinding well past six.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — attributed to InsideSales.com / MIT Sloan contact-rate data as relayed by the author.

### [FOUND:LIGHTDISC] Lightweight discovery on the inbound callback
**Move:** Before booking the demo, verify the signifiers the form captured, probe size of prize, and establish who else should attend and who signs — deferring deep discovery to the pitch.
**APPLY WHEN:** you reach an inbound lead live.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns into a full discovery call that consumes the reason for the demo.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing pre-demo routine.

### [FOUND:QUALACCOUNT] Qualify the account, not the lead
**Move:** When the requester looks unqualified, first determine whether the organization is unqualified or merely this contact; if the account qualifies, work with them to loop in the right people; if not, decline candidly and explain who the product is for.
**APPLY WHEN:** word of mouth pulls in adjacent, unfit requesters.
**AVOID WHEN:** never — selling into a known-unfit account produces an unhappy customer, support drag and negative word of mouth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=any

### [FOUND:HARDQUAL] Hard-qualifier email
**Move:** For leads clearly unfit from form data, skip the call and send a short email naming who the product is for, stating your read of why they may not fit, inviting correction, and including a value-carrying asset they might pass to a colleague who does fit.
**APPLY WHEN:** clear negative signal from submitted data.
**AVOID WHEN:** the signal is ambiguous — a wrong hard-qualifier discards a real deal.

### Stage 7 — Pitch preparation

### [FOUND:PVCB] Maximize the three multiplicative terms
**Move:** Before and during the pitch ask which term is weakest — potential value (wrong account), comprehension (they don't get it), or belief (they doubt they'll realize it) — and apply the matching lever: better targeting, clearer materials, or proof points and pilots.
**APPLY WHEN:** diagnosing a stalled or unconvincing pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as post-hoc rationalization instead of a pre-call plan; it is a mental model, not a measurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the author's own framework, stated without empirical support.

### [FOUND:INPERSONFIRST] Default to in-person for the first customers
**Move:** For early deals meet face to face even though it caps you at one or two meetings a day, prioritize geographically near accounts for that reason, batch travel by lining up several targets per city, and continue digitally after the first in-person meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** first customers, unproven pitch, high learning value per meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** at scale with a proven pitch and small deal sizes, where inside-sales throughput wins; or where flying to a single prospect isn't justified.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — throughput figures given from field experience.

### [FOUND:PRIZESIZE] Compute pain signifiers and size of prize before the call
**Move:** Enter the meeting knowing the observable demand signifiers, the per-unit value your solution delivers, and how much the account could consume — then steer to a realistic first purchase rather than a minimum order.
**APPLY WHEN:** all pre-call planning.
**AVOID WHEN:** pushing org-wide adoption from a young company — know the ceiling without selling to it immediately.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin/HIRABL/New Relic worked examples.

### [FOUND:STACKCHECK] Read the tech stack, incumbents and capacity to pay
**Move:** Use technology-detection tooling and public funding signals to learn how they solve the problem today, whether they already pay for it, and whether money exists; prepare the differentiation argument against any incumbent found.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-call planning on any researchable account.
**AVOID WHEN:** detection is unreliable for the category, or funding is treated as proof of willingness rather than capacity.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing research checklist.

### [FOUND:STAKEMAP] Map stakeholders and speak each one's value language
**Move:** Identify users, the decision-maker's boss, internal customers and where relevant the CEO, classify each as end user / first-line manager / second-line or CXO, and prepare the argument in that register: users buy less drudgery and career upside, managers buy ROI and headcount deferral, CXOs buy revenue, company value and cash — up to translating annual savings into valuation impact via a multiple. Reference them by name in the meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** any multi-meeting deal, mixed-seniority rooms, or entering above or below the true decision-maker.
**AVOID WHEN:** pitching one universal argument and assuming ROI language works on end users, who aren't spending their own money; and skip the valuation-multiple argument at large enterprises where one tool's savings are noise (scoped to roughly ≤500 people).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked examples across TalentBin/Immediately-style deals; the three-register taxonomy is attributed to Skip Miller without data.

### [FOUND:CREDTHREAT] Implied access as leverage on the intermediary
**Move:** Let the contact below the decision-maker understand that you know who the decision-maker is and could reach them, while offering the contact the role of the person who brought the innovation in — hero framing forward, missed-opportunity framing implied.
**APPLY WHEN:** you entered a level below the buyer and need progression, not blockage.
**AVOID WHEN:** stated as an explicit threat, or when the contact is a genuine champion whose trust is worth more than the shortcut — the most coercive lever in the book, kept unstated by design and pure pressure on an unqualified account.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeatedly recommended, framed as "not stated, but mildly implied".

### [FOUND:CONTEXT-DEMO] Demo against the prospect's own public context
**Move:** Before the call pull the prospect's public facts — careers page, listed roles and locations, public ratings, their product page with your call-to-action injected via dev tools — and run the demo against those exact inputs, saying so out loud, so you propose the example rather than asking for one.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live demo where public data about the prospect exists and the customization is cheap and visibly theirs.
**AVOID WHEN:** referencing their data would feel intrusive in a privacy-sensitive market, or the artifact is embarrassing enough to put them on the defensive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard TalentBin rep practice plus several named product examples.

### [FOUND:ICEBREAK] Researched icebreaker with a planned exit ramp
**Move:** Pick one rapport hook in advance — shared connection (especially an existing customer), employer, school, location, sports, side project — open with it for two or three minutes, then deliberately escalate from personal to professional ("how long have you been recruiting?") as the segue into business.
**APPLY WHEN:** the first minutes of any pitch, most critically on remote calls where rapport doesn't build itself.
**AVOID WHEN:** politics and religion, always; a shared contact who thinks poorly of you; anything so thin it reads as filler; or when small talk starts eating the pitch's time budget.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — with explicit exclusions and an illustrative transition line.

### [FOUND:UNKNOWNS] Write the known-unknowns list
**Move:** During prep, list explicitly what you could not find, so discovery targets exactly those gaps; over time distill it into a pre-call planning checklist and a discovery question checklist stored in the CRM.
**APPLY WHEN:** every pre-call planning block.
**AVOID WHEN:** not contraindicated; the failure mode is unstructured discovery that re-asks what you already knew.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the compounding output of repeated demos.

### [FOUND:PITCHGOAL] State one goal per meeting
**Move:** Before the call name the outcome — win consideration of a purchase, convert an internal customer into a sponsor, or secure a second meeting with the actual decision-maker — and steer back toward it as the conversation wanders.
**APPLY WHEN:** every pitch, especially with a non-decision-maker in the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied rigidly at the cost of following genuinely new information.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing pre-call step.

### [FOUND:PRECALL-ENERGY] Pre-call state management
**Move:** Deliberately raise physical energy before the call — stand, move, take calls standing — on the premise that projected confidence carries the recommendation.
**APPLY WHEN:** phone and screen-share pitches especially.
**AVOID WHEN:** none stated; treat the confidence-projection claim as unproven.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — personal habit, no support offered.

### [FOUND:PITCHKIT] Own your presentation infrastructure
**Move:** Carry the full kit and assume the prospect's environment fails: laptop with deck and demo loaded, all display adapters, your own internet via hotspot, a paper notebook, and an offline fallback with key screens pre-loaded. For digital pitches use no-download screen share and a headset; with a single prospect, presenting side by side off the laptop beats a projector.
**APPLY WHEN:** every pitch, on-site especially.
**AVOID WHEN:** not contraindicated — the rationale is that the prospect blames you for their own broken projector or WiFi.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — detailed operational checklist; tool names date quickly.

### [FOUND:PITCHFORMAT] Fixed pitch sequence, slides for the model and demo for the proof
**Move:** Run the meeting in a consistent order — brief pleasantries, discovery, slides, live demo, success proof points, then pricing — because discovery supplies the ROI argument and slides install the mental model needed to interpret the demo. Open the slide portion with what slides carry better than a product (pain, why current solutions fail, conceptual framing, proof), hold feature/value slides back as post-demo leave-behinds, and transition with an explicit agreement request.
**APPLY WHEN:** first substantive pitch meetings.
**AVOID WHEN:** discovery reveals the pain is absent — end the meeting rather than presenting; if a live demo is genuinely impossible (rare — engineer around it, even with dummy data), feature slides carry that load.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's consistently best-performing format, argued against the "jump straight into the product" school.

### [FOUND:SPLITDISCO] Split discovery from the demo (or deliberately don't)
**Move:** Decide up front whether discovery gets its own short call. Split when the product needs a long demo to be understood; keep it combined when the demo runs about ten minutes or research already confirms the qualifying signals. For the first few dozen demos keep them combined and long.
**APPLY WHEN:** demo volume is rising and calendar time is the binding constraint; or conversely you're in your first dozens of pitches.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product demos in ten minutes or signifiers are externally observable — coordination cost plus reschedule risk exceeds the benefit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author's operating rule; the no-show rate is self-reported.

### Stage 8 — Discovery

### [FOUND:DISCOFRAME] Frame discovery as a service to the prospect
**Move:** Open the discovery block by saying you like to start with questions so you can understand their day-to-day and focus the rest of the time on what will be valuable to them; allocate the first 5–10 minutes of a presentation slot.
**APPLY WHEN:** start of any presentation or demo, especially when combined and the prospect expects a walkthrough.
**AVOID WHEN:** you already ran a separate discovery call — cite what you learned rather than re-interrogating.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard opening across pitches.

### [FOUND:URGENCYQUAL] Qualify on pain and urgency, not on budget and timeline
**Move:** Run the checklist (problem exists, org has a buying process, decision-makers identified, urgency) but do not disqualify a new-category prospect for lacking a pre-existing budget line or timeline; substitute magnitude of problem and urgency to solve as the primary gates.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling a category the buyer did not know existed.
**AVOID WHEN:** the organization has no mechanism at all for spending money on tools — that is a disqualifier, not an objection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — argued from TalentBin's early market, where buyers knew the pain but not the category.

### [FOUND:LEADRESEARCH] Leading discovery from pre-call research
**Move:** Instead of asking whether a known condition exists, state the researched fact and attach a frustration question to it — cite their public rating, or the geographic spread of their reps, and ask whether that frustrates them. Validate the fact aloud so they know you know it.
**APPLY WHEN:** qualifying signals are publicly observable and you did the research.
**AVOID WHEN:** the researched "fact" is shaky — a wrong assertion at the open costs credibility for the rest of the call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked examples from LifeGuides, Immediately, TalentBin.

### [FOUND:PAINCASCADE] Cascade down the pain
**Move:** Start with volume and scope of the relevant activity, ask a leading "would you call that easy?", then step down: what do you use today, have you tried the obvious alternative, how well does it work, what does it cost in time. Normalize each step ("are you like most folks I talk to…") so admitting pain is low-status-risk, and record every answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** early discovery once basic fit is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** they answer "no" to the alternative-attempt question — the pain may not exist; stop cascading and re-check fit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — reconstructed TalentBin discovery ladder used repeatedly.

### [FOUND:DOWNSTREAMSTAKES] Surface the downstream consequence of not solving
**Move:** Ask why the goal matters and what happens if they miss it, so the prospect names their own exposure — a publicly committed CEO goal, a boss's expectation — rather than you asserting stakes.
**APPLY WHEN:** pain is confirmed but urgency is unclear.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is too new for a personal-consequence question to land as anything but pressure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Textio diversity-hiring example with an illustrative answer.

### [FOUND:FEATUREQMAP] One discovery question per feature
**Move:** Map each shipped feature to the pain it removes and write a discovery question that validates that pain exists here, so the answer sets up the feature you'll demo.
**APPLY WHEN:** building or revising the discovery question list.
**AVOID WHEN:** the feature isn't one you'll demo for this persona — you're spending discovery minutes on nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeatable construction method with LifeGuides examples.

### [FOUND:AUTHCOMMERCIALS] Ask the buying-process questions directly
**Move:** After pain is established, ask whether users of the solution exist and how many; who decides purchases in this area; whether they've bought something like this before; whether there's a formal evaluation window or discretionary budget outside it. Prior spend on the same problem is itself proof of a purchasing process.
**APPLY WHEN:** pain and urgency check out and you're about to invest demo time.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skip out of squeamishness — but if the contact lacks authority that's fine; the goal is to identify who has it, not abandon them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard qualification practice with worked phrasing.

### [FOUND:DISCODOC] Keep discovery questions as a living document
**Move:** Maintain the discovery question list in a shared doc, revised as you learn, so it can be handed to the first reps you hire.
**APPLY WHEN:** from the first pitches onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable — treated as unconditional hygiene.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — good practice without supporting data.

### Stage 9 — Presentation and demo delivery

### [FOUND:USECASE-RANK] Order the demo by importance, not by product architecture
**Move:** Pick the use cases that are simultaneously most common, most important and most impressive, rank them, lead with the top one, then let the sequence follow the user's natural chronology through their job.
**APPLY WHEN:** every demo, especially short slots or calendars that run over.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect has stated a specific priority — theirs outranks your ranking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin's "Search, Qualify, Reach Out, Automate" demo run repeatedly.

### [FOUND:DONT-ASK-HARDEST] Research the pain instead of asking "what's hardest?"
**Move:** Choose the demo scenario yourself from research; asked cold, the buyer names their single hardest edge case, which is the wrong demo. Pick the highest-volume version of their pain, where the money is.
**APPLY WHEN:** public data lets you infer volume; discovery is possible before the demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly states a priority use case, or your research is thin enough that guessing misfires.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — TalentBin's "don't ask which role is hard to fill" example.

### [FOUND:PRELOAD-DATA-DEMO] Run the demo on data the prospect sends ahead
**Move:** A week ahead, ask the prospect for their own operational data; load it into a dedicated instance, run your analysis, open with a light framing of problem and value, then turn to the findings from their data and convert directly.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product produces a verifiable finding from customer data and the buyer will share pre-sale.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot handle their data securely or compliantly, or the analysis might return nothing — an empty result kills the deal live. Advanced motion; not having it is no reason to delay selling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — HIRABL's described process; the close rate is self-reported.

### [FOUND:DEMO-FEATURES-AS-REVENUE] Ask product for demo-enabling features
**Move:** When feeding requirements to product management, include features whose value is making the demo customizable — embedding the prospect's name or logo, importing their data — and argue for them on revenue grounds even with no post-purchase value.
**APPLY WHEN:** you influence the roadmap and demo conversion is a bottleneck.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would displace features that fix real churn drivers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning offered without data.

### [FOUND:WORKFLOW-NARRATIVE] Narrate the workflow with built-in pauses
**Move:** Walk the buyer through their real sequence of work using one realistic example that matches their needs, tying each step back to earlier ones; break the script into segments that leave room for discussion. The quality signal is the buyer volunteering how much this will help.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product sits inside a repeatable daily workflow.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product is a one-off event, or the buyer isn't the user.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the TalentBin script as run across many demos.

### [FOUND:REPEAT] Deliberate repetition of narrative pillars
**Move:** Return to the same handful of messaging buckets at every stage — pain slides, solution slides, proof slides, demoed features — explicitly connecting each back to its pillar; accept a level of repetition that would feel excessive in writing.
**APPLY WHEN:** any first presentation to a prospect new to the category.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect is a category veteran who has demonstrated command of the concepts — curate instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a learned adjustment from writing instincts, illustrated by the TalentBin demo script.

### [FOUND:PERMITNOTKNOW] Give permission not to know
**Move:** When checking whether the prospect knows a referenced concept, pre-absolve them: it's fine if they haven't heard of it, these are new or obscure, you didn't know them yourself before starting the company.
**APPLY WHEN:** referencing niche vocabulary, tools or market context the buyer may not know.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would read as condescending to a domain expert.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's habit on the TalentBin specialty-networks slide.

### [FOUND:SPECIFICCHECK] Replace "does that make sense?" with a specific question
**Move:** After each point ask something requiring a specific answer tied to that pain, feature or proof ("do your field reps have this same issue?"), which tests attention and comprehension while eliciting agreement; on phone calls also check literal attention, and periodically open the floor without judgment.
**APPLY WHEN:** every substantive point, especially on remote pitches where you can't see the prospect.
**AVOID WHEN:** never use the bare "does that make sense?" as your only check — it invites a face-saving "uh-huh".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated technique with multiple worked variants.

### [FOUND:ROLLDISCO] Rolling discovery through the presentation
**Move:** Distribute discovery questions across the presentation, asking each next to the slide or feature it relates to, rather than stacking a ten-minute interrogation at the front; when a question was already answered, cite their prior answer back and connect the matching feature to the problem in their own words.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have more discovery questions than the front block can hold, or a comprehension check reveals a gap.
**AVOID WHEN:** re-asking something already answered — that signals you weren't listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin response-rate examples used across pitches.

### [FOUND:AGREESCORE] Score agreement continuously
**Move:** At every point where agreement is plausible, use a leading question to make the prospect voice it — yes they have the pain, yes it's org-wide, yes they tried the alternative — and welcome the disagreements this surfaces. By the close the purchase should read as the obvious conclusion of a chain they already assented to.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout presentation and demo.
**AVOID WHEN:** the agreement is manufactured on points they haven't actually accepted — it collapses at the close as a late objection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's stated organizing principle for the pitch.

### [FOUND:PACING] Deliberate pacing and planned pauses
**Move:** Consciously slow delivery once you know the pitch well (familiarity and nerves both accelerate speech), enunciate, and build in pauses for questions and comprehension tests.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, doubly on phone pitches where the prospect has no facial information.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated from experience without supporting data.

### [FOUND:CURATE] Curate slides and features to the account
**Move:** Drop whole slides and skip demo features that won't resonate with this account and persona; double down on those that will; move non-linearly — backtrack when a comprehension check fails, jump to appendix on questions, skip ahead when they're already convinced.
**APPLY WHEN:** prospecting and discovery gave you real account context, or the prospect already knows the category and wants differentiators.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no account context — which itself indicates the prospecting/discovery work wasn't done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin example contrasting category-naive and category-savvy prospects.

### [FOUND:MICROCONTRACT] Micro-contracts before, during and after each step
**Move:** Before each activity — discovery questions, slides, demo, involving a new stakeholder — state what you want to do, why, and ask agreement that it's the right next step. Close every call by articulating deal state, what you will do and what they will do, and asking them to confirm it. Calendar next meetings in the moment, restate the contract when the next meeting opens, and if the prospect later diverges, name it plainly and ask whether the deal is still a priority.
**APPLY WHEN:** every stage transition from appointment-setting through implementation.
**AVOID WHEN:** never soften it into "I'll send you some information, let me know what you think" — the vagueness is what kills deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — credited to Sandler's "upfront contracts"; author reports using it throughout the funnel.

### [FOUND:OFFRAMP] Graceful disqualification mid-call
**Move:** When discovery shows no pain or no ability to pay, end the meeting early on good terms: say plainly that what you do doesn't look helpful, state specifically who you do help, offer slides or a demo video, and propose giving back the remaining thirty minutes.
**APPLY WHEN:** the account lacks pain or purchasing capability (not merely when your contact lacks authority — then partner toward the decision-maker).
**AVOID WHEN:** it happens often — that signals broken prequalification or an appointment-setting process putting junk on the calendar.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented with a full script and a credibility/referral rationale.

### [FOUND:REALDATADEMO] Demo on the prospect's live environment
**Move:** Wherever the product allows, run the demo against their real environment; if you have freemium usage inside their org, show what their own named employees are already doing plus the paid view they'd unlock. Treat "how will this be demoable with customer data?" as a product design question.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product can ingest or point at live customer data quickly (the Postini spam-filter redirect is the archetype).
**AVOID WHEN:** it can't be pointed at real data quickly — forcing it creates a broken demo.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — retold Postini story plus the freemium-instance pattern.

### [FOUND:DEMODATA] Maintain demo-ready data
**Move:** Curate the demo instance so every use case and proof point lands without excuses; treat any "ignore that" or "imagine if…" as a defect in your demo data; refresh it every time a feature ships, and escalate to engineering if the state can't be built by hand.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, especially before a feature's first demos.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable — bad demo data is an active trust liability implying you take shortcuts elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — author reports watching many demos fail on this.

### [FOUND:DEMOPRIORITY] Prioritize the features that matter to this buyer
**Move:** With only 15–30 minutes of actual product time, pre-select which features get the minutes based on prospecting and discovery, know in advance which script parts are cuttable, and tailor to stakeholder type as well as account.
**APPLY WHEN:** every demo, once discovery has produced specifics.
**AVOID WHEN:** you'd cut a core use case carrying the central point of the narrative.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=consultative

### [FOUND:PAD-THE-OPENING-PRICE] Build discount room into sticker and proposal

**AVOID WHEN:** value is not yet established — then it's a pricing objection, not a negotiation, and discounting papers over unproven value.

### Stage 19 — Scaling gates and sales management

### [FOUND:WIN-RATE-SCALE-GATE] Gate the first rep hire on win rate
**Move:** Measure the share of first meetings that eventually close won. Reliably in the 15–30% band: hire one or two reps and test whether they match you. **APPLY WHEN:** the founder has run enough first meetings for the rate to be meaningful.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is a handful of deals, or wins came from network relationships a hired rep cannot reproduce.

If sure, accelerate immediately: strong candidates leave the market fast and visible dithering makes your team question your closing ability.

### [FOUND:COMP-FROM-UNIT-ECONOMICS-AND-COMPS] Set pay from deal math plus analogous-company benchmarks

**Move:** Pay variable per appointment set and held (or per booking), leave upside uncapped so over-performers reveal what the true target should be, and avoid all-or-nothing gates — a rep who hits four of five meetings and gets nothing is demoralized. **APPLY WHEN:** setting SDR/appointment-setting comp with little historical attainment data.

### [FOUND:COVER-YOUR-OWN-COST-PLAN] Lemkin-style risk-shifted plan
**Move:** Offer a competitive base, pay no commission until the rep has covered roughly 125% of their own base, then pay roughly double the normal rate — pushing the entire cost of sales to the rep — with an accelerator for cash paid up front, all payable only on cash receipt, and bad debt landing on the rep.
**APPLY WHEN:** you want to hedge against low performers and bad hires while making strong upside available.
**AVOID WHEN:** ramping reps whose pipeline isn't built, or when you promised generosity on founder-assisted and force-majeure deals; presented as one option, not the default.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to Jason Lemkin via a SaaStr framework post, not the author's dataset.

### [FOUND:PRINCIPLED-TWO-STEP-OFFER] Verbal offer with stated rationale, then paper only on a verbal yes
**Move:** Email to book a call, then deliver the outcome by phone. If declining, say only that the team weighed input and concluded it wasn't a fit, and hold that line politely. If proceeding, state compensation and its reasoning proactively rather than asking what they want, and ask directly whether they'll sign if you send it — you only want to send an offer letter that will be signed, so it can't become leverage elsewhere. Prefer not to negotiate; sell development, progression, and if needed a scheduled six-month review.
**APPLY WHEN:** offering after a completed authentication process, especially when scaling many identical roles where pay variance breeds discord.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely scarce talent in a market where refusing to negotiate loses the hire.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — includes an explicitly legal-defensive rationale for the vague decline script.

### [FOUND:SIGN-TO-START-MOMENTUM] Compress signature-to-start and land people in classes
**Move:** Minimize the gap between signed offer and start date so second thoughts and counteroffers can't creep in, target cohorts starting together, and use the interval productively — trackable pre-work, onboarding materials lined up, incoming hires invited to team socials. Treat a signed offer like an unsigned contract until they're at the desk.
**APPLY WHEN:** between offer acceptance and day one.
**AVOID WHEN:** waiting for a cohort would push a strong individual hire out by months — cohorting is a benefit, not a reason to delay.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — same momentum logic as deal closing.

### Stage 21 — Onboarding, enablement and the leader handoff

### [FOUND:COHORT-BOOTCAMP] Run onboarding as named classes, not one-offs
**Move:** Hire and onboard in cohorts with a structured one-to-two-week curriculum per class — starting as a single forked doc with sections highlighted as covered, evolving to linked docs plus a checklist. Give cohorts team names. The payoff is force multiplication, peer coverage of what one person missed, and natural sparring partners.
**APPLY WHEN:** hiring more than one rep in a window and able to align start dates.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely single hires far apart; and don't let cohorting delay a strong individual start.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin cohorts plus curriculum evolution across iterations.

### [FOUND:TRACKED-PREWORK] Assign auditable pre-work before day one
**Move:** Use the pre-start excitement window: send a specific hyperlinked set of materials — recorded good and bad sales calls organized by customer type, feature demo videos, webinars, support content, readings — state explicitly that it is not optional, that execution is tracked and there will be a test, and deliver it so progress is visible. Keep volume proportionate, roughly ten hours over two weeks.
**APPLY WHEN:** any gap between signed offer and start date.
**AVOID WHEN:** the material is busywork or low quality — that sets the wrong tone; or the window is too short for the load.

### [FOUND:EQUIP-FOR-EXPECT] Equip for the behavior you intend to audit
**Move:** Before the start date put on the desk every artifact the expected workflow requires — desk setup, laptop plus monitor, keyboard, mouse, desk phone and headset, note pad and quality pens, swag — then state the workflow that uses each item and audit against it later. Then sit side by side with the new hire to push through payroll and similar paperwork in one sitting.
**APPLY WHEN:** every new start, especially the first cohort of sellers after founder-led sales worked.
**AVOID WHEN:** fully remote or contract sellers whose environment you cannot control (remote-first orgs need the shipped-and-configured equivalent); pre-PMF, when you should still be selling yourself.

### [FOUND:PREPROVISION-STACK] Pre-provision accounts, defer configuration
**Move:** Stand up every account from the admin dashboard before day one — identity/email, CRM, email automation, tracking, dialer, phone — and add the hire to all recurring meetings. Do not pre-configure; leave that for a group session.
**APPLY WHEN:** a cohort of two or more starts on the same day.
**AVOID WHEN:** a single ad-hoc hire where a group config session is impossible; the specific vendor list is dated and should be re-picked.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=saas · market=stage

### [FOUND:CONFIG-PARTY] Run a codified configuration party
**Move:** Keep configuration steps as a shared spreadsheet checklist; block one to two hours with the cohort and walk it top to bottom, saying a sentence about why each setting exists — browser setup and bookmarks, signature, keyboard shortcuts, undo-send and auto-advance, plug-ins and BCC-to-CRM, voicemail, screenshot tool, mobile mail, a demo environment.
**APPLY WHEN:** new cohort onboarding; also after a tool-chain change that resets settings.
**AVOID WHEN:** never delegate it to reps individually — the claim is it will either not get done or get done badly.

**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard practice across the author's onboarding cohorts.

### [FOUND:STATE-THE-TENETS] Open onboarding with explicit, named cultural tenets
**Move:** Deliver a candid statement of what is valued and what is not okay, framed inside the org's history — where it started, where it went, where it's going. The author's three: operate with an engineering mindset without being an engineer; treat yourselves as the product managers of the sales org, prioritizing process and materials by revenue impact; hold intellectual honesty paramount. Start articulating them during hiring so non-fits self-select out.
**APPLY WHEN:** day one of every class, and earlier in the funnel.
**AVOID WHEN:** the tenets are aspirational rather than enforced — unenforced tenets teach cynicism.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one org's specific tenets, offered as an example to adapt.

### [FOUND:THREE-SUBJECT-BUCKETS] Teach market, business drivers and technology before the product
**Move:** Run three distinct blocks. Market: the field's history, technology epochs, current state, major and adjacent vendors, so nobody meets an integration question with silence. Business drivers: how the client's business makes and spends money, which levers you touch, how it differs by segment, and the metric vocabulary buyers use. Technology: the handful of terms that let a rep speak as a peer. Deliver via a genuine subject-matter expert, record once good, and test on it.
**APPLY WHEN:** evangelical, consultative sales of an innovative product where reps must engage buyers as equals.
**AVOID WHEN:** simple transactional offers with a straightforward value proposition, where high-volume orgs deliberately don't require this depth; testing harnesses are unnecessary until large distributed scale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — "The Brad Class" at TalentBin, later recorded and tested on hundreds of staff.

### [FOUND:FEATURE-TO-PAIN-CHAIN] Train the demo as chaptered feature→use case→business pain chains
**Move:** Do a low-formality product walk-through first, mapping each element to user use cases and the business driver it moves. Then train the formal presentation as named chapters with their intent. Then perform a live mock demo to the class contextualizing each section as "this shows features A, B, C, which let the user do X, Y, Z, which solves pains M, N, O."
**APPLY WHEN:** onboarding any customer-facing rep, ahead of drills and repetitions.
**AVOID WHEN:** before market and business-driver grounding is in place — the sequence is argued to be load-bearing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — TalentBin curriculum, cross-referencing the materials chapter.

### [FOUND:OBJECTIONS-FOLDED-NOT-TAUGHT] Deprioritize standalone objection-handling training
**Move:** Don't march through every possible objection during onboarding — the list is unbounded. Inject common objections and prospect confusion into the presentation and demo sections as they're trained, maintain an exhaustive reference list, and make sure reps know where it lives. Cover competitors as a separate late block.
**APPLY WHEN:** designing a fixed-length boot camp where curriculum time is the scarce resource.
**AVOID WHEN:** a market where a small number of objections dominate and explicit drilling would pay — a case the book doesn't cover.

### [FOUND:BROWSER-GTD] Browser hygiene as focus infrastructure

**APPLY WHEN:** onboarding any rep, junior or senior.
**AVOID WHEN:** as a substitute for pipeline coaching — this is hygiene, not selling skill.

### [FOUND:INBOX-ZERO-AUDIT] Archive-if-no-next-action, then audit it
**Move:** Teach that a read email with no next action leaves the inbox immediately, kill push notifications so email stops behaving like instant messaging, and have reps work from CRM and calendar. Then actually audit — look at the inbox and call out stale read mail.
**APPLY WHEN:** reps are missing follow-ups or losing client threads in the inbox.
**AVOID WHEN:** cultures where desk-side inspection reads as surveillance; remote teams where the audit mechanic doesn't exist.

### [FOUND:EMAIL-CRAFT] Standards for client-facing email
**Move:** Train explicit standards: specific topical subject lines instead of filler; correct CC and reply-all so the thread stays intact; whitespace, bold, bullets and headings for skimmability; naming who is being answered; wording chosen so the thread is findable in archive search; proofreading rigor. State that all of it is captured in CRM and will be reviewed.
**APPLY WHEN:** reps write directly to buyers and champions, especially where the champion forwards internally.
**AVOID WHEN:** leaning on this as a hiring substitute — writing quality should have been screened for already.

### [FOUND:TEMPLATE-CULTURE] Templating mindset with a shared repository
**Move:** Treat repeated messages as templates by default, supply templates for new releases, and push reps to author their own and share them, since one rep's need is usually four reps' need. Store them in a common repository, never buried in someone's mailbox.
**APPLY WHEN:** any repeated communication — outreach, follow-up, release announcements.
**AVOID WHEN:** highly bespoke consultative or executive correspondence where a recognizable template destroys credibility.

### [FOUND:KEYSTROKE-DRILL] Drill mail keyboard shortcuts explicitly

**APPLY WHEN:** high-volume top-of-funnel roles where message throughput is the constraint.

### [FOUND:CRM-OR-IT-DIDNT-HAPPEN] "If it's not in the CRM, it doesn't count"

## Where this book stops working

- **Consulting, services and expertise offers.** The book's spine assumes a demoable product whose superiority can be shown numerically in a live screen share. In consulting, trust precedes any product-benefit argument, so DIRECT-ASK, INEVITABILITY, PRICEPARRY and the pain-signifier-first outreach templates invert the correct order. There is no demo instance, no demo-data problem, no freemium instance, no seat-tier ladder; PRELOAD-DATA-DEMO becomes a paid diagnostic rather than a free pre-sale asset. What transfers: COST-MODEL, COMPARATOR-PRICE, STATUSQUOCOST, MICROCONTRACT, pipeline hygiene. What does not: the entire demo-mechanics, usage-instrumentation and certification layer.
- **Very high ACV, committee-driven enterprise.** JUMP-PREPARE-MIDAIR, GOOD-TIME-GOOD-OPPS, SHALLOW-MANY and RUTHLESS-PIPE-CLEANING assume a large addressable set and many at-bats. With a few dozen named accounts and multi-year cycles, patient nursing and deep multithreading beat truncation and volume, and silence is structural rather than terminal.
- **Micro-ACV / self-serve / freemium / PLG.** Explicitly out of scope in the introduction: ad-monetized, freemium-$9.99 and self-serve models. The customer-success chapter openly assumes roughly $1K+ per user per year — at $20/month the implementation labor model collapses. BOTTOM-UP-DEFER is deferred rather than taught.
- **Regulated and privacy-sensitive markets.** SPEED-OVER-POLISH, LOGOS-MSA and EMAIL-PATTERN-FORM run into legal review, MSA scrutiny and anti-spam/privacy law; the book's dismissal of personal-address concerns is jurisdiction-dependent, not settled.
- **Local/SMB transactional motions.** Much of the narrative apparatus (seven-beat deck, "what changed", QBRs) is overkill; the book itself routes these to phone-first outreach and contrasts them with high-volume orgs that deliberately skip the market/business-driver curriculum.
- **Remote-first orgs.** INBOX-ZERO-AUDIT, AMBIENT-REVIEW, EQUIP-FOR-EXPECT and the in-office standup rhythm assume physical co-location and have no direct remote analogue.
- **Post-2020 tooling reality.** Open/click telemetry (PIXELREAD, SIGNALCALL, CLICK-TARGETS) is weakened by pixel-blocking and privacy proxies; every named vendor should be read as a category slot.

## What the book debunks

- **"Sprinkle some sales pros on it."** The book's named destructive misconception: hiring a rep or VP Sales to discover the message, materials and product fit the founder never proved. Blamed for delayed or never-reached PMF; the failure gets misattributed to the departing first VP rather than to the founders who hired for the wrong epoch.
- **The rep-less go-to-market myth**, fed by Dropbox/Twilio stories — the counter is that those companies' own staff pages are full of sales people.
- **Sales as innate talent, "being salesy", or purely right-brain work** with no metrics; and the inverse, "I don't care about inputs, only outputs", called the signature of a manager afraid of both management and metrics.
- **Pricing to perfection** — charging so much that every star must align; harms are lower win rates and higher renewal churn. The inverse tell: if the buyer says "oh, that's easy, sure", you left money on the table. Also debunked: premature three-tier segmentation, and deferring pricing entirely because you don't want to hear "no".
- **Collateral perfectionism** — that assets must be spit-shined, legally reviewed and universally signed off before showing; and the technical founder's pure-demo-without-slides, which damages the value argument.
- **"Drip marketing is spam"** — rejected conditionally: spam is a function of bad targeting and self-centered messaging, not automation. But mass merge without real signifiers is called counterproductive, and over-personalizing every message in a sequence is inefficient overkill.
- **The polite meeting is the dangerous one** — the buyer who never understood the pitch, nodded along and vanished, which is why comprehension checkpoints exist and why "does that make sense?" invites a face-saving lie.
- **Strict-letter BANT** disqualifies exactly the innovative-category prospects an early-stage founder wants, because a buyer who didn't know the category existed cannot have budget or timeline for it.
- **"We don't have the need"** is named the most concerning objection and usually masks budget, authority or priority. **"No budget"** is often a failure of imagination — but a genuine absence of any tool-purchasing process is a disqualifier, not an objection, and the book declines to teach buyers to spend money at all.
- **Unstructured trials** predictably produce non-usage, week-by-week extensions and a seed of doubt about post-purchase adoption; handing over credentials is not a sales strategy.
- **Winning every deal is a warning sign** — it indicates too few or over-cherry-picked opportunities; typical new-solution win rates are 10–30%.
- **Selling to anyone with a heartbeat destroys the business**: unqualified demos burn scarce runway, and a badly-fit customer churns, badmouths you, consumes support capacity and steers your roadmap wrong.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Assuming customers notice their own success** — unreported ROI erodes into churn, lost upsell and an opening for competitors; and assuming a good year auto-converts to renewal without a renewal process.
- **Making support hard**, which drives complaints onto public social channels; blaming the user; closing tickets on silence.
- **Premature scaling** (Zenefits selling volume without accounting for servicing cost; Square hiring ~20 senior AEs for an unvalidated segment) versus **lagged scaling** — less existential but paid in opportunity cost, runway and enterprise value.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Hiring myths**: "a sales dude is a sales dude"; "sales is sales is sales" ignoring role slice; "industry" as a coarse label; hire-the-Rolodex; pedigree as a decision rather than one term; self-reported quota taken at face value; the old-school class of five to ten AEs with an expected 50% washout, called a wasteful vestige; "sell me this pen"; video interviews as narrative rather than ability authentication.
- **"Experienced hires don't need onboarding"** and "get them in front of customers immediately" — both named false economies; and the belief that information transfer plus a quiz equals readiness, when muscle memory must exist before live calls.
- **"Meetings are for big companies"** — skip the venue and the need reasserts itself as back-channel morale erosion and work chosen by preference rather than priority. Manager teaming as the default audit tool is called managerial laziness masquerading as productivity.
- **The "VP of Sales" title is noise** — hire against stage-appropriate scope; and expecting a fine-tuned machine is wrong, since repeatability, not smoothness, is the bar.

## Numbers that matter

All figures below are the author's, and almost all are self-reported by companies he founded or advised (TalentBin, HIRABL, Textio, LifeGuides, Immediately, Teamable) or relayed second-hand. Treat them as directional shape, not validated effect sizes.

**Company arc:** TalentBin ~$6M/year run rate at acquisition with ~20 sales and CS staff. Master deck grew to ~120 slides.

**Win rates and pipeline:** 20–30% win rate called solid for a new innovative solution; 10–20% typical for a new solution or new market, 30% doing well; 15–30% the band gating the first rep hire. Deals go sideways more than 50% of the time. Live pipeline a few dozen to 100+ concurrent conversations; a dozen to ~100 concurrent opps at cycles of 30/45/60/180 days. 40–50 (elsewhere 40–60) selling hours a week; ten demos a week counts as full utilization; three net-new demos a day as a carrying cap. Each TalentBin demo internally valued at $5,000 (ACV × win rate). Demo hold rates should be 80%+; 10–20% of booked meetings are missed and rescheduled.

**Prospecting and outreach:** target list of 50–100 accounts; TalentBin minimum criteria ≥1 recruiter and ≥3 open engineering reqs; five incumbent seats ≈ $50K of displaceable budget. Twenty demos to non-prospects ≈ 2 hours each ≈ a week of selling time destroyed. Human-customized first outreach ~12% response vs ~5% unpersonalized (vendor-reported); across a seven-email drip, a human-personalized first email yields ~30% more responses than heavy auto-personalization and ~15x a one-and-done send; response by sequence position peaks at the 2nd email (~18% vs ~12% for the 1st), declining after the fifth. Roughly five minutes to hand-personalize a first email; ~30 seconds of attention on a live cold connect; 30–90 second call scripts; demo asks of 20 or 30 minutes with two named day options.

**Inbound:** ~100x drop in contact rates between responding within 5 minutes and 30 minutes, ~10x between 5 and 10 (attributed to MIT Sloan / InsideSales.com); each additional attempt adds ~15% chance of contact, cumulating to ~90% by the sixth, then falling off.

**Pitch and pricing:** 5–10 minutes of discovery inside a presentation; 15–30 minutes of actual product demonstration; ~2 in-person meetings a day for field reps vs ~5–6 45-minute digital sessions inside. Fifteen minutes of prep worth ~$2K per demo at a 15%→25% win-rate lift on $20K ACV. TalentBin pricing ladder $99→$199→$299→$399→$499/month; seat price appears inconsistently as $6,000, $7,000 and $10,000 across examples — illustrative, not one price book. LinkedIn Recruiter ~$8,000–$9,000/yr as the comparator. HIRABL: ~1 recoverable missed fee per 500 submissions, average fee $15,000, a $20,000 contract implying ~$200,000 recovered (~10x), observed range ~6x–15x; ~1 in 300 permanent-placement submissions becomes a backdoor hire. Proposal ladder example: 5 seats for the price of 4.25, 10 for the price of 7; price points $10,000 / $19,500 / $24,000.

**Negotiation and closing:** buyers commonly push for ~30% below rack rate; a quick 5% off the top is cheap closing insurance; short-term contracts at 2–3x the annualized rate; $10K up front vs $12K quarterly; monthly at a ~33% premium ($9,000 upfront vs $999/month); outside counsel at ~$300/hour can eat a small contract during redlining. Re-engagement default interval sixty days.

**Customer success:** implementation ≈ 120 minutes per customer, a $50K/year CS rep doing fifteen a week ≈ $70 of labor per implementation — which fails against a $20/month product; the chapter assumes ~$1K+ per user per year. Implementation fee ≈ 3x labor cost. Illustrative churn model over 58 months: 0% churn ≈ $4M MRR, 2.5% monthly churn ≈ $2.8M, 5% below $2M, 2.5% monthly expansion ≈ over $7M. One complainer implies ~10x silent sufferers. 30-day check-in, surveys ~45 days after kickoff, final QBR ~3 months before term end, renewal call ~6 weeks out, remedial extension 1–2 months. CS specialist trigger ~12 onboarded customers; late-stage CS coverage 100/200/300 accounts.

**Scaling and comp:** cost of sales target 20–25%, 30% at the highest; a $100K rep should book roughly $500K. Illustrative rep: 5 new demos/week plus 10 follow-ups, ~20 new opportunities monthly, 20% win rate at $10K ACV → ~$480K/year. SDR salary $40–60K booking 5–10 demos/week (3–5 for higher-ACV). Premature-scaling burn example: ~$70K/month added. SF Bay SaaS SDR $45–55K base / $65–75K OTE; AE $50–60K base / $100–120K OTE; higher-ticket AE ~$100K/$200K; splits ~50/50 for new-business AEs, 60/40 or 70/30 for market development and account management; equity a few basis points over four years, explicitly below engineering. Agency fee 20–30% of first-year salary; referral bonus $2,500–$5,000; ~a dozen good resumes a week is healthy posting flow. Span of control 6–9 reports; a stretched manager costs an estimated 10–30% underperformance. Sales ops can pay off at ~10 reps. Traditional expectation of losing 30–50% of a hiring class within six months, which the author disputes as improvable. Boot camp one to two weeks; pre-work ~10 hours over two weeks; standup under 30 seconds per person and under 10 minutes total; team meeting 60 minutes; pipeline meeting 60–120 minutes at pods of ~5 AEs; 1:1s every two weeks, 30–60 minutes; untouched-opportunity threshold 30 days; contacts-per-account target of 3 for enterprise. Exit criterion for the leader hire: 1–3 sellers at repeatability, with per-rep monthly revenue milestones of $30K, $50K, $100K and up.

## Blind spots of this book

- **One company, one market, one era.** The evidence base is a single arc (TalentBin, ~$6M run rate, acquired) plus companies the author was close to or advised. Nearly every number is vendor self-reported, and the strongest external citations (MIT Sloan / InsideSales.com, Outreach campaign data) arrive without verifiable references. There is no counterfactual anywhere: we see what worked in the author's deals, not what a control group did.
- **Survivorship in the scaling chapters.** The author concedes most premature-scaling examples are unrecognisable because those companies died — which means the anti-patterns are reconstructed from famous failures rather than sampled.
- **Buyer psychology is thin.** The book is superb on operations and almost silent on why buyers decide — no treatment of status, risk aversion, internal politics beyond stakeholder mapping, or the emotional economics of change. Persuasion is modelled as comprehension plus proof, which under-explains deals lost for reasons the buyer never states.
- **Ethical load is acknowledged but not resolved.** Several moves run on implied leverage or consent shortcuts — CREDTHREAT, GATEHERO's doghouse half, WRONGPERSON's named escalation, LOGOS-MSA's buried publicity rights, SOFT-NO-DEFLECT's manufactured inevitability, URGENCY-LEVERS' scarcity claims, SENDINFO's guilt lever. The book pairs them with a genuine-relevance precondition and calls out lying to gatekeepers, but it offers no principle for when implied pressure becomes coercion. On an unqualified account these are pure pressure with no value behind them.
- **Legal and jurisdictional exposure is waved off.** Inferred and personal email addresses, call and screen recording, publicity rights in the MSA, and cold-calling compliance are treated as US-startup-normal. Non-US operators and regulated categories get no guidance.
- **Tooling is load-bearing and dated.** The mechanisms survive but the 2016–2020 product names do not, and some mechanisms depend on tooling that has degraded — notably open/click telemetry under pixel-blocking.
- **Remote and distributed work is essentially absent.** Ambient listening, desk audits, the staged desk, in-office standups and the beer interview all assume co-location, which the book never problematizes.
- **The author marks his own weakest section.** He explicitly declines authority on hiring a sales leader, self-critiques his stage definitions and his metrics harness (self-graded B/B+ at under 10 reps), and discloses that he co-founded a sales analytics company built on the metrics thesis — a disclosed but unexamined interest in the chapter arguing for metrics-first management.
- **Nothing on partners, channel, marketing-led demand, or international expansion.** The go-to-market taught is direct, founder-led, single-geography outbound plus incidental inbound; other routes to market are out of frame entirely.
