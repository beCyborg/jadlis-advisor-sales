# advisor-spin — Moves

Layer: **дискавери: вопросы** — what the seller asks, in what order, and what the buyer's
own words prove about deal health. Citation prefix `[SPIN:]`.

Single source: Rackham, *SPIN Selling* (1988), canon, evidence ceiling `field-study`.
No move below claims stronger evidence than that ceiling. Because this lens rests on one
book, no cross-book code collisions were possible; every `###` code below is unique inside
the lens and must stay so.

Evidence ladder (strong → weak): `field-study` → `external-research` →
`practitioner-n-many` → `practitioner-anecdote` → `assertion`.

Standing caveat carried by every `field-study` token here: the underlying dataset is
Huthwaite's own commercial research, never externally replicated or peer-reviewed. Read all
effect sizes as directional. Details in `myths-and-demotions.md`.

## Stage 0 — Call planning, architecture and enablement

### [SPIN:CALL4STAGE] Split any call into the four stages before judging it
**Move:** Break the call into Preliminaries, Investigating, Demonstrating Capability and Obtaining Commitment, and estimate the share of time each took. Judge that balance against the call's stated purpose and its position in the cycle — never against a fixed ideal ratio.
**APPLY WHEN:** reviewing or coaching an observed/recorded call; deciding in advance where the time of an upcoming call should go.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied as a rigid template — legitimate calls exist where Preliminaries ate roughly four fifths of the time (relationship-heavy regional selling) or Investigating consumed most of a day (consulting).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** field-study — descriptive taxonomy induced from thousands of coded calls; the prescription about *balance* is much weaker than the taxonomy itself.

### [SPIN:SPINSEQ] Let questions drift S → P → I → N, without treating it as a gate
**Move:** Start with a handful of Situation questions, move to Problem questions inside areas you can actually fix, then Implication questions that widen the consequences, then Need-payoff questions that make the buyer voice the value of a fix. Successful reps interleave; only the clustering of Situation questions at the front is stable.
**APPLY WHEN:** the Investigating stage of a multi-call, value-justified deal where the buyer's problem is unquantified or unowned.
**AVOID WHEN:** one-call low-value transactions that convert on a pitch plus an ask; also whenever Situation questions become the bulk of the call. Modern practice tightens this further — research the situation before the call instead of mining it live.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the four question categories were induced by coding successful against unsuccessful calls; the frequency claims are Huthwaite-internal.

### [SPIN:ADVANCEOBJ] Write the call objective as a specific buyer action, before the call
**Move:** Commit in writing, pre-call, to the Advance you will ask for, phrased as something the buyer *does*: attends a demo, introduces you to their manager, opens a department to you. Rule out "gather information", "build the relationship" and "make a good impression" — those can only produce Continuations.
**APPLY WHEN:** planning any call in a multi-call cycle; coaching a rep labelled a "weak closer", a label that usually misnames an objective-setting or Investigating failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** the written objective is treated as binding — a better Advance that surfaces mid-call replaces it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — interview contrast between high and low performers, plus the methodology of scoring outcomes against pre-declared objectives.

### [SPIN:INVESTTIME] Put the weight of the call into Investigating
**Move:** Allocate more call time to Investigating and Demonstrating Capability than to the warm-up and the commitment ask. When the need has been fully developed, the commitment step shrinks to something short and frictionless.
**APPLY WHEN:** any large-deal call, especially where the rep's reflex is to rush the questions and get to the demo or the ask.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has signalled a hard time cap — observed calls have legitimately compressed Investigating down to a single question.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — time allocation compared across successful and unsuccessful calls; Huthwaite-internal.

### [SPIN:PRECALL3] Pre-call list: three solvable problems plus their Problem Questions
**Move:** Before each call, write down at least three problems this buyer plausibly has that you can actually solve, then draft the literal wording of the Problem Questions that would bring each to the surface.
**APPLY WHEN:** every call, first meetings above all; also as a manager's minimum required pre-call artifact.
**AVOID WHEN:** no real contraindication; the yield drops when the account's problems are already documented and confirmed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard advice across Huthwaite training populations; the supporting case is a single long-tenured Kodak rep's testimony.

### [SPIN:IMPLPLAN] Draft Implication Questions on paper via a "so what?" chain
**Move:** Pick one problem you solve and assume the buyer concedes it exists but calls it minor. In three columns write: the problem; the difficulties it could lead to, preferring those that make it look worse than at first glance; one question per difficulty. Convert every argument you would have asserted into a question the buyer answers instead — what this does to throughput, what it adds to cost, what it does to your best people's motivation. The book's worked chain: a machine that is hard to operate → too few qualified operators → overtime spend and recruiting difficulty.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparing a call where you expect the buyer to minimize the problem; a deal frozen at an acknowledged-but-tolerated problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no hypothesis about the problem yet — do discovery first; also when the problem is already urgent and funded, where piling on implications just depresses the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Huthwaite's house planning method and the author's own routine; the underlying claim that Implication Questions raise success is field-study.

### [SPIN:PRECALLQ] Spend the pre-call minutes on questions, not on the opening line
**Move:** Take the anxious minutes reps burn rehearsing an opening and redirect them into drafting the questions the call will actually turn on.
**APPLY WHEN:** any planned call in a consultative sale.
**AVOID WHEN:** no stated contraindication.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — from the author's field travel with salespeople.

### [SPIN:PRODPROB] Re-describe your product as a list of problems
**Move:** Stop cataloguing the offer by Features and Advantages. Write out the problems it exists to solve, then derive the question bank from that list.
**APPLY WHEN:** building enablement material, battlecards, discovery guides.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicates the exercise, but the resulting list is raw material for questions, not a script to recite at the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — implementation advice from training practice.

### [SPIN:NEWPRODPROB] Launch new products as problem lists, never as feature dumps
**Move:** When a new product lands, before absorbing its specs write the problems it solves and, for each, the Problem / Implication / Need-payoff Questions that would surface and grow that problem; then list the accounts where those problems plausibly exist. Enablement should brief the field the same way.
**APPLY WHEN:** any launch; also for a rep who has become over-excited about something they just inherited.
**AVOID WHEN:** mid-cycle technical evaluation, where a genuine appetite for Features exists and product depth — often through sales engineers — is exactly what the buyer wants.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — medical diagnostic-equipment launch, where the problem-briefed group outsold the rest of the force by 54% in year one; self-reported, one client, one year.

## Stage 1 — Preliminaries (opening the call)

### [SPIN:OPENVARY] Rotate your openings; never repeat one on the same buyer
**Move:** Keep several openings in rotation and choose per call. Never reuse a scripted opening on someone who has already heard it — the second delivery reads as mechanical and spends the credibility the first one earned.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-call cycles; repeat meetings with the same buyer or the same account team.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicated; the only cost is preparation effort.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — just over 300 observed calls in which effective sellers opened differently every time; the absolute "never repeat" rule leans on author anecdote.

### [SPIN:OPEN3] Establish three things in the opening, then stop
**Move:** Cover exactly who you are, why you are here (purpose, with no product detail), and your right to ask questions — then stop. The success test for the stage is simply that the buyer is willing to answer. Do not chase polish; observed top performers were sometimes hesitant in the first minutes.
**APPLY WHEN:** a first meeting with a new buyer in a larger sale.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer opens by demanding product specifics, or has a hard five-minute window — a compressed purpose statement then beats a role-setting frame.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Huthwaite's recommended framework, not a measured behavioral split.

### [SPIN:OPEN20] Cap the warm-up, then move to business
**Move:** Keep Preliminaries short; treat a habit of spending more than roughly a fifth of call time there as a warning sign. Do not open with solutions or capabilities. If you notice you are answering the buyer's questions rather than asking your own, rebuild the opening so the questioner role lands with you.
**APPLY WHEN:** calls that repeatedly run out of time before the decisive point; senior or professional buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** relationship-repair meetings, or cultures where jumping to business reads as rude.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the 20% figure is the author's personal worry threshold, explicitly offered without measurement.

### [SPIN:OPENPERS] Handle personal-interest openings with restraint
**Move:** Do not systematically fish for a personal hook — photos, trophies, hobbies — to open large-sale calls. Follow a personal thread only when the buyer starts it. Assume senior buyers price their time high and may read the fishing as manipulation.
**APPLY WHEN:** professional purchasers, senior executives, high-volume urban buyers, first calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** small transactional sales inside long-tenured relationships — there the personal reference correlated *positively* with success.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the Imperial Group study, carrying the author's own confound caveat (rural reps had longer tenure and less busy customers), plus buyer-side anecdote.

### [SPIN:OPENIBS] Do not default to an opening benefit statement
**Move:** Treat the initial benefit statement as an occasional option, never the standard opener. Two traps: it drags you into product detail before value exists, and it hands the questioning role to the buyer, who typically arrives at price early. If you fall in, take the questioner role back and steer to needs.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-form calls of forty minutes and up in larger sales.
**AVOID WHEN:** very short calls where you have only minutes — the technique's original research base was six-minute pharmaceutical calls, where a substance-first opening is defensible.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — just over 300 observed calls showed no relationship in either direction between opening benefit statements and call outcome.

## Stage 2 — Investigating: surfacing needs

### [SPIN:NEEDDEF] Code a buyer statement as a need only by the stated-want test
**Move:** In review, mark as a need only those buyer utterances that voice a want or a concern your offer could satisfy. Do not credit needs you inferred, and do not split "needs" from "wants" — collapse both into one category.
**APPLY WHEN:** call review, deal qualification, any check of whether Investigating produced anything at all.
**AVOID WHEN:** it is used to inflate qualification — one stated concern is not yet a developed or explicit need.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a coding operationalization adopted for the research, stated without independent support.

### [SPIN:NEEDCLASS] Tag every need statement Implied or Explicit, in separate columns
**Move:** During and after the call, label each buyer statement about their world as either a problem/dissatisfaction (Implied) or a stated want/intention to act (Explicit). Keep two columns. Treat Implied statements as raw material still to be developed, not as progress.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery and call review, above all when a rep reports a "great call" and cites agreed problems as the evidence.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional one-call deals with trivial cost — there the distinction stops paying for itself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 1,406 recorded larger calls, average contract $27,000: Explicit Needs roughly twice as frequent in successful calls while Implied Needs stayed flat.

### [SPIN:SIGNALGATE] Score buying signals on action language, not problem admissions
**Move:** Decide whether the call advanced by whether the buyer used action language — replacing X next year, looking for a system with these three properties. Treat agreed problems as a one-way signal only: no problems means no sale, but their presence proves nothing.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-call debrief, forecast review, deciding whether to spend another cycle on an account.
**AVOID WHEN:** small transactional sales, where problem admissions genuinely do predict the close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the same 1,406-call dataset, plus an interview contrast between a low performer and a rep ranked in the top five of four hundred.

### [SPIN:SITCUT] Delete every researchable Situation Question before the call
**Move:** Do the fact-finding homework in advance and strike from the plan any question whose answer is already obtainable. Keep only Situation Questions with an explicit purpose — each one should set up a specific problem you intend to probe next.
**APPLY WHEN:** first meetings and new accounts, especially with professional buyers who answer the same background questions daily.
**AVOID WHEN:** operational specifics no research can supply; or mid-call, when you need one fact to make an implication concrete.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** field-study — behavior counts show Situation Question frequency running higher in failed calls and higher among inexperienced reps.

### [SPIN:PROBQ] Move to Problem Questions early and on purpose
**Move:** After minimal grounding, ask directly about dissatisfaction with the current way of working: what is difficult, what is unreliable, what breaks under peak load. Each question should invite the buyer to state a problem you could plausibly solve.
**APPLY WHEN:** early discovery at any deal size; whenever the rep is stuck cycling on background facts.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot solve the area you are probing — you will build a need for a competitor. Cultures where implying the buyer has problems is an insult need softer framing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** field-study — 646 smaller sales showed Problem Question levels roughly twice as high in successful calls; the link weakens in larger sales.

### [SPIN:VALUEEQ] Run the value equation before you price the conversation
**Move:** Before naming a price or proposing, ask whether the problem size the buyer *currently perceives* outweighs the total cost of your solution — money plus risk plus disruption plus their personal career exposure. If it does not, stop selling and keep developing the need rather than discounting.
**APPLY WHEN:** any moment where price shock is plausible; deals where your solution costs a multiple of the pain as currently described.
**AVOID WHEN:** solution cost is trivial next to the pain — the equation is already satisfied and further development is overkill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — an inferred model rather than a measured behavior, grounded in the Implied/Explicit split across the small- and large-sale datasets.

## Stage 3 — Investigating: developing the need (Implication and Need-payoff)

### [SPIN:IMPLCHAIN] Chain implications until the buyer re-rates the problem
**Move:** Take the minimized problem and ask, in sequence, what it does to output, to staffing, to cost, to downstream quality — following each dismissal ("we trained three people for that") into the next-order consequence: turnover → training cost → bottlenecks → overtime → outsourcing → quality → late delivery. Do not argue. Only ask what follows from what the buyer just said.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer admits the problem but frames it as minor; solution cost sits far above the currently perceived pain.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already treats the problem as urgent — more pressure only depresses; or when the chain runs into consequences your product cannot touch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — Implication frequency linked to success in the large-sale datasets; the Contortomat/Easiflo dialogues are illustrative reconstructions, not data.

### [SPIN:IMPLCOST] Make the buyer do the arithmetic
**Move:** When an implication touches money, ask for the cost of the consequence instead of asserting it — training cost per operator, the overtime multiplier, hours lost. Let the buyer produce the number, then feed the running total back to them.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is analytical, owns a budget, and the consequence is countable.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer lacks the data and guessing would put them on the defensive; or the consequence is reputational rather than numeric.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — demonstrated only through reconstructed dialogue, never separately measured.

### [SPIN:IMPLSUM] Summarize the accumulated implications back
**Move:** Before proposing anything, restate the whole chain in one compressed pass — the original problem plus every consequence the buyer named — and let the buyer draw the conclusion about severity.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of an implication sequence, before any capability statement or price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chain is thin — one or two consequences summarized back sounds like exaggeration.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — shown twice in reconstructed calls (Contortomat, word-processor rework).

### [SPIN:IMPLDM] Reserve consequence-level questions for the person who signs
**Move:** With users and influencers, Problem Questions may be enough to get an outcome. With the economic buyer, deliberately lead with consequence-level questions instead of operational detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** meetings with economic buyers and executives; high-tech or high-perceived-risk purchases.
**AVOID WHEN:** mature, slow-moving categories where the buyer has bought the same class of thing for years and already holds every implication.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — reported research finding plus post-call decision-maker feedback; the author concedes he cannot explain the high-tech effect.

### [SPIN:NPQFLIP] Flip to Need-payoff once the problem is big enough
**Move:** After the buyer agrees the problem justifies action, switch to solution-centered questions: why solving it matters to them, how it would help, whether there is any other way it would help. Let them enumerate the payoffs in their own words.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-implication, once severity is established; sales into existing customers where the tone of the relationship matters.
**AVOID WHEN:** early in the call before needs exist — the "if I showed you something interesting, would you be interested?" pattern puts buyers on the defensive; also when the surfaced need is one you cannot meet.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any/expansion · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — calls high in Need-payoff Questions were rated by customers as positive, constructive and helpful.

### [SPIN:NPQNOCLAIM] Let the buyer argue that a partial fix is worth it
**Move:** When your solution only addresses part of a multi-cause problem, do not claim the improvement yourself. Ask whether an easier, faster or cheaper element would help and in what way, then keep asking whether there is any other way it could help until the buyer has built the case.
**APPLY WHEN:** complex problems with many causes; a buyer already primed to list the parts you do not solve.
**AVOID WHEN:** simple sales with an exact problem-solution fit — claiming it directly is faster and carries no penalty.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/product/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — Need-payoff linked to fewer objections; the reject-rate dialogue pair is illustrative only.

### [SPIN:NPQREHEARSE] Rehearse the champion in needs language, not product language
**Move:** Instead of briefing the champion on specs to repeat upward, ask Need-payoff Questions that make them articulate in their own words what the change is worth to the organization and to the specific executive they will face. Then probe whether the benefit they named is the one that executive actually cares about.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals where most of the persuasion happens in rooms you are not in; immediately before a champion's internal meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** single-decision-maker deals you personally attend end to end.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one process-control sales manager's "director of a play" account plus reconstructed dialogue; never separately measured.

### [SPIN:QUINCY] Sort your own questions with the sad/happy test
**Move:** In review, label each developing question: problem-centered questions that make the situation feel worse are Implication; solution-centered questions about the usefulness of fixing it are Need-payoff. Then check the ratio and the ordering.
**APPLY WHEN:** coaching and self-review, when the two categories keep blurring.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicated — it is a labeling heuristic, not a tactic to run on a buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — "Quincy's Rule", named after an eight-year-old who out-sorted the research team; a mnemonic, never published or tested.

## Stage 4 — Demonstrating capability and objection prevention

### [SPIN:VALUENOTSPECS] Answer big-ticket hesitation with value, not product detail
**Move:** When a buyer stalls on a large purchase, resist the reflex to explain the product harder. The gap is perceived value against the size of the decision, so develop how costly the current state is rather than how good your product is.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is visibly nervous about the spend; the rep is reaching for literature, specs or feature depth; the rep recently moved up-market from cheaper goods.
**AVOID WHEN:** the blocker is a genuine technical qualification question; or value is already established and only mechanics remain.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Buffalo copier call plus a study of reps transferring from low-value goods that was abandoned before completion.

### [SPIN:FABTAG] Tag your own statements F / A / B in review
**Move:** Recall or transcribe the capability statements you made and label each one: a fact about the product (F), a how-it-helps claim with no voiced want behind it (A), or a match to a want the buyer explicitly stated (B). Then count the ratio.
**APPLY WHEN:** coaching, win/loss review, diagnosing reps whose calls "feel good" and then stall.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicated; the yield is low in short transactional calls where Advantages work fine.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 18,000 calls analyzed for Feature levels; 5,000 high-tech calls for the Benefit-versus-outcome comparison.

### [SPIN:BENEFITGATE] Make a capability statement only against a voiced Explicit Need
**Move:** Withhold "here is how we help" until the buyer has said they want that specific outcome. If all they have described is a problem — an Implied Need — then what you are about to say is an Advantage; go back to Implication and Need-payoff first.
**APPLY WHEN:** any demonstrating-capability moment in a large deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** small transactional sales, where offering Advantages against Implied Needs converts perfectly well.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — across 5,000 calls Benefits ran significantly higher in calls ending in Orders or Advances, while Advantages showed no significant difference.

### [SPIN:PREMEET] Insist on a needs premeeting before a forced product presentation
**Move:** When an account demands a product presentation with no discovery first, negotiate at minimum one prior conversation with a key person to surface needs, so that the presentation can contain at least some real Benefits rather than pure Advantages.
**APPLY WHEN:** RFP-style or committee-mandated presentations; buyers who front-load product show-and-tell.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicates asking, but the premeeting may simply be refused — then expect an Advantage-heavy presentation and plan the need development for afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — prescriptive advice from repeated client work, not a measured experiment.

### [SPIN:OBJPREVENT] Replace objection handling with value building
**Move:** When a rep faces frequent objections, do not train responses. Train the developing questions: hold the capability statement back, run Implication Questions on the admitted problem, then Need-payoff Questions on the value of fixing it, summarize, and only then state the Benefit tied to the need the buyer voiced. Read a rising objection count as a signal to move effort upstream, not to rehearse rebuttals.
**APPLY WHEN:** reps with abnormally high objection rates; deals where the same three objections recur across accounts; the discovery-to-proposal transition.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objections are true and rooted in a real capability gap or competitor superiority — answer those straight; also weak in very short transactional calls with no room for a probing sequence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — eight high-objection reps trained purely in need development (the word "objection" never used) saw objections per selling hour drop 55%; self-reported client study, and the causal direction is argued from correlation plus training experience, so the mechanism claim is weaker than the correlation.

### [SPIN:OBJDIAG] Diagnose whether the objections are self-inflicted
**Move:** Before prescribing anything to a rep drowning in objections, audit three markers. (1) Advantage count per call — a high objection rate together with above-average Advantage usage means self-inflicted. (2) Timing — buyers rarely object to questions, so early objections mean solutions were offered before needs existed. (3) Framing — "too expensive", "not worth switching", "we're fine as we are" mean the need was never grown to a size that justifies action. Any marker routes the fix to need development, not to objection handling.
**APPLY WHEN:** sales-management coaching; comparing reps selling the same product to the same buyer profile; the post-mortem after a price stall.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection spike coincides with a real competitive or pricing change — check the environment first; and late-stage objections about specific contract or technical terms, which are genuine and which this diagnostic misreads as seller error.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — behavior-analysis data in which all ten high-objection reps examined were above average in Advantage usage; the timing and framing markers are practitioner-n-many, stated as a pattern rather than a measured split.

### [SPIN:FEATPRICE] Cut Feature volume when you are the premium option
**Move:** If your product is priced above the competition, deliberately reduce feature enumeration and shift airtime to need development, because feature lists raise the buyer's price sensitivity. Reverse the rule only when you are the cheap, feature-rich option.
**APPLY WHEN:** premium positioning; reps hired out of a cheaper competitor who carry a feature-heavy style with them.
**AVOID WHEN:** low-cost feature-rich offers, where price sensitivity works in your favor; and mid-cycle technical evaluation where a genuine Features appetite exists.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — Linda Marsh's correlation work linking Features to price concern, plus the recruited-competitor case where price objections ran 30% above the rest of the force selling an identical product.

### [SPIN:PRICEFEAT] Cut Features when price objections spike mid-cycle
**Move:** When price pushback starts dominating a live cycle, reduce the number of Features stated per call and move that airtime into Problem, Implication and Need-payoff Questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** repeated price objections across the current pipeline; a feature-heavy demo culture; a product with a long spec sheet.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is running a formal technical evaluation and has explicitly asked for spec depth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — rests on the earlier Features/price-concern correlation; the prescription itself is the author's inference, not a separate measurement.

## Stage 5 — Obtaining commitment

### [SPIN:NOPRESSURE] Never apply closing pressure you cannot cash the same day
**Move:** Gate pressure on whether the buyer can actually decide in the room. If the decision cannot land now, drop hard-sell moves entirely and spend the call on need development instead; pressure without an immediate order buys you a buyer who avoids the next meeting and never tells you why.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-call cycles, deals carrying post-sale support, any account you intend to sell to again.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely single-call, decision-in-the-room selling with no future relationship — there push is explicitly permitted by the book's own data.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — supported by the closing studies; the specific mechanism (the buyer quietly refuses the next meeting) is author anecdote.

### [SPIN:ONECLOSE] Exactly one clean commitment request per call — not zero, not a stack
**Move:** End the call with one explicit request for commitment. Do not leave without asking, and do not chain a second or third attempt after the first is declined.
**APPLY WHEN:** any call where a next step exists; professional-services selling above all, where the opposite failure dominates and buyers are irritated by calls that end with no defined next step.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has just declined and the impulse is to re-ask with a different technique — that is the more-than-two-closes pattern with the worst measured outcomes. Note the dose data comes from relatively small transactions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** field-study — the American Airlines agent study, on small transactions; extending the dose curve to major sales is the author's stated inference.

### [SPIN:NOTRICKCLOSE] Retire manipulative closes entirely on large deals
**Move:** Strike Assumptive, Alternative, Standing-room-only/scarcity, Last-chance/price-rise and Order-blank closes from the playbook for large, relationship-bearing deals, and substitute a stated proposal of the next step.
**APPLY WHEN:** professional buyers, procurement, senior executives, any account with post-sale delivery attached.
**AVOID WHEN:** low-value, one-off, unsophisticated-buyer retail or door-to-door contexts — the only setting where these measurably shortened transactions and slightly raised conversion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — office-equipment study (190 calls), photo-store price-split study, a closing-training evaluation, a buyer-side survey and a post-sale satisfaction study; all Huthwaite-internal and never peer-reviewed.

### [SPIN:OUTCOME4] Classify every call Order / Advance / Continuation / No-sale, by action
**Move:** After each call, label the outcome by the buyer's committed *actions*, not their compliments. Order = a firm purchase commitment. Advance = an agreed action that moves the deal (demo attendance, access to a higher decision maker, a trial, entry into a new part of the account). Continuation = the deal lives but nothing was agreed. No-sale = the buyer actively refuses your principal objective. Count Continuations as failures.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline hygiene, call reviews, forecast scrubs; any time "great meeting, they loved it" is the reported result.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely one-call sales where only Order and No-sale exist; and do not let the label become post-hoc — reps will rationalize whatever happened as the objective they intended.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — outcome taxonomy built to make call success measurable across thousands of coded calls.

### [SPIN:PIVOTADVANCE] Trade a blocked Advance for a different one inside the same call
**Move:** When the planned Advance becomes unavailable — the target person is unreachable, internal politics block it — scan the call for openings the buyer just disclosed (a new project, a team forming) and close on a different concrete action instead of settling for a friendly Continuation.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-call, once the primary objective is refused or made impossible; multi-threaded accounts where a second entry point plausibly exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** the substitute "advance" is a vague future meeting the buyer offers to end the conversation — that is a Continuation wearing a costume.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one interviewed top performer in industrial pumping equipment.

### [SPIN:CONCERNCHECK] Ask for leftover concerns before proposing anything
**Move:** Right before moving to commitment, take the initiative and invite the buyer to name anything still unexplained or unresolved; handle it, then proceed. Never use a close as the instrument for flushing out doubts.
**APPLY WHEN:** complex offers, multi-topic calls, anything where the buyer may be silently holding a question about pricing mechanics, contract terms or delivery.
**AVOID WHEN:** it degrades into an open-ended "anything else?" loop with a buyer who is finished and wants a next step — its own species of weak ending.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — a behavior consistently present in reps who obtained commitment, contrasted with the antagonistic protest a close provokes.

### [SPIN:BENSUMMARY] Summarize the agreed benefits before the next-step proposal
**Move:** Pull the discussion together in a short recap naming the specific payoffs the buyer agreed matter — speed, simplicity, cost control, the reliability problem they raised — and then move to the proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** long or multi-topic calls where the buyer cannot be assumed to hold the whole picture; immediately before any decision moment.
**AVOID WHEN:** short low-value transactions where the summary adds nothing; and when the "benefits" you would summarize were never actually confirmed by the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — observed in successful large-sale calls.

### [SPIN:PROPOSENEXT] Propose the next step rather than asking for the order
**Move:** State the logical next step instead of requesting permission — the sensible move is X. Choose the highest next step the buyer can realistically grant that also genuinely advances the deal. Asking behaviors dominate every earlier stage; at commitment, telling wins.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of a call where concerns are cleared and benefits summarized.
**AVOID WHEN:** the proposal exceeds what the buyer can authorize — successful reps never push past achievable limits, and an oversized proposal converts a winnable Advance into a No-sale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — behavior of high performers in coded calls.

### [SPIN:COMMITCSP] Check → summarize → propose as the standing commitment sequence
**Move:** Run the three in order at the end of the call: check that the buyer's key concerns are covered, summarize the Benefits they agreed to, then propose a commitment level matched to where the deal actually is rather than where you wish it were.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of any call in a multi-call sale; as the default replacement for every closing technique.
**AVOID WHEN:** small transactional sales, where classic closing techniques still work.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — a recap of the closing research; the three-step sequence itself is the book's synthesis of observed high-performer behavior.

### [SPIN:BEHAVIORNOTATTITUDE] Judge reps on observed call behavior, not attitude to closing
**Move:** Do not hire, screen or diagnose reps by their stated enthusiasm for closing. Observe and code what they actually do in calls. In the data, reps with a favorable attitude toward closing were performing below target.
**APPLY WHEN:** sales hiring, coaching diagnosis, and the manager's complaint that "he's a weak closer".
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication stated; attitude scales keep some use for surfacing what a team believes, but not for predicting results.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — Fishbein & Ajzen (1970) on the weak attitude–behavior link, cited by name, plus Huthwaite's own 38-rep chemical-company attitude study.

### [SPIN:CLOSESCALE] Score closing attitude, then route by deal type
**Move:** Rate the 15 statements of the Appendix B scale 1–5 and sum them. Read 45 as the theoretical neutral point but use above 50 as the practical marker of a pro-closing attitude, since most salespeople sit slightly above neutral. Then route on deal type: a high score is defensible for low-value goods, unsophisticated buyers and no post-sale relationship; the same score in large sales with sophisticated buyers and an ongoing relationship is a liability to correct.
**APPLY WHEN:** diagnosing a rep whose large-deal calls stall; hiring or reassigning between transactional and consultative motions.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a standalone hiring filter — it is a self-report attitude measure calibrated on a single 1980s study, not a validated selection instrument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — the closing-attitude study found the best performers scoring below 50; the scale's own psychometric properties are never reported.

## Stage 6 — Post-call review and skill acquisition

### [SPIN:REVIEW] Plan–do–review, at detail level
**Move:** After every call ask four things: did I hit my objective, what would I do differently, what did I learn about this account, what transfers elsewhere. Then refuse global verdicts like "it went well" and interrogate specifics — which parts went better and why, which questions moved the buyer, which needs were felt strongly, which needs changed during the conversation and why, which of your own behaviors had the most impact.
**APPLY WHEN:** after every call; multi-call cycles above all.
**AVOID WHEN:** no stated contraindication.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the two distinguishing traits (systematic review, obsession with detail) come from the author's travel with top performers, not from a measured comparison.

### [SPIN:LEARN1] One behavior at a time
**Move:** Pick exactly one behavior to practice and do not add a second until the first feels comfortable. Resist the reflex to overhaul closing, questioning and presenting simultaneously.
**APPLY WHEN:** any skill-change effort, self-directed or coached.
**AVOID WHEN:** no stated contraindication; the only risk is slower apparent coverage.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Huthwaite training experience; the Tom Landry and Franklin 1771 Autobiography references are illustration, not evidence.

### [SPIN:LEARN3] The three-attempt rule
**Move:** Never judge whether a new behavior works until you have used it in at least three real calls. Expect the first attempts to feel artificial and to land poorly.
**APPLY WHEN:** introducing Implication or Need-payoff Questions for the first time.
**AVOID WHEN:** the behavior is visibly damaging a relationship with a key account — stop earlier there, which is precisely why practice belongs in safe accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Huthwaite's standing recommendation; the golf-lesson survey is supporting illustration only.

### [SPIN:LEARNQTY] Quantity before quality
**Move:** While practicing, maximize the raw count of the new behavior and ignore how smoothly you execute it. Customer reactions in the field sort quality out faster than pre-scripted refinement does.
**APPLY WHEN:** early skill acquisition; designing sales training.
**AVOID WHEN:** behaviors where one clumsy instance is costly — probing a sensitive political issue, for example — and in key accounts generally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Huthwaite's own program redesign, where a quantity-first rebuild outperformed the quality-first version; the supporting language-learning "experiments" are cited with no named source, so that part is assertion.

### [SPIN:LEARNSAFE] Practice only in safe situations
**Move:** Try new behaviors on small accounts, familiar customers, or calls with nothing at stake. Before your most important meeting, deliberately fall back on what you already do well.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after training, or after first reading the model.
**AVOID WHEN:** no exception is offered — the rule is stated absolutely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the company-president negotiation story plus general training experience.

### [SPIN:SPINLADDER] Acquire the SPIN behaviors in a staged sequence
**Move:** Build in order. (1) If your calls are telling-heavy, just raise the total question count — mostly Situation Questions is acceptable at this stage, for a few weeks. (2) Plan and ask Problem Questions, targeting at least six per average call, ignoring quality. (3) Only then add Implication Questions, expecting a couple of months before they feel natural. (4) Finally shift from giving Benefits to asking Need-payoff Questions, again by volume first.
**APPLY WHEN:** an individual rep development plan; an onboarding ramp.
**AVOID WHEN:** live high-value deals — step 1 deliberately tolerates an excess of Situation Questions, which the same book shows to be neutral-to-negative for the buyer. Never run this ladder on strategic accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — implementation advice from Huthwaite's training practice, layered on top of the field-study question taxonomy.

### [SPIN:NPQDRILL] Drill Need-payoff Questions on a non-sales topic
**Move:** Practice with a friend or family member on a topic where they plausibly have a need — a car, a trip, a camera. Ask only value-of-solution questions and watch their enthusiasm build. The generic stems transfer to live calls: why is that important, how would that help, would it be useful if, is there any other way this could help.
**APPLY WHEN:** rep onboarding and skill-building; low-stakes rehearsal before real accounts.
**AVOID WHEN:** you try the same drill for Implication Questions — those are problem-specific, cannot be drilled generically, and need per-account planning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's exercise with his son, plus one Xerox rep whose friend reportedly bought a car after the drill.

## Stage 7 — Evaluating a method or an enablement program

### [SPIN:EVAL3] Run the three-test disproof ladder on any sales method
**Move:** Before believing a method raised revenue, pass three gates in order: observe the target group's own successful versus failed calls to confirm the behaviors predict success *in this population*; observe the same people after training to confirm behavior frequency actually changed; only then compare productivity against a control group. Failure at gate 1 or gate 2 means any revenue lift belongs to something else.
**APPLY WHEN:** evaluating a methodology rollout, a new playbook, or a vendor's claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** teams too small for a comparable control group — the design degrades into anecdote.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the design was executed at Motorola Canada and at a second multinational business-machines company.

### [SPIN:RIVALEXPL] Demand the equally plausible alternative explanation
**Move:** Against any before/after result, force the question of what else could explain it just as well, and walk the usual suspects: new product launches, redrawn territories, changed managers or comp plans, a competitor exiting, price moves, advertising, branch age measured against the length of the sales cycle, seasonality.
**APPLY WHEN:** reading a case study, a vendor ROI claim, or your own pilot result.
**AVOID WHEN:** nothing contraindicated — it is a reading discipline, not a tactic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — demonstrated by the author dismantling four of Huthwaite's own favorable results.

### [SPIN:HAWTHORNE] Isolate the attention effect by splitting the trained cohort
**Move:** Give one cohort an identical dose of training attention, then split it by *measured adoption* of the target behaviors into high- and low-adopter subgroups, and compare their productivity against each other and against an untrained control. Equal gains across subgroups mean the lift was attention; divergent and persisting gains in the high-adopter subgroup mean the method did the work.
**APPLY WHEN:** any enablement pilot whose sponsors will claim credit for a lift.
**AVOID WHEN:** cohorts too small to split, or where adoption cannot be measured behaviorally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 55 trained people split 27/28 and compared against 52 untrained controls.
