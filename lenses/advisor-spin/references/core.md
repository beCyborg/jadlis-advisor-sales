# advisor-spin — Core

The method's components, ranked by how strongly each is actually supported — strongest
evidence token first. The point of this file is not "SPIN works" but which parts of it rest
on coded call data and which are house doctrine wearing the same clothes.

Ladder: `field-study` → `external-research` → `practitioner-n-many` → `practitioner-anecdote`
→ `assertion`. Book ceiling: `field-study`. Nothing here may claim higher.

**Global discount applied to every token below.** The dataset (35,000+ calls, 10–12 years,
10,000+ observed salespeople, 20+ organizations, ~$1M spend) belongs to Huthwaite, the firm
that sold the training. None of it was peer-reviewed or externally replicated. `field-study`
here means "coded observational data from a commercially interested party", which is still
far above the genre's baseline of memoir — and still not causal proof. Treat effect sizes as
directional only.

---

## Tier 1 — field-study: components resting on coded call data

**1. Implied vs Explicit Needs is the load-bearing distinction of the whole method.**
Buyer statements split into problems/dissatisfactions (Implied) and stated wants/intentions
(Explicit). In 1,406 larger calls averaging $27,000, Explicit Needs ran roughly twice as
frequent in successful calls while Implied Needs stayed flat; in 646 smaller sales the
opposite held and Implied Need count predicted the close. This single asymmetry generates
almost every other prescription in the lens. [SPIN:NEEDCLASS], [SPIN:SIGNALGATE]

**2. Feature / Advantage / Benefit, redefined against the buyer's voiced want.**
A Benefit is not a well-phrased claim; it is a statement that meets a want the buyer has
already spoken aloud. Across 5,000 high-tech calls, Benefits so defined were significantly
higher in calls ending in Orders or Advances; Advantages showed no significant difference.
Across 18,000 calls, Features ran slightly higher in unsuccessful calls. The definitional
move — most "benefits" in sales training are Advantages here — is what makes the finding
non-trivial. [SPIN:BENEFITGATE], [SPIN:FABTAG]

**3. Closing technique fails as deal size and relationship length rise.**
The strongest negative result in the book, and the one with the most independent measurement
lines behind it: 190 observed calls where the 30 highest-close calls produced 11 sales
against 21 from the 30 lowest-close; a buyer survey (n=54) where 2 were more likely to buy
under closing pressure, 18 indifferent, 34 less likely; a post-sale study of 145 customers
showing lower satisfaction and repurchase intent from closing-trained sellers; and a photo
store where closes helped at the low-value counter (72% → 76%) and hurt at the high-value
one (42% → 33%). Same reps, same day, opposite sign. [SPIN:NOTRICKCLOSE], [SPIN:NOPRESSURE],
[SPIN:CLOSESCALE]

**4. Situation Questions are neutral at best and negatively associated at worst.**
Frequency runs higher in failed calls and higher among inexperienced reps; in the second
multinational study they ran 4% lower in successful calls. One observed call reached 35
Situation questions with zero Problem, Implication or Need-payoff questions before the buyer
ended the meeting. This is the component the lens now pushes hardest against its own source:
the durable form is deletion before the call, not moderation during it. [SPIN:SITCUT]

**5. The four call outcomes, defined by buyer action.**
Order / Advance / Continuation / No-sale, judged on what the buyer committed to *do*.
The taxonomy exists because "good meeting" is unmeasurable; Continuation counted as failure
is the operational bite. Fewer than 10% of major-account calls end in an Order or a No-sale,
so nearly all measurement lives in the Advance/Continuation distinction. [SPIN:OUTCOME4],
[SPIN:ADVANCEOBJ]

**6. Objection frequency is a seller-side variable, not a buying signal.**
694 calls in a business-machines corporation: a higher objection share meant a lower
probability of call success. Inside one eight-person team, one rep took ten times the
objections per selling hour of a teammate selling the same product to the same customer
size. All ten high-objection reps examined were above average in Advantage usage. The
correlation is solid; the causal claim that need development *prevents* objections rests on
one eight-rep experiment (55% drop, self-reported), so the mechanism sits a rung lower than
the correlation. [SPIN:OBJDIAG], [SPIN:OBJPREVENT]

**7. Features raise price sensitivity.**
Correlational work linking Feature statements to price concern, plus a natural experiment:
reps recruited from a cheaper competitor ran 30% more price objections while selling an
identical product. The prescription that follows — cut Features when you are the premium
option, or when price objections spike — is the author's inference on top of the
correlation. [SPIN:FEATPRICE], [SPIN:PRICEFEAT]

**8. Implication and Need-payoff Questions are the developing behaviors that matter.**
Both are linked to success in the large-sale datasets; Need-payoff-heavy calls were rated
by customers as positive and constructive; top performers asked 10x+ more Need-payoff
Questions per call than average performers. Important internal crack: the two flagship
studies disagree on which of the two is strongest — Motorola Canada points at Need-payoff,
the second multinational at Implication. Treat "develop the need" as supported and any
ranking between I and N as unsettled. [SPIN:IMPLCHAIN], [SPIN:NPQFLIP]

**9. Openings are the least consequential stage, and varying them is the only stable finding.**
Just over 300 observed calls: no relationship in either direction between opening benefit
statements and outcome, and effective sellers opened differently each time. Everything else
about first impressions is small-sale folklore. [SPIN:OPENVARY], [SPIN:OPENIBS]

**10. Commitment behaviors of high performers: check → summarize → propose.**
Checking for unresolved concerns is consistently present in reps who obtain commitment;
summarizing agreed benefits precedes the decision moment; and at commitment, telling
outperforms asking — the reverse of every earlier stage. High performers also never propose
past what the buyer can authorize. [SPIN:CONCERNCHECK], [SPIN:BENSUMMARY],
[SPIN:PROPOSENEXT], [SPIN:COMMITCSP]

**11. Investigating carries the large sale; call time should follow.**
Successful large-sale calls allocate mass to Investigating and Demonstrating Capability.
The four-stage split itself is a clean induction from coded calls; the *balance*
prescription on top of it is much softer, and the book names legitimate calls that violate
it wildly. [SPIN:CALL4STAGE], [SPIN:INVESTTIME], [SPIN:SPINSEQ]

**12. Evaluation methodology — the most transferable part of the book.**
The three-test disproof ladder, the rival-explanation checklist, and the split-cohort design
that separates adoption effects from attention effects (55 trained split 27/28 against 52
controls). This is also the machinery the author turns on his own results, discarding four
favorable Huthwaite findings as confounded. Any lens claim about "the method lifted revenue"
should survive these gates first. [SPIN:EVAL3], [SPIN:RIVALEXPL], [SPIN:HAWTHORNE]

**13. Personal-interest openings degrade with buyer seniority.**
The Imperial Group study found the personal reference correlating positively in small
transactional sales with tenured relationships and negatively with professional/urban
buyers — with the author's own confound caveat attached (tenure and customer busyness moved
together with the variable of interest). [SPIN:OPENPERS]

## Tier 2 — external-research: the one imported finding

**14. Attitude predicts behavior weakly; code behavior instead.**
Fishbein & Ajzen (1970) is the only genuinely external citation carrying weight in the
argument, and it is used to justify observing calls rather than surveying reps about
closing. Huthwaite's own 38-rep attitude study is the internal companion, not the support.
This is the one place where the lens is not arguing purely from its own data.
[SPIN:BEHAVIORNOTATTITUDE]

## Tier 3 — practitioner-n-many: house practice across many trained populations

**15. The value equation as a decision rule.**
Purchase happens when perceived problem size exceeds perceived total cost — money, risk,
disruption, the buyer's own career exposure. It is an inferred model built on the
Implied/Explicit asymmetry, not a separately measured behavior. It is nonetheless the lens's
best single explanation of why price objections are usually a discovery failure.
[SPIN:VALUEEQ]

**16. Planning artifacts: three problems, drafted Problem Questions, "so what?" columns.**
Standard Huthwaite planning method, recommended across training populations. The *content*
being planned rests on Tier 1; the planning ritual itself does not. [SPIN:PRECALL3],
[SPIN:IMPLPLAN], [SPIN:PRODPROB]

**17. Product framed as a problem list, including at launch.**
The launch case (problem-briefed group outselling the force by 54% in year one) is a single
client, single year, self-reported — strong-sounding, weakly designed. The general practice
is training advice. [SPIN:NEWPRODPROB]

**18. Skill acquisition rules: one behavior, three attempts, quantity first, safe accounts.**
Huthwaite's training doctrine. The quantity-first rule has an internal counter-case behind
it (a quality-obsessed program that collapsed in pilot after 9 months and $650,000), which
is evidence of a sort — but it is one program, not a design. [SPIN:LEARN1], [SPIN:LEARN3],
[SPIN:LEARNQTY], [SPIN:SPINLADDER]

**19. The three-point opening frame and the needs premeeting.**
Both are recommended frameworks, not measured behavior splits. Note the internal tension:
the opening advice is prescriptive precisely where the data says openings barely matter.
[SPIN:OPEN3], [SPIN:PREMEET]

## Tier 4 — practitioner-anecdote: single stories doing prescriptive work

**20. Making the buyer compute the cost, and summarizing the implication chain back.**
Both are demonstrated only through reconstructed dialogue. They are good craft and they are
unmeasured. [SPIN:IMPLCOST], [SPIN:IMPLSUM]

**21. Rehearsing the champion in needs language.**
One sales manager's account of directing a play, plus reconstructed dialogue. This is the
lens's only real contact with committee dynamics and it is its weakest-supported move — the
committee layer belongs to other lenses. [SPIN:NPQREHEARSE]

**22. Pivoting to a substitute Advance mid-call.** One interviewed top performer.
[SPIN:PIVOTADVANCE]

**23. Detail-level post-call review; practicing only in safe accounts; the Need-payoff drill.**
All from the author's field travel and personal exercises. Sensible, unmeasured.
[SPIN:REVIEW], [SPIN:LEARNSAFE], [SPIN:NPQDRILL]

**24. Answering big-ticket hesitation with value rather than specs.**
One copier call, plus a study of reps moving up-market from cheap goods that was never
finished. The conclusion is consistent with Tier 1, but this specific support is thin.
[SPIN:VALUENOTSPECS]

## Tier 5 — assertion: stated without support, and to be labelled as such

**25. The 20% warm-up threshold.** The author's personal worry line, explicitly offered
without measurement. Quote it as a heuristic or not at all. [SPIN:OPEN20]

**26. The coding definition of "need" and the deliberate collapse of need vs want.**
A research operationalization adopted for convenience. It is defensible and it is a
definition, not a finding. [SPIN:NEEDDEF]

**27. Quincy's Rule (sad questions vs happy questions).** A mnemonic named after an
eight-year-old, never published or tested. Useful for sorting your own questions in review;
worthless as an argument. [SPIN:QUINCY]

## Structural claims underneath the ranking

- **The small/large axis is the real variable, not the publication year.** Nearly every
  finding flips sign between transactional and large sales: Situation Questions, Features,
  Advantages, problem-count-as-signal and closing pressure all work acceptably in cheap
  one-call deals and degrade or invert as cost and stakeholder count rise. When this lens is
  applied to a micro-ACV motion, it should expect to be wrong.
- **Persuasion decays between calls.** Fewer than half the key points of a presentation are
  recalled a week later, and stated buying intent decays with them. This is why the method
  moves the argument into the buyer's own mouth — it is the only version that survives the
  gap between meetings. Average cycle in the business-machines study: 7.8 calls.
- **The causal core is correlational.** Behavior-frequency-versus-outcome correlations carry
  the prescriptions. The one strong counter-design (splitting a trained cohort by adoption)
  exists but underwrites almost none of the individual moves.
- **The author's own epistemics are the best asset here.** Appendix A dismantles four
  favorable Huthwaite results; the book states that over 90% of claimed sales-training
  increases were better explained by market or management factors. A lens that inherits that
  scepticism and does not apply it to SPIN's own numbers has read the book badly.
