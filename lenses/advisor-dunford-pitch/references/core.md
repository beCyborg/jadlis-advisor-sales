# advisor-dunford-pitch — Core principles, ranked by evidence

Layer: **структура устного питча**. Source: *Sales Pitch* (Dunford, 2023), evidence ceiling `practitioner-n-many`.

Read this file to know **how much weight a principle can carry**, not to learn the method. Ranking is by evidence ladder token — `external-research` → `practitioner-n-many` → `practitioner-anecdote` → `assertion` — not by how central the idea is to the book. Several of the method's load-bearing beams sit at the bottom of this list; that asymmetry is the point.

**Ceiling rule:** nothing in this lens may be cited above `practitioner-n-many`. The book contains no win-rate data, no control group, and no A/B of the eight-step structure against the deck it replaces. Its own prescribed validation is one rep's judgement (PASSFAIL) — explicitly non-statistical. So the central claim *"this structure wins more deals"* is untested by the book's own standard and must never be stated as measured fact.

---

## Tier 1 — `external-research` (exactly one anchor)

### 1. Most considered B2B purchases die against the status quo, not against a competitor
40–60% of B2B purchase processes end in no decision, attributed to Dixon & McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (2022). This is the **only** external-research anchor in the entire book, and it is borrowed, not generated. Everything the method does — handing over a rubric, teaching the market, shrinking the switch — is downstream of this one number.
Moves: [PITCH:SQFIRST], [PITCH:NODECISION-RENAME], [PITCH:CHANGE-FEAR].
Caveat: the number licenses "treat the status quo as a competitor". It does **not** license the further claim that *this particular pitch structure* is the remedy — that step is the author's, not the research's.

## Tier 2 — `practitioner-n-many` (the bulk of the method; n large, uncontrolled, self-reported)

Base for everything in this tier: ~200+ tech companies positioned, 200+ sales pitches built over ~15 years, ~12 in-house pitches with earlier versions of the structure, ~30 years in positioning. All self-reported, no counterfactual, no failure census.

### 2. Buying is hard; selling is not the bottleneck
The champion arrives at call one without a correct requirements list, buried in conflicting vendor, review-site and analyst sources, and defaults to the lowest-risk option. Vendors are experts in the solution space; buyers are experts only in their own pain. Refusing to have a point of view on the market therefore abandons the buyer.
Moves: [PITCH:BUYRUBRIC], [PITCH:APPROACHGRP], [PITCH:ALTDISCOVERY].

### 3. The question to answer is "why you over everything else I could pick today", not "why you"
This reframing is what makes the Alternatives step structural rather than defensive, and it is what turns competitor mapping into a service to the buyer.
Moves: [PITCH:ALTSHORTLIST], [PITCH:APPROACHGRP], [PITCH:NAMEHOLD].

### 4. Positioning is upstream of the pitch; a weak pitch is usually a positioning defect
Five components in fixed order — competitive alternatives → unique capabilities → differentiated value → best-fit customers → market category — each feeding a specific pitch step (Insight = differentiated value × best-fit customers; Alternatives = competitive alternatives; Perfect World = value restated as criteria; Introduction = market category; Differentiated Value = value plus the capabilities behind it). Garbage in, garbage out.
Moves: [PITCH:POS5], [PITCH:SOWHAT], [PITCH:VALUE3], [PITCH:POSTHESIS].
Weakness: this principle also functions as the method's universal escape hatch — see myths-and-demotions.md.

### 5. The pitch is a running order, not a set of behaviours: setup then follow-through
Insight → Alternatives → Perfect World (setup, ~5–10 min of an hour) then Introduction → Differentiated Value (the longest segment) → Proof → Objections (optional) → Ask. Eight steps, of which only Objections is genuinely droppable.
Moves: [PITCH:SETUP8], [PITCH:SETUPCLOCK], [PITCH:INSIGHT-SHORT].
Provenance: an IBM first-call process that opened with a point of view on the market, adapted by the author and reused across her in-house and consulting work. That lineage is reported, not documented.

### 6. Only *differentiated* value is a reason to buy
Value is the answer to "so what?", carried to a business outcome the buyer's boss would care about. Shared value is interesting but not a decision driver; required-to-buy capabilities are table stakes and belong in objection handling.
Moves: [PITCH:SOWHAT], [PITCH:TABLESTAKES-DEFER], [PITCH:VALUEDEMO].

### 7. The demo is part of the value step and is organised by value theme
Not a product menu, not a guided tour: state the combination of themes, then show only the features that support each. Non-demoable value (services, pricing model, packaging) still belongs in the same step.
Moves: [PITCH:VALUEDEMO], [PITCH:THEMESANDWICH], [PITCH:SHOWBEST], [PITCH:NONDEMOABLE].
This is the lens's strongest and most operationally specific contribution.

### 8. The audience of the first call is one person, deliberately
5–8 people touch a B2B decision; all of them can kill it, only the champion can start it. So the pitch is tuned to the champion and its second job is to arm them for rooms the seller is not in.
Moves: [PITCH:CHAMPFIRST], [PITCH:TABLESTAKES-DEFER], [PITCH:PROOF-MATCH].

### 9. The vendor asserting something is not proof
Proof must come from outside the seller's own voice: case studies, third-party verification, certifications, customer-validated statistics, customer quotes, analyst reviews, industry research, awards. Experiential value is proved by demo plus customers narrating their own experience, not by numbers you made up.
Moves: [PITCH:PROOFMENU], [PITCH:THIRDPARTY-VOICE], [PITCH:PROOF-MATCH].

### 10. A pitch is a shipped, tested, maintained artifact — not a rep's personal style
Storyboard built cross-functionally → deck/demo/script built by a small sales+marketing group → tested by one respected rep on real prospects → rolled out with training and a certification gate → owned by one steward and re-checked against drift.
Moves: [PITCH:XFUNC], [PITCH:STANDARD-DECK], [PITCH:MEDIUMMAP], [PITCH:PILOT-REP], [PITCH:CALL-DEBRIEF], [PITCH:FREEZECORE], [PITCH:ROLLOUT-KIT], [PITCH:RECERT], [PITCH:OWNER].

### 11. Every call ends with a named next step chosen by the seller
An open-ended close, or "call us if you have questions", is treated as a defect. Where the process is immature, one default CTA is held constant so it can be tested.
Moves: [PITCH:ASK-NEXTSTEP], [PITCH:POC-LOGISTICS], [PITCH:ASK-STANDARDIZE], [PITCH:CUSTOMLATER].

### 12. Qualification and discovery are assumed, not taught
Fit criteria live upstream of the first call; discovery happens as dialogue inside the Alternatives step. The book explicitly declares qualification method, discovery technique, objection technique and negotiation out of scope.
Moves: [PITCH:QUALGATE], [PITCH:ALTDISCOVERY], [PITCH:DISQUALIFY-FAST].
Consequence for the council: on those four layers this lens has **no standing** and must defer.

### 13. The story outlives the call
Insight + Alternatives convert into a buyer's guide; the value narrative into PLG onboarding; the insight alone (never the pitch) into public content.
Moves: [PITCH:BUYERSGUIDE], [PITCH:PLGSTORY], [PITCH:INSIGHT-CONTENT].

## Tier 3 — `practitioner-anecdote` (single case, no counterfactual)

### 14. The rubric mechanism itself is illustrated consumer-side
The three-criteria toilet-store story (3 weeks of research → 0 purchases; 3 criteria → 15 minutes) is the book's clearest demonstration of [PITCH:BUYRUBRIC] — and it is a retail anecdote, not a B2B deal. Cite it as illustration, never as B2B support.

### 15. Show the capability nobody asked for
The investment-bank CRM story — no prospect ever requested the killer feature, yet it drove the wins. One company. [PITCH:SHOWBEST].

### 16. Narrow-landscape and unfamiliar-category variants
[PITCH:COMPRESS-ALTS] rests on the Gearset walkthrough; [PITCH:CATEGORY-DEFINE] on the Funnel walkthrough. Each is a single reconstructed pitch. They exist precisely because the eight-step default misfires when there is nothing to compare, or nothing to compare *to*.

### 17. Skipping the demo
The database company that rarely demoed, because one SQL query looks like any other. One case. [PITCH:NODEMO].

### 18. Scale claims as differentiation
[PITCH:HARDNUMBER] rests on Gearset's self-reported figures (2,000+ paid deployments, >4× the nearest competitor). Company-reported, unaudited, and structurally unavailable to the smaller player.

## Tier 4 — `assertion` (stated, not evidenced — argue with these freely)

### 19. Losses to the status quo are mislabelled by sales teams
The reasoning behind [PITCH:NODECISION-RENAME] is the author's; no data on how teams actually log losses.

### 20. Three value themes because buyers cannot hold more
The memory-limit rationale under [PITCH:VALUE3] is asserted. The *practice* of capping at three is practitioner-n-many; the cognitive justification is not.

### 21. "The deal is yours to lose" after the Perfect World yes
The claim that a confirmed criteria list flips the deal's default is the pivot the whole setup exists to reach — and it is assertion. [PITCH:RIGHTGATE].

### 22. The ethical boundary
"Present it as objectively as possible and never state something false" is the entire safeguard on [PITCH:BUYRUBRIC], [PITCH:RECATEGORIZE] and [PITCH:RIGHTGATE]. There is no external check and no test for when a criteria list has become self-serving. Treated as a principle it is unenforceable; treated as a design constraint it is the only thing separating this method from criteria-gerrymandering.

### 23. Placement rules stated as preference
[PITCH:PROOF-INLINE], [PITCH:NONDEMOABLE] and [PITCH:VISUALASSET] are offered as the author's preferences without supporting cases.

## How to weight this lens in a council verdict

- On **pitch/demo structure, narrative order, value-theme construction, and pitch rollout hygiene** — this is the strongest voice available; nothing else in the council covers the running order of an hour.
- On **discovery mechanics, qualification, objection technique, negotiation and pricing** — defer entirely (Tier 2 §12).
- On **whether the structure raises win rates** — no lens-internal evidence exists. Say so rather than borrowing authority from the 40–60% figure, which is about the problem, not about the remedy.
