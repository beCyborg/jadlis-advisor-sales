# advisor-dunford-pitch — Moves

Layer: **структура устного питча** (the running order of a competitive first call).
Citation prefix: `[PITCH:]`. Source: *Sales Pitch* (Dunford, 2023) — canon, evidence ceiling `practitioner-n-many`.

Single-source lens: no cross-book code collisions exist, and every `###` code below is unique inside this file. Cross-references between moves use the same codes.

Grouped by deal stage: Stage 0 (pre-pitch inputs) → Stage 7 (the Ask) → Stage 8–9 (build, test, reuse).

---

## Stage 0 — Before the pitch: positioning, structure, qualification

### [PITCH:POS5] Derive positioning in fixed order before touching the deck
**Move:** Work the five components in sequence — list what customers would do without you, then what you have that those alternatives lack, then run "so what?" on each to get value, then define which accounts care most about that value, then choose the market category that points those accounts at your value.
**APPLY WHEN:** before any pitch build; when marketing and sales disagree on what the differentiators are.
**AVOID WHEN:** pre-launch with almost no paying customers — then it produces a thesis, not positioning (see POSTHESIS).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's positioning methodology, illustrated end-to-end on LevelJump.

### [PITCH:POSTHESIS] Treat pre-launch positioning as a thesis and keep it loose
**Move:** For an unlaunched product or one with a handful of paying customers, document positioning anyway but label it a thesis, expect substantial shifts as the first customer wave lands, and tighten only after the segment validates it; re-check positioning a couple of times a year and whenever competitors, differentiators or buyer behaviour move.
**APPLY WHEN:** pre-PMF and early-stage companies; also as an ongoing maintenance cadence.
**AVOID WHEN:** the team uses "it's just a thesis" as licence to keep the pitch unstructured indefinitely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — reported pattern across the author's early-stage clients.

### [PITCH:SOWHAT] Run the "so what?" ladder from feature to business value
**Move:** Take each differentiated capability and ask "so what?" repeatedly until the answer is a business outcome the buyer's boss would care about; keep the last rung and drop the intermediate ones from the pitch.
**APPLY WHEN:** building or auditing a pitch; whenever a rep's talk track is a list of capabilities.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chain terminates in a value every competitor also claims — that means the feature is not a differentiator and must not anchor the pitch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard positioning drill, illustrated with database, CRM, accounting and LevelJump chains.

### [PITCH:VALUE3] Cluster capabilities into at most three value themes
**Move:** Group differentiated capabilities under the value they enable, hold the theme count to three or fewer, and accept that individual themes may not beat every competitor as long as the combination does.
**APPLY WHEN:** compiling positioning into a pitch spine; whenever the value list has grown past what a buyer can repeat.
**AVOID WHEN:** compressing to three would hide the theme that is the actual deciding factor for a major segment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the memory-limit rationale is asserted, but the three-theme cap is applied consistently across the author's engagements.

### [PITCH:ALTSHORTLIST] Only position against alternatives that actually reach shortlists
**Move:** Split competitive alternatives into status-quo and direct types, take the buyer's view of both, and exclude competitors your reps never meet in real deals — revisit the list if they start appearing.
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding what the alternatives step will cover.
**AVOID WHEN:** product/strategy planning, where horizon competitors still matter even though sales ignores them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated guidance from the author's positioning engagements.

### [PITCH:SETUP8] Build the pitch as setup plus follow-through, eight steps
**Move:** Lay the first call out as insight, alternatives, perfect world, introduction, differentiated value, proof, optional objections, ask — and check that the deck leaves explicit room for discovery and for a demo inside the value step rather than bolting the demo on afterwards.
**APPLY WHEN:** any first-call pitch rebuild for a differentiated B2B product.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product has no defensible differentiated value yet — the structure exposes that rather than fixing it; also weak fit for purely transactional or unconsidered purchases.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's central framework, reported as used across 200+ companies from seed-stage to global; no outcome data.

### [PITCH:PICKNARR] Match narrative structure to the job, not to habit
**Move:** Reserve the vision narrative for investors, the hero's journey for case studies and recruiting stories, and use the setup/follow-through sales structure for competitive first calls; audit any inherited deck for which pattern it actually follows.
**APPLY WHEN:** rebuilding a first-call deck; noticing the sales deck is a recycled investor deck.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is highly educated and their current tool closely resembles yours — a walkthrough can be adequate there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — comparative judgement drawn from the author's experience with inherited decks across start-ups.

### [PITCH:XFUNC] Build the storyboard cross-functionally, details later
**Move:** Put sales, marketing, product, customer success and the founder/CEO in one room to agree the narrative flow as a storyboard first; only then hand it to a small sales+marketing group to turn into a branded deck, demo and script.
**APPLY WHEN:** pitch or positioning rebuild; visible internal disagreement about differentiators or competitors.
**AVOID WHEN:** a solo founder, or a team small enough that the meeting is pure overhead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard workshop format; the claim that weak positioning comes from misalignment is asserted.

### [PITCH:QUALGATE] Qualify before the pitch, or fold qualification into discovery
**Move:** Keep fit criteria (size, revenue, technical fit, budget) upstream of the first call — form or BDR screen — so the pitch can assume a qualified listener; if no such step exists, insert those questions into the alternatives/discovery conversation rather than into the opening.
**APPLY WHEN:** any repeatable motion where reps' time is the constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** founder-led selling in a brand-new category where every conversation doubles as market learning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/channel · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as standard practice across the companies the author works with; qualification method itself is declared out of scope.

### [PITCH:CHAMPFIRST] Tune the pitch to the champion, arm them for everyone else
**Move:** Write the first-call pitch for the person who will build the shortlist and make the recommendation; anticipate IT, end-user, legal and purchasing objections separately, and either touch them lightly in the first call or hand the champion material to carry those conversations later.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-stakeholder B2B deals where the person on the call is not the budget holder.
**AVOID WHEN:** founder-led or small deals where the economic buyer is the one on the call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as the consistent structure of the technology purchases the author has worked in.

### [PITCH:NODECISION-RENAME] Stop logging status-quo losses as "no decision"
**Move:** In pipeline reviews, classify a lost-to-status-quo deal as a loss to a specific alternative approach, and feed that approach back into the alternatives step of the pitch instead of writing it off as an absent buyer.
**APPLY WHEN:** win/loss review, pitch rebuild, or when a large share of pipeline dies without a competitor named.
**AVOID WHEN:** —— (diagnostic hygiene; no real contraindication).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the author's reasoning about how sales teams mislabel losses; no data given.

## Stage 1 — Framing the buyer's problem (Insight)

### [PITCH:BUYRUBRIC] Hand the buyer a short decision rubric instead of a feature tour
**Move:** Open by naming the two or three dimensions on which any solution in this category should be judged, explain what each dimension eliminates, and let the buyer knock out whole groups of options themselves before you position your own product inside the survivors.
**APPLY WHEN:** first call with a champion who has never bought this category before; crowded market; buyer says they are unsure what to look for.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a repeat expert purchaser with fixed criteria; or the rubric would be gerrymandered so narrowly that only you survive — that reads as manipulation and burns the guide role.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core of the method the author reports applying across 200+ pitches; the illustrative toilet-store story is a consumer anecdote, not B2B evidence.

### [PITCH:SQFIRST] Treat the status quo as the primary competitor, always
**Move:** Put the buyer's current way of doing the job (spreadsheet, manual process, an intern, a module of their ERP/CRM) into the alternatives set explicitly, and build the case for change against it even when the deal looks like a vendor bake-off.
**APPLY WHEN:** every consultative deal; especially when the current solution is free, well known and "good enough".
**AVOID WHEN:** a compliance deadline or contract expiry already forces a change — hammering the status quo then wastes call time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's consistent practice, leaning on Dixon & McKenna's no-decision numbers as the one external support.

### [PITCH:INSIGHT-RE] Reverse-engineer the insight from your differentiated value
**Move:** Start from the value only you deliver, ask what a best-fit buyer would have to believe about the problem for that value to matter, and state that belief as the opening frame — rewriting the buyer's implicit question (from "how do we train reps efficiently" to "how do we shorten ramp so reps hit quota sooner", in the LevelJump case).
**APPLY WHEN:** building the opening of a first call; when the current opener is a problem statement every competitor could also make.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot name a value competitors lack — the insight then collapses back into a generic trend.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the method the author teaches; illustrated with LevelJump and Postman.

### [PITCH:INSIGHT-SHORT] Keep the insight to about a minute, and never skip it
**Move:** Spend one slide and a minute or two establishing the frame, check that the buyer finds it credible, then move to alternatives; optionally back it with industry research when the claim needs proof, but do not linger.
**APPLY WHEN:** every first call using this structure.
**AVOID WHEN:** —— (the book's caution runs the other way: skipping is the primary error, over-investing the secondary one).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — timing recommendation from the author's client work.

## Stage 2 — Alternatives and discovery

### [PITCH:APPROACHGRP] Group competitors into approaches, not a vendor list
**Move:** Sort every alternative — including manual work and spreadsheets — into roughly three named approaches (e.g. manual solutions / easy all-purpose tools / enterprise tools) and discuss trade-offs at the approach level, so the buyer gets a map of the market rather than a product comparison table.
**APPLY WHEN:** crowded categories; buyer confused about how vendors differ.
**AVOID WHEN:** only one real alternative exists (the status quo), where grouping adds nothing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated pattern in the author's pitch builds, shown with the agency project-management example.

### [PITCH:PROSCONS] Write pros and cons per approach for a qualified buyer
**Move:** For each approach, state one honest upside and the downside that matters specifically to your best-fit buyer, tuned to the fact that unfit prospects were already screened out; render it as a sparse table or a four-by-four map, or run it with no slide at all as a lightly scripted conversation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the alternatives step of the first call.
**AVOID WHEN:** the table becomes a wall of text a rep reads aloud — it is a conversation guide, not a script.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's preferred artifact across engagements.

### [PITCH:ALTDISCOVERY] Do discovery inside the alternatives step, as dialogue
**Move:** Interleave teaching with three questions — how are you handling this today, how is that working, what else are you looking at — then categorise whatever the buyer names into your approach groups and probe the gap in the one they like.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer has an incumbent tool or a partial shortlist; first call with an inexperienced buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns into interrogation before you have taught anything — the failure mode the book explicitly warns about.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — scripted dialogue examples from the author's pitch work (Help Scout, LevelJump, a PM tool); discovery technique itself is out of scope of the book.

### [PITCH:NAMEHOLD] Name competitors only when they are certainly on the list
**Move:** Default to unnamed approach groups so you never introduce a vendor the buyer would then go research; ask what they are considering and categorise their answer instead — but name the one competitor that shows up in nearly every deal rather than pretending it is not there.
**APPLY WHEN:** the alternatives step in a fragmented market.
**AVOID WHEN:** a dominant competitor is obviously in the room; dodging it looks evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as the author's general preference with an explicit exception.

### [PITCH:RECATEGORIZE] Categorise a competitor your way — only if it is objectively true
**Move:** Place competitors where your point of view says they belong once marketing language is stripped away, even if they and some analysts would object; present the case as objectively as possible and never state something false.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitors overstate capabilities in ways that mislead your buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the reframing is not defensible on facts — being caught in a lie destroys the guide role the entire method depends on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's stated practice, with the ethical limit stated in the same breath; the limit itself is assertion.

### [PITCH:COMPRESS-ALTS] Collapse the alternatives step when the landscape is narrow
**Move:** With few competing approaches, state the insight, then jump directly to a numbered list of the factors a good solution must have (which pre-loads your differentiation), and only then present the value.
**APPLY WHEN:** limited competitive set; rivals claim near-identical value and mirror your messaging.
**AVOID WHEN:** buyers are weighing several structurally different approaches — they need the comparison to place themselves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — drawn from the single Gearset pitch walkthrough.

## Stage 3 — Closing the setup (Perfect World)

### [PITCH:RIGHTGATE] Close the setup with a criteria list and an explicit "right?"
**Move:** Summarise the perfect solution for a buyer like them as a short list of criteria that combines the upsides of the alternatives while avoiding their downsides — a list that by construction matches your differentiated value — and ask for confirmation; treat a yes as agreed purchase criteria and a no as self-disqualification.
**APPLY WHEN:** end of setup, after alternatives have been discussed and the buyer has volunteered their situation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the criteria list is only reachable by ignoring a real strength of an alternative — the buyer will feel steered, and a coerced yes buys nothing later; also skip lingering here if the buyer is already visibly aligned.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the pivot the author reports as the decisive turn in the pitches she builds; the "deal is yours to lose" claim is assertion.

### [PITCH:SETUPCLOCK] Timebox the setup to five to ten minutes
**Move:** Move through insight, alternatives and perfect world briskly — target roughly five to ten minutes of an hour-long first call, less on shorter calls — so the bulk of the meeting lands on differentiated value.
**APPLY WHEN:** every first call using this structure.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuinely complex market where the buyer keeps engaging with the market map — then the setup is doing its job.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — timing observed across the author's client pitches (reported range 90 seconds to 10 minutes).

### [PITCH:CATEGORY-DEFINE] Define the ideal solution first, then reveal your category (Funnel pattern)
**Move:** When your product's category is unfamiliar, run insight → alternatives with their gaps → a bulleted definition of what a perfect solution would have to do, then introduce your product as that thing; only afterwards attach demoable value points to each bullet.
**APPLY WHEN:** prospects keep comparing you to products with a fraction of your scope; buyers have no mental slot for what you are.
**AVOID WHEN:** the category is well understood and the buyer already knows the comparison set.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reconstructed from the single Funnel pitch the author walks through.

## Stage 4 — Introduction and Differentiated Value (including the demo)

### [PITCH:INTRODIAG] Introduce with a category line and one orienting diagram
**Move:** Give the market category and a one-line statement of what the product does, supported by a family, platform or marketecture diagram depending on whether you are selling a portfolio, a platform or infrastructure — then move on quickly.
**APPLY WHEN:** transition out of the setup; multi-product companies; technical buyers who need to see integration boundaries.
**AVOID WHEN:** a single-product company where a diagram would inflate a one-sentence introduction; or when the diagram drags the call into a pricing/packaging discussion you wanted later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — three diagram archetypes the author reports seeing repeatedly in client pitches.

### [PITCH:VALUEDEMO] Organise the demo around value themes, not the product menu
**Move:** Script the demo as: here is the combination of value A, B and C; here are the features behind A; behind B; behind C — showing only what supports a theme, and leaving out login, setup and other non-differentiating screens.
**APPLY WHEN:** the value step of a first call; any time the existing demo is a guided tour.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a repeat expert who has explicitly asked for a specific workflow walkthrough — but even then the differentiator still gets shown (see SHOWBEST).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core prescription of the step, reported across the author's pitch builds.

### [PITCH:THEMESANDWICH] Sandwich the demo between value-theme slides
**Move:** Open the value step with a low-text slide naming the themes, run the demo along exactly those themes, then return to the same slide to restate them as the takeaway — so what the buyer remembers is the value, not a feature list; script the demo tightly enough that a brand-new rep can run it without a sales engineer, and loosen the script as reps gain comfort.
**APPLY WHEN:** Differentiated Value is carried by a demo; value themes are abstract, or the demo is long enough that the frame gets lost.
**AVOID WHEN:** slides are banned by the buyer's format; the deck tempts reps into listing every feature on the theme slide; or the demo would wander into feature tours unconnected to the themes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the arrangement the author says she prefers among the variants her clients use.

### [PITCH:SHOWBEST] Show your best capability in the first demo whether or not it was requested
**Move:** Include your strongest differentiating capability in every first demo, even when the buyer never asked for it and did not know it was possible, because their stated criteria are incomplete.
**APPLY WHEN:** first call; category where you hold a capability competitors lack; the buyer's request list looks generic.
**AVOID WHEN:** the capability is irrelevant to this buyer segment, or showing it derails a tightly timeboxed call before the core value themes land.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the investment-bank CRM story where no prospect ever asked for the killer feature yet it drove the wins; single case.

### [PITCH:CUSTOMLATER] Defer the customized demo to the next meeting and make it the Ask
**Move:** Run a standard value-organized demo in call one and do not attempt a tailored one; name the customized demo as the recommended next step and close the call by booking it, once discovery has told you what to tailor to.
**APPLY WHEN:** enterprise-ish deals where reps feel pressure to customize up front; product needs prospect-specific configuration or data to demo credibly; discovery is still shallow after call one.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's process explicitly requires a scripted scenario in the first meeting (formal RFP, scheduled bake-off); short transactional cycles where a second meeting kills momentum.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard sequencing advice for complex enterprise software; the underlying "customize later" rule is stated as her opinion without data.

### [PITCH:NODEMO] Skip the demo when showing the product adds nothing
**Move:** Drop the first-call demo when the buyer has already trialled the product, or when the product shows badly in isolation, and instead make sure the buyer conceptually grasps the capabilities that produce your unique business value.
**APPLY WHEN:** a self-serve trial preceded the call; infrastructure/developer products whose screens are generic.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer booked the meeting specifically to see the product, or when the experience of using it *is* the differentiator.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the database company that rarely demoed because one SQL query looks like any other.

### [PITCH:SAMPLEIMPL] Use an illustrative example implementation instead of a live demo
**Move:** Build a representative "here is what a customer deployment looks like" artifact and use pieces of it inside Differentiated Value to show specific capabilities; state explicitly which parts ship in the base product and which are custom work.
**APPLY WHEN:** first call on infrastructure-class or heavily configured products where a generic demo would be meaningless; prospect needs to picture the end state.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product demos well as-is — a real demo beats an illustration; also avoid if you cannot clearly draw the in-product / custom line, since prospects then assume the bespoke work is included in the base price.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeatedly observed with start-ups the author advised, including the mispricing failure mode.

### [PITCH:NONDEMOABLE] Route non-demoable value themes to slides or script
**Move:** Cover value that lives outside the product UI — support and professional services, pricing model, packaging — in the script or on a slide inside the same value step rather than dropping it or postponing it.
**APPLY WHEN:** a differentiator is commercial or human rather than functional.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns into an early pricing negotiation the sales process wants later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — practical guidance stated without a supporting case.

### [PITCH:TABLESTAKES-DEFER] Park table-stakes capabilities in the objections step
**Move:** Identify requirements every credible vendor in your category must satisfy (e.g. SOC 2, working alongside an incumbent ERP), keep them out of the differentiated-value narrative, and answer them in Step 7 so the prospect never assumes you lack them.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling into regulated or heavily integrated environments where "we can't buy unless X" surfaces as a deal-breaker-shaped objection.
**AVOID WHEN:** the capability genuinely is unique to you — then it is real differentiated value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring correction the author makes in start-up pitch work.

### [PITCH:HARDNUMBER] Beat copycat claims with specific counts and named customers
**Move:** Where competitors assert the same leadership, replace adjectives with checkable figures and recognisable references — Gearset cites over 2,000 paid deployments, more than 4× the nearest competitor, with customers including IBM and Johnson & Johnson.
**APPLY WHEN:** an undifferentiated-sounding market where "leader" claims are cheap, and you hold a real scale advantage.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are the smaller player — the same move then favours the incumbent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — single company example (Gearset); the figures are company self-reported.

## Stage 5 — Proof

### [PITCH:PROOFMENU] Pick from the eight proof formats
**Move:** For each value theme, select backing from: customer case studies; third-party verification; certifications; customer-validated statistics; customer quotes; analyst quotes or reviews; third-party industry research or surveys; awards.
**APPLY WHEN:** any first-call pitch that has made value claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skip proof entirely — only its placement is optional (see PROOF-INLINE).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's working checklist from pitch engagements.

### [PITCH:THIRDPARTY-VOICE] Let the customer make the claim you cannot
**Move:** Where value resists measurement, combine a demo showing the behaviour with customers narrating their own experience, and where possible a comparative data point (e.g. users report being fully trained in a day versus a week elsewhere).
**APPLY WHEN:** value themes are experiential — ease of setup, adoption, time-to-competence.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have clean customer-validated empirical numbers; use those first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a general rule the author applies with start-ups.

### [PITCH:PROOF-MATCH] Match the proof to the prospect's industry and role
**Move:** Swap the case study, logo set and quotes per prospect so the referenced company sits in the same vertical and the quoted person holds a comparable role and seniority — including matching the status quo they migrated from.
**APPLY WHEN:** you serve multiple verticals or buyer personas from one product.
**AVOID WHEN:** your customer base is homogeneous enough that one story fits everyone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeated correction, listed again in the common-mistakes chapter.

### [PITCH:PROOF-INLINE] Fold proof into the value section when it flows naturally
**Move:** Attach proof to each value point as you make it; the standalone Proof step then only needs to cover whatever was left unproven.
**APPLY WHEN:** proof material is short and value points are few.
**AVOID WHEN:** it turns the value section into a credential recital and buries the differentiation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as the author's preference without supporting cases.

## Stage 6 — Objections (optional step)

### [PITCH:UNSPOKEN-PREEMPT] Raise the deal-critical objection the prospect did not voice
**Move:** List the objections that recur across deals and that you would not want left unanswered, and address each head-on in the meeting even when unprompted.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect agrees with your market view and value but still reads as hesitant; a known blocker predictably kills deals late.
**AVOID WHEN:** no such recurring unspoken objection exists — skip the step rather than manufacture doubt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — pattern the author reports across client pitches.

### [PITCH:OBJ-SCRIPT] Answer with mechanism plus a concrete number, not reassurance
**Move:** For each anticipated objection give the specific machinery and a figure: deployment worries → a dedicated deployment team and a typical two-week schedule; budget worries → explain the pricing model, show what it looks like for three customer types, offer a tailored quote on request; adoption worries → a dedicated training team plus customer voices on onboarding; scope worries → a modular platform with a described start-small-then-expand path.
**APPLY WHEN:** objections are about risk of execution rather than about value.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no credible mechanism or number behind the answer — see NOANSWER.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — scripted response patterns the author supplies to client sales teams; the figures are illustrative examples, not measured outcomes.

### [PITCH:CHANGE-FEAR] Shrink the switch: small first deployment, POC, no-IT migration, prebuilt integrations
**Move:** Acknowledge switching is never easy, then offer the smallest credible entry — a scoped initial deployment your team helps define (as little as a week to live), or a proof of concept on the prospect's own data with migration handled without their IT (as little as a day) — plus prebuilt integrations with an API fallback; for regulated buyers name certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001) with comparable customers already satisfied and offer to meet their IT team.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect is replacing an incumbent or has a change-averse organisation; regulated industry.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot actually deliver the compressed timeline — an overstated number becomes a broken promise in implementation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern across the companies the author worked with.

### [PITCH:NOANSWER] Do not surface an objection you handle badly
**Move:** If a common objection has no good answer, leave it unraised unless the prospect brings it up — and treat its existence as a signal to raise fixing the underlying problem in company priorities.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the pitch exposes a weakness you cannot yet answer.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection is already on the table — then it must be answered honestly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — explicit recommendation from the author's pitch-building practice.

## Stage 7 — The Ask

### [PITCH:ASK-NEXTSTEP] Close with a specific recommended next step
**Move:** End every first call by naming what you believe the right next move is and getting agreement on it; never leave the choice open-ended, and never substitute an invitation to call with questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** every first call with a fit prospect.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect has become visibly disqualified — then the honest next step is elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a recurring gap the author observes in start-up pitches.

### [PITCH:POC-LOGISTICS] Work the four next-step logistics questions on the call
**Move:** If the next step is a proof of concept, walk through: who must be involved; whether another meeting with those people is needed; what other steps gate it (NDA, security review); and how each of those gets completed.
**APPLY WHEN:** best-fit customers typically run a POC and you want to encourage that path.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prospect is not yet qualified enough to justify POC resources.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's guideline question set.

### [PITCH:DISQUALIFY-FAST] Design the call so bad fits opt out
**Move:** Accept that upstream qualification will not filter everyone; let the first call make misfit obvious and let those prospects exit rather than dragging them forward.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline contains poorly qualified inbound; sales capacity is the constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** never as an excuse for a weak pitch — the same call must excite good-fit buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a goal-setting principle for first calls.

### [PITCH:ASK-STANDARDIZE] Pick one default call-to-action and hold it constant to test it
**Move:** If the sales process is immature, standardise what you ask for after the first call so you have a stable baseline, then test alternative CTAs against it.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-stage company without a documented multi-step sales process.
**AVOID WHEN:** a mature, tested process already prescribes stage-specific next steps.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — advice given to less-evolved sales organisations.

## Stage 8 — Building, testing and rolling out the pitch

### [PITCH:STANDARD-DECK] Ship a standard first-call deck plus script
**Move:** Build one canonical first-call deck with an accompanying script (full prose or bullets) that new reps can run unaided; expect experienced reps to add their own flair on top.
**APPLY WHEN:** more than a couple of reps, or any plan to scale hiring.
**AVOID WHEN:** treat it as a floor, not a cage — do not enforce robotic delivery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reports every mature sales organisation she knows has one.

### [PITCH:MEDIUMMAP] Choose the medium step by step
**Move:** Insight — slide(s), or a quick conversation covering the whole setup; Alternatives — a deliberately word-sparse comparison table plus bulleted pros/cons and a set of discovery questions, delivered free-flowing rather than scripted; Perfect World — optional slide, or spoken as the conclusion of discovery; Introduction — a slide, often with a graphic when selling a suite or platform; Differentiated Value — a demo scripted to follow the value themes, or slides/screenshots for highly customisable products; Proof — case study, logo slide, awards; Objections — slide or scripted talk track; Ask — slide or scripted discussion.
**APPLY WHEN:** converting an approved storyboard into materials.
**AVOID WHEN:** letting slides dominate the Alternatives step — reading a slide displaces the discovery conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own repeated build pattern.

### [PITCH:PILOT-REP] Validate with one respected rep before team rollout
**Move:** Pick a single salesperson the team looks up to, ideally one who helped build the deck/demo/script, train them to comfort on flow and timing, and have them run the new pitch with real qualified prospects while the authors listen in.
**APPLY WHEN:** any new or substantially reworked first-call pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chosen rep has no credibility with peers — the rollout leverage disappears.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard rollout recommendation; explicitly a non-statistical design.

### [PITCH:CALL-DEBRIEF] Debrief every test call on three questions
**Move:** Immediately after each test call, compare notes on where the prospect got lost, where they got excited, and which questions hint at confusion in the pitch; fix vocabulary and small gaps in the alternatives coverage.
**APPLY WHEN:** during the controlled test phase.
**AVOID WHEN:** as a licence to restructure — see FREEZECORE.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — prescribed test-phase routine.

### [PITCH:FREEZECORE] Iterate wording, freeze the core, during the test
**Move:** Allow small changes (phrasing, an added objection answer, a missing alternative) but refuse structural changes to the pitch mid-test, since substantial rewrites invalidate the test.
**APPLY WHEN:** the test is running and enthusiasm for redesign appears.
**AVOID WHEN:** the test has genuinely failed — then restart the creation process rather than patch.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — explicit caution from the author's test process.

### [PITCH:PASSFAIL] Use the rep's two-criteria verdict as pass/fail
**Move:** Call the pitch validated only when (1) the test rep considers it no longer in need of refinement, and (2) the rep chooses the new pitch over the old one despite their comfort bias; if the rep has run it several times, stopped iterating, and still prefers the old pitch, treat it as a failed test.
**APPLY WHEN:** cycles are long or deal volume too low for statistically meaningful short-term metrics.
**AVOID WHEN:** you do have fast, high-volume feedback — then measure progression and close rates directly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's stated validation criteria; explicitly qualitative, judged by one person, not measured.

### [PITCH:ROLLOUT-KIT] Roll out with a recorded pitch, practice reps, and a gate
**Move:** Package training as a video of the test rep delivering the pitch plus Q&A, give every rep practice runs, and require them to pass a check with sales leadership before pitching prospects; keep the test rep visible in training because they outrank marketers in credibility with the team.
**APPLY WHEN:** moving from a validated pitch to full team adoption.
**AVOID WHEN:** the pitch has not yet passed the single-rep validation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's suggested training package.

### [PITCH:RECERT] Recertify reps periodically against drift
**Move:** Schedule regular check-ins where reps deliver the pitch to a sales executive, to catch the gradual unintended mutation of the story.
**APPLY WHEN:** the pitch has been live for months; multiple reps; positioning stable.
**AVOID WHEN:** positioning has actually shifted — then update the pitch instead of enforcing the old one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — observed in the mature sales organisations the author worked with.

### [PITCH:OWNER] Name one steward for positioning and the pitch
**Move:** Assign a single owner — product marketing is the natural home — responsible for documenting positioning and pitches, holding approved assets, running periodic cross-functional positioning reviews, and propagating positioning shifts into the pitch and training.
**APPLY WHEN:** after rollout, in any company where positioning is created cross-functionally.
**AVOID WHEN:** ownership becomes a substitute for cross-functional creation — the author still wants the pitch built by a mixed team (XFUNC).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing answer to a frequently asked question.

## Stage 9 — Reuse of the story beyond the sales call

### [PITCH:BUYERSGUIDE] Convert Insight + Alternatives into a buyer's guide
**Move:** Publish a guide that teaches how a knowledgeable buyer should evaluate this category: start from your Step 1 insight to establish what matters, then use the Alternatives step to build categorised value/feature checklists with commentary on which kinds of buyers should require which features and why.
**APPLY WHEN:** many prospects are first-time buyers of your category and must justify a choice upward; you want a high-intent lead magnet (interest in the guide signals active shopping).
**AVOID WHEN:** the checklist would advantage a competitor's feature set — it is still a positioning document.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reports creating dozens of such guides, each reusing pitch components.

### [PITCH:PLGSTORY] Carry the value story into onboarding — but check who it is addressed to
**Move:** In product-led motions, express the differentiated value narrative through onboarding, guided walkthroughs, tips and help content so first product experiences set the same expectations as the pitch; where free end users differ from the enterprise economic buyer, run two deliberately different narratives and know which one each surface is telling.
**APPLY WHEN:** a free tier or trial is effectively the start of the sales process.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not separated end-user positioning from buyer positioning — mixing them muddles both.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=saas/product · market=transactional/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern with a caution the author has seen play out.

### [PITCH:VISUALASSET] Build a positioning graphic
**Move:** Create a deliberate visual to carry differentiation: the product suite, the company portfolio, a "how we fit into your existing infrastructure" diagram, or a workflow graphic that dramatises one value point such as eliminating manual work.
**APPLY WHEN:** selling a suite/platform, or when fit with an existing stack is a live question.
**AVOID WHEN:** the graphic decorates rather than argues a differentiation point; or when a platform diagram makes optional components look mandatory.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — recommended without supporting cases; the book's examples point to downloadable materials.

### [PITCH:INSIGHT-CONTENT] Reuse the insight (not the pitch) in public content
**Move:** Take only the setup-phase market insight into outward formats — a home-page explainer video following the pitch structure, a conference talk exposing your point of view on the market, sponsored or annual research and white papers exploring the insight's themes (repurposable into posts), or a book articulating the insight at length.
**APPLY WHEN:** founder-led demand generation; category education is the bottleneck.
**AVOID WHEN:** on a conference stage — the author considers delivering the actual sales pitch there inappropriate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/consulting · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — examples: HubSpot's *Inbound Marketing* by Brian Halligan and Dharmesh Shah, Postman's graphic novel *The API-First World*, and this book itself, which the author says follows her own pitch structure.
