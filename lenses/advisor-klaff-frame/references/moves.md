# advisor-klaff-frame — moves

Layer: frame and status inside a live meeting. Citation prefix `[FRAME:]`.
Sources: *Pitch Anything* (2011) and *Flip the Script* (2019), both by Oren Klaff. Same author, same school — agreement between them is echo, never independent confirmation. Evidence ceiling for the whole set: `practitioner-anecdote`.

Extraction rule in force: only the tactical frame/status layer is carried. The explanatory neuro-layer of both books is refuted and lives only in `myths-and-demotions.md`.

---

## A. Before the room — preparation, research, staffing

### [FRAME:PRESELECT] Pick the counter-frame before the meeting
**Move:** Classify the counterpart in advance — ego-driven, numbers-driven, or structurally stacked against you — and load one prepared counter (power-busting, intrigue, or time-plus-prize) rather than improvising; keep a second ready to switch to mid-meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** You know who will be in the room and hold any prior read on how they behave.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cold first contact with no read — an aggressive frame chosen wrongly burns the meeting inside the first minute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as the author's standing pre-meeting habit, no counts.

### [FRAME:NORAPPORT] Skip the personal dossier, build one theme instead
**Move:** Do not assemble background on the buyer's private life or plan a small-talk opening. Spend that preparation time constructing a single narrative theme about what the decision would mean for them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Senior decision-makers who sit through many pitches; short slots; you are the underdog on credentials.
**AVOID WHEN:** Referral-led or relationship-led markets, long consultative cycles, and cultures where rapport is the entry ticket — in the book's own case study the heavily researching competitor nearly won.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting/service · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a claim about the futility of small talk with no named source; contested by mainstream trust-building research.

### [FRAME:LEGACY] Reframe the decision from money to status and legacy
**Move:** Identify what the decision-maker wants to be known for, name it aloud as the real subject of the deal, and position your plan as the vehicle for it, with money demoted to a supporting detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer has personal reputational stake — founder, civic project, turnaround, long history with the asset.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement or committee scoring on hard criteria; buyers whose incentive literally is the number; and any case where you cannot actually deliver the status outcome you invoke — that is the exact point where positioning becomes manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "American Legacy" theme in one airport-mandate pitch.

### [FRAME:CUTHOT] Rehearse, then cut to the intrigue floor
**Move:** Rehearse the whole pitch, then repeatedly delete any passage that carries neither intrigue nor emotional charge, minutes at a time over weeks, until roughly twenty minutes of core survives.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any high-stakes set-piece presentation with real preparation time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Technical evaluations where the deleted detail is the deciding criterion, or the buyer explicitly asked for depth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a 55-minute rehearsal reported as cut to a 20:52 delivery.

### [FRAME:TONECAL] Calibrate tone to the buyer's working register
**Move:** Infer the buyer's day-to-day professional register and set the pitch to match — serious and respectful rather than somber — while genuinely enjoying the delivery, since visible strain in the presenter transfers to the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** Regulated, institutional or bureaucratically shaped buyers; unfamiliar professional cultures.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually generate the enjoyment — the source concedes it is unfakeable, and forced levity in a formal room is worse than flatness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning about one buyer group's background with no outcome attributed.

### [FRAME:HOTVISUAL] Physical boards instead of a deck
**Move:** Build large physical display boards showing the object the buyer already cares about and reveal one every few minutes instead of running slides; leave the boards standing in the room afterwards.
**APPLY WHEN:** The category has strong visual appeal; small rooms; you want the persistence and tactility slides do not give.
**AVOID WHEN:** Abstract or financial offers with no visual hook (the source admits it is much harder there); remote or virtual pitches; buyers who read spectacle as a substitute for substance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch to one group of aviation people.

### [FRAME:VENUE-VETO] Refuse venues that cannot be framed
**Move:** Decline public and semi-public spaces for a real pitch — cafés, cafeterias, convention floors, exhibitor booths — and relocate to a rented hospitality suite, hotel meeting room or borrowed private office.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer proposes coffee; conference or trade-show context; any space where third parties can interrupt.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early low-stakes discovery where a casual venue lowers resistance, or when refusing the venue costs you the only access you have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — accounts of interruptions wrecking pitches in public spaces.

### [FRAME:TIPOFFPREP] Source the Tip-Off by interviewing three peers of the target
**Move:** Before the meeting, interview three people at the target's own level, capture how they brief each other peer-to-peer, and assemble the Tip-Off from that vocabulary and those live open issues; memorize it.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have lead time and access to genuine peers of the target; entering an unfamiliar vertical.
**AVOID WHEN:** No access to real peers — vocabulary reconstructed from public content produces a detectable imitation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated as the author's team method, no counts.

### [FRAME:FLASHWRITE] Write the Flash Roll to a four-part skeleton
**Move:** Draft it as problem faced → your assessment → solution applied → economics (time, cost, measured result); write in the register you use with your own technical staff, keep the jargon, delete every reflective or editorial line, target roughly 138–250 words, and rehearse it like stage lines.
**APPLY WHEN:** Preparing for a high-skepticism room; onboarding a founder or rep who is knowledgeable but not persuasive.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no genuinely solved case to draw on — fabricated technical detail is the failure mode this format invites.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — scripts the author wrote for one client rep and one founder.

### [FRAME:VIABILITY] Deal Viability Test before committing capital
**Move:** Before signing, call roughly ten realistic buyers, describe the strongest features, gauge interest and decide go/no-go on demand; the expected shape is a couple of hard yeses, a few "tell me more", the rest declining.
**APPLY WHEN:** Being stuck with the asset would be existential; you have a warm list reachable within a day.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small reversible bets where launching costs less than testing (the source puts the prototype threshold near $50K); the callable pool is not the buying pool; conceptual enthusiasm is easily mistaken for demand.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one test of 16 calls returning soft nos, plus the author's stated routine.

### [FRAME:TRENDCALLS] Harvest a category from the market before you pitch
**Move:** Call analysts, brokers and operators with open low-stakes questions about what is trending and what the next deals look like, log the answers, and hunt for an existing category your unusual asset can be filed under; research comparable transactions sharing the theme in parallel.
**APPLY WHEN:** Your offer is genuinely unlike what the buyer normally purchases and you need an existing frame to place it in.
**AVOID WHEN:** The timeline cannot absorb an expensive dead end (the author's own team burned a week and nearly missed a deadline); no honest category exists — inventing one is fabrication rather than framing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal, where the insight arrived accidentally from a subordinate's complaint.

### [FRAME:IMMERSE] Buy domain immersion before the pitch
**Move:** Put the pitch team physically on the buyer's ground — factory, museum, shops, home city — mine the origin story, product lineage and local self-image, and pitch the buyer's own brand back to them in their terms before proposing anything.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-value competitive pitch, a buyer with strong heritage or identity, and days rather than hours available.
**AVOID WHEN:** Timeline or budget cannot carry it, or the buyer reads the travel as posturing. This is expensive preparation dressed as a technique; the transferable part is the research, not the trip.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — six days of preparation on one deal.

### [FRAME:TALENTSTACK] Rank-match your team to theirs
**Move:** Staff the deal so every counterpart has a same-rank interlocutor — analyst to analyst, counsel to counsel, finance to finance — leaving you speaking only to their decision-maker, and keep yourself scarce and on lead-role work only.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-party enterprise or investor deals where the other side fields a full bench.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo or small-team selling — the fallback is a minimal virtual bench (assistant, technical person, accountant) for alignment and load; over-staffing a small deal reads as theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own standing eight-role stack.

### [FRAME:LOCALPROXY] Put a native in-group voice on stage
**Move:** When the audience's language and culture are not yours, deploy a presenter who is credibly of that world, train them on the script's *structure* rather than its words, and let them localize the details.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cross-border or cross-culture pitch to an audience that will not accept an outsider, where the message is structural and survives translation.
**AVOID WHEN:** The proxy cannot answer live technical questions, or their lack of accountability for delivery becomes visible; high-variance — the source account describes three days of conflict with a temperamental hire and reports the outcome as a lucky break.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one hire (€2,000 for the week), one performance.

### [FRAME:CALLAUDIT] Diagnose the floor from recorded calls, not from live coaching
**Move:** Before prescribing anything, work through a stack of recorded calls spanning different reps, buyers and SKUs, and look for the pattern in how calls *end* rather than for individual mistakes; treat a repeated ending — deferral to an unavailable third party, "let me check and get back to you" — as one systemic cause rather than many individual ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** You own or advise a team whose calls are recorded, win rate is flat, and per-rep coaching has not moved it.
**AVOID WHEN:** The sample is one rep or a handful of calls — you would be promoting noise into a "system" problem.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one six-hour listening session at one company, no dataset.

### [FRAME:PERSONAAUDIT] Audit a call for persona switching
**Move:** Replay a call and label each stretch with which of the five sales characters is speaking, count the transitions, treat each transition as a trust cost, and rewrite the call so a single consistent character carries it end to end.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-call review; calls that "went fine" and ended in deferral; onboarding a rep who mimics a top performer.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a live checklist during a call — mid-conversation self-monitoring produces exactly the stiffness it is meant to remove; weak as a hiring or firing signal, since it is a coaching lens and not a measurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a typology from the author's own listening, with no coding scheme, no inter-rater check and no link from switch count to win rate.

### [FRAME:OWNSCRIPT] Write a personal script instead of copying the star
**Move:** Have each rep write their own call script in their own vocabulary and values rather than imitating the team's natural closer, on the claim that compellingness comes from consistency with self rather than borrowed style.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enabling a team around one outlier performer; onboarding reps whose calls sound performed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The rep has no domain competence yet — an authentic script from someone who cannot answer technical questions still fails; expertise is a prerequisite, not a substitute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one two-day training with self-reported outcomes.

### [FRAME:STICKGUNS] Pre-decide the concession answers and deliver them flat
**Move:** Fix your answers to the standard pressure asks in advance and deliver them politely without justification theatre: discount — no; free upgrade — no; "send me a proposal" — answered with a question about whether terms are already verbally agreed. Consistency is the mechanism; the buyer's remaining options are to work with you or leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** Concession requests arrive as reflex tests early, before value is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** The concession is a real commercial lever in a live negotiation with a qualified buyer, or your pricing has room the buyer already knows about; rigidity against a buyer with power and alternatives simply ends deals, and the source concedes losing some — a cost, not a free win.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one in-house refusal story plus a claimed quarterly revenue doubling with no baseline.

## B. Access and first contact — status sorting

### [FRAME:PEERENTRY] Open with a shared prior deal, not with your offer
**Move:** Approach with a low-arousal opener naming a concrete engagement you both touch, hands at your sides, no forced handshake, no pitch posture — buying a few seconds of attention before the Tip-Off.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cold approach in a social or event setting; the target is unreachable through normal channels.
**AVOID WHEN:** The shared reference is thin or unverifiable, or the target is mid-performance or surrounded — wait for the crowd to thin.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one retold approach that preceded a $25M investment.

### [FRAME:TIPOFF] Three-element Status Tip-Off
**Move:** Deliver a compact three-sentence statement carrying (1) specific industry vocabulary an outsider could not produce, (2) a recent consequential action you took in that industry, (3) a live issue the industry is currently arguing about — then stop and let the target respond.
**APPLY WHEN:** First contact with a decision-maker, short window, and you need to be sorted as an insider before any deal content.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot back the claims under one follow-up question; the "insider" facts are stale, since naming a resolved issue marks you as an outsider faster than silence; and on repeat — this is a single-use move per counterpart, recognized the second time and read as a rehearsed act.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one conference approach, wide reuse claimed without counts.

### [FRAME:TIPOFFSTORY] In-group detail story as Tip-Off
**Move:** Tell a short specific story containing a detail only a genuine member of the audience's world would know — a quirk of a local product, a shared inconvenience — and let the audience supply the punchline themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** The first minute with a skeptical audience whose group you actually belong to, especially when your appearance signals outsider.
**AVOID WHEN:** The detail is researched rather than lived — a wrong or generic detail marks you as a tourist faster than saying nothing; and on repeat, since it is explicitly single-use per audience.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one factory-floor delivery, audience reaction the only evidence offered.

### [FRAME:STATUSALIGN] Status Alignment via a contrarian shared frame
**Move:** Open, especially from a weak position, by voicing a belief the room already privately holds and outsiders usually do not, and let agreement register before advancing any proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the underdog, an outsider, or previously rejected, and the audience shares an identity you can honestly align with.
**AVOID WHEN:** The shared frame is invented or the alignment faked — a contrarian cultural claim you do not hold reads as pandering and is unrecoverable; also mismatched to a diverse room with no single in-group, and a half-executed attempt is worse than none.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — read off body language in one room; nods are a weak proxy for agreement.

### [FRAME:STATUSTIP] Name the buyer's situation as a category
**Move:** Before the buyer speaks, give their situation a category name, describe how that category usually ends badly, cite your track record with exactly that category — ideally with a living example they can see — then name the good outcome as a category too.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer is distracted or treats you as low-status, and you genuinely have comparable cases.
**AVOID WHEN:** The label is inaccurate or insulting and gets rejected outright; your track record cannot survive a reference check; or the buyer experiences being categorized as being handled.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one conversation; the track-record claim is the seller's own.

### [FRAME:NOPITCHYET] Treat "what's the deal?" as a two-second audition
**Move:** When the aligned target asks about your deal, give one or two compressed lines (size, headline economics), exchange contact details and end the encounter — do not open a twenty-minute presentation in a social setting.
**APPLY WHEN:** The alignment signal has just fired — the target asks your name, turns toward you, asks what you are working on.
**AVOID WHEN:** It is a booked meeting with allocated time, or failing to state substance now means there is no second contact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the same single encounter as [FRAME:PEERENTRY].

### [FRAME:CURIOSITY] Standing threat plus a withheld insight
**Move:** To get a high-status person's attention cold, imply their position or reputation is quietly shifting and that people are talking, then say you hold something valuable they can use without revealing it, staying visibly non-needy throughout.
**APPLY WHEN:** Approaching or recruiting someone who currently has no reason to talk to you and whose standing depends on ranking.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot substantiate the implied gossip — this manipulates social anxiety and collapses if challenged; buyers with no status stake; any relationship that must stay clean long-term; and on repeat use with the same person, where it is recognized immediately.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=consultative/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one approach to a single player, called near-universal without evidence.

### [FRAME:EQUALCLOCK] Open by framing both calendars as equally scarce
**Move:** Start with a line treating the meeting as a mutual scheduling achievement and the agenda as dense, then move straight into substance — signalling that your time costs what theirs does and that you are not asking a favor.
**APPLY WHEN:** Meetings obtained through a favor or intermediary, where the status default is supplicant.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship genuinely is asymmetric and the counterpart reads the line as posturing, or the intermediary's goodwill is the real asset you are spending.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one venture-fund room.

### [FRAME:REOPEN] Reopen a decision that has already gone the other way
**Move:** Call the decision owner after the loss, name a defensible procedural reason the first round was unrepresentative, request a narrow second review rather than a full restart, quietly note the reputational cost to *them* if the market concludes their large accounts always route to the incumbent, then immediately concede their right to keep the incumbent and pivot into substance.
**APPLY WHEN:** A competitive loss is announced but not signed, you have a genuinely different angle, and the buyer has ongoing reputational exposure to a vendor market.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have nothing new to say — the second meeting burns the relationship; the reputational beat lands as a threat rather than an observation; reopening an awarded decision is procedurally impossible; and on repeat, since it is openly manipulative and single-use per relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one phone call, one buyer, reported as succeeding with light resistance.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-OPEN] Set the prize frame in the first sentence
**Move:** Open with a low-risk line that quietly signals scarcity and a hard stop — glad this fit in, there is another meeting straight after, let's start.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any first meeting on their turf; the safest entry point of the whole prize system.
**AVOID WHEN:** It is untrue and checkable, or with existing customers in an expansion conversation, where manufactured scarcity reads as arrogance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — offered explicitly as the beginner-safe version of prizing.

### [FRAME:TIME-CONSTRAINT-OPEN] Announce a short, self-imposed limit
**Move:** Open by telling the room you have roughly twenty minutes for the big idea and time to discuss before you must leave; walk in already holding a plausible reason you are needed elsewhere and state it once, casually, so your time is visibly finite before the pitch starts.
**APPLY WHEN:** First substantive pitch meeting; any audience that does not know how long they are committed for; any meeting you suspect will run over or end limp.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has explicitly cleared a long working session or an on-site day — a false constraint there reads as disrespect and is easily called out; technical deep-dives where cutting short reads as evasion. Documented misuse: announcing twenty minutes and running an hour, which destroys the credibility the pattern buys.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's default opener, no outcome data.

### [FRAME:GATEKEEPER-BYPASS] Refuse the gatekeeper script
**Move:** Decline the "let me see if he's in" ritual and state your requirement flatly instead of asking permission to be admitted.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are owed something, expected, or holding real leverage.
**AVOID WHEN:** Ordinary prospecting — the source's illustrating episode escalates to walking past reception during a fraud investigation with police on standby; as a sales tactic this is a fast path to being ejected and blacklisted, so treat the extreme version as biography rather than instruction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single extraordinary episode.

### [FRAME:MOMENTUM-EARLY] Create status in the first minutes, and arrive on time
**Move:** Establish position early rather than waiting, on the claim that each minute of deference reinforces theirs; be punctual whenever you expect to open from the weaker position, since lateness forfeits standing you then have to buy back.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any meeting you expect to open in the low position.
**AVOID WHEN:** Punctuality is never contraindicated; the "force a collision early" half is contraindicated with committees that punish visible friction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule without supporting cases.

## C. Entry rituals and environmental frames — on their turf

### [FRAME:NO-RITUAL] Decline the host's deference rituals
**Move:** Recognize the arrival sequence — badge, guest book, "take a seat", stale coffee, hand-off to an aide, a handed agenda — and treat compliance as optional theatre: skip thanking them for squeezing you in, stay unimpressed by displays of wealth and rank, and do not wait where you were placed.
**APPLY WHEN:** On their turf, outnumbered, first meeting, being processed as one more vendor, or the host visibly runs a status apparatus.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement, compliance or security processes where the ritual is a legal requirement; cultures and committees where reciprocity ritual is the actual gate; when the person running the ritual is your champion; and with an existing customer in expansion, where coldness reads as arrogance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — lobby and fund-office stories, no measured comparison and no counter-cases.

### [FRAME:LOCAL-STAR] Create local star power inside a domain you own
**Move:** As soon as you have attention, narrow the discussion to the slice where your knowledge is unassailable and hold it there, demonstrating depth visibly rather than asserting general credentials — whoever owns the domain gives the instructions inside it. Pair it with a mildly surprising but friendly act to capture attention.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are outranked on wealth or title but have real depth on a narrow relevant dimension, and the audience can out-argue you on general business but not on your specialty.
**AVOID WHEN:** The depth is faked — it inverts on the first specific question; the buyer's own expert is in the room and better than you; or the delivery is humourless, which the source warns produces unpredictable reactions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — occupational analogies plus the author's own meetings.

### [FRAME:DEFY-SMALL] Small, friendly, well-timed defiance
**Move:** Perform one mildly shocking but non-hostile act that breaks the host's script at the moment it presents itself, then immediately convert the pause into your framing point — in the source's illustration, appropriating and dividing the host's snack and tying the split to how his deals share value.
**APPLY WHEN:** The status gap is too wide for verbal frame control alone and the host is performing dominance — reviewing documents while you talk, seating you below him, summoning subordinates.
**AVOID WHEN:** The act would read as aggression, contempt or a cultural insult; long-standing relationships where the shock has no upside; audiences containing people who must defend the host afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted meeting, outcome self-reported, and the author calls it risky himself.

### [FRAME:DENIAL] Small denial — tease, then withhold
**Move:** Put a visibly interesting artifact within reach — a labelled folder, a visual, a page of the deck — let them reach for it, then withdraw it lightly with a playful "not yet", never sharply.
**APPLY WHEN:** The opening minutes of a meeting you must control, against a target who habitually runs the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is already in evaluation mode and asked for the material formally; committees where one offended stakeholder can veto; anything sent in writing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as a repeatable opener with one illustrating scenario.

### [FRAME:STATUS-SEQ-6] The six-step status sequence
**Move:** Run in order: ignore power rituals and environmental traps; stay unaffected by the buyer's global status; take a small denial or defiance; move immediately into your expert domain; apply a prize frame; get the buyer to say something that confirms your standing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pitching on someone else's territory; any first meeting where status is undefined.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship is already collaborative and status settled — running the sequence then reads as manufactured conflict.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — post-hoc reconstruction of the author's own meetings.

### [FRAME:STATUS-WAITER-SEQ] Defiance → isolate → rescue → ally, in a group
**Move:** A small defiant correction of the nominal high-status person; a controlled delay that leaves him waiting; a manufactured minor mistake you then rescue him from while publicly crediting him; then conspicuous recognition of one other person present, so that attacking you means attacking them too.
**APPLY WHEN:** Group settings where one person is trying to run the room and the regard of other participants matters.
**AVOID WHEN:** Anything touching your actual buyer's dignity in front of their team — humiliating a stakeholder is unrecoverable; committees where the humiliated party holds a veto. The source presents this as observed behaviour, not a verbatim recommendation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one observed dinner reconstructed by its target; the story's own moral is that the operator chose moderation rather than maximum extraction.

### [FRAME:AVOID-BETA-TRAP] Drop the recognisable sales repertoire
**Move:** Remove the visible technique set — rapport preamble, feature/benefit recitation, scripted objection handling, trial closes — on the grounds that experienced buyers recognize each one and use the recognition to hold you subordinate.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sophisticated buyers who have been sold to for years.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have nothing to replace them with; transactional or inbound motions where the standard sequence measurably converts; junior reps without the standing to carry a status play.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — a broad claim about decades of sales training with no data on which techniques still convert.

### [FRAME:MRBIG-15] Decision-maker absent — grant a deadline, then leave
**Move:** If the principal skips the meeting, refuse to present to proxies: grant a short fixed wait (fifteen minutes), say plainly you must leave after it, and if he does not appear, leave without presenting, without leaving materials and without apologizing.
**APPLY WHEN:** A single-shot pitch that only works told first-hand, and you have real alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The absent person is one of several stakeholders and those present are genuine influencers; long enterprise cycles where the champion in the room is the actual path in; when you have no other pipeline.

### [FRAME:HOMETURF] Reschedule only on your ground
**Move:** If you still want the account after a no-show, offer to reschedule while acknowledging that misses happen — on the condition that the next meeting is at your location.
**APPLY WHEN:** After the other side has created a documented social debt.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are the challenger with no leverage; buyer-side procurement rules forbid it; travel is genuinely one-directional in their favour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted from practice.

### [FRAME:QUALIFY-BACK-EARLY] Make the buyer explain himself, early
**Move:** Early in the meeting, ask the buyer to explain himself to you and state that you are selective about who you work with, leaving the implied question — why would I want this business — unanswered.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in a meeting on their turf, when the buyer is treating you as an interchangeable vendor.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are demonstrably the smaller party with no track record — unearned selectivity reads as bluff; commodity or transactional purchases where the buyer holds real alternatives.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as routine practice with no outcome data.

### [FRAME:HOSTAUDIT] Audit the host's environment aloud, then pivot to their operational pain

**APPLY WHEN:** You are physically on the buyer's turf, you have researched a verifiable weakness, and their team prides itself on rigor.
**AVOID WHEN:** The weakness is guessed — a wrong diagnosis delivered as certainty destroys the frame; risky where face-loss is culturally costly and the audit humiliates rather than aligns.

## D. Frame collisions in the room

### [FRAME:PLOW] Plowing — hold the frame through social pressure
**Move:** At the moment of collision, felt as anxiety, commit fully and keep moving without self-correction, apology or renegotiation of your own position; expect the discomfort and do not read it as a signal to soften.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have made a frame move and the other side pushes back once.
**AVOID WHEN:** The pushback contains new information, or resistance reaches three or more rounds — the source supplies no stop rule, and this is the single most damaging move in the set to misapply.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — self-narrated; the author notes the counterparty's rational move would have been to refuse.

### [FRAME:DEFY-TIME] Match and raise the time squeeze
**Move:** When told you have a short slot, answer with a shorter one of your own, delivered smiling but seriously, and let it escalate into a light game if they play along.
**APPLY WHEN:** The target opens with a time restriction used as a status move.
**AVOID WHEN:** The constraint is genuine and logistical; long-cycle enterprise deals where you actually need the airtime; when you cannot afford to be held to the number you just named.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — meetings reportedly compressed to a couple of minutes this way.

### [FRAME:TIME-BREAK] Break the "I only have ten minutes" opener
**Move:** Instead of thanking them for the slot, decline the terms and qualify back: say you do not work that way, that rescheduling is pointless without mutual trust, and ask whether they keep appointments and hold a schedule.
**APPLY WHEN:** An on-site visit opened with a status-signalling time limit.
**AVOID WHEN:** The constraint is real and their calendar visibly collapsed; early relationship with an enterprise buyer whose calendar is not theirs to control.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a script with an idealised reported response.

### [FRAME:EXPERTBUST] Dissolve a manufactured constraint with a checkable fact
**Move:** When pressure rests on a factual claim, answer with a precise verifiable number that dissolves the constraint, then immediately set a small demand of your own.
**APPLY WHEN:** The other side's pressure rests on a fact you genuinely know better than they do.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are guessing — being wrong hands them the frame permanently; also when the constraint is real and public.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted exchange between the author and his own partner.

### [FRAME:TRIVIAL-NEG] Move the dominance contest onto something worthless
**Move:** When the counterpart is openly ignoring you, physically reclaim the object of his distraction and open a mock-serious negotiation over something trivial, then return to the real agenda.
**APPLY WHEN:** Visible contempt or inattention from a dominant principal, and the meeting is otherwise lost.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any relationship you need to survive the meeting intact; committee settings; when the counterpart is a customer rather than a peer investor, where it reads as insubordination.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting, one favourable outcome, reported by the person telling the story.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-RIGHT] Hold a position of rightness against a rationaliser
**Move:** When the other side stalls behind detailed rational explanation, refuse to engage the detail at all: state what is owed, state that explanation is irrelevant, repeat.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are genuinely and verifiably in the right — contractual, legal, factual — and the other side is spinning.
**AVOID WHEN:** The facts are contested or you are the one asking a favour; ordinary commercial disagreements, where moral framing is a relationship-ender. The illustrating case is debt recovery from an actual fraud, not a sale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single high-drama recovery episode.

### [FRAME:CADENCE] Convert a won frame into rules plus a deliverable clock
**Move:** Once you hold control, state the operating rules explicitly and attach a short recurring interval on which something concrete must be delivered to you, with the meeting not ending until the full amount clears.
**APPLY WHEN:** Recovery, escalation, or a stalled commitment where the counterparty is stalling for time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Normal sales cycles — imposing a deliverable clock on a prospect who owes you nothing collapses the deal; committees with their own governance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one six-hour session recounted.

### [FRAME:HIGHLEVEL-REDIRECT] Answer detail at summary level, then pivot to fit
**Move:** When a drill-down starts, answer directly with one line of top-level figures, note they are verifiable later, and immediately return to the relationship question — are we a fit, should we work together. Same pattern for premature price questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** An analytical audience starts calculating mid-pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** Formal diligence, procurement, or any stage where refusing detail reads as concealment; technical buyers who evaluate precisely on the detail you are deflecting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a standing script for financial deals.

### [FRAME:DETAIL-GATE] Keep deep technical material out of the room
**Move:** Physically separate deep technical and financial material from the presentation and release it after the meeting, so the audience cannot start computing during the pitch.
**APPLY WHEN:** You control the materials and the meeting is a decision-to-engage rather than an evaluation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Buyer-driven evaluation, security or technical review, or when disclosure is contractually or ethically required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated method, forward-referenced within the book.

### [FRAME:THREAD-PRUNE] Drop threads that do not advance the deal
**Move:** Once you hold the frame, silently abandon conversational branches that do not serve the deal and amplify the ones that do — no acknowledgment, no polite follow-up on digressions.
**APPLY WHEN:** The meeting drifts into social chatter, war stories or side topics after you have gained attention.
**AVOID WHEN:** The digression is the buyer surfacing a real objection or a political constraint in coded form.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated as the author's own rule.

### [FRAME:EASYONBUYER] Go easy once a buyer's power frame breaks
**Move:** Expect most buyers to open with a power frame and expect it to break easily under prizing, intrigue and time frames; once it breaks, deliberately do not press the advantage, since most buyers have never met resistance and will be off balance.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers accustomed to vendors complying with every logistical whim.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have mistaken a legitimate constraint for a power play, or you would be escalating against a buyer with internal political exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a general observation about buyer behaviour.

### [FRAME:NOCONFUSE] Refuse the confusion frame
**Move:** When the counterpart uses confusion, translation, or "explain it better" to slow you and lower your standing, decline the premise aloud: state that you will not let them be confused, name their competence, declare the deal ordinary and simple, and note that the time constraint requires taking your account at face value for now.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer weaponizes incomprehension or delegates you to interpreters and juniors, and status is being pushed down.
**AVOID WHEN:** The confusion is genuine — a real language gap or real technical unfamiliarity — where refusing it destroys trust; risky with a buyer who outranks you and enjoys the power play.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single retelling in which the author admits nerves and improvisation.

### [FRAME:QUEUEQ] Serialize the interrogation and pick the question you want
**Move:** When the room shifts into rapid-fire questioning, interrupt and impose one question at a time by naming a specific person and inviting their question — choosing the questioner whose topic sits inside your prepared strength, which becomes the launchpad for the Flash Roll.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multiple stakeholders are firing risk questions, certainty is collapsing, and you have a rehearsed answer in one domain.
**AVOID WHEN:** The questions concern a domain you cannot answer — ordering the queue only delays exposure; also when the senior buyer resents being managed procedurally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one scene.

## E. Certainty and expert authority

### [FRAME:FLASHROLL] Deliver a 60–90 second technical mastery burst
**Move:** Deliver a memorized block of roughly 250 words at about double normal speaking pace: state the problem as routine, give your assessment in dense domain jargon, name the actions taken, land a specific numeric outcome — no hedging verbs, no emotion, no bid for validation.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer doubts your competence or your fit for their vertical; a skeptical technical audience; the moment a question implicitly challenges your domain depth.
**AVOID WHEN:** The audience can interrogate the specifics and you cannot survive it; comprehension rather than credibility is what is missing; and on repeat to the same buyer, where it registers as a rehearsed act rather than mastery.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one raise plus one rep's reported turnaround.

### [FRAME:FLASHTHEIRS] Flash Roll aimed at the buyer's own product line
**Move:** After alignment and before any proposal, deliver 30–60 seconds of fast jargon-dense analysis of the buyer's *own* line — model names, trims, prices against named competitors, feature lists, performance figures — ending in a concrete opportunity framed as a number; where the Tip-Off makes you one of them, this deliberately separates you as the technical authority.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer has a hard problem in exactly the domain you can speak to, early in the meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot sustain follow-up at that depth, or the audience is technically deeper than you — the burst reads as mastery only to someone less prepared; and never reuse the same roll with the same audience.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two performances; "silence means acceptance" is the author's read, not a measurement.

### [FRAME:MARKETFLASH] Flash Roll variant for market rather than craft
**Move:** Run the same dense fast register over market structure: total market size, the single segment actually earning margin, three reasons the buyer's customer values the category, your price point with the multiple by which it undercuts the field, then the technical spec block that justifies it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Investor audiences needing market credibility and technical credibility inside one slot.
**AVOID WHEN:** The figures are estimates you cannot defend line by line, or the price advantage is temporary and the audience knows it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one retold pitch.

### [FRAME:FLASHEXIT] Exit the Flash Roll flat
**Move:** Close with a shrug-level line that this is simply what you would do given what you see, then move straight to the next section — never wait for a reaction or approval.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always, as the terminator of any Flash Roll variant.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never within this frame — waiting for validation is what collapses the expert posture; the caveat carries over to any repeated delivery, where an obviously scripted exit line loses its flatness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a delivery rule without a supporting case.

### [FRAME:ANALYSTMODE] Present the way the buyer's own function would present
**Move:** Mirror the professional register of the room rather than the entrepreneur register: demonstrate instead of asserting quality, compress the case to under a hundred words, skip pleasantries, strip optimism and adjectives, use every second.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pitching to analysts, underwriters or technical review boards — audiences paid to find your flaws.
**AVOID WHEN:** The audience is a founder or marketing buyer who reads flat delivery as absence of conviction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one venture-fund pitch.

### [FRAME:PRESTRESS] Attack your own numbers before they do
**Move:** In roughly ninety words, state that you have already run your model through the harshest named stress test in their world and layered worst-case assumptions on top — slower hiring than competitors, materially higher input costs, customers paying late — so the projection you are about to show is cash actually collectible.
**APPLY WHEN:** Analyst or committee audiences whose default posture is dismantling projections.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your numbers cannot survive the stated test, since the claim invites verification; or the named test is not one this audience recognizes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one room, effect judged from observed body language only.

### [FRAME:NATIVEFORMAT] Give data in the format the buyer would have built themselves
**Move:** Replace standard projections with the artifact the buyer's own analysts would assemble — raw contracts, customer agreements and invoices in their working layout — physically distributed so the whole room handles it simultaneously.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer side is staffed with analysts whose job is verification and you have real underlying documents.
**AVOID WHEN:** The underlying evidence is thin — this format invites scrutiny at maximum resolution; remote meetings; buyers who want a decision summary rather than source data.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a deliberate tactic in one meeting.

## F. Pitch construction and framing the case

### [FRAME:STRONG-SEQ] Run the pitch as a fixed six-step sequence
**Move:** Structure any pitch as set the frame → tell the story → reveal the intrigue → offer the prize → nail the hookpoint → get a decision, and treat each step as a discrete failure point, diagnosing which one broke rather than blaming the deal terms.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-stakes single-shot meeting with a principal, where you control the agenda and have 15–25 minutes.
**AVOID WHEN:** Multi-call consultative cycles where discovery precedes any pitch; technical evaluations where the decision is a scorecard rather than a moment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own capital raises, no external validation offered.

### [FRAME:SEQUENCE] Run the five-step certainty sequence and hand over control last
**Move:** Sequence the meeting deliberately — in-group alignment first, technical authority second, the three buyer questions third, risk-reducing positioning fourth, buying criteria fifth — and only then relinquish the decision without pressure, discounting or cornering.
**APPLY WHEN:** A single high-stakes meeting where you will not get many turns: investor pitches, competitive finals, board-level presentations.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deal is multi-threaded over months with different audiences per session — the sequence is built for one room, one sitting; inapplicable where a scored RFP drives procurement and status has no channel.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "taught to thousands" with no measurement; the sequence rests on two narratives.

### [FRAME:MSG-SIMPLIFY] Design the message for hostile triage by a busy evaluator
**Move:** Strip abstractions, lead with concrete verifiable visual chunks, offer two clearly contrasted options rather than a nuanced spectrum, make the idea legibly novel in the first minute, and assume fine distinctions will not survive.
**APPLY WHEN:** First exposure to a busy decision-maker with no prior context on you.
**AVOID WHEN:** Technical due diligence, RFP responses, or any stage where the buyer is explicitly comparing specifications — over-simplifying there reads as evasion; note the source's own correction that statistics simplify hard while people-and-relationship detail can stay rich.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — delivery advice stated without study support and originally justified by an explanatory layer that is demoted here.

### [FRAME:FRAMECHECK] Check for the interpretive frame before you sell the specs
**Move:** Before presenting technical differentiators, establish whether this audience already reads those numbers as good; if not, either restrict the pitch to audiences who do, or drop the specs and route the message through the three buyer questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Technical product, mixed-sophistication audience, short slot.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is a specialist who resents simplification, or regulatory and procurement processes require the specs regardless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one marketing illustration, no data.

### [FRAME:TRACKRECORD-AVG] One great credential, not a résumé
**Move:** Answer the background question with wins only — things you built, projects that worked — in under two minutes, and stop after the strongest item instead of appending merely good ones; the structure is degree, one quantified home run, what you left to do this.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in any pitch, right after the framing exchange.
**AVOID WHEN:** Due-diligence or vendor-qualification settings where a complete history is required, or when the buyer is explicitly assessing team depth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one funding pitch; the supporting "impressions are averaged, not summed" claim is uncited and demoted.

### [FRAME:WHYNOW-3F] Three-market-forces "Why now?"
**Move:** Before describing the idea, place it against three converging forces — economic, social, technological — each with a concrete consequence, then narrate the idea's origin and conclude that a short window is open that your plan is timed to; only then present the plan.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any new offer or category; deals that could be suspected of having been shopped around; buyers who believe nothing has changed; timing is a genuine differentiator.
**AVOID WHEN:** The forces are fabricated, generic or unverifiable — a hollow trend story invites a market-thesis argument you cannot win, and a well-informed buyer collapses the whole opening; also weak with buyers who know the market better than you or are already urgent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two product illustrations plus one delivered pitch citing a claimed quarter-over-quarter increase in available investor money.

### [FRAME:MOVE-NOT-STATES] Show motion, not before-and-after
**Move:** Never present a static old-way-versus-new-way contrast; narrate the market moving from the old state to the new one with the forces doing the pushing, so your success reads as a consequence of momentum rather than as a claim.
**APPLY WHEN:** Explaining why the idea is timely or inevitable; wherever a slide would otherwise be a two-column comparison.
**AVOID WHEN:** The movement is not real — sophisticated buyers test the trend claim directly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — supported in the source only by an analogy that is demoted; the rhetorical pattern stands on its own.

### [FRAME:IDEA-INTRO] One-minute idea introduction skeleton
**Move:** Deliver the big idea in about a minute on a fixed skeleton — for [target customers] who are dissatisfied with [current offerings], my product is a [new category] that provides [key benefit], unlike [competing product], mine [key differentiator] — then stop; details come later.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the "Why now?" frame, and as a reset when the room has lost the thread.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has already bought the category and wants implementation specifics; and on repeat with someone who has heard it, where the template reads as canned.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three worked examples; the skeleton is attributed in the source to Geoff Moore (1999) without a citation.

### [FRAME:BIGIDEAINTRO] Introduce the big idea with a social reward attached
**Move:** Present the idea as different in kind from the me-too options, then attach three pulls at once — a social reward, the chance to be the protagonist of the story, and the money — revealing the theme physically as you name it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Crowded competitive fields; buyers with reputational upside available.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's mandate is purely financial return with no discretionary narrative, or you cannot substantiate the differentiation immediately afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one delivered pitch; its supporting "three truths" are demoted claims.

### [FRAME:BESTIDEA] Open by defining the criterion that favours you
**Move:** Start by telling the room what the decision should actually be judged on — the quality of the idea rather than size, track record or polish — and attach gravity to the choice so a wrong pick has consequences.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the smaller vendor, the competitor's opening is predictably a credentials parade, and the buyer has discretion over criteria.
**AVOID WHEN:** Scoring is pre-published and weighted, or the buyer's actual risk driver is vendor size — you would be arguing against their fiduciary logic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one competitive pitch, won.

### [FRAME:PRAISEBUST] Demote rivals by praising the right attributes
**Move:** Compliment the competition warmly for exactly the traits you want held against them — huge teams, armies of junior researchers, no expense spared — so the praise performs the demotion.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitors are in the room or known, and their scale is genuinely a cost or agility liability for this buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their scale is the buyer's actual risk mitigation; the buyer has an existing relationship with them; you cannot deliver the agility you implicitly promise — transparent versions read as sniping and cost you the moral high ground.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one delivered pitch, where the author reads a competitor's scowl as confirmation.

### [FRAME:BIGCOSMALL] The "you'll be a small account there" reframe
**Move:** Concede up front that hiring the large incumbent is the safe, blameless, defensible choice — naming real firms, founding dates, headcounts and revenues to make the concession credible — then reframe the consequence: at that scale the buyer's account is minor and gets staffed by juniors, whereas at your firm it gets senior people who chose this work.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the smaller challenger against a brand-safe incumbent and the decision is driven by career risk rather than outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually staff it senior — the claim is checkable and collapses on reference calls; or the buyer values the incumbent's scale and coverage for real operational reasons.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as a script delivered many times, still the author's own account with no outcome data.

### [FRAME:MONEYLANG] Replace craft language with money language
**Move:** Strip differentiators any competitor could also claim — full-service, collaborative, award-winning, combined years of experience — and lead with a single quantified outcome sized to be simultaneously large in absolute terms and modest as a percentage of the buyer's existing base.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competing in a category where vendors look interchangeable and the decision is drifting toward brand safety or price.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot support the number — a fabricated projection is a liability once finance models it; note that in the source's own case the competitor made this identical move and still lost, so the money number is table stakes rather than the differentiator.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one reconstructed engagement told from the author's side.

### [FRAME:BIGTHREE] Answer the three gating questions before any detail
**Move:** Format the opening so the buyer receives, in order, a slow-moving threat in their environment they should already be tracking, a large payoff that is easy to measure, and evidence that the terms are fair because you carry risk too — only then release the prepared detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any first substantive presentation, especially to committees.
**AVOID WHEN:** The threat has to be manufactured to work — invented doom is the failure mode; also when the buyer arrives already sold and threat framing reopens risk analysis.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to unnamed psychologists with no study, sample or citation.

### [FRAME:DOOMCONTEXT] Open with the slow-moving threat, specifically staffed
**Move:** Begin with concrete, named, current threat actors and their economics in the buyer's own domain — who is doing the damage, at what scale, at what cost — establishing that the environment has become materially more dangerous than assumed, before introducing your solution.
**APPLY WHEN:** Security, compliance, risk or continuity offers where the threat is verifiable and current.
**AVOID WHEN:** The threat is speculative or the numbers unsourced — sophisticated buyers fact-check in the room; inappropriate where fear framing violates the buyer's procurement norms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one warm-up built on the "Winter Is Coming" idea.

### [FRAME:WINTER] Winter Is Coming — the structural-shift threat beat
**Move:** Establish the buyer's proven durability with concrete history, then open the threat: a structural change in their industry that will eliminate their current way of operating faster than expected, evidenced by two or more specific converging threats named by name and direction, closing with a narrow window in which action still works. Never claim your product is better than theirs — claim the playing field is being replaced and position yourself as already operating on the new one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers who are currently profitable, comfortable or complacent and see no reason to move, in an industry with a visible shift you can evidence with named competitors and figures.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is already in crisis (fear is saturated — they need a floor, not a threat); regulated or compliance-heavy sales where doom framing reads as FUD; expansion conversations with existing customers; and when the threats are vague or invented, where generic urgency undoes the status you built.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one public corporate turnaround used as illustration plus two of the author's own deliveries, no control.

### [FRAME:TWOX] The 2X reward claim
**Move:** Name one dimension the buyer cares about and show your deal doubles it — output, speed, satisfaction — or halves its cost, on a defined horizon, tied directly to the threats just named, and ask for the specific thing you need (time, budget, the account) as the mechanism; subjective doublings are permitted as long as the formula stays 2X.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the threat beat; when the buyer must abandon a working incumbent and switching cost rather than price is the real objection; when a doubling is arguable from the buyer's own base rates.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot substantiate the multiple — an unbacked 2X invites a due-diligence teardown and is the easiest claim in the set to turn into an unfalsifiable promise; incremental or compliance purchases where a modest provable gain is safer.

### [FRAME:SKIN] Skin in the Game proof, delivered as a sacrifice inventory
**Move:** Instead of asserting trustworthiness, show what you personally lose if the deal fails — money, time, reputation, contractual lock-in — ideally more exposure than you are asking of them and something a larger competitor structurally cannot match; one delivery pattern enumerates visible frugality, lets the audience ask where the money went, then reveals it is inside the company. Close by aligning yourself with the buyer against the shared threat.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer's unspoken question is whether you will still be there when things get hard, the founder or principal is in the room, and you can actually make the commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no real exposure — fabricated sacrifice is a credibility bomb, and an "all in" you cannot deliver converts a won deal into a churned one; employee sellers of a large vendor where personal stakes are implausible; buyers who read personal financial exposure as solvency risk; reserved committees where the martial register misfires.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/product/saas · market=investor/stage/enterprise

### [FRAME:ARCHETYPE] Compress with a stock archetype
**Move:** Describe the deal, the market or a person by invoking a widely recognized type in roughly fifty words, so the listener completes the picture themselves and experiences a fast "I get it" instead of processing new information.
**APPLY WHEN:** A complex situation must be understood in seconds by a room that will not read a deck.
**AVOID WHEN:** The archetype carries a stereotype that offends or misfires; technical buyers who will pick the analogy apart; when it flattens a distinction that actually matters to the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage/consultative

### [FRAME:POINTCALL] Make the threat personal to one person in the room
**Move:** During the threat segment, single out one attendee and walk them through how the abstract risk lands on them specifically, then leave the silence for the room to absorb.
**APPLY WHEN:** Live presentation to a group that is emotionally disengaged, where the risk genuinely applies to individuals present.
**AVOID WHEN:** The target could be humiliated or is the economic buyer's ally; health, money-loss or job-loss framings aimed at a named individual, which read as an attack; any recorded or remote setting where the pressure cannot be read and defused.

### [FRAME:PARTNERSKEPTIC] Interrogate your own partner in front of the buyer

**APPLY WHEN:** The key risk is a person rather than the product, and two or more of your side are in the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** Unrehearsed — an unprepared partner turns staged doubt into real doubt; when the doubt raised is unresolvable; with any buyer who would perceive the exchange as theatre, which it is, and which is repeatable only against a buyer who has not seen it before.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage

### [FRAME:BUDGET-SKILL] Pitch budgeting competence, not revenue forecasts

**APPLY WHEN:** Investor pitches; any deal where financial credibility is under scrutiny; early-stage companies whose forecasts will be discounted anyway.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's mandate is growth-rate driven and forecasts are the actual decision variable.

**AVOID WHEN:** You have no honest answer on switching cost, since you would be inviting the question you cannot answer.

### [FRAME:OFFER-CONCRETE] State delivery in two minutes

**AVOID WHEN:** The giveaway is the entirety of your value — you have just given away the deal; low-ACV or high-volume motions where the economics do not survive; token gestures that will be recognized as such.

## G. De-risking the offer — Plain Vanilla / New Normal

### [FRAME:NOVCHUNK] Novelty Chunking
**Move:** List everything new, quirky or risky about your deal, find the single category they all belong to, and present that category as the one and only difference; the stated rationale is that buyers count risks rather than weighing their size, so many small novelties score worse than one large one.
**APPLY WHEN:** The offer has many unfamiliar attributes and the buyer is conservative; big-ticket, career-risk decisions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The chunk would hide material defects the buyer must know — in the source's own example the grouped items included weak-credit tenants, one-year leases, cash-only trade and no curb appeal, and the theme label suppressed disclosure; early adopters buying precisely for the novelty; regulated diligence where every item resurfaces anyway.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an observational claim illustrated by one deal and one reconstruction.

### [FRAME:VANILLASCRIPT] Plain Vanilla script structure
**Move:** Sequence: praise the incumbent sincerely and enumerate everything that stays identical, bundled into one "we are like everyone else and that is fine" statement; acknowledge they were not looking to replace anything; introduce exactly one novelty chunk as the whole memory hook; then show why that difference is now widespread. In deal form, list the boring compliance facts first and only then name the distinctive feature.
**APPLY WHEN:** Displacing a deeply entrenched default, in a mature category with a conservative buyer, where novelty would frighten rather than attract.
**AVOID WHEN:** The incumbent is failing and the buyer wants a break rather than continuity; the buyer is explicitly shopping for transformation; the single difference is not real; or your value genuinely is the aggregate of many differences and flattening them undersells it. Applied partially it fails — stacking several novelties overwhelms the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reconstructed scripts and one closed deal; the category-convergence claims are asserted, not sourced.

### [FRAME:NEWNORMAL] Prove the new normal with three comparables
**Move:** Open by naming the trend, then physically drop the files of three or four comparable transactions that share your novelty chunk, with their prices, before describing your own deal — the comparables carry the claim that the difference is normal now.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer's objection is "too different" and real precedent transactions exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your comparables are not comparable to a specialist who knows the market — a sophisticated buyer will name the difference; or no precedents exist at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting with a self-reported outcome.

### [FRAME:PREEMPTFLAW] Surface the worst flaw before the buyer finds it
**Move:** Volunteer the most obvious defect of your offer early and matter-of-factly, and acknowledge that failure is possible, rather than waiting for the buyer to raise it at the worst moment.
**APPLY WHEN:** The flaw is discoverable in diligence and the buyer is skeptical and searching for the catch.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used selectively as a decoy — disclosing a small flaw to buy credibility for hiding a larger one is the misuse mode this creates; contexts where disclosure carries legal weight and belongs with counsel rather than a script.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal plus a general assertion that hidden negatives stall the sale.

## H. Attention and tension management

### [FRAME:PING] Intrigue ping to re-open a closing attention window
**Move:** When the listener visibly disengages mid-pitch, drop one short concrete slightly incongruous fact that implies a bigger story, then continue without explaining it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Attention drops mid-presentation; the target starts multitasking or interrupting for pace.
**AVOID WHEN:** The disengagement is a real objection in disguise — a ping papers over a substantive doubt, which resurfaces later and harder.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted meeting with a banker who reversed position.

### [FRAME:NOVELTY-INJECT] Manufacture novelty to create desire
**Move:** Introduce something that pleasantly violates expectation — a short live demo, an unfamiliar framing of a known problem, a vivid metaphor for a complex mechanism, unusual physical objects — and deploy it when you sense attention drifting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Attention drops; the audience has heard similar pitches; a complex mechanism needs an on-ramp.
**AVOID WHEN:** Novelty tips into confusion or spectacle — the source warns the dose must stay moderate, since too much reads as anxiety-inducing; avoid gimmicks with technically skeptical buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's practice; the supporting chemistry rationale is demoted.

### [FRAME:INTRIGUE-STORY] Prepared first-person story, cut off before the ending
**Move:** Carry one rehearsed, true, short story meeting six criteria — brief and relevant, you at the centre, real risk and uncertainty, a ticking clock, an obstructing force, serious consequences — deploy it the moment an analytical drill-down starts, stop at the peak without resolving it, move straight on, and close the loop only after the pitch is done; keep it about what the character *did* under pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** An analyst-frame attack or technical questions arriving too early; a target sliding into a disengaged non-reactive state; when the buyer needs evidence of how you behave when a deal goes wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The story is untrue, embellished or not genuinely relevant — it functions as a character claim and collapses on contradiction; audiences who read the interruption as evasion of the number they asked for; regulated or diligence-heavy contexts where cliffhanger withholding reads as evasive; and on repeat with an audience that has heard it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three self-narrated stories generalised as personal experience.

### [FRAME:INTRIGUE-THIRDPARTY] Introduce a compelling character the buyer cannot meet yet
**Move:** Reference a partner or associate who is interesting and slightly eccentric, illustrate them with one story of decisive action, and make meeting them conditional on the deal progressing — the reward is access, not information.
**APPLY WHEN:** Heavily technical buyers; when you want to defer detail questions until the basic deal is liked.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person does not exist as described or would not endorse the framing; when the buyer reads gatekeeping as a status game.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recurring narrative in the author's pitches.

### [FRAME:PUSHPULL-3LEVEL] Push/pull tension at three intensities
**Move:** Alternate a push (distance, doubt about fit) with a pull (a reason it could be excellent), pausing between them. Low: name a real possibility you are not right for each other, pause, then say the combination would be strong if it worked. Medium: argue that deals turn on the character of the people, so a mismatch there would sink it — pause — then concede they obviously do value people. High: say the reactions you are reading suggest a poor fit, propose wrapping up, pause, and start packing, genuinely prepared to leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** The room is agreeable but flat, there are no questions, and the meeting is drifting toward a polite non-decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot afford to lose the meeting, or the buyer has no authority to stop you leaving — the high-intensity version only works if walking out is credible; repeated pushes with no pull read as manipulation and the buyer disengages permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own patterns plus a television scene used as illustration.

### [FRAME:PUSHPULL-OFFER] Push/pull immediately around the offer
**Move:** After the numbers, push — concede the plan is bold and say plainly that if they dislike bold plans you are probably not a fit and you are fine with that — then pull, describing vividly what the combination would look like if it did work; keep the push short and the pull rich.
**APPLY WHEN:** The room may be feeling cornered by your own selling; committees of dominant personalities.
**AVOID WHEN:** Fragile or risk-averse buyers who will simply take the exit you offered; early in a relationship before any desire exists; when your commercial position cannot absorb a lost deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted delivery; the autonomy-threat rationale is the author's own theory, not cited work.

### [FRAME:TAKEBACK] Reclaim the materials to create stakes
**Move:** When an analytical audience sits stone-faced with no questions, announce that since there are no questions you will take the materials back, and collect them physically — gently but persistently — converting indifference into something to lose.
**APPLY WHEN:** Analyst-heavy or evaluator-heavy rooms; total non-response; you have distributed something they are already reading.
**AVOID WHEN:** Materials were requested for a formal evaluation process; the audience genuinely cannot decide anything and the theatrics only irritate; long-standing accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one conference episode with self-reported follow-on revenue.

### [FRAME:SELF-TIMEBOX] End before the attention curve does
**Move:** Watch for the room cooling and stop the pitch yourself, with your own time reason, rather than waiting to be wrapped up; do not speed up to fit the remaining material in.
**APPLY WHEN:** Visible attention decay mid-presentation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Contracted training or demo slots you are paid to fill; when the unfinished part contains something the buyer explicitly asked for.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted; the sub-claim that speeding up reduces retention is offered without source.

### [FRAME:DISENGSCAN] Read disengagement as a cue to step back, not to push
**Move:** Treat watch-glancing, body turning away, folder closing and phone-checking as a signal to shorten and withdraw, not as a signal to add material or ask for approval.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-presentation with room energy dropping; long committee meetings late in the day.
**AVOID WHEN:** The disengagement is caused by an unanswered objection — withdrawing there just leaves the objection standing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a behavioural rule with no support beyond the author's reading of rooms.

## I. Prizing, qualification, neediness control

### [FRAME:NOWANT] Three-rule neediness protocol
**Move:** Before the meeting, set three operating rules — want nothing from this specific outcome, plan to demonstrate exactly one thing you are genuinely excellent at, and pre-decide the moment you will pull back instead of pressing — and run them as a private loop on the way in.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-stakes single-shot meetings; you are commercially dependent on this deal; your pipeline is thin and you can feel it.
**AVOID WHEN:** Long-cycle collaborative accounts where the buyer expects visible investment and enthusiasm, or as an excuse to under-prepare or skip follow-up; the protocol assumes you can afford to lose the deal, and run while desperate it produces brittle theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch after three failures; the rules are borrowed from a film's philosophy, not a study.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-INT] Prize frame — internal stance
**Move:** Hold three sentences internally before and during the meeting: I am the prize; you are trying to impress me; you are seeking my approval. The source claims the frame ultimately rests on conviction rather than wording.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any meeting where you catch yourself supplicating.
**AVOID WHEN:** The conviction is unfounded — this is precisely the mechanism by which the technique degenerates into arrogance with nothing behind it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as an internal discipline with no supporting case.

### [FRAME:QUALIFY-BACK-LATE] Half-joking qualification once you hold status
**Move:** Late in the meeting, after the idea is understood, ask a light half-joking qualifying question back, let the laugh happen, then accept their serious answer casually; repeat until it approaches awkwardness, then stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already hold some status, the buyer has subordinates present, and the meeting is late.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are behind on credibility; the buyer is genuinely senior to your entire company in the relationship; cultures that read teasing as disrespect. Documented misuse: sarcasm that lands as contempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one scripted exchange with a bank client.

### [FRAME:EARN-TASK] Make the buyer complete a real task to get access
**Move:** Put a small legitimate qualifying obligation in front of the buyer before access is granted — the cited external example is a limited-run car requiring a signed care commitment before purchase.
**APPLY WHEN:** Supply-constrained offering where you can justify the requirement on substantive grounds.
**AVOID WHEN:** The hoop is fake — buyers detect manufactured scarcity fast; high-volume or transactional motions where friction just kills conversion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one third-party example cited without verification.

### [FRAME:MONEY-COMMODITY] Reframe capital as the commodity
**Move:** Internally and in framing language, treat money as fungible and abundant and the deal as singular — capital needs a place to work and can do nothing until it buys what you have.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fundraising and investor conversations; any market with many funding sources.
**AVOID WHEN:** Tight capital markets or a single realistic buyer; product sales where the buyer's budget genuinely is the scarce side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted from the author's position inside capital markets.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-EXT] Prize frame — external script
**Move:** Late in the meeting, state that you are glad you found time to come, that you must wrap up, that deals like this are scarce and include you, and that you must choose which counterparties to accept; then ask them why you would enjoy working with them, what previous partners said, and how they behave when a deal goes sideways, referencing a partner you must justify them to.
**APPLY WHEN:** You genuinely have alternatives; the buyer has been passive or entitled; end of pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no alternatives and cannot walk — the frame is a bluff that unravels the moment you chase; buyers whose procurement rules force them to treat you as a vendor regardless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's script, illustrated by one story of the same flip performed on him.

### [FRAME:HARDBUY] Raise desire by making a want-product harder to buy
**Move:** For products bought out of desire rather than need, stop smoothing the path — no unprompted discounts, no free upgrades, no eagerness to ship tomorrow — and make the buyer work toward the purchase while you keep feeding the desire.
**APPLY WHEN:** The product is discretionary or status-driven and supply is genuinely limited.
**AVOID WHEN:** The product is a need-purchase or a commodity with substitutes a click away — friction there simply routes the buyer to a competitor; also fails as delivered, since the author's own team could not act on the instruction until it was translated into concrete scripts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product · market=transactional

### [FRAME:ONEPER] Forbid the action you want taken
**Move:** Cap or restrict access explicitly with a stated limit per buyer, so the restriction itself creates the pull.
**APPLY WHEN:** The offer is genuinely limited and the limit is verifiable.
**AVOID WHEN:** The scarcity is fabricated — the source itself lists "last one in stock" and "another buyer on the line" among the legacy pressure scripts it condemns, and fake limits trigger exactly the resistance they are meant to exploit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/info-product · market=transactional

### [FRAME:DISQUALIFY] Back away from buyers who reject expert status
**Move:** If the buyer will not accept your framing or evaluation criteria and insists on running their own process off public research, treat that as a signal to disengage rather than a challenge to win.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have deal flow and can afford to walk; buyer behaviour predicts high service cost and low margin.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot afford to lose the deal, or the buyer's independence is competence rather than obstinacy — this rule conveniently classifies well-informed price-comparing buyers as bad customers, which is a self-serving test.

## J. Objections and boundary-setting

### [FRAME:NONEEDY] Let the objection stand
**Move:** When the buyer raises a hard objection, do not counter it: acknowledge they are right that they do not need you, let them hold the negative thought, and keep only the invitation to look at what you have — physically stopping a teammate from arguing if necessary.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer has real alternatives and any pushback would become an ego contest.
**AVOID WHEN:** The objection is a factual error that will spread internally; procurement contexts where an unanswered objection becomes a scoring deduction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recruitment conversation.

### [FRAME:BUYERFORM] The Buyer's Formula as an invisible fence
**Move:** In place of asking for the sale, teach the buyer how to evaluate a deal in your category: establish independent-expert status by volume of repetitions; list the obvious ways buyers in their position fail; list the counterintuitive failures they think they already understand, teaching at least one real insight; list the obvious actions they half-know; give the non-obvious hacks only repetition teaches, with named choices and budgets; then hand over autonomy with a detaching line and stop talking. Five or six failure items before switching to positives is the stated dose.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer must feel the decision is theirs; you have genuine domain depth and can name specifics; the end of a competitive pitch where you would otherwise ask for questions; the buyer has no decision criteria of their own.
**AVOID WHEN:** You lack real expertise — invented failure modes get caught; buyers who know the domain better than you; before credibility is established, where the lesson reads as condescension; when the criteria are transparently reverse-engineered from your own strengths and a buyer with a real scorecard sees the shape. It is a boundary-setting device, so steering the buyer away from legitimate concerns — price, a second decision-maker — is where it turns deceptive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one fully worked retail script, one recruitment case and two deliveries in a single narrative; described as the author's standard method.

### [FRAME:BUYERASIDE] Narrate how you would evaluate the deal, as an aside
**Move:** While disengaging, describe casually how you personally decide whether to put money into this class of deal, including the part you would scrutinize hardest, framed as routine helpfulness rather than as an argument for buying.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer is analytical and resists being told what to conclude, and you have real repetitions in the category to speak from.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot survive a follow-up on the criteria you just named, or the buyer has already committed and this reopens scrutiny.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as a companion tool, illustrated once.

### [FRAME:REDIRECT] Redirect out-of-bounds questions
**Move:** When the buyer raises a topic you already labelled a rookie mistake, hear the objection out fully, then redirect: that worry is common and never turns out to matter, there are many things to worry about here but only about five that decide the outcome — focus on those.
**APPLY WHEN:** The boundary-setting step has already been delivered and accepted.
**AVOID WHEN:** The out-of-bounds topic is the buyer's actual decision criterion — the source openly uses this against price comparisons and absent co-deciders, which in a committee sale means suppressing the real evaluation; regulated procurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a scripted example with no outcome data.

### [FRAME:SLOWFENCE] Present constraints as the standing norm
**Move:** Introduce limits the way safety instructions are given on an aircraft — as how it works, not as a request open to debate — and introduce changes gradually rather than announcing a new rule; never state that a topic is off-limits, since naming it guarantees fixation.
**APPLY WHEN:** You need scope, sequence or agenda control without visible control.
**AVOID WHEN:** The constraint is not actually normal in the industry and can be checked; buyers who will resent it retroactively when they notice; anything material to their decision rights.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — supported in the source by a film plot and a loose appeal to attention research; the supporting claim is demoted.

### [FRAME:NO-TRIAL-CLOSE] Delete the temperature check
**Move:** Ban the trial-close family — are we in the ballpark, what do you think so far, is this something you would be interested in, any questions before we move forward. The *Pitch Anything* replacement is a light challenge delivered as a statement you do not seek an answer to; the *Flip the Script* version simply deletes the question and moves on. The stated reason: a submissive temperature check does not measure interest, it manufactures objections and signals that discounts exist.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid- or late-call, when the urge to check status appears; a scarce, differentiated offering; past deals that died right after such a question.
**AVOID WHEN:** You genuinely need to detect misalignment early or surface hidden blockers before investing in a proposal; committee sales where silent doubt kills deals later; undifferentiated offers where the implied scarcity is false; buyer organizations that require explicit consent to proceed — deleting the question wholesale can leave you pitching into silent disqualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — merged from both books of the same author (stated preference in *Pitch Anything*, reasoned from the persona model with one invented objection in *Flip the Script*); the agreement is one school, not confirmation.

### [FRAME:LOCALSTAKE] Repair a "what's in it for our side" objection by re-specifying use of funds locally
**Move:** When the buyer objects that the value leaves their territory or division, immediately restate the plan with concrete local commitments — a local office, named local hires, a partnership with a local institution, board meetings on their premises — framed as the best part of the plan rather than as a concession.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cross-border or cross-division investment where the objection is about benefit distribution rather than economics.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually deliver the local commitments — improvising them creates promises the deal must later honour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one improvised recovery after a teammate's misstep.

### [FRAME:HALFHALF] Split payment so both sides can damage each other
**Move:** Structure payment as half in advance and half on delivery so each party holds leverage over the other, and present it as the normal boring arrangement rather than as protection you demanded.
**APPLY WHEN:** Service or consulting engagements with future delivery, where buyer anxiety is about being taken advantage of rather than about price.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement mandates net terms or milestone schedules, or the deliverable is continuous rather than discrete.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the annual transaction volume attributed to this structure is given with no source.

## K. Endgame — stack, close, withdraw

### [FRAME:STACK-4] Run the four-frame stack when the room turns analytical
**Move:** In the final minutes, or the moment the buyer starts drilling into technical detail, deliver intrigue, then prize, then time, then moral authority in fast succession without pausing for evaluation between them.
**APPLY WHEN:** End of pitch; the buyer shifts to cold detail-hunting before any wanting exists; the audience drifts toward "we'll think about it".
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's process legitimately requires analysis now — formal diligence, technical validation. The author's own case shows the stack driving a decision on a transaction that turned out badly, so running it on someone whose interests you are responsible for is the documented misuse mode.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one transaction in which the author was the *target*, generalised afterwards.

### [FRAME:TIME-TRAIN] Time frame as a real, external constraint
**Move:** Acknowledge openly that nobody enjoys time pressure, compare a solid deal to a train that stops, boards and departs on schedule, affirm that they should not proceed unless they genuinely want it, then name a specific date framed as driven by the process or market rather than by you — and say explicitly that a no is acceptable.
**APPLY WHEN:** A genuine timeline exists — closing date, allocation, capacity, seasonality — and the prize frame has already landed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deadline is fabricated: the source itself warns that heavy-handed scarcity reads as a cheap retail tactic and damages professional standing; also where the buyer's committee cadence makes the date impossible.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one worked example plus the standing script.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-STANDARD] Set a standard of conduct both sides must meet
**Move:** Assert the standard the deal will be run by — clean documentation, no re-trading, reputational care — and ask the counterparty to commit to the same, shifting the evaluation from price and power to whether both sides meet the standard.
**APPLY WHEN:** Last frame in the stack; counterparties who compete on status or price; situations where you cannot outrank them on wealth or scale.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot meet the standard yourself, or it becomes a lever to shame a buyer into deciding — the misuse mode the source flirts with.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a trader's clean-paper claim plus one recounted account of a values frame overriding a status frame.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-STEWARD] Elevate the decision to stewardship of a third party
**Move:** Raise the decision to a values question competitors cannot outbid — stewardship of a community, a history, of people who cannot be in the room — and position yourself as the party that recognized it first.
**APPLY WHEN:** A genuine third-party stake exists and you intend to honour it, late in the pitch once desire is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** The moral claim is decorative — buyers punish it hard on discovery, and it is the most reputation-damaging move in this layer to fake; also where the buyer is legally constrained to score on other factors.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch, deployed alongside four other frames simultaneously, so attribution is impossible.

### [FRAME:NOASK] Close without asking, hand the next step to the buyer
**Move:** Never instruct the buyer what to conclude and never ask for the commitment: finish the case, stop talking, and if they signal interest, respond with an open question about the shape of the collaboration rather than proposing a next step yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer is a committee or senior principal who resents being sold to, and you have already delivered proof, threat and reward.
**AVOID WHEN:** Transactional or high-volume motions with short attention windows, where absence of an ask kills the deal; junior buyers who need an explicit path; any process with a required procurement step.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two recounted closes.

### [FRAME:ALLOWNO] Grant explicit permission to refuse, then require one of two answers
**Move:** Compress the ask into two named alternatives — tell me you do not want the deal and we part cleanly, or tell me you like it and we move — state that either is acceptable to you, then stop talking.
**APPLY WHEN:** Time is nearly out, the buyer has been stalling or controlling the frame, and you would otherwise get "send materials and we'll think about it".
**AVOID WHEN:** Early discovery; the buyer needs internal socialization before any answer; a real committee cannot answer in the room; and on repeat, since it reads as manipulation once recognized and is single-use per relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one software deal.

### [FRAME:HOLDSILENCE] Let the silence be uncomfortable for the buyer
**Move:** After the two-option ask, do nothing — no clarifying, no reframing, no new data — even for minutes, letting discomfort push the buyer to speak first and ask for something, which counts as the buyer chasing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after [FRAME:ALLOWNO] or any binary ask.
**AVOID WHEN:** Remote or phone calls where silence reads as a dropped connection; rooms where the counterpart is content to end the meeting in silence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the same single scene as [FRAME:ALLOWNO], three minutes of silence reported.

### [FRAME:POSTPITCH] Two-minute post-pitch discipline
**Move:** After your last line, say nothing that asks for reassurance: ban the three reflex questions — is this still a good deal, so what do you think, we can sign today if you want — sit still and let the silence run until they respond to you.
**APPLY WHEN:** The end of every formal presentation, and the pause after any price or terms statement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has asked a direct process question — silence there is theatre, not control; also unsuitable when a genuine next-step confirmation is the whole purpose of the call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own failed pitch where he asked exactly those questions and lost.

### [FRAME:QUALTRIAD] Closing triad that qualifies the buyer
**Move:** Close with three statements instead of a request: the round or slot fills within a stated short window; we do not actually need your money, we want a specific strategic benefit from you; and we are not yet sure you are the right partner, so tell us what you bring. Then stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** Genuine competitive demand exists, you can survive this buyer saying no, and the buyer is used to being courted.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no other options and the scarcity claim is fabricated — sophisticated buyers check; buyers who already suspect you are running a script; regulated or procurement-driven sales where posturing invalidates you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch with a self-reported valuation outcome and no comparison case.

### [FRAME:LOVEORLEAVE] "Only like it? Then throw us out"
**Move:** State that a merely positive reaction is worse than a negative one, that if they do not love the idea they should reject you now, and that you are genuinely fine with that, because you could not work together without shared conviction.
**APPLY WHEN:** As the final frame, after the emotional peak, when you have real alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The committee has not aligned internally and you would be handing a lukewarm member a ready-made veto; you need this deal; the buyer's culture reads ultimatums as arrogance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the recounted closing sequence of one won pitch.

### [FRAME:WITHDRAWCLOSE] Close by withdrawing
**Move:** End without asking for anything: invite them to come to you when they feel the time is right, then physically leave rather than lingering for reactions.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have generated genuine desire; competitive situations where every other bidder will end with an eager request.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing has been established yet — withdrawal without prior desire is just an absent close; also where a concrete next step is required by the buyer's process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch; the contrast case is the author's framing of the losing rival.

### [FRAME:EXITSIGNAL] Leave instead of asking for the yes
**Move:** When the buyer shows the first sign of pulling toward you, visibly prepare to depart — booking a ride, naming the next meeting — rather than escalating to a close, and let the buyer be the one to say they want to proceed.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer has just re-engaged after silence and you have another credible commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** The interest signal is weak or merely polite; the exit is transparently theatrical; and on repeat, since returning buyers recognize the manoeuvre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own account of one close.

### [FRAME:DEPARTBY] Anchor the decision to your departure, flatly
**Move:** When the buyer proposes to think it over and come back, state your departure date and that you need the answer by then — delivered as logistics, with no pressure language and no consequences attached.
**APPLY WHEN:** Travel-anchored meetings where the constraint is real.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deadline is invented — a fake constraint is the same artificial-urgency script the source condemns elsewhere; also where procurement runs a documented cycle.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one case with the answer reported the following morning.

### [FRAME:DEATHRATTLE] Read the three-beat kill sequence as a lost deal, not a warm one
**Move:** Treat the sequence — buyer praises the product, asks for more time with the numbers, then defers to an absent partner or committee — as one signal that certainty was never built and the deal is over; stop advancing and rebuild status and certainty instead of sending the requested proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** End of a pitch, where the buyer is agreeable but keeps adding intermediaries or documents.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement genuinely requires written proposals on a documented cycle, or a real committee sign-off exists and the absent partner is verifiable — this is the single most over-applied read in the layer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a recurring pattern the author reports from his own deals, with no counts.

## L. Skill-building outside live deals

### [FRAME:TRAPSPOT] Notice environmental traps first, step around them later
**Move:** First only notice: in daily life, identify arrangements designed to control your behaviour — being told to wait, seating, sequencing — and mentally rehearse a way around them; only then start stepping around them for real, with a partner, beginning with small ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** Training phase; low-risk environments; before any attempt at live frame collisions with buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes reflexive contrarianism — some waiting is just logistics, and treating every process step as a dominance test damages relationships and wastes goodwill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's self-taught path, described as many thousands of hours of trial and error with early deals lost.

### [FRAME:LINGUA] Label frames out loud with a practice partner
**Move:** Learn a fixed vocabulary — frame control, power-busting, frame collision, prizing, environmental trap, seizing status, local star power, push/pull — use it to name in real time what is coming at you, and debrief with a partner afterwards.
**APPLY WHEN:** Team selling; post-call reviews; building a shared playbook across a sales team.
**AVOID WHEN:** In front of the buyer — naming the game to the target destroys it; also note that the source vocabulary carries discredited explanatory terms, which must be dropped rather than taught.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as the fix for the author's own difficulty teaching the method.

### [FRAME:SAFEDRILL] Practise collisions on safe targets, soft touch, stop on defence
**Move:** Initiate frame collisions only with people who pose no career risk, always with visible good humour; if the other side goes defensive, treat that as the signal you came on too strong — pause, do not press, change partner or setting and restart.
**APPLY WHEN:** Skill-building, and any time you feel yourself forcing the technique.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never run this on your CEO, a live strategic account, or anyone with power over you while learning; the source explicitly warns that the humourless version gets you escorted out of the building.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author reports having had to reset many times himself.

### [FRAME:APPRENTICE] Apprentice to better frame operators
**Move:** Once basically competent, seek out people better than you, keep working in pairs or a small group, hold your active repertoire to a few frames, and start teaching others.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-basics; team enablement.
**AVOID WHEN:** No such practitioners are within reach — the only alternative on offer is self-study, which the author himself calls costly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — an analogy to martial arts and apprenticeship, no support.
