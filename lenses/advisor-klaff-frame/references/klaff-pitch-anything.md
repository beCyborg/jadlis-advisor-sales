# Pitch Anything — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens takes one thing from this book: a working theory of **who is evaluating whom**, and a repertoire of behavioural moves that shift that answer inside a single meeting. Where other sources optimise the message (structure, proof, objection handling), this one treats the message as secondary to the social position it is delivered from — the claim is that the outcome of a pitch is largely set before the content starts, by whoever is reacting to whom.

Its unique contributions to the council: (1) a named, teachable set of *frame collisions* (power, time, analyst) with concrete counters; (2) *prizing* — inverting the qualification direction so the buyer works to earn access; (3) an explicit protocol against neediness, including the two minutes after you stop talking; (4) a time-budgeted pitch skeleton (~20 min) with attention treated as a decaying resource.

Everything the book offers as *explanation* is demoted (see `## Neuromyth demotions`) and must never be transmitted. Evidence ceiling for every move below is **practitioner-anecdote**: one operator's capital-raising deals, self-reported outcomes, no counter-cases and no base rate.

## Core model

- **Frames collide, they do not blend.** One frame absorbs the other; the operational test is directional — whoever is reacting has lost. Diagnostic the book supplies: if you have to explain your authority, you do not have it.
- **Three opposing frames** recur from the buyer side: *power* (rank/ritual), *time* ("I only have 10 minutes"), *analyst* (drill-down into figures). **Four counters:** power-busting, time-constraining, intrigue, and the universal *prize* frame.
- **Status is situational.** Global status (wealth, title) is fixed and unfakeable; status inside a narrow domain is fluid and manufacturable — "local star power". It evaporates on leaving the room and must be rebuilt.
- **Beta traps** are the host's environmental rituals (badge, guest book, waiting lobby, small room, escorted walk) that install and hold you in the subordinate role. Bentonville-style vendor processing is the industrialised extreme; the book's claim is that no technique survives a frame that tightly controlled.
- **STRONG** — the six-step pitch sequence: **S**et the frame, **T**ell the story, **R**eveal the intrigue, **O**ffer the prize, **N**ail the hookpoint, **G**et a decision. The *hookpoint* is the moment the listener starts pulling information out of you.
- **Four pitch phases / ~20 minutes:** self + big idea (5), budget + secret sauce (10), the offer (2), frame stack (3).
- **Frame stacking / hot cognition (Ch. 5):** in the last minutes, run intrigue → prize → time → moral authority in fast succession to produce *wanting* before analysis. The book's counterpart term, borrowed from Bruner, is *paradigmatic mode* — cold evaluation where forward-looking elements get discarded.
- **Neediness (Ch. 6)** is framed as a situational reflex, not a trait; the cure is behavioural (pre-loaded exit, one excellence demonstration, planned withdrawal).
- **Ch. 7** is the single extended case (a $1B airport redevelopment mandate) where the whole apparatus is shown end-to-end; **Ch. 8** is the apprenticeship ladder.
- Replaces "Always Be Closing" with **"Always Be Leaving"**.

## Moves

### A. Before the room — preparation and positioning

### [FRAME:PRESELECT] Pick the counter-frame before the meeting
**Move:** Classify the counterpart in advance (dominant/ego-driven, numbers-driven, or stacked-against-you) and pre-load one counter — power-busting, intrigue, or time+prize — instead of improvising; stay ready to switch mid-meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** You know who is in the room and have any prior read on their behaviour.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cold first calls with no read — a mis-picked aggressive frame burns the meeting in the first minute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as the author's standing pre-meeting habit.

### [FRAME:NORAPPORT] Skip deep-rapport research, invest the time in one theme
**Move:** Do not build a dossier on the buyer's personal life or plan to open with small talk. Spend that preparation time constructing a single narrative theme tuned to what the decision would mean for them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Senior decision-makers who sit through many pitches; short slots; you are the underdog on credentials.
**AVOID WHEN:** Relationship-led or referral-driven markets, long consultative cycles, and cultures where rapport is the entry ticket — the book's own competitor allegedly researched heavily and still nearly won.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting/service · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — "my research showed" small talk is fruitless, no named source.

### [FRAME:LEGACY] Reframe the decision from money to status and legacy
**Move:** Identify what the decision-maker wants to be known for, name it explicitly as the real subject of the deal, and make your plan the vehicle for it. Money becomes a supporting detail rather than the headline.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer has personal reputational stake, long history with the asset, or an available legacy narrative (founder, civic project, turnaround).
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement or committee scoring on hard criteria; buyers whose incentive is literally the number; when you cannot deliver the status outcome you invoke — that is the point where positioning becomes manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "American Legacy" theme in the winning airport pitch.

### [FRAME:CUTHOT] Rehearse and cut to the intrigue floor
**Move:** Rehearse the full pitch, then repeatedly delete any passage carrying neither intrigue nor emotional charge — minutes at a time, over weeks — until the core survives in about twenty minutes.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any high-stakes set-piece presentation with real preparation time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Technical evaluations where the omitted detail is the deciding criterion; the buyer explicitly asked for depth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a 55-minute rehearsal cut to a delivered 20:52.

### [FRAME:TONECAL] Calibrate tone to the buyer's working environment
**Move:** Infer the buyer's day-to-day register (in the case: career federal aviation regulators) and set the pitch tone to match — serious and respectful, not somber — while genuinely enjoying the delivery, since visible strain in the presenter spreads to the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** Regulated, institutional or bureaucratically shaped buyers; unfamiliar cultures.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot actually generate the enjoyment — the book concedes it cannot be faked, and forced levity in a formal room is worse than flatness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning about one buyer's background, no outcome attribution.

### [FRAME:HOTVISUAL] Physical boards of category imagery instead of slides
**Move:** Build large, thick physical display boards showing the object the buyer already loves, and reveal one every few minutes instead of running a deck. The boards stay in the room after the reveal.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer's category has strong visual appeal; small rooms; you want persistence and tactility slides do not give.
**AVOID WHEN:** Abstract or financial products with no visual hook (the book admits this is much harder there); remote or virtual pitches; buyers who read spectacle as a substitute for substance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch, one buyer group of aviation people.

### [FRAME:VENUE-VETO] Refuse venues that cannot be framed
**Move:** Decline public and semi-public spaces for a real pitch — cafés, cafeterias, convention floors, exhibitor booths. Around a conference, move it to a rented hospitality suite, hotel meeting room, or borrowed private office.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer proposes "let's grab a coffee"; conference or trade-show context; any space where third parties can interrupt.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early low-stakes discovery where a casual venue lowers resistance; when refusing the venue costs you the only access you have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — accounts of interruptions destroying pitches in public spaces.

### B. Entry and status — the first minutes

### [FRAME:NO-RITUAL] Decline the host's deference rituals
**Move:** Recognise the arrival sequence (badge, guest book, "take a seat", stale coffee, hand-off to an aide, an agenda you were handed) and treat compliance as optional theatre: skip the thanks for being squeezed in, stay unimpressed by displays of wealth and rank, do not perform gratitude for the meeting, and do not wait where you were placed.
**APPLY WHEN:** On their turf, outnumbered, first meeting, or being processed as one more vendor; host visibly runs a status apparatus (lavish lobby, deliberate lateness, low chair).
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement, compliance or security processes where the ritual is a legal requirement; cultures and committees where reciprocity ritual is the actual gate; when the person running the ritual is your champion; with an existing customer in expansion, where coldness reads as arrogance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — lobby and hedge-fund-office stories; no measured comparison, no counter-cases.

### [FRAME:MOMENTUM-EARLY] Force the frame collision early, and arrive on time
**Move:** Create status in the first minutes rather than waiting — each minute of deference reinforces their position. Be punctual whenever you expect to open from the weaker position; lateness forfeits standing you then have to buy back.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any meeting you expect to open in the low position.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never contraindicated on punctuality; the "force a collision" half is contraindicated with committees that punish visible friction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule without supporting cases.

### [FRAME:LOCAL-STAR] Create local star power inside a domain you own
**Move:** As soon as you have attention, narrow the discussion to the slice where your knowledge is unassailable (deal mechanics, technical specifics you own) and hold it there, demonstrating depth visibly rather than asserting general credentials — whoever owns the domain gives the instructions inside it. Pair it with a mildly surprising but friendly, humorous act to capture attention.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are outranked on wealth or title but have real depth on a narrow relevant dimension; the audience can out-argue you on general business but not on your specialty.
**AVOID WHEN:** The depth is faked — it inverts on the first specific question; the buyer's own expert is in the room and better than you; humourless delivery, which the book warns produces unpredictable reactions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — golf-pro and French-waiter analogies plus the author's own meetings.

### [FRAME:DEFY-SMALL] Small, friendly, well-timed defiance
**Move:** Perform one mildly shocking but non-hostile act that breaks the host's script at the moment it presents itself, then immediately convert the pause into your framing point. The book's illustration: the host bites an apple mid-meeting; the author fetches a knife, halves the rest, takes a share, and ties it to how his deals split value.
**APPLY WHEN:** The status gap is too wide for verbal frame control alone; the host is performing dominance (reviewing documents while you talk, seating you below him, summoning subordinates).
**AVOID WHEN:** The act would read as aggression, contempt or a cultural insult; long-standing relationships where the shock has no upside; audiences containing people who must defend the host afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted hedge-fund meeting, outcome self-reported; the author calls it risky himself.

### [FRAME:DENIAL] Small denial — tease, then withhold
**Move:** Put a visibly interesting artifact within reach (a labelled folder, a visual, a page of the deck), let them reach for it, then withdraw it lightly — "not yet, you'll get this when I say". Keep it playful, never sharp.
**APPLY WHEN:** Opening minutes of a meeting you must control; a target who habitually runs the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is already in evaluation mode and asked for the material formally; committees where one offended stakeholder can veto; anything sent in writing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as a repeatable opener, one illustrating scenario.

### [FRAME:STATUS-SEQ-6] The six-step status sequence
**Move:** Run in order: (1) ignore power rituals and environmental traps; (2) stay unaffected by the buyer's global status; (3) take small denials or defiances that strengthen your frame; (4) move immediately into your expert domain; (5) apply a prize frame — you are the reward for choosing to do business; (6) get the buyer to say something that confirms your higher standing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pitching on someone else's territory; any first meeting where status is undefined.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship is already collaborative and status settled — running the sequence then reads as manufactured conflict.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — post-hoc reconstruction of the author's own meetings.

### [FRAME:STATUS-WAITER-SEQ] Defiance → isolate → rescue → ally, in a group
**Move:** A small defiant correction of the nominal high-status person; a controlled delay that leaves him waiting; a manufactured minor mistake you then rescue him from while publicly crediting him; then lavish recognition on one other person present, so that attacking you means attacking them too.
**APPLY WHEN:** Group settings where one person is trying to run the room and other participants' regard matters.
**AVOID WHEN:** Anything touching your actual buyer's dignity in front of their team — humiliating a stakeholder is unrecoverable; committees where the humiliated party holds a veto. The book presents this as observed behaviour, not a verbatim recommendation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one observed dinner, reconstructed by the person it was done to; note the story's own moral — the operator chose moderation at the end rather than extracting the maximum.

### [FRAME:AVOID-BETA-TRAP] Refuse the recognisable sales repertoire
**Move:** Drop the visible technique set — rapport-building preamble, feature/benefit recitation, scripted objection handling, trial closes — on the grounds that experienced buyers recognise each one and use the recognition to hold you subordinate.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sophisticated buyers who have been sold to for years.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have nothing to replace them with; transactional or inbound motions where the standard sequence measurably converts; junior reps without the standing to carry a status play.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — a broad claim about 40+ years of sales training, with no data on which techniques still convert.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-OPEN] Set the prize frame in the first sentence
**Move:** Open with a low-risk line that quietly signals scarcity and a hard stop — glad I could fit this in, there's another meeting right after, let's start.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any first meeting where you arrived on their turf; the safest entry point of the whole prize system.
**AVOID WHEN:** It is untrue and checkable; existing customers in expansion conversations.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — offered explicitly as the beginner-safe version of prizing.

### [FRAME:TIME-CONSTRAINT-OPEN] Announce a short, self-imposed limit
**Move:** Open by telling the room you have roughly 20 minutes for the big idea, leaving time to discuss before you must leave — walk in already holding a plausible reason you are needed elsewhere and state it once, casually. It signals professionalism, caps the meeting on your terms, and pre-empts the "we'll get back to you" drift by making your time visibly finite before the pitch starts.
**APPLY WHEN:** First substantive pitch meeting; any audience that does not know how long they are committed for; any meeting you suspect will run over or end limp.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has explicitly cleared a long working session or an on-site day — a false constraint there reads as disrespect and is easily called out; technical deep-dives where cutting short reads as evasion. Misuse mode: announcing 20 minutes and running an hour, which destroys the credibility the pattern buys.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's standard opener and default habit; no outcome data.

### [FRAME:GATEKEEPER-BYPASS] Refuse the gatekeeper script
**Move:** Decline the "let me see if he's in" ritual and state your requirement flatly instead of asking permission to be admitted.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are owed something, expected, or holding real leverage.
**AVOID WHEN:** Ordinary prospecting — the book's example escalates to walking past reception during what turned out to be a fraud investigation; as a sales tactic this is a fast path to being ejected and blacklisted. Treat the extreme version as biography, not instruction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single extraordinary episode with police already on standby.

### [FRAME:MRBIG-15] Decision-maker absent — grant a deadline, then leave
**Move:** If the principal skips the meeting, refuse to present to proxies: grant a short fixed wait (he uses 15 minutes), say plainly you must leave after it, and if he does not appear, leave without presenting, without leaving materials, and without apologising.
**APPLY WHEN:** Single-shot pitch that only works told first-hand; you have real alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The absent person is one of several stakeholders and those present are genuine influencers; long enterprise cycles where the champion in the room is the actual path in; when you have no other pipeline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a standing script, outcome reported qualitatively.

### [FRAME:HOMETURF] Reschedule only on your ground
**Move:** If you still want the account after a no-show, offer to reschedule while acknowledging that misses happen — on the condition that the next meeting is at your location.
**APPLY WHEN:** After the other side has created a documented social debt.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are the challenger with no leverage; buyer-side procurement rules; when travel is genuinely one-directional in their favour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted from practice.

### [FRAME:QUALIFY-BACK-EARLY] Make the buyer explain himself, early
**Move:** Early in the meeting, ask the buyer to explain himself to you and state that you are selective about who you work with; let the implied question — why would I want this business — sit unanswered.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in a meeting on their turf; the buyer is treating you as an interchangeable vendor.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are demonstrably the smaller party with no track record — unearned selectivity reads as bluff; commodity or transactional purchases where the buyer holds real alternatives.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as routine practice, no outcome data.

### C. Frame collisions in the room

### [FRAME:PLOW] Plowing — hold the frame through social pressure
**Move:** At the moment of collision (felt as anxiety), commit fully and keep moving forward without self-correction, apology, or renegotiation of your own position; expect the discomfort and do not read it as a signal to soften.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have made a frame move and the other side pushes back once.
**AVOID WHEN:** The pushback contains new information; three or more rounds of resistance — the book supplies no stop rule, and this is the single most damaging move to misapply.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — self-narrated; the author notes the counterparty's rational move would have been to refuse.

### [FRAME:DEFY-TIME] Match and raise the time squeeze
**Move:** When told you have a short slot, answer with a shorter one of your own, delivered smiling but seriously ("that works, I only have 12"); let it escalate into a light game if they play along.
**APPLY WHEN:** The target opens with a time restriction as a status move.
**AVOID WHEN:** The constraint is genuine and logistical; long-cycle enterprise deals where you actually need the airtime; when you cannot afford to be held to the number you just named.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — meetings reportedly compressed to a couple of minutes this way.

### [FRAME:TIME-BREAK] Break the "I only have 10 minutes" opener
**Move:** Instead of thanking them for the slot, decline the terms and qualify back: say you don't work that way, that rescheduling is pointless without mutual trust, and ask whether they keep appointments and hold a schedule.
**APPLY WHEN:** On-site visit opened with a status-signalling time limit.
**AVOID WHEN:** The constraint is real (their calendar visibly collapsed); early relationship with an enterprise buyer whose calendar is not theirs to control.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a script with an idealised reported response.

### [FRAME:EXPERTBUST] Dissolve a manufactured constraint with a checkable fact
**Move:** When pressure rests on a factual claim ("my car is waiting", "the plane leaves in an hour"), answer with a precise, verifiable number that dissolves the constraint, then immediately set a small demand of your own (you're buying lunch).
**APPLY WHEN:** The other side's pressure rests on a fact you actually know better than they do.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are guessing — being wrong hands them the frame permanently; also avoid when the constraint is real and public.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted exchange between the author and his own partner.

### [FRAME:TRIVIAL-NEG] Move the dominance contest onto something worthless
**Move:** When the counterpart is openly ignoring you, physically reclaim the object of his distraction and open a mock-serious negotiation over something trivial (in the recounted case, buying the doodle he was drawing on the pitch book), then return to the real agenda.
**APPLY WHEN:** Visible contempt or inattention from a dominant principal and the meeting is otherwise lost.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any relationship you need to survive the meeting badly; committee settings; when the counterpart is a customer rather than a peer investor — it reads as insubordination.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting, one favourable outcome, reported by the person telling the story.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-RIGHT] Hold a position of rightness against a rationaliser
**Move:** When the other side stalls behind detailed rational explanation, refuse to engage the detail at all: state what is owed, state that explanation is irrelevant, repeat.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are genuinely and verifiably in the right (contractual, legal, factual) and the other side is spinning.
**AVOID WHEN:** The facts are contested or you are the one asking a favour; ordinary commercial disagreements — moral framing on a normal negotiation is a relationship-ender. The illustrating case is debt recovery from an actual fraud, not a sale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Southwest Exchange recovery, a single high-drama episode.

### [FRAME:CADENCE] Convert a won frame into rules plus a deliverable clock
**Move:** Once you hold control, state the operating rules explicitly and attach a short recurring interval on which something concrete must be delivered to you (in the story: a wire confirmation every 15 minutes; the meeting does not end until the full amount clears).
**APPLY WHEN:** Recovery, escalation, or a stalled commitment where the counterparty is stalling for time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Normal sales cycles — imposing a deliverable clock on a prospect who owes you nothing collapses the deal; committees with their own governance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one six-hour session recounted.

### [FRAME:HIGHLEVEL-REDIRECT] Answer detail at summary level, then pivot to fit
**Move:** When a drill-down starts, answer directly with one line of top-level figures, note they are verifiable later, and immediately return to the relationship question — are we a fit, should we work together. Same pattern for premature price questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** An analytical audience starts calculating mid-pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** Formal diligence, procurement, or any stage where refusing detail reads as concealment; technical buyers who evaluate precisely by the detail you are deflecting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a standing script for financial deals.

### [FRAME:DETAIL-GATE] Keep deep technical material out of the room
**Move:** Physically separate deep technical and financial material from the presentation and release it after the meeting, so the audience cannot start computing during the pitch.
**APPLY WHEN:** You control the materials and the meeting is a decision-to-engage, not an evaluation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Buyer-driven evaluation, security or technical review, or when disclosure is contractually or ethically required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated method, forward-referenced within the book.

### [FRAME:THREAD-PRUNE] Drop threads that do not advance the deal
**Move:** Once you hold the frame, silently abandon conversational branches that do not serve the deal and amplify the ones that do — no acknowledgment, no polite follow-up on digressions.
**APPLY WHEN:** The meeting drifts into social chatter, war stories or side topics after you have gained attention.
**AVOID WHEN:** The digression is the buyer surfacing a real objection or a political constraint in coded form.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated as the author's own rule.

### [FRAME:EASYONBUYER] Go easy once a buyer's power frame breaks
**Move:** Expect most buyers to open with a power frame and expect it to break easily under prizing, intrigue and time frames. Once it breaks, deliberately do not press the advantage — most buyers have never met resistance and will be off balance.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers accustomed to vendors complying with every logistical whim.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have mistaken a legitimate constraint for a power play; escalating against a buyer with internal political exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a general observation about buyer behaviour.

### D. Pitch construction and delivery

### [FRAME:STRONG-SEQ] Run the pitch as a fixed six-step sequence
**Move:** Structure any pitch as: set the frame → tell the story → reveal the intrigue → offer the prize → nail the hookpoint → get a decision; treat each step as a discrete failure point and diagnose which one broke rather than blaming the deal terms.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-stakes single-shot meeting with a principal; you control the agenda and have 15–25 minutes.
**AVOID WHEN:** Multi-call consultative cycles where discovery precedes any pitch; technical evaluations where the decision is a scorecard, not a moment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own capital raises; no external validation offered.

### [FRAME:MSG-SIMPLIFY] Design the message for hostile triage by a busy evaluator
**Move:** Strip abstractions; lead with concrete, verifiable, visual chunks; offer two clearly contrasted options rather than a nuanced spectrum; make the idea legibly novel in the first minute; assume fine distinctions will not survive.
**APPLY WHEN:** First exposure to a busy decision-maker with no prior context on you.
**AVOID WHEN:** Technical due diligence, RFP responses, or any stage where the buyer is explicitly comparing specifications — over-simplifying there reads as evasion. Note the book's own later correction: statistics simplify hard, people-and-relationship detail can stay rich.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — the delivery advice is stated without study support and was justified in the book by an explanatory layer that is demoted here.

### [FRAME:TRACKRECORD-AVG] One great credential, not a résumé
**Move:** Answer "what's your background" with wins only — things you built, projects that worked — in under two minutes, and stop after the strongest item instead of appending merely good ones. Structure: degree, one quantified home run, what you left to do this.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in any pitch, right after the framing exchange.
**AVOID WHEN:** Due-diligence or vendor-qualification settings where a complete history is required; when the buyer is explicitly assessing team depth.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a colleague's Boeing funding pitch; the supporting "impressions are averaged, not summed" claim is uncited in the book.

### [FRAME:WHYNOW-3F] Three-market-forces "Why now?"
**Move:** Before describing the idea, place it against three converging forces — economic (costs, credit, rates, price points), social (behaviour or awareness), technological (what became possible or cheap) — each with a concrete consequence; then narrate the idea's origin (what changed, effect on cost and demand) and conclude that a short window is open that your plan is timed to. Only then present the plan.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any new offer or category; deals that could be suspected of having been shopped around; buyers who believe nothing has changed; timing is a genuine differentiator.
**AVOID WHEN:** The forces are fabricated, generic or unverifiable — a hollow trend story invites a market-thesis argument you cannot win, and a well-informed buyer collapses the whole opening; also weak with buyers who already know the market better than you or are already urgent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the UpRight wearable and airport-deal illustrations, plus one delivered pitch citing a claimed $5B quarter-over-quarter increase in available investor money.

### [FRAME:MOVE-NOT-STATES] Show motion, not before-and-after
**Move:** Never present a static "old way vs new way" contrast; narrate the market moving from the old state to the new one with the forces doing the pushing, so your success reads as a consequence of momentum rather than a claim.
**APPLY WHEN:** Explaining why the idea is timely or inevitable; whenever a slide would otherwise be a two-column comparison.
**AVOID WHEN:** The movement is not real — sophisticated buyers test the trend claim directly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — supported in the book only by an analogy that is demoted (see Neuromyth demotions).

### [FRAME:IDEA-INTRO] One-minute idea introduction skeleton (attributed to Geoff Moore, 1999)
**Move:** Deliver the big idea in about a minute on a fixed skeleton: for [target customers] / who are dissatisfied with [current offerings] / my product is a [new category] / that provides [key benefit] / unlike [competing product] / mine [key differentiator]. Then stop — details come later.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the "Why now?" frame; also as a reset when the room has lost the thread.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has already bought the category and wants implementation specifics; repeating the template verbatim to someone who has heard it reads as canned.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three worked examples; the attribution to Moore is given without a source.

### [FRAME:BIGIDEAINTRO] Introduce the big idea with a social reward attached
**Move:** Present the idea as different in kind from the me-too options, then attach three pulls at once: a social reward, the chance to be the hero of the story, and the money. Reveal the theme physically as you name it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Crowded competitive fields; buyers with reputational upside available.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's mandate is purely financial return with no discretionary narrative; you cannot substantiate the differentiation immediately afterwards.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one delivered pitch; the supporting "three truths" are demoted claims.

### [FRAME:BESTIDEA] Open by defining the criterion that favours you
**Move:** Start by telling the room what the decision should actually be judged on — the quality of the idea, not size, track record or polish — and attach gravity to the choice so a wrong pick has consequences.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the smaller vendor; the competitor's opening is predictably a credentials parade; the buyer has discretion over criteria.
**AVOID WHEN:** Scoring is pre-published and weighted (formal RFP); the buyer's actual risk driver is vendor size — you would be arguing against their fiduciary logic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one competitive pitch, won.

### [FRAME:PRAISEBUST] Demote rivals by praising the right attributes
**Move:** Compliment the competition warmly for exactly the traits you want held against them — huge teams, armies of junior researchers, no expense spared — so the praise does the demotion for you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitors are in the room or known; their scale is genuinely a cost or agility liability for this buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their scale is the buyer's actual risk mitigation; the buyer has an existing relationship with them; you cannot deliver the agility you implicitly promise. Transparent versions read as sniping and cost you the moral high ground.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one delivered pitch; the author reads the competitor's scowl as confirmation.

### [FRAME:BUDGET-SKILL] Pitch budgeting competence, not revenue forecasts
**Move:** Present the budget first and use it to display disciplined cost control and cash resilience through bad quarters, while keeping revenue-projection talk minimal — treat forecasting as the easy part anyone can do. Assume the buyer already expects you to call your projections conservative.
**APPLY WHEN:** Investor pitches; any deal where financial credibility is under scrutiny; early-stage companies whose forecasts will be discounted anyway.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's mandate is growth-rate driven and forecasts are the actual decision variable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — supported by a quotation from Gordon Bell's *High Tech Ventures* on absurdly aggressive startup plans.

### [FRAME:COMPETE-2Q] Answer the two competition questions explicitly
**Move:** Pre-empt the competition question with two specifics: how hard it is for a new entrant to join the game, and how hard it is for a customer to swap you out. Admit competition exists rather than claiming none.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after the budget; whenever the buyer's next thought will obviously be "who else does this".
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no honest answer on switching cost — you would be inviting the question you cannot answer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as the right framing, without cases.

### [FRAME:SECRET-SAUCE] Name the unfair advantage, capped at ten minutes
**Move:** Describe the one durable advantage that keeps you from being a flash in the market, and cap the entire mechanism explanation at ten minutes so five remain for the offer and the frame stack.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-pitch, after budget and competition.
**AVOID WHEN:** The advantage is generic ("great team", "better UX") — an unsupported claim here invites exactly the analytical mode the method is trying to avoid.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a requirement of the format.

### [FRAME:OFFER-CONCRETE] State delivery in two minutes
**Move:** Say precisely what the buyer receives, when, and how it is delivered, plus their own roles and responsibilities in fulfilment — summarised facts, no deep detail — so their mental picture is complete. Present yourself as part of what is delivered.
**APPLY WHEN:** After the mechanism is explained; before frame stacking.
**AVOID WHEN:** Commercial terms are genuinely unsettled — an invented delivery picture creates later conflict.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's fixed pitch structure.

### [FRAME:WITNESS] Cast a real outsider as a live witness
**Move:** During research, find a real person affected by the decision, prepare nothing beyond asking them to speak from their own experience, and bring them into the room mid-pitch to say it in their own words. Do not rush the moment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deals with a visible human or community consequence, where you have actually done field research and the story is true.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person is coached, paid, or misrepresented — that converts a legitimate proof point into staged emotional pressure; committees whose mandate excludes such considerations; when the tangent has no payoff (the book concedes the digression is risky).
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single staged appearance by a local mechanic in the airport pitch.

### [FRAME:GIVEAWAY] Unconditional deliverable as status redistribution
**Move:** Complete a real piece of work at your own cost, hand it to everyone in the room during the meeting, and state that you want it used regardless of who wins the business.
**APPLY WHEN:** You can afford the work, it is genuinely valuable, and you want to convert seized status into credibility by handing some back.
**AVOID WHEN:** The giveaway is the entirety of your value — you have just given away the deal; low-ACV or high-volume motions where the economics do not survive; token gestures that will be recognised as such.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one instance in the airport pitch.

### E. Attention and tension management

### [FRAME:PING] Intrigue ping to re-open a closing attention window
**Move:** When the listener visibly disengages mid-pitch, drop one short, concrete, slightly incongruous fact that implies a bigger story (e.g. naming an unexpected existing investor), then continue without explaining it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Attention drops mid-presentation; the target starts multitasking or interrupting for pace.
**AVOID WHEN:** The disengagement is a real objection in disguise — a ping papers over a substantive doubt, which resurfaces later and harder.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted meeting with a banker who reversed position.

### [FRAME:NOVELTY-INJECT] Manufacture novelty to create desire
**Move:** Introduce something that pleasantly violates expectation — a short live demo, an unfamiliar framing of a known problem, a vivid metaphor for a complex mechanism, unusual physical objects — and deploy it when you sense attention drifting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Attention drops; the audience has heard similar pitches; a complex mechanism needs an on-ramp.
**AVOID WHEN:** Novelty tips into confusion or spectacle — the book warns the dose must stay moderate, since too much reads as anxiety-inducing; avoid gimmicks with technically sceptical buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's practice; the supporting chemistry rationale is demoted.

### [FRAME:INTRIGUE-STORY] Prepared first-person story, cut off before the ending
**Move:** Carry one rehearsed, true, short story meeting six criteria — brief and relevant, you at the centre, real risk and uncertainty, a ticking clock, an obstructing force, serious consequences — built as a person in a difficult situation with real obstacles attacking and the question of escape left open. Deploy it the moment an analytical drill-down starts, stop at the peak without resolving it, move straight on, and close the loop only after the pitch is done. Keep it about what the character *did* under pressure; the extremity belongs in the emotional stakes, not in the events.
**APPLY WHEN:** Analyst-frame attack or technical questions arriving too early; a target sliding into a disengaged, non-reactive state; when the buyer needs evidence of how you behave when a deal goes wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The story is untrue, embellished, or not genuinely relevant — it functions as a character claim and collapses on contradiction; audiences who read the interruption as evasion of the number they asked for; regulated or diligence-heavy contexts where cliffhanger withholding reads as evasive; repeat audiences who have heard it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's aviation story, the missing-investor story and the Joshua wire-transfer story, all self-narrated and generalised as "in my experience".

### [FRAME:INTRIGUE-THIRDPARTY] Introduce a compelling character the buyer cannot meet yet
**Move:** Reference a partner or associate who is interesting and slightly eccentric, illustrate them with one story of decisive action, and make meeting them conditional on the deal progressing — the reward is access, not information.
**APPLY WHEN:** Heavily technical buyers; you want to defer detail questions until the basic deal is liked.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person does not exist as described or would not endorse the framing; when the buyer reads gatekeeping as a status game.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Joshua narrative as delivered in the author's pitches.

### [FRAME:PUSHPULL-3LEVEL] Push/pull tension at three intensities
**Move:** Alternate a push (distance, doubt about fit) with a pull (a reason it could be excellent), pausing between them. Low: say there is a real possibility you two are not right for each other, pause, then say that if it worked the combination would be strong. Medium: argue that deals turn on the character of the people rather than the idea, so a mismatch there would sink it — pause — then concede they obviously do value people. High: say the reactions you are reading suggest a poor fit, propose wrapping up and meeting on the next one, pause, and start packing up, genuinely prepared to leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** The room is agreeable but flat; no questions; the meeting is drifting toward a polite non-decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot afford to lose the meeting, or the buyer has no authority to stop you leaving — the high-intensity version only works if walking out is credible and the audience decides; repeated pushes with no pull read as manipulation and the buyer disengages permanently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own patterns plus a *Mad Men* scene used as illustration.

### [FRAME:PUSHPULL-OFFER] Push/pull immediately around the offer
**Move:** After the numbers, push: concede the plan is bold and say plainly that if they dislike bold plans you two are probably not a fit and you are fine with that. Then pull: describe vividly what the combination would look like if it did work. Keep the push short and the pull rich.
**APPLY WHEN:** The room may be feeling cornered by your own selling; committees of dominant personalities.
**AVOID WHEN:** Fragile or risk-averse buyers who will simply take the exit you offered; early in a relationship before any desire exists; when your commercial position cannot absorb a lost deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted delivery; the autonomy-threat rationale is the author's own theory, not cited work.

### [FRAME:TAKEBACK] Reclaim the materials to create stakes
**Move:** When an analytical audience sits stone-faced with no questions, announce that since there are no questions you will take the materials back, and collect them physically — gently but persistently. The removal converts indifference into something to lose and typically starts the questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Analyst-heavy or evaluator-heavy rooms; total non-response; you have distributed something they are already reading.
**AVOID WHEN:** Materials were requested for a formal evaluation process; the audience genuinely cannot decide anything and the theatrics only irritate; long-standing accounts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one conference episode; follow-on revenue self-reported.

### [FRAME:SELF-TIMEBOX] End before the attention curve does
**Move:** Watch for the room cooling and stop the pitch yourself, with your own time reason, rather than waiting to be wrapped up; do not speed up to fit the remaining material in.
**APPLY WHEN:** Visible attention decay mid-presentation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Contracted training or demo slots you are paid to fill; when the unfinished part contains something the buyer explicitly asked for.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted; the sub-claim that speeding up reduces retention is offered without source.

### [FRAME:DISENGSCAN] Read disengagement as your cue to step back, not to push
**Move:** Treat watch-glancing, body turning away, folder closing and phone-checking as a signal to shorten and withdraw, not as a signal to add material or ask for approval.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-presentation with room energy dropping; long committee meetings late in the day.
**AVOID WHEN:** The disengagement is caused by an unanswered objection — withdrawing there just leaves the objection standing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a behavioural rule with no support beyond the author's reading of rooms.

### F. Prizing, qualification, neediness control

### [FRAME:NOWANT] Three-rule neediness protocol
**Move:** Before the meeting, set three operating rules: want nothing from this specific outcome, plan to demonstrate exactly one thing you are genuinely excellent at, and pre-decide the moment you will pull back instead of pressing. Run it as a private loop on the way in.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-stakes single-shot meetings; you are commercially dependent on this deal; your pipeline is thin and you can feel it.
**AVOID WHEN:** Long-cycle collaborative accounts where the buyer expects visible investment and enthusiasm; as an excuse to under-prepare or skip follow-up. The protocol assumes you can afford to lose the deal — run while desperate it produces brittle theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's fourth VC pitch after three failures; the rules are borrowed from a film's philosophy, not a study.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-INT] Prize frame — internal stance
**Move:** Hold three sentences internally before and during the meeting: I am the prize; you are trying to impress me; you are seeking my approval. The book claims the frame ultimately rests on conviction rather than wording.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any meeting where you catch yourself supplicating.
**AVOID WHEN:** The conviction is unfounded — this is precisely the mechanism by which the technique degenerates into arrogance with nothing behind it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — no supporting case, offered as an internal discipline.

### [FRAME:QUALIFY-BACK-LATE] Half-joking qualification once you hold status
**Move:** Late in the meeting, after the idea is understood, ask a light, half-joking qualifying question back ("remind me why I want to do business with you", "have you done a deal this size before"), let the laugh happen, then accept their serious answer casually. Repeat until it approaches awkwardness, then stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already hold some status; the buyer has subordinates present; late in the meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are behind on credibility; the buyer is genuinely senior to your entire company in the relationship; cultures that read teasing as disrespect. Misuse mode: sarcasm that lands as contempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a scripted exchange with a bank client.

### [FRAME:NO-TRIAL-CLOSE] Replace trial closes with a challenge statement
**Move:** Ban temperature-check questions ("are we in the ballpark", "what do you think so far"); instead issue a light challenge as a statement, not a question — many buyers, one of you, how will you compete for the attention — and deliberately do not seek an answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-pitch, when the urge to check status appears; scarce, differentiated offering.
**AVOID WHEN:** You genuinely need to detect misalignment early; committee sales where silent doubt kills deals later; undifferentiated offers where the claim of scarcity is false.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated as a preference; the criticism of trial closes carries no data.

### [FRAME:EARN-TASK] Make the buyer complete a real task to get access
**Move:** Put a small, legitimate qualifying obligation in front of the buyer before access is granted (cited external example: a limited-run BMW M3 requiring a signed care commitment before purchase).
**APPLY WHEN:** Supply-constrained offering; you can justify the requirement on substantive grounds.
**AVOID WHEN:** The hoop is fake — buyers detect manufactured scarcity fast; high-volume or transactional motions where friction just kills conversion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one third-party example cited without verification.

### [FRAME:MONEY-COMMODITY] Reframe capital as the commodity
**Move:** Internally and in framing language, treat money as fungible and abundant and the deal as singular — capital needs a place to work and can do nothing until it buys what you have.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fundraising and investor conversations; any market with many funding sources.
**AVOID WHEN:** Tight capital markets or a single realistic buyer; product sales where the buyer's budget genuinely is the scarce side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted from the author's position inside capital markets.

### [FRAME:PRIZE-EXT] Prize frame — external script
**Move:** Late in the meeting, state that you are glad you found time to come, that you must wrap up, that deals like this are scarce and include you, and that you must choose which counterparties to accept; then ask them to tell you why you would enjoy working with them, what previous partners said about them, and how they behave when a deal goes sideways. Reference a partner you must justify them to.
**APPLY WHEN:** You genuinely have alternatives; the buyer has been passive or entitled; end of pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no alternatives and cannot walk — the frame is a bluff that unravels the moment you chase; buyers whose procurement rules force them to treat you as a vendor regardless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's script; the animal-shelter adoption story illustrates the same flip performed on him.

### G. Endgame — stack, close, withdraw

### [FRAME:STACK-4] Run the four-frame stack when the room turns analytical
**Move:** In the final minutes — or the moment the buyer starts drilling into technical detail — deliver intrigue, then prize, then time, then moral authority in fast succession, without pausing for evaluation between them.
**APPLY WHEN:** End of pitch; the buyer shifts to cold detail-hunting before any wanting exists; the audience drifts toward "we'll think about it".
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's process legitimately requires analysis now (formal diligence, technical validation). The author's own case shows the stack can drive a decision on a deal that turns out badly — running it on someone whose interests you are responsible for is the misuse mode.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one distressed-debt transaction where the author was the *target*, generalised afterwards.

### [FRAME:TIME-TRAIN] Time frame as a real, external constraint
**Move:** Acknowledge openly that nobody enjoys time pressure, compare a solid deal to a train that stops, boards and departs on schedule, affirm that they should not proceed unless they genuinely want it, then name a specific date framed as driven by the process or market rather than by you. Say explicitly that a "no" is acceptable.
**APPLY WHEN:** A genuine timeline exists (closing date, allocation, capacity, seasonality); after the prize frame.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deadline is fabricated — the book itself warns that heavy-handed scarcity reads as a cheap retail tactic and damages professional standing; also where the buyer's committee cadence makes the date impossible.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Geomark/Boeing example and the "deal train" script.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-STANDARD] Set a standard of conduct both sides must meet
**Move:** Assert the standard the deal will be run by — clean documentation, no re-trading, reputational care — and ask the counterparty to commit to the same, shifting the evaluation from price and power to whether both sides meet the standard.
**APPLY WHEN:** Last frame in the stack; counterparties who compete on status or price; situations where you cannot outrank them on wealth or scale.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot meet the standard yourself; when it becomes a lever to shame a buyer into deciding — the misuse mode the book flirts with.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the trader's clean-paper claim, plus the Mother Teresa / Scripps Clinic account of a values frame overriding a status frame.

### [FRAME:MORAL-AUTH-STEWARD] Elevate the decision to stewardship of a third party
**Move:** Raise the decision to a values question competitors cannot outbid — stewardship of a community, a history, of people who cannot be in the room — and position yourself as the party that recognised it first.
**APPLY WHEN:** A genuine third-party stake exists and you intend to honour it; late in the pitch, once desire is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** The moral claim is decorative — buyers punish it hard on discovery, and it is the most reputation-damaging move in this book to fake; also where the buyer is legally constrained to score on other factors.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch, deployed alongside four other frames at once, so attribution is impossible.

### [FRAME:POSTPITCH] Two-minute post-pitch discipline
**Move:** After your last line, say nothing that asks for reassurance. Ban the three reflex questions — is this still a good deal, so what do you think, we can sign today if you want. Sit still and let the silence run until they respond to you.
**APPLY WHEN:** The end of every formal presentation; also the pause after any price or terms statement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has asked a direct process question — silence there is theatre, not control; also unsuitable when a genuine next-step confirmation is the whole purpose of the call.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's third failed VC pitch, where he asked exactly those questions and lost.

### [FRAME:QUALTRIAD] Closing triad that qualifies the buyer
**Move:** Close with three statements instead of a request: the round or slot fills within a stated short window; we do not actually need your money, we want a specific strategic benefit from you; and we are not yet sure you are the right partner, so tell us what you bring. Then stop.
**APPLY WHEN:** Genuine competitive demand exists; you can survive this buyer saying no; the buyer is used to being courted.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no other options and the scarcity claim is fabricated — sophisticated buyers check; buyers who already suspect you are running a script; regulated or procurement-driven sales where posturing invalidates you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one VC pitch, self-reported $14M valuation outcome, no comparison case.

### [FRAME:LOVEORLEAVE] "Only like it? Then throw us out"
**Move:** State that a merely positive reaction is worse than a negative one, that if they do not love the idea they should reject you now, and that you are genuinely fine with that — because you could not work together without shared conviction.
**APPLY WHEN:** As the final frame, after the emotional peak; when you have real alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The committee has not aligned internally and you would be handing a lukewarm member a ready-made veto; you need this deal; the buyer's culture reads ultimatums as arrogance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the recounted closing sequence of one won pitch.

### [FRAME:WITHDRAWCLOSE] Close by withdrawing
**Move:** End without asking for anything: invite them to come to you when they feel the time is right, then physically leave rather than lingering for reactions.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have generated genuine desire; competitive situations where every other bidder will end with an eager request.
**AVOID WHEN:** Nothing has been established yet — withdrawal without prior desire is just an absent close; also where a concrete next step is required by the buyer's process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one pitch; the contrast case (a rival ending on "we eagerly await your decision") is the author's framing of the loser.

### H. Skill-building outside live deals

### [FRAME:TRAPSPOT] Notice environmental traps first, step around them later
**Move:** First only notice: in daily life, identify arrangements designed to control your behaviour (being told to wait, seating, sequencing) and mentally rehearse a way around them. Then gradually start stepping around them for real, with a partner, beginning with small ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** Training phase; low-risk environments; before any attempt at live frame collisions with buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes reflexive contrarianism — some waiting is just logistics, and treating every process step as a dominance test damages relationships and wastes goodwill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's self-taught path, described as 10,000+ hours of trial and error with early deals lost.

### [FRAME:LINGUA] Label frames out loud with a practice partner
**Move:** Learn a fixed vocabulary (frame control, power-busting frame, frame collision, prizing, environmental trap, seizing status, local star power, push/pull, hot cognition), use it to name in real time what is coming at you, and debrief with a partner afterwards.
**APPLY WHEN:** Team selling; post-call reviews; building a shared playbook across a sales team.
**AVOID WHEN:** In front of the buyer — naming the game to the target destroys it. Note also that the book's original vocabulary carries its discredited explanatory terms; those should be dropped rather than taught (see Neuromyth demotions).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as the fix for the author's own difficulty teaching the method.

### [FRAME:SAFEDRILL] Practise collisions on safe targets, soft touch, stop on defence
**Move:** Initiate frame collisions only with people who pose no career risk, always with visible good humour. If the other side goes defensive, that is the signal you came on too strong: pause, do not press, change partner or setting and restart.
**APPLY WHEN:** Skill-building; any time you feel yourself forcing the technique.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never run this on your CEO, a live strategic account, or anyone with power over you while learning; the book explicitly warns the humourless version gets you escorted out of the building.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author reports having had to reset many times himself.

### [FRAME:APPRENTICE] Apprentice to better frame operators
**Move:** Once basically competent, seek out people better than you, keep working in pairs or a small group, hold your active repertoire to a few frames, and start teaching others.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-basics; team enablement.
**AVOID WHEN:** No such practitioners are within reach — the alternative on offer (self-study) is the path the author himself calls costly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — analogy to martial arts and apprenticeship, no support.

## Where this book stops working

The entire corpus is one operator's experience raising capital: institutional deals, roughly $10–25M rounds against a $1B mandate at the top end, decided in single-shot meetings by principals with discretion. Outside that shape it degrades, in specific ways:

1. **Low and mid ACV, high-volume motions.** Prizing, walk-outs, denial, take-backs and qualification triads cost meetings. Where the economics depend on conversion rate rather than deal size, the friction is pure loss — the book never prices the meetings its moves burn. Gates marked `acv=high` are not decoration.
2. **Multi-call consultative and technical cycles.** STRONG, the 20-minute budget and the frame stack assume the decision happens in the room. Where discovery precedes any pitch and the decision is a scorecard, the sequence has nothing to attach to; deflecting detail (HIGHLEVEL-REDIRECT, DETAIL-GATE) reads as concealment exactly when evaluation is the buyer's job.
3. **Procurement, RFP, regulated and compliance-gated buying.** Rituals the book calls status traps are legal requirements; published weighted criteria make BESTIDEA an argument against the buyer's own governance; time frames collide with committee cadence; posturing can disqualify you outright.
4. **Committees and internal politics.** Most status moves are one-on-one instruments. A humiliated stakeholder holds a veto; a lukewarm member handed LOVEORLEAVE has a ready-made exit; visible friction in a consensus culture is a defect, not leverage.
5. **Existing customers, expansion and renewal.** Coldness, denial and manufactured scarcity read as arrogance in a relationship that already has a status settlement.
6. **Weak or undifferentiated positions.** Every prize move assumes real alternatives. Without them it is a bluff, and the book supplies no test for whether you actually hold the leverage it assumes. It also concedes the whole method is unnecessary when the product is already irresistible — i.e. it is a remedy for under-differentiated offers pitched to distracted principals.
7. **Cross-cultural and reciprocity-first markets**, and any market where referral and rapport are the entry ticket — NORAPPORT and NO-RITUAL are the most portable-looking and least portable moves in the book.
8. **When you cannot afford to lose the deal.** The neediness protocol, the push/pull ladder and every walk-away are theatre without a real exit; run desperate, they produce brittle performance the buyer reads accurately.

## What the book debunks

- **"Keep the audience's attention" advice** — attacked as unactionable; the book's counter is that attention needs both interest and stakes, and that a pitch with only upside gets polite silence.
- **The law of large numbers school of selling** (100 calls → 2 sales) — read as hiding a weak social position behind volume.
- **Trial closing, spin selling, fast talk, scripted objection handling, interviewing the customer, invasive questioning, supplicating** — all read by experienced buyers as neediness and as markers of subordinate position.
- **"Never take no for an answer"** — badgering occasionally converts and reliably produces buyer's remorse.
- **Pressure closing as a technique** — reframed as a *symptom* of an already lost status position, producing random wins and regret.
- **Framing money as the prize** — named as a common and often fatal error.
- **"Simplicity always wins"** — over-simplifying reads as naive or uninformed; the stated goal is tuning to how this audience reasons (statistics simplified hard, people-and-relationship detail kept rich). This sits in visible tension with the book's own MSG-SIMPLIFY advice — hold both, and let the audience decide which applies.
- **"Charisma without conflict"** — an entirely pleasant meeting is named as the author's most common cause of failed pitches: everyone leaves happy, nothing happens.
- **Long biographies** — more background makes you look more average, not more credible.
- **Long pitches and "a strong presenter is exempt from attention limits"** — countered with the Seinfeld anecdote against a banker's boast that he could hold a room for an hour reading a phone book.
- **"A better way of organising information wins deals"** and "more information wins pitches" — the presenter's real problem is selection, not accumulation; the risk of more information is pushing the buyer into analysis.
- **"The biggest, most credentialed team wins by default"** — the book's case is one counter-example (with the concession that the larger London team could plausibly have won).
- **The rational decision picture** (identify problem → evaluate options → decide) — argued to be reversed in practice, with data serving post-hoc justification.
- **The comforting post-mortem "they didn't get it"** — the book puts the failure in your own signalling.
- **Documented failure modes it names against itself:** abusing control once you have it; the humourless version of the method getting you escorted out; too many frames at once; practising on high-risk targets; coming on too strong and triggering defensiveness; and the plain concession that adding time pressure reduces decision quality — an argument against its own technique. Its strongest self-contained failure evidence: the four-frame stack produced a confident yes in the author on a transaction that left a competitor down 15% two years later.
- **Its own rapport claims are contested:** the assertions that rapport-building contributes nothing to social rank and that trying to fit in lowers perceived value run against mainstream sales research on trust-building. Treat as the author's position, not settled.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No base rate, ever.** Not one case is reported where a frame move lost a deal. Every outcome is narrated by the person who executed it, after the fact, with the winning interpretation attached.
- **No attribution within a deal.** The case study fires five or more frames simultaneously; the book itself concedes the losing competitor might have won on other grounds and that a bad market had cleared the field. No move in it is isolable.
- **No test for whether you hold the leverage the method assumes.** Prizing, walking, deadlines and selectivity all presuppose real alternatives; the book supplies no way to check, and no fallback for the case where you do not have them.
- **No stop rule.** Plowing, push/pull and status moves have escalation instructions but no defined point of retreat other than "if it stops being fun" and "stop if they go defensive". The most damaging moves are the ones with the least specified exit.
- **Buyer-side cost is unmodelled.** Meetings burned, relationships strained and reputational spend are never counted; the author's own economics (few, enormous deals) make those costs invisible in a way they are not for volume sellers.
- **Committees, procurement and multi-threading are barely present.** Champions, blockers, internal consensus, security review and legal are almost absent from a book whose deals were decided by individuals.
- **No post-sale horizon.** Delivery, renewal, expansion and reference risk do not appear; a technique optimised for one-shot capital raises has no account of what the seized status costs you in month six.
- **No cultural variance.** The moves are presented as universal human behaviour; every example is US, mostly finance, mostly male, mostly 2000s.
- **Self-selection in the evidence.** The author was recruited into the case study by the very moves he teaches, including a withheld material fact — offered as proof the method works, though it is equally readable as proof it works on people who already believe it.
- **The bravado layer.** "You set the rules and can never lose" is self-promotion, not method; per this lens's extraction rule it is discarded along with the explanatory apparatus below.

## Neuromyth demotions

Everything in this section is what the lens must **never transmit as fact**. The behavioural moves above stand or fall on their own evidence, which is anecdotal throughout.

- **The three-stage brain model as the architecture of persuasion** — the book's central explanatory device, in which a primitive survival structure filters all input before reasoning, and messages composed in the neocortex are received by a "croc brain". REFUTED as neuroanatomy by peer-reviewed work (Cesario et al. 2020; Steffen et al. 2022): human brains did not evolve in three stacked layers, and decision-making is distributed rather than tiered. Also refuted in its derived forms: the claimed 5-million-year evolutionary gap between the layers; the "neocortex vs crocodile brain" picture of two competing audiences inside one buyer; the claim that reasoning structures can be bypassed or short-circuited; and "every croc brain responds identically everywhere" (ignore boring, fight or flee danger, truncate complexity) — the underlying regularities are ordinary attention and summarisation effects, not evidence for the architecture. Reframe permitted: fast affective reaction versus deliberate analysis, with no anatomical claim attached.
- **"~90% of a message is discarded by an early filter"** — UNSOURCED; a self-reported figure dressed as a neuroscience finding.
- **"A neurotoxin gets attached to a complex message and routes it to the amygdala for destruction"** — FABRICATED mechanism; metaphor presented as physiology.
- **"Neurofinance" as the author's field, with claimed professor interviews and executive experiments** — MARKETING TERM / UNVERIFIABLE; no data, protocol or publication is given.
- **Hot vs cold cognition as mutually exclusive brain states** — OVERSTATED. Real work exists on affect/analysis interaction; the absolute impossibility claim is asserted without source. The practical heuristic survives: do not invite the room to start calculating mid-pitch.
- **Attention as a two-chemical cocktail** (a desire transmitter plus a tension transmitter that "add up", tunable by giving rewards and taking things away) — OVERSIMPLIFIED to the point of being wrong. Keep only the behavioural pattern: interest plus stakes holds attention.
- **Dopamine as the novelty/reward chemical acting in ~1/20 second, "poured in" by status objects; norepinephrine "injected" by tension language; a dopamine flood explaining why irresistible products need no pitch** — OVERSTATED popular-science shorthand, unsourced and unmeasured; neurotransmitter levels are not controllable by pitch phrasing in the manner described. Keep only the honest concession that a strongly differentiated product needs less framing.
- **Haynes's button-press study read as "decisions are made ~7 seconds before awareness, therefore buyers cannot reason"** — MISAPPLIED; the study concerned trivial motor choices, not commercial judgment.
- **Change blindness used to argue a before/after contrast is literally invisible** — MISAPPLIED; it is a visual-attention phenomenon, not a model of how comparisons are understood in conversation. The advice to narrate movement stands without it.
- **"Social threats engage the same threat system as physical threats"** (cyberball-style study, unnamed in the text) — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED in the original literature but contested and far weaker than the book's use; demote to unverified.
- **"Impressions are averaged, not summed"** — plausible in the impression-formation literature, but cited without a source; unverified as used.
- **"22 times as much information in the affective part of a message" (attributed to Zajonc)** — UNVERIFIED figure, no retrievable derivation.
- **"Heightened emotion creates strong memories"** — DIRECTIONALLY SUPPORTED in the wider literature, but the book cites nothing and leans on flashbulb-memory folklore, itself less reliable than popularly assumed. Do not cite this book as evidence.
- **"Reducing the autonomy of choice triggers a threat response"** — labelled by the author himself as one of his guiding theories, i.e. ASSERTION; the psychology of reactance exists independently and is not what the book cites.
- **"The brain is wired to do things for status, not money"** — ASSERTION, no source; usable only as a rhetorical heuristic about buyer motives.
- **"The mind is not a camera but a machine for observing motion and predicting what comes next"** (used to justify the three-forces pattern) — ASSERTION dressed as neuroscience; the pattern may still work rhetorically as a "why now" device.
- **Theory of mind invoked as licence to retune messages** — the concept is real; the book's use is a metaphor for audience adaptation, not a finding.
- **Framing as a survival instinct ("frames are rooted in survival and seek dominance")** — ASSERTION built on the refuted layer. Frame competition may be described as an observable social phenomenon; its evolutionary explanation must not be.
- **The "energy field" analogy for frames** — flagged by the author as an analogy; harmless as metaphor, not a mechanism.
**Quarantined numbers** (demoted, do not re-transmit as fact): ~90% of a message discarded before higher processing; dopamine acting in ~1/20 second; Zajonc's 22× affective-information figure; Haynes's ~7-second pre-awareness prediction; "100 years" of time-pressure research; the unnamed jaywalking-conformity study.
