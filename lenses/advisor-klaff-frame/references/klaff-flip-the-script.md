# Flip the Script — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens uses this book for one mechanism: getting a sophisticated buyer to state the conclusion themselves, because pushing toward a decision raises resistance. Where other sources give the council discovery frameworks, qualification math or objection libraries, this one supplies room-level choreography — status sorting, expertise bursts, threat/reward/fairness sequencing, and a handover that removes the ask entirely. It is strongest on the single high-stakes meeting with a professional evaluator who can decide, and on why an argument-winning seller loses the deal weeks later. Everything is single-source and self-reported; the ceiling is practitioner-anecdote throughout, and several signature moves are one-shot — they work on a counterpart who has not seen them before.

## Core model

Klaff's sequence, in the order the book itself recaps it:

1. **Status Alignment** — attention from a decision maker is gated by perceived peer rank, not likeability. Delivered via the **Status Tip-Off**: insider vocabulary + a recent consequential action + a live open industry issue. Obedience (rank/badge) buys compliance; influence (peer level) can change belief.
2. **Certainty** — buyers stall from a *Certainty Gap*, not an information gap. More logos and more answers widen it. The replacement is the **Flash Roll**: a short, fast, jargon-dense, emotionless demonstration of mastery that ends flat, with no bid for approval.
3. **Pre-Wired Ideas** — three buyer questions must be answered before any detail: why should I care (*Winter Is Coming*, a structural threat), what's in it for me (*2X*, a doubling), why you (*Skin in the Game*, demonstrated personal exposure).
4. **New Normal / Plain Vanilla** — novelty buys attention and destroys commitment. Keep the deal maximally ordinary on every measured dimension, concentrate all strangeness into one named difference (**Novelty Chunking**), then show that difference is now standard.
5. **Buyer's Formula** — instead of asking for the sale, teach the buyer how to evaluate a deal in your category (obvious failures → counterintuitive failures → obvious actions → non-obvious hacks), then detach explicitly and hand over autonomy.

The load-bearing psychology is **ownership bias**: people price and defend ideas they believe they generated far above ideas handed to them. The success condition the book states is inversion — the buyer proposes the deal back to you. The stated failure mode is reverting to argumentative objection-handling, which feels productive to the seller and produces agreements the buyer abandons later. A parallel thread (the Devils Lake sales floor, the "Five Personalities of the Sales Apocalypse") argues that persona-switching inside one call is what destroys trust, and that the cure is a fixed, visible, non-negotiable set of values plus expert positioning rather than borrowed charisma.

## Moves

### Stage: pre-work, diagnosis and staffing

### [FRAME:TIPOFFPREP] Source the Tip-Off by interviewing three peers of the target
**Move:** Before the meeting, interview three people at the target's own level and capture how they catch each other up peer-to-peer; assemble the Tip-Off from that vocabulary and those live open issues, then memorize it.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have lead time and access to genuine peers of the target; entering an unfamiliar vertical.
**AVOID WHEN:** no access to real peers — lingo reconstructed from public content produces a detectable imitation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — stated as the author's team method, no counts.

### [FRAME:FLASHWRITE] Write the Flash Roll to a four-part skeleton
**Move:** Draft it as problem faced → your assessment → solution applied → economics (time, cost, measured result); write in the register you use with your own technical staff, keep the jargon, cut every reflective or editorial line; target 138–250 words; rehearse it like a stage script.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparing for any high-skepticism room; onboarding a founder or rep who is knowledgeable but not persuasive.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real solved case to draw on — fabricated detail is the failure mode this technique invites.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's written scripts for a client rep and for the Geneva founder.

### [FRAME:VIABILITY] Deal Viability Test before committing capital
**Move:** Before signing, call roughly ten realistic buyers, describe the strongest features, gauge interest, and decide go/no-go on demand. Expected shape: a couple of hard yeses, a few "tell me more," the rest declining.
**APPLY WHEN:** the downside of being stuck with the asset is existential; you have a warm list reachable in a day.
**AVOID WHEN:** small reversible bets where launching is cheaper than testing (the book puts the prototype threshold near $50K); the pool you can call is not the pool that would actually buy; conceptual enthusiasm is easy to mistake for demand.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Mahalo test (16 calls, all soft no) plus his stated routine.

### [FRAME:TRENDCALLS] Harvest a category from the market before you pitch
**Move:** Call analysts, brokers and operators with open, low-stakes questions about what is trending and what the next big deals look like; log answers and hunt for an existing category your unusual asset can be filed under. Run comparable-deal research in parallel for precedents sharing the theme.
**APPLY WHEN:** your offer is genuinely unlike what the buyer normally purchases and you need an existing frame to place it in.
**AVOID WHEN:** timeline cannot absorb an expensive dead end (the author's own team burned a week and nearly missed a deadline); no honest category exists — inventing one is fabrication, not framing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal; the insight arrived accidentally from a subordinate's complaint.

### [FRAME:IMMERSE] Buy domain immersion before the pitch
**Move:** Put the pitch team physically on the buyer's ground — factory, museum, shops, the local city — mine the origin story, product lineage and local self-image, then pitch the buyer's own brand back to them in their terms before proposing anything.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-value competitive pitch, buyer has strong heritage or identity, and you have days rather than hours.
**AVOID WHEN:** timeline or budget cannot carry it, or the buyer reads travel as posturing. This is expensive preparation dressed as a technique — the transferable part is the research, not the trip.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — six days of prep, one deal.

### [FRAME:TALENTSTACK] Rank-match your team to theirs
**Move:** Staff the deal so every counterpart has a same-rank interlocutor — analyst to analyst, counsel to counsel, finance to finance — leaving you talking only to their decision maker; keep yourself scarce and doing only lead-role work.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-party enterprise or investor deals where the other side fields a full bench.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo or small-team selling — the fallback is a minimal virtual bench (assistant, technical person, accountant) for alignment and load only; over-staffing a small deal reads as theater.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own 8-role standing stack.

### [FRAME:LOCALPROXY] Put a native in-group voice on stage
**Move:** When the audience's language and culture are not yours, deploy a presenter who is credibly of that world, train them on the script *structure* (Tip-Off → Flash Roll → Pre-Wired Ideas → Buyer's Formula) rather than on the words, and let them localize the details.
**APPLY WHEN:** cross-border or cross-culture pitch to an audience that will not accept an outsider; the message is structural and survives translation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the proxy cannot answer live technical questions, or their lack of accountability for delivery becomes visible. High-variance: the source account describes three days of conflict with a temperamental hire and presents the outcome as a lucky break.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one hire (a Czech soap actor, €2,000 for the week), one performance.

### [FRAME:CALLAUDIT] Diagnose the floor from recorded calls, not from live coaching
**Move:** Before prescribing anything, sit with a stack of recorded calls across different reps, buyers and SKUs, and look for the pattern in how calls *end* rather than for individual mistakes. Treat a repeated ending — deferral to an unavailable third party, "let me check and get back to you" — as one systemic cause, not many individual ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** you own or advise a team whose calls are recorded; win rate is flat and per-rep coaching has not moved it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is one rep or a handful of calls — you would be pattern-matching noise into a "system" problem.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one six-hour listening session at one company; no dataset, no counts.

### [FRAME:PERSONAAUDIT] Audit a call for persona switching
**Move:** Replay a call and label each stretch with which of the five characters is speaking (Ultimate Nice Guy → ShamWow Guy → The Sorcerer → The Angel → The Wolf). Count the transitions, treat each as a trust cost, and rewrite the call so one consistent character carries it end to end.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-call review; calls that "went fine" but ended in deferral; onboarding a rep who mimics a top performer.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a live checklist during a call — mid-conversation self-monitoring produces exactly the stiffness it is meant to remove. Weak as a hiring or firing signal: a coaching lens, not a measurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a typology derived from the author's own listening; no coding scheme, no inter-rater check, no link from switch count to win rate.

### [FRAME:OWNSCRIPT] Write a personal script instead of copying the star
**Move:** Have each rep write their own call script in their own vocabulary and values rather than imitating the team's natural closer; compellingness comes from consistency with self, not borrowed style.
**APPLY WHEN:** enabling a team around one outlier performer; onboarding reps whose calls sound performed.
**AVOID WHEN:** the rep has no domain competence yet — an authentic script from someone who cannot answer technical questions still fails. Expertise is a prerequisite, not a substitute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Devils Lake two-day training, outcomes self-reported.

### [FRAME:STICKGUNS] Stick to your guns on concessions
**Move:** Pre-decide answers to the standard pressure asks and deliver them flatly and politely, without justification theater: discount — no; free upgrade — no; "send me a proposal" — answered with a question about whether terms are already verbally agreed. Consistency is the point; the buyer's remaining options are to work with you or leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** concession requests arrive as reflex tests early in a deal, before value is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** the concession is a real commercial lever in a live negotiation with a qualified buyer, or your pricing has room the buyer knows about. Rigidity against a buyer with power and alternatives simply ends deals — the source concedes losing some deals, which is a cost, not a free win.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one in-house story (the refused Moto Guzzi V7R film prop) plus a claimed quarterly revenue doubling.

### Stage: access, first contact and status alignment

### [FRAME:PEERENTRY] Open with a shared prior deal, not with your offer
**Move:** Approach with a low-arousal opener naming a concrete engagement you both touch (a deal you worked on for their organization), hands at your sides, no handshake-forcing, no pitch posture; buy a few seconds of attention before the Tip-Off.
**APPLY WHEN:** cold approach in a social or event setting; target is unreachable through normal channels.
**AVOID WHEN:** the shared reference is thin or unverifiable; when the target is mid-performance or surrounded — wait for the crowd to thin.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one retold approach that preceded a $25M investment.

### [FRAME:TIPOFF] Three-element Status Tip-Off
**Move:** Deliver a compact three-sentence statement containing (1) specific industry vocabulary an outsider could not produce, (2) a recent consequential action you took in that industry, (3) a live open issue the industry is currently arguing about — then stop and let the target respond.
**APPLY WHEN:** first contact with a decision maker; short window; you need to be sorted as insider before any deal content.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot back the claims under one follow-up question; when the "insider" facts are stale — naming a resolved issue marks you as an outsider faster than silence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Beverly Hills conference approach; wide reuse claimed, no counts.

### [FRAME:TIPOFFSTORY] In-group detail story as Tip-Off
**Move:** Tell a short, specific story containing a detail only a genuine member of the audience's world would know — a quirk of a local product, a shared inconvenience — and let the audience supply the punchline themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** first 60 seconds with a skeptical audience whose group you actually belong to; especially when your appearance signals outsider (a suit in front of line workers).
**AVOID WHEN:** the detail is researched rather than lived — a wrong or generic detail marks you as a tourist faster than saying nothing. Single-use per audience.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Kovat 480 story on the factory floor; audience response is the only evidence offered.

### [FRAME:STATUSALIGN] Status Alignment via a contrarian shared frame
**Move:** Open, especially from a weak position, by voicing a belief the room already privately holds and outsiders usually do not — here, that the buyer's regional values are not inferior to the dominant market's — and let agreement register before advancing any proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are the underdog, an outsider, or previously rejected; the audience shares an identity you can honestly align with.
**AVOID WHEN:** the shared frame is invented or the alignment is faked — a contrarian cultural claim you do not hold reads as pandering and is unrecoverable. Mismatched to a diverse room with no single in-group.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — read off body language in one room; nods are a weak proxy for agreement.

### [FRAME:STATUSTIP] Name the buyer's situation as a category
**Move:** Before the buyer speaks, give their situation a category name ("you're a late-career athlete"), describe how that category usually ends badly, cite your track record handling exactly that category — ideally with a living example the buyer can see — then name the good outcome as a category too.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is distracted or treats you as low-status; you genuinely have comparable cases.
**AVOID WHEN:** the label is inaccurate or insulting and gets rejected outright; your track record cannot survive a reference check; buyers who experience being categorized as being handled.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one conversation; the track-record claim is the seller's own.

### [FRAME:NOPITCHYET] Treat "what's the deal?" as a two-second audition
**Move:** When the aligned target asks about your deal, give one or two compressed lines (size, headline economics), exchange contact details, and end the encounter — do not open a twenty-minute presentation in a social setting.
**APPLY WHEN:** the alignment signal just fired (target asks your name, turns toward you, asks what you are working on).
**AVOID WHEN:** it is a booked meeting with allocated time; when failing to state substance now means no second contact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — same encounter as PEERENTRY.

### [FRAME:CURIOSITY] Standing threat plus a withheld insight
**Move:** To get a high-status person's attention cold: imply their position or reputation is quietly shifting and that people are talking, then say you hold something valuable they can use — without revealing it. Stay visibly non-needy throughout.
**APPLY WHEN:** approaching or recruiting someone who currently has no reason to talk to you and whose status depends on ranking.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot substantiate the implied gossip — this manipulates social anxiety and collapses if challenged; buyers with no status stake; any relationship that must stay clean long-term; repeat use on the same person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=any · market=consultative/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Gamecon approach to a single eSports player; called near-universal without evidence.

### [FRAME:EQUALCLOCK] Open by framing both calendars as equally scarce
**Move:** Start with a line treating the meeting as a mutual scheduling achievement and the agenda as dense, then move straight into substance — signalling your time costs what theirs does and you are not asking a favor.
**APPLY WHEN:** meetings obtained through a favor or intermediary, where the status default is supplicant.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship genuinely is asymmetric and the counterpart reads it as posturing; when the intermediary's goodwill is the real asset.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one venture-fund room.

### [FRAME:REOPEN] Reopen a decision that has already gone the other way
**Move:** Call the decision owner after the loss, name a defensible procedural reason the first round was unrepresentative (rushed meeting, wrong team), request a narrow second review rather than a full restart, and quietly note the reputational cost to *them* if the market concludes their large accounts always route to the incumbent — then immediately concede their right to keep using that firm and pivot into substance.
**APPLY WHEN:** a competitive loss is announced but not signed; you have a genuinely different angle; the buyer has ongoing reputational exposure to a vendor market.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have nothing new to say (the second meeting burns the relationship); when the reputational beat lands as a threat rather than an observation; where reopening an awarded decision is procedurally impossible. Openly manipulative and single-use.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one phone call, one buyer, reported as succeeding with light resistance.

### Stage: building certainty and expert authority

### [FRAME:FLASHROLL] Deliver a 60–90 second technical mastery burst
**Move:** Deliver a memorized ~250-word block at roughly double normal speaking pace: state the problem as routine, give your assessment in dense domain jargon, name the actions taken, land a specific numeric outcome — no hedging verbs, no emotion, no bid for validation, no pride.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer doubts your competence or your fit for their vertical; a skeptical technical audience; the moment a question implicitly challenges your domain depth.
**AVOID WHEN:** the audience can interrogate the specifics and you cannot survive it; when comprehension rather than credibility is what is missing; repeated to the same buyer it registers as a rehearsed act.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Geneva $10M raise plus a sawmill-software rep's turnaround.

### [FRAME:FLASHTHEIRS] Flash Roll aimed at the buyer's own product line
**Move:** After alignment and before any proposal, deliver 30–60 seconds of fast, jargon-dense, specific analysis of the buyer's *own* line — model names, trims, prices versus named competitors, feature lists, performance figures — ending in a concrete opportunity framed as a number. Where the Tip-Off makes you one of them, this deliberately separates you as the technical authority.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer has a hard problem in exactly the domain you can speak to; early in the meeting.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot sustain follow-up at that depth, or the audience is technically deeper than you — the burst reads as mastery only to someone less prepared. Do not reuse the same roll with the same audience.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two performances (agency selection, Volka product line); "silence means acceptance" is the author's read, not a measurement.

### [FRAME:MARKETFLASH] Flash Roll variant for market rather than craft
**Move:** Run the same dense, fast register over market structure: total market size, the single segment actually earning margin, three reasons the buyer's customer values the category, your price point with the multiple by which it undercuts the field, then the technical spec block that justifies it.
**APPLY WHEN:** investor audiences needing both market credibility and technical credibility inside one slot.
**AVOID WHEN:** figures are estimates you cannot defend line by line; when the price advantage is temporary and the audience knows it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the genetics-testing pitch as retold.

### [FRAME:FLASHEXIT] Exit the Flash Roll flat
**Move:** Close with a shrug-level line that this is simply what you would do given what you see, then move immediately to the next section — never wait for reaction or approval.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, as the terminator of any Flash Roll variant.
**AVOID WHEN:** never within this frame — waiting for validation is what collapses the expert posture.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a delivery rule without a supporting case.

### [FRAME:HOSTAUDIT] Audit the host's environment aloud, then pivot to their operational pain
**Move:** Open in their space by reading it with genuine domain detail (architecture, period, design constraints), let them accept the compliment, then turn the same observational register onto a real operating weakness — compliance gap, latency, exposure — stated as a conclusion rather than a question, and offer a bounded fix before returning to your agenda.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are physically on the buyer's turf; you have researched a verifiable weakness; their team prides itself on rigor.
**AVOID WHEN:** the weakness is guessed — a wrong diagnosis delivered as certainty destroys the frame; risky where face-loss is culturally costly and the audit humiliates rather than aligns.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Geneva bank meeting, weeks of rehearsal reported.

### [FRAME:QUEUEQ] Serialize the interrogation and pick the question you want
**Move:** When the room shifts into rapid-fire questioning, interrupt and impose one-question-at-a-time order by naming a specific person and inviting their question — choosing the questioner whose topic sits inside your prepared strength, which becomes the launchpad for the Flash Roll.
**APPLY WHEN:** multiple stakeholders firing risk questions; certainty is collapsing; you have a rehearsed answer in one domain.
**AVOID WHEN:** the questions concern a domain you cannot answer — ordering the queue only delays exposure; when the senior buyer resents being managed procedurally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one Geneva scene.

### [FRAME:NOCONFUSE] Refuse the confusion / language-barrier frame
**Move:** When the counterpart uses confusion, translation, or "explain it better" to slow you and lower your standing, decline the premise aloud — state that you will not let them be confused, name their competence, declare the deal ordinary and simple, and note that the time constraint requires taking your account at face value for now.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer weaponizes incomprehension or delegates you to interpreters and juniors; status is being pushed down.
**AVOID WHEN:** the confusion is genuine (real language gap, real technical unfamiliarity) — refusing it then destroys trust; risky with a buyer who outranks you and enjoys the power play.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single Moscow retelling; the author admits nerves and improvisation.

### [FRAME:ANALYSTMODE] Present the way the buyer's own function would present
**Move:** Mirror the professional register of the room rather than the entrepreneur register: demonstrate instead of asserting quality, compress the case to under a hundred words, skip pleasantries and weather talk, strip optimism and adjectives, use every second.
**APPLY WHEN:** pitching to analysts, underwriters, technical review boards — audiences paid to find your flaws.
**AVOID WHEN:** the audience is a founder or marketing buyer who reads flat delivery as lack of conviction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the venture-fund pitch for a $22M raise.

### [FRAME:PRESTRESS] Attack your own numbers before they do
**Move:** In roughly ninety words, state that you have already run your model through the harshest named stress test in their world and layered worst-case assumptions on top — hiring slower than competitors, paying materially more for everything, customers paying late — so the projection you are about to show is cash actually collectible.
**APPLY WHEN:** analyst or committee audiences whose default posture is dismantling projections.
**AVOID WHEN:** your numbers cannot survive the stated test — the claim invites verification; when the named test is not one this audience recognizes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one venture-fund room; effect judged by observed body language only.

### Stage: framing the case (Pre-Wired Ideas)

### [FRAME:FRAMECHECK] Check for the interpretive frame before you sell the specs
**Move:** Before presenting technical differentiators, establish whether this audience already reads those numbers as good; if not, either restrict the pitch to audiences who do, or drop the specs entirely and route the message through the three buyer questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** technical product, mixed-sophistication audience, short slot.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a specialist who resents simplification; when regulatory or procurement processes require the specs regardless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the professional-camera marketing illustration, no data.

### [FRAME:BIGTHREE] Answer the three gating questions before any detail
**Move:** Format the opening so that, in order, the buyer gets: a slow-moving threat in their environment they should already be tracking; a large payoff that is easy to measure; and evidence the terms are fair and you carry risk too — only then release the prepared detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** any first substantive presentation, especially to committees.
**AVOID WHEN:** the threat has to be manufactured to work — invented doom is the failure mode; also when the buyer arrives already sold and threat framing reopens risk analysis.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to unnamed psychologists; no study, sample or citation.

### [FRAME:DOOMCONTEXT] Open with the slow-moving threat, specifically staffed
**Move:** Begin with concrete, named, current threat actors and their economics in the buyer's own domain — who is doing the damage, at what scale, at what cost to them — establishing that the environment has become materially more dangerous than assumed, before introducing your solution.
**APPLY WHEN:** security, compliance, risk or continuity offers where the threat is verifiable and current.
**AVOID WHEN:** the threat is speculative or the numbers unsourced — sophisticated buyers fact-check in the room; inappropriate where fear framing violates the buyer's procurement norms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Geneva warm-up built on the "Winter Is Coming" idea.

### [FRAME:WINTER] Winter Is Coming — the structural-shift threat beat
**Move:** Establish the buyer's proven durability with concrete history, then open the threat: a structural change in their industry that will eliminate their current way of operating faster than they expect, evidenced by two or more specific, credible, converging threats named by name and direction, closing with a narrow window in which action still works. Never claim your product is better than theirs — claim the playing field is being replaced, and position yourself as someone who already knows how to operate on the new one. Reference case used: Nadella's 2014 pivot at Microsoft.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyers who are currently profitable, comfortable or complacent and see no reason to move; the industry genuinely has a visible shift you can evidence with named competitors and figures.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is already in crisis (fear is saturated — they need a floor, not a threat); regulated or compliance-heavy sales where doom framing reads as FUD; expansion conversations with existing customers; when the threats are vague or invented — generic "the market is changing" urgency reads as pressure and undoes the status you built.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Microsoft narrative as illustration plus two of the author's own deliveries; no test against a control.

### [FRAME:TWOX] The 2X reward claim
**Move:** Name one dimension the buyer cares about and show your deal doubles it (output, speed, satisfaction) or halves its cost, on a defined horizon, tied directly to the threats just named — and ask for the specific thing you need (time, budget, the account) as the mechanism. Subjective doublings are allowed ("half the hassle") as long as the formula stays 2X.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after the threat beat; when the buyer must abandon a working incumbent and switching cost, not price, is the real objection; when a doubling is arguable from the buyer's own base rates.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot substantiate the multiple — an unbacked 2X invites a due-diligence teardown, and this is the easiest move in the book to turn into an unfalsifiable promise; incremental or compliance purchases where a modest, provable gain is safer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "my team and I have observed" on replacement cycles plus a "double sales in five years" stage claim with no model shown.

### [FRAME:SKIN] Skin in the Game proof, delivered as a sacrifice inventory
**Move:** Instead of asserting trustworthiness, show what you personally lose if the deal fails — money, time, reputation, contractual lock-in — ideally more exposure than you are asking of them, and something a larger competitor structurally cannot match (in one delivery: all key staff dedicated for six months). One delivery pattern: enumerate visible frugality (small apartment, old car, cheap watch, back-row flight), let the audience ask where the money went, then reveal it is all inside the company alongside the patents and years of work. Close by aligning yourself with the buyer against the shared threat.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer's unspoken question is whether you will still be there when things get hard, or whether they get deprioritized after signature; founder or principal is in the room; you are the smaller vendor and can actually make the commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real exposure — fabricated sacrifice is a credibility bomb, and a rhetorical "all in" you cannot deliver converts a won deal into a churned one; employee sellers of a large vendor where personal stakes are implausible; buyers who read personal financial exposure as a solvency risk; reserved or risk-averse committees, where the martial register misfires.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/product/saas · market=investor/stage/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two recounted pitches; applause and audience reaction as the only outcome measures.

### [FRAME:ARCHETYPE] Compress with a stock archetype
**Move:** Describe the deal, the market or a person by invoking a widely recognized type (the book's example runs about 50 words) so the listener completes the picture themselves and experiences a fast "I get it" instead of processing new information.
**APPLY WHEN:** a complex situation must be understood in seconds by a room that will not read a deck.
**AVOID WHEN:** the archetype carries a stereotype that offends or misfires; technical buyers who will pick apart the analogy; when it flattens a distinction that actually matters to the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a mechanism with no source; one illustration only.

### [FRAME:POINTCALL] Make the threat personal to one person in the room
**Move:** During the threat segment, single out one attendee and walk them through how the abstract risk lands on them specifically, then leave the silence for the room to absorb.
**APPLY WHEN:** live presentation to a group that is emotionally disengaged; the risk genuinely applies to individuals present.
**AVOID WHEN:** the target could be humiliated or is the economic buyer's ally; health, money-loss or job-loss framings aimed at a named individual where that reads as an attack; any recorded or remote setting where the pressure cannot be read and defused.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=stage/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single pitch, self-reported outcome.

### [FRAME:PARTNERSKEPTIC] Interrogate your own partner in front of the buyer
**Move:** Publicly voice the audience's harshest doubt about your own side (age, health, capability) and press your partner on it, so the strongest counter-evidence comes out of the partner's mouth rather than as your claim. Requires a rehearsed cue and a partner who delivers the number under pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** the key risk is a person, not the product; two or more of your side are in the room.
**AVOID WHEN:** unrehearsed — an unprepared partner turns staged doubt into real doubt; when the doubt raised is unresolvable; with any buyer who would perceive the exchange as theatre, which it is, and which is repeatable only against a buyer who has not seen it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one staged exchange with a research partner.

### [FRAME:NATIVEFORMAT] Give data in the format the buyer would have built themselves
**Move:** Replace standard projections with the artifact the buyer's own analysts would assemble — raw contracts, customer agreements and invoices in their working layout — physically distributed so the room handles it simultaneously (in the recounted case, oversized fold-out sheets, one packet per attendee).
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer side is staffed with analysts whose job is verification; you have real underlying documents.
**AVOID WHEN:** the underlying evidence is thin — this format invites scrutiny at maximum resolution; remote meetings; buyers who want a decision summary, not source data.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a deliberate tactic in one meeting.

### [FRAME:MONEYLANG] Replace craft language with money language
**Move:** Strip differentiators any competitor could also claim (full-service, collaborative, award-winning, X years of combined experience) and lead with a single quantified outcome, sized so it is simultaneously large in absolute terms and modest as a percentage of the buyer's existing base.
**APPLY WHEN:** competing in a category where vendors look interchangeable and the decision is drifting toward brand safety or price.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot support the number — a fabricated projection is a liability once finance models it. Note the source's own case shows the competitor making this identical move and still losing, so the money number is table stakes, not the differentiator.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single reconstructed engagement told from the author's side.

### [FRAME:BIGCOSMALL] The "you'll be a small account there" reframe
**Move:** Concede up front that hiring the large incumbent is the safe, blameless, defensible choice — naming real firms, founding dates, headcounts and revenues to make the concession credible. Then reframe the consequence: at that scale the buyer's account is minor and gets staffed by juniors, whereas at your firm it gets senior people who chose this work and will stake their careers on it.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are the smaller challenger against a brand-safe incumbent and the decision is driven by career risk rather than outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot actually staff it senior — the claim is checkable and collapses on reference calls; when the buyer values the incumbent's scale or coverage for real operational reasons.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as a Flash Roll script delivered many times, still the author's own account, no outcome data.

### Stage: de-risking the offer (Plain Vanilla / New Normal)

### [FRAME:NOVCHUNK] Novelty Chunking
**Move:** List everything new, quirky or risky about your deal, find the single category they all belong to, and present that category as the one and only difference. Rationale offered: buyers count risks rather than weighing their size, so many small novelties score worse than one large one.
**APPLY WHEN:** the offer has many unfamiliar attributes and the buyer is conservative; big-ticket, career-risk decisions.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chunk hides material defects the buyer must know — in the source's own example the grouped items included weak-credit tenants, one-year leases, cash-only trade and no curb appeal, and the theme label suppressed disclosure; early adopters buying precisely for the novelty; regulated diligence where every item resurfaces anyway.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "my team and I have found," illustrated by one deal and one reconstruction.

### [FRAME:VANILLASCRIPT] Plain Vanilla script structure
**Move:** Sequence: (1) praise the incumbent sincerely and enumerate everything that stays identical, bundled into one "we're like everyone else, and that's fine" statement; (2) acknowledge they were not looking to replace anything; (3) introduce exactly one novelty chunk and make it the whole memory hook; (4) show why that difference is now widespread. In deal form, list the boring compliance facts first — insured, bonded, code-compliant, permits long-dated, standard by the numbers — and only then name the distinctive feature.
**APPLY WHEN:** displacing a deeply entrenched default; the category is mature and the buyer's risk posture conservative; novelty would frighten rather than attract.
**AVOID WHEN:** the incumbent is failing and the buyer wants a break, not continuity; the buyer is explicitly shopping for transformation; the single difference is not real; or your value genuinely is the aggregate of many differences and flattening them undersells it. Applied partially it fails — stacking several novelties overwhelms the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reconstructed scripts, one closed deal, plus an "all cars are good now, ours is tough" positioning whose category-convergence claims are asserted, not sourced.

### [FRAME:NEWNORMAL] Prove the new normal with three comparables
**Move:** Open by naming the trend, then physically drop the files of three or four comparable transactions that share your novelty chunk, with their prices, before describing your own deal. The comparables carry the claim that the difference is normal now.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer's objection is "too different" and real precedent transactions exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** your comparables are not comparable to a specialist who knows the market — a sophisticated buyer will name the difference; when no precedents exist at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting, self-reported outcome.

### [FRAME:PREEMPTFLAW] Surface the worst flaw before the buyer finds it
**Move:** Volunteer the most obvious defect of your offer early and matter-of-factly (in the Mahalo pitch, that HVAC would need replacing within five years), and acknowledge that failure is possible, rather than waiting for the buyer to raise it at the worst moment.
**APPLY WHEN:** the flaw is discoverable in diligence; the buyer is skeptical and searching for the catch.
**AVOID WHEN:** used selectively as a decoy — disclosing a small flaw to buy credibility for hiding a larger one is the misuse mode this creates; contexts where disclosure has legal weight and belongs with counsel, not a script.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal plus a general assertion that hidden negatives stall the sale.

### Stage: pessimism, objections and boundary-setting

### [FRAME:NONEEDY] Let the objection stand
**Move:** When the buyer raises a hard objection (including "this is professional suicide"), do not counter it. Acknowledge they are right that they do not need you, let them hold the negative thought, and keep only the invitation to look at what you have. Physically stop a teammate from arguing if necessary.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer has real alternatives and any pushback becomes an ego contest.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection is a factual error that will spread internally; procurement contexts where an unanswered objection becomes a scoring deduction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recruitment conversation.

### [FRAME:BUYERFORM] The Buyer's Formula as an invisible fence
**Move:** In place of asking for the sale, teach the buyer how to evaluate a deal in your category: (1) establish independent-expert status by volume of reps; (2) list the obvious ways buyers in their position fail; (3) list the counterintuitive failures they think they already understand, teaching at least one real insight; (4) list the obvious actions they should take and already half-know; (5) give the non-obvious hacks only repetition teaches, with named choices and budgets; (6) hand over autonomy with a detaching line — that is just what I would do, only you can decide — then stop talking. Five or six failure items before switching to positives is the stated dose. The ordered criteria should pre-block the failure modes you would lose to (e.g. buy from someone who has lived with the product; do not chase discount size for its own sake; pick someone you can argue with rather than someone you merely like; prefer domain love over category craft).
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer must feel the decision is theirs; you have genuine domain depth and can name specifics; the end of a competitive pitch where you would otherwise ask "any questions?"; the buyer has no clear decision criteria of their own.
**AVOID WHEN:** you lack real expertise — invented failure modes get caught; buyers who know the domain better than you; when credibility has not yet been established, so the lesson reads as condescension; when the criteria are transparently reverse-engineered from your own strengths and a buyer with a real scorecard sees the shape. It is a boundary-setting device, so using it to steer the buyer away from legitimate concerns (price, a second decision-maker) is where it turns deceptive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a fully worked bike-shop script, one recruitment case, and two deliveries in the Volka narrative; described as his standard method.

### [FRAME:BUYERASIDE] Narrate how you would evaluate the deal, as an aside
**Move:** While disengaging, describe casually how you personally decide whether to put money into this class of deal, including the tricky part you would scrutinize; frame it as routine helpfulness, not as an argument for buying.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is analytical and resists being told what to conclude; you have real repetitions in the category to speak from.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot survive a follow-up on the criteria you just named; the buyer has already committed and this reopens scrutiny.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as the sixth Inception tool, illustrated once.

### [FRAME:REDIRECT] Redirect out-of-bounds questions
**Move:** When the buyer raises a topic you already labeled a rookie mistake, hear the objection out fully, then redirect: that worry is common and never turns out to matter; there are many things to worry about here but only about five that decide the outcome — focus on those.
**APPLY WHEN:** the boundary-setting step has already been delivered and accepted.
**AVOID WHEN:** the out-of-bounds topic is the buyer's actual decision criterion — the source openly uses this against price comparisons and against absent co-deciders, which in a committee sale means suppressing the real evaluation; regulated procurement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/consulting · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — scripted example, no outcome data.

### [FRAME:SLOWFENCE] Present constraints as the standing norm
**Move:** Introduce limits the way safety instructions are given on an aircraft — as how it works, not as a request open to debate — and introduce changes gradually rather than announcing a new rule. Never state that a topic is off-limits; naming it guarantees fixation.
**APPLY WHEN:** you need scope, sequence or agenda control without visible control.
**AVOID WHEN:** the constraint is not actually normal in the industry and can be checked; buyers who will resent it retroactively when they notice; anything material to their decision rights.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — supported by a film plot and a loose appeal to attention research; see Neuromyth demotions.

### [FRAME:DISQUALIFY] Back away from buyers who reject expert status
**Move:** If the buyer will not accept your framing or evaluation criteria and insists on running their own process off internet research, treat that as a signal to disengage rather than a challenge to win.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have deal flow and can afford to walk; buyer behavior predicts high service cost and low margin.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot afford to lose the deal; the buyer's independence is competence rather than obstinacy — this rule conveniently classifies well-informed, price-comparing buyers as bad customers, which is a self-serving test.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — "I've seen this a thousand times," no cases given.

### [FRAME:NOSOFTCLOSE] Cut the soft trial close
**Move:** Delete "So what do you think? / Is this something you'd be interested in? / Any questions before we move forward?" from the call. The claim: a submissive temperature check does not measure interest, it manufactures objections and signals that discounts are available.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid- or late-call, when you would reflexively check temperature; when past deals died right after such a question.
**AVOID WHEN:** you genuinely need to surface hidden blockers before investing in a proposal, or the buyer's organization requires explicit consent to proceed — killing the question wholesale can leave you pitching into silent disqualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reasoned from the persona model, illustrated with one invented objection.

### [FRAME:HARDBUY] Raise desire by making a want-product harder to buy
**Move:** For products bought out of desire rather than need, stop smoothing the path: no unprompted discounts, no free upgrades, no eagerness to ship tomorrow. Make the buyer work toward the purchase while you keep feeding the desire.
**APPLY WHEN:** the product is discretionary or status-driven (collectible, exotic, luxury) and supply is genuinely limited.
**AVOID WHEN:** the product is a need-purchase or a commodity with substitutes a click away — friction there just routes the buyer to a competitor. Also fails as delivered: the author's own team could not act on the instruction until it was translated into concrete scripts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted on stage to one team; the reported revenue lift followed later scripting work, so attribution is unclear.

### [FRAME:ONEPER] Forbid the action you want taken
**Move:** Cap or restrict access explicitly (a stated limit per buyer) so the restriction itself creates the pull; used as the simplest illustration of buyer-generated desire.
**APPLY WHEN:** the offer is genuinely limited and the limit is verifiable.
**AVOID WHEN:** the scarcity is fabricated — the book itself lists "last one in stock" and "another buyer on the line" among the hated legacy pressure scripts; fake limits trigger the reactance they are meant to exploit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/info-product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — asserted as universal, no study or dataset named.

### Stage: close, handover and reading the room

### [FRAME:NOASK] Close without asking, hand the next step to the buyer
**Move:** Never instruct the buyer what to conclude and never ask for the commitment. Finish the case, stop talking, and if they signal interest, respond with an open question about the shape of the collaboration ("how do you see us working together on this?") rather than proposing a next step yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is a committee or senior principal who resents being sold to; you have already delivered proof, threat and reward.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional/high-volume motions with short attention windows, where absence of an ask kills the deal; junior buyers who need an explicit path; any process with a required procurement step.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two recounted closes (a committee vote, a $10M wire).

### [FRAME:ALLOWNO] Grant explicit permission to refuse, then require one of two answers
**Move:** Compress the ask into two named alternatives — tell me you do not want the deal and we part cleanly, or tell me you like it and we move — state that either is acceptable to you, then stop talking.
**APPLY WHEN:** time is nearly out; the buyer has been stalling or controlling the frame; you would otherwise get "send materials and we'll think about it."
**AVOID WHEN:** early discovery; the buyer needs internal socialization before any answer; a real committee cannot answer in the room. Reads as manipulation once recognized — single-use per relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Moscow $10M software deal.

### [FRAME:HOLDSILENCE] Let the silence be uncomfortable for the buyer
**Move:** After the two-option ask, do nothing — no clarifying, no reframing, no new data — even for minutes; let discomfort push the buyer to speak first and ask for something, which counts as the buyer chasing.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after ALLOWNO or any binary ask.
**AVOID WHEN:** remote or phone calls where silence reads as a dropped connection; rooms where the counterpart is content to end the meeting in silence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the same Moscow scene, three minutes of silence.

### [FRAME:EXITSIGNAL] Leave instead of asking for the yes
**Move:** When the buyer shows the first sign of pulling toward you, visibly prepare to depart (booking a ride, naming the next meeting) rather than escalating to a close; let the buyer be the one to say they want to proceed.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer has just re-engaged after silence; you have another credible commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** the interest signal is weak or merely polite; when the exit is transparently theatrical — repeat buyers recognize it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own account of the close.

### [FRAME:DEPARTBY] Anchor the decision to your departure, flatly
**Move:** When the buyer proposes to think it over and come back, state your departure date and that you need the answer by then — delivered as logistics, without pressure language or consequences.
**APPLY WHEN:** travel-anchored meetings where the constraint is real.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deadline is invented — a fake constraint is the same artificial-urgency script the book condemns elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — answer reported the following morning.

### [FRAME:DEATHRATTLE] Read the three-beat kill sequence as a lost deal, not a warm one
**Move:** Treat the sequence — buyer praises the product, asks for more time with the numbers, then defers to an absent partner or committee — as one signal that certainty was never built and the deal is over; stop advancing and rebuild status and certainty instead of sending the requested proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** end of a pitch; the buyer is agreeable but keeps adding intermediaries or documents.
**AVOID WHEN:** procurement genuinely requires written proposals on a documented cycle; a real committee sign-off exists and the absent partner is verifiable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a recurring pattern the author reports from his own deals; no counts.

### [FRAME:LOCALSTAKE] Repair a "what's in it for our side" objection by re-specifying use of funds locally
**Move:** When the buyer objects that the value leaves their territory or division, immediately restate the plan with concrete local commitments — a local office, named local hires, a partnership with a local institution, board meetings on their premises — framed as the best part of the plan rather than a concession.
**APPLY WHEN:** cross-border or cross-division investment; the objection is about benefit distribution, not economics.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot actually deliver the local commitments; improvising them creates promises the deal must later honor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=investor/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — improvised recovery in the Geneva meeting after the founder's misstep.

### [FRAME:HALFHALF] Split payment so both sides can damage each other
**Move:** Structure payment as half in advance and half on delivery so each party holds leverage over the other; present it as the normal, boring arrangement rather than as protection you demanded.
**APPLY WHEN:** service or consulting engagements with future delivery; buyer anxiety is about being taken advantage of, not about price.
**AVOID WHEN:** procurement mandates net terms or milestone schedules; when the deliverable is continuous rather than discrete.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the trillion-dollar annual volume attributed to this structure is given with no source.

### [FRAME:SEQUENCE] Run the five steps in order and hand over control last
**Move:** Sequence the meeting deliberately — in-group alignment first, technical authority second, the three buyer questions third, risk-reducing positioning fourth, buying criteria fifth — and only then relinquish the decision without pressure, discounting or cornering.
**APPLY WHEN:** a single high-stakes meeting where you will not get many turns; investor pitches, competitive finals, board-level presentations.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deal is multi-threaded over months with different audiences per session — the sequence is built for one room, one sitting. Inapplicable where a scored RFP drives procurement and rapport has no channel.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "taught to thousands" with no measurement; the sequence rests on two narratives.

## Where this book stops working

- **Volume and transactional motion.** The whole apparatus assumes a low-frequency, high-consequence decision with a decision maker who can say yes in the room. In micro/low-ACV inbound selling with short attention windows, removing the ask (NOASK) and the temperature check (NOSOFTCLOSE) leaves nothing to convert; the only moves that survive intact are the floor-level ones (CALLAUDIT, OWNSCRIPT, PERSONAAUDIT, HARDBUY, ONEPER).
- **Real multi-stakeholder committees with a process.** The book systematically treats "I need to check with my partner" as an invented exit. REDIRECT and BUYERFORM are explicitly aimed at price comparison and absent co-deciders — in an enterprise deal with a scorecard, a security review and a champion who must sell internally, that is suppressing the real evaluation, and it loses deals rather than winning them.
- **Regulated and procurement-driven buying.** DOOMCONTEXT/WINTER read as FUD; SLOWFENCE constraints get checked against the RFP; PREEMPTFLAW has legal weight and belongs with counsel; DEPARTBY collides with a documented cycle. Scored RFPs give rapport and status no channel at all (SEQUENCE).
- **Repeat contact and long cycles.** TIPOFF, TIPOFFSTORY, FLASHROLL, FLASHTHEIRS, ALLOWNO, EXITSIGNAL, REOPEN, PARTNERSKEPTIC and CURIOSITY are one-shot: they depend on the counterpart not having seen them before. Land-and-expand, renewals and account management are outside the book's range — this is the same limitation as the author's earlier work.
- **Buyers deeper than you.** Every Flash Roll variant only reads as mastery to someone less prepared. A technically superior audience hears a recitation, and the format cannot survive follow-up.
- **Employee sellers of large vendors.** SKIN, STICKGUNS and DISQUALIFY assume principal-level discretion — personal exposure, the right to refuse money, the deal flow to walk away. A quota-carrying rep has none of these.
- **Cultures and rooms where the status play backfires.** NOCONFUSE, HOSTAUDIT and POINTCALL humiliate rather than align where face-loss is costly; STATUSALIGN is unrecoverable if the shared frame is faked; a half-executed alignment attempt is worse than none.

## What the book debunks

- **The legacy sequence** — rapport → features and benefits → trial close → fight objections — is discarded wholesale, along with the pressure scripts built on artificial scarcity and discount urgency, on grounds of psychological reactance: buyers do the opposite to prove they are not being steered. The author claims car manufacturers' own studies found high-pressure scripts net-harmful, and points at the industry's move to published pricing; no study is named or citable.
- **Icebreaker rapport.** Shared surface interests (hometown, sports team, same T-shirt, same travel spot) produce warmth but not the alignment that moves a deal. Status mis-alignment in either direction — too low or too high — ends in being handled like a customer-service agent. Bad rank matchups between teams are a predictable deal-killer.
- **Answering technical interrogation to build certainty** is a losing move with sophisticated buyers; rapid-fire questions signal the certainty gap widening, not interest. Related documented backfire: volunteering the wrong operational detail (where the money physically goes) reopened the whole gap in one sentence in the author's own Geneva meeting.
- **The novelty pitch** — "we're new, different, disruptive," innovation awards, "the Uber of X" — wins meetings and loses decisions. Early/late-adopter segmentation stops predicting behavior once stakes rise: the larger the decision, the more conservative the criteria. Improvements below roughly 2X do not motivate switching, so "better product" claims fail on their own.
- **The analyst-facing entrepreneur package** — charismatic story, up-and-to-the-right charts, projections labeled "conservative" — invites dismantling.
- **Optimism plus objection-crushing.** Seller optimism creates buyer stress and violates autonomy; objection-crushing turns a sale into a debate that buyers concede to end, then abandon later. It "works" psychologically for the seller because it preserves the sensation of having a shot.
- **The standard closing questions** — "what do you think?", "any questions?", "does this make sense?", "is this something you'd be interested in?" — hand control to the buyer, manufacture objections, signal that discounts exist, and produce the send-it-by-email stall.
- **Charisma as looks + brains + energy.** The author reports testing appearance, intelligence and enthusiasm and rejecting all three; enthusiasm is singled out as actively repelling buyers, and intelligence is dismissed via Harry Markopolos, who documented Madoff's fraud years early and could not get anyone to act.
- **Modeling the natural top performer.** Reps imitating a star produce a persona buyers see through. Generic self-confidence and "win friends" seminar material are demoted as insufficient; the replacement is fixed visible values plus expert positioning.
- **Its own opening move.** An abstract principle ("make it harder to buy") delivered to a non-abstract audience produced blank stares and zero adoption until it was rewritten into concrete scripts — the most transferable lesson in the book, and it outranks any individual script.
- **Self-flagged failure modes.** Teaching the buyer what to question can be perceived as telling them what to think, and once perceived the buyer withdraws — the technique depends on remaining invisible, which is also its ethical problem. Novelty Chunking is a disclosure-shaping technique: the same mechanics that soothe a buyer can bury a defect. PREEMPTFLAW can be used as a decoy — a small disclosure buying credibility for a larger concealment. The Flash Roll's stated design goal is credibility, not comprehension, and the author concedes he did not fully understand a script he wrote for a client and that the executives might not parse it either; that is precisely the "repackaged persuasion trick" objection reviewers raise, and it is a genuine ethical edge — the technique manufactures certainty independent of the buyer's understanding.

## Neuromyth demotions

- **"Wired by evolution to distrust outside information and favor what originates inside us."** Evolutionary just-so story, not a finding. The observable effect — people overvalue and defend self-generated ideas — is real and studied (endowment/IKEA-effect and self-generation research), but the causal origin story adds nothing checkable. Keep the effect, drop the story.
- **"A spike in the superior temporal gyrus produces the feeling of certainty when an idea is our own; re-create that sequence and you trigger Inception."** Overreach. Insight-related right anterior temporal activity is reported in the insight literature (Kounios & Beeman line of work), but no published method targets that region through conversation, and nothing cited links it to buyer certainty. Decorative garnish on a rhetorical technique.
- **"Idea receptors" — including "pre-wired at birth: threats, rewards, fairness," sourced to prominent psychologists.** Unnamed sources, no studies; across all three chunks this is the book's own packaging metaphor with no neural referent. Threat salience, reward sensitivity and fairness concerns are genuine research areas, but no discrete receptor exists for social concepts, and the three-item hierarchy is the author's. Keep "Pre-Wired Ideas" as the framework's name only; the usable residue is ordinary — familiar, well-structured narratives are easier to process and recall.
- **"Your brain built an idea receptor" for stock characters.** Stereotype/schema effects on rapid judgment are real behaviorally; the hardware story is invented. Demote to a behavioral heuristic.
- **"Slot machines, drugs, pornography and sugar hijack pre-existing circuitry, and the same route works in a pitch."** The addiction analogy is rhetorical; equating a sales opener with reinforcement-schedule stimuli is unsupported and, taken literally, is the ethically loaded part of the chapter.
- **Cosmides and Tooby's cheater-detection research, cited as confirming a pre-wired fairness system.** The authors and the selection-task work are real, but the strong modular interpretation is contested, and the tactic (show real exposure) needs no evolutionary claim at all.
- **Berlyne's exploratory-behavior work and the novelty inverted-U.** Real mid-century research, largely on rodents; extrapolating a small animal's approach-avoidance cycle to a real-estate investment committee is the author's leap, unsupported here. Related: **"preference for novelty is wired deep into our brains / imprinted on our DNA over millions of years"** — untestable adaptationist storytelling; demote to assertion.
- **"That hit the fear center."** There is no single fear center; read as ordinary alarm, not neuroanatomy.
- **Change blindness / choice detection / the flicker paradigm**, invoked to claim that slowly imposed rules go unnoticed. These are real visual-attention phenomena, but nothing in that literature licenses the claim that negotiation boundaries can be installed undetected (SLOWFENCE). Demote to assertion.
- **Evolutionary framing in the status chapters** — "a quarter of all human males killed every year," status determined purely by physical dominance for a million years, a new hierarchy appearing "around the first millennium." Unsourced and inconsistent with paleodemographic estimates. The Ötzi passage additionally misstates the specimen's age by more than an order of magnitude (70,000 years claimed vs. roughly 5,300 actual). Preserve the operational point — people read status from small verbal and visual cues — and discard the prehistory.
- **"Programmed to respond to the immediate demands of any social situation and modify behavior accordingly,"** attributed vaguely to unnamed cognitive psychologists on the author's payroll. Situational behavioral adaptation is real and well studied; the claim that it produces five fixed sales personas in a fixed order is the author's typology, not a research finding.
- **"Built to seek out relationships that offer consistency in character."** Overstated folk-evolutionary framing; treat as a plausible social-psychology intuition. The practical guidance — do not switch personas mid-call — survives without it.
- **"The less cognitive strain you place on the buyer, the better your chances."** Directionally supported by processing-fluency literature outside the book, but the book cites nothing; remains `assertion` as delivered.
- **Pitch rhetoric about humans living far past what nature "planned"** (life expectancy 84 vs a supposed natural 32) — a persuasion line from a script, not a research claim; the accompanying 350-million-person population framing is loose. Likewise **"you lose eighty percent of your body heat through your head,"** stated in-narrative and uncontradicted — a well-known myth, listed for completeness.
- **"Buyers pull away under pressure; psychological reactance."** This one survives demotion as a construct: reactance is an established social-psychology finding (Brehm), though the book cites it loosely and attributes supporting studies to unnamed car manufacturers. Use the construct, not the claimed evidence.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No base rates, no failure cases, no controls.** Every move is single-source and self-reported, several narratives are fictionalized, and the outcomes are told exclusively from the author's side. The Volka recovery is one recounted win with no reported failures of the same sequence, and its decisive moment — the CEO deciding within a second, before rebuttal — is not explained by any of the moves. Survivorship framing throughout.
- **Unfalsifiable mechanism claims.** The Volka narrative shows the losing side making the correct textbook move (a big, well-supported money number with ROI and CAC metrics) and losing anyway, with the difference attributed to status and cultural fit — which cannot be tested as told.
- **Recognition and repeat play are unmodeled.** The book never asks what happens on the second encounter, in a renewal, or when a buyer has read it. Its most distinctive moves are one-shot by construction, and it offers no guidance for the recognized case.
- **No account of the buyer's internal process.** Champions, procurement, security review, legal, budget cycles and multi-session deals barely exist; the model is one room, one sitting, one decider. Committees appear as an audience to be moved, never as a process to be navigated.
- **No post-sale layer.** Onboarding, delivery, expansion and churn are absent — which matters because several moves (SKIN's commitments, LOCALSTAKE's local promises, BIGCOSMALL's senior staffing) create obligations the deal must later honor.
- **Ethics are treated as an aside, not a constraint.** The techniques are described as working best when invisible, and the book flags the tension without resolving it: manufactured certainty independent of understanding, disclosure-shaping via Novelty Chunking, boundary-setting used to route the buyer away from price and co-deciders. The line between helping a buyer decide and preventing them from evaluating is never drawn.
- **Persuasion research is invoked, not engaged.** Neuroscience garnish substitutes for citation, and the genuine constructs the book leans on (reactance, ownership/endowment effects, processing fluency, schema-driven judgment) are used loosely enough that the reader cannot tell where the evidence ends — which is the substance behind the "repackaged persuasion material without attribution" complaint in reviews.
- **Seller-side skill and status are assumed.** Real domain depth, principal-level discretion, deal flow to walk away, and a bench to staff are prerequisites for most of the stack; the book offers little to a seller who lacks them beyond "write your own script."
