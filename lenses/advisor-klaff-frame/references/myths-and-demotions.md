# advisor-klaff-frame — myths and demotions

What this tradition debunks, what must be debunked *inside* it, and what must never leave this file as fact. Sources: *Pitch Anything* (2011), *Flip the Script* (2019), both Klaff.

This is the only file in the lens where the refuted neuro-vocabulary of the books may appear at all — and only as the object of refutation, never as explanation.

---

## Neuromyth demotions

The explanatory apparatus of both books is discarded wholesale. The behavioural moves in `moves.md` stand or fall on their own evidence, which is anecdotal throughout. Nothing in this section may be transmitted as fact, as rationale, or as a reason a move works.

### The central architecture claim

- **The three-stage stacked-brain model as the architecture of persuasion** — a primitive survival structure filtering all input before reasoning, so that a message composed in the "neocortex" is received by a "croc brain". **REFUTED as neuroanatomy** by peer-reviewed work (Cesario et al. 2020; Steffen et al. 2022): human brains did not evolve in three stacked layers, and decision-making is distributed rather than tiered. Refuted equally in its derived forms — the claimed multi-million-year evolutionary gap between layers; the "two audiences inside one buyer" picture; the claim that reasoning can be bypassed or short-circuited; and "every crocodile brain responds identically everywhere" (ignore boring, flee danger, truncate complexity), whose apparent regularities are ordinary attention and summarisation effects. **Permitted reframe:** fast affective reaction versus deliberate analysis, with no anatomical claim attached. The terms *croc brain / crocodile brain / reptilian brain / lizard brain / triune brain / limbic / amygdala hijack / ancient brain / primitive brain / old brain* and their paraphrases must never appear in advice.
- **"Frames are rooted in survival and seek dominance"** — ASSERTION built on the refuted layer. Frame competition may be described as an observable social phenomenon; its evolutionary explanation must not be.
- **The "energy field" analogy for frames** — flagged by the author himself as an analogy; harmless as metaphor, never a mechanism.

### Fabricated, unsourced or misapplied mechanisms

- **"~90% of a message is discarded by an early filter"** — UNSOURCED; a self-reported figure dressed as a neuroscience finding.
- **"A neurotoxin is attached to a complex message and routed to the amygdala for destruction"** — FABRICATED; metaphor presented as physiology.
- **"Neurofinance" as the author's field, with claimed professor interviews and executive experiments** — MARKETING TERM / UNVERIFIABLE; no data, protocol or publication given.
- **Hot vs cold cognition as mutually exclusive brain states** — OVERSTATED. Affect/analysis interaction is real work; the absolute impossibility claim is unsourced. Surviving heuristic: do not invite the room to start calculating mid-pitch ([FRAME:HIGHLEVEL-REDIRECT], [FRAME:DETAIL-GATE]).
- **Attention as a two-chemical cocktail that "adds up", tunable by giving rewards and taking things away** — OVERSIMPLIFIED to the point of being wrong. Keep only the behavioural pattern: interest plus stakes holds attention ([FRAME:PING], [FRAME:TAKEBACK]).
- **Dopamine as the novelty chemical acting in ~1/20 second, "poured in" by status objects; norepinephrine "injected" by tension language; a dopamine flood explaining why irresistible products need no pitch** — OVERSTATED popular-science shorthand, unsourced and unmeasured. Keep only the honest concession that a strongly differentiated product needs less framing.
- **Haynes's button-press study read as "decisions are made ~7 seconds before awareness, therefore buyers cannot reason"** — MISAPPLIED; the study concerned trivial motor choices, not commercial judgment.
- **Change blindness / the flicker paradigm**, used in 2011 to argue a before/after contrast is literally invisible, and in 2019 to argue that gradually imposed rules go unnoticed — MISAPPLIED in both. These are visual-attention phenomena; nothing in that literature licenses either claim. [FRAME:MOVE-NOT-STATES] and [FRAME:SLOWFENCE] survive as rhetorical tactics, demoted to assertion.
- **"Social threats engage the same threat system as physical threats"** — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED in the original literature but contested and far weaker than the books' use; demote to unverified.
- **"Impressions are averaged, not summed"** — plausible in the impression-formation literature, cited without a source; unverified as used in [FRAME:TRACKRECORD-AVG].
- **"22 times as much information in the affective part of a message" (attributed to Zajonc)** — UNVERIFIED figure with no retrievable derivation.
- **"Heightened emotion creates strong memories"** — DIRECTIONALLY SUPPORTED in the wider literature, but the book cites nothing and leans on flashbulb-memory folklore, itself less reliable than popularly assumed. Never cite these books as evidence for it.
- **"Reducing the autonomy of choice triggers a threat response"** — labelled by the author himself as one of his guiding theories, i.e. ASSERTION. Reactance exists independently and is not what the book cites.
- **"The brain is wired to do things for status, not money"** — ASSERTION, no source; usable only as a rhetorical heuristic about buyer motives ([FRAME:LEGACY]).
- **"The mind is not a camera but a machine for observing motion and predicting what comes next"** — ASSERTION dressed as neuroscience; [FRAME:WHYNOW-3F] may still work rhetorically without it.
- **Theory of mind invoked as licence to retune messages** — the concept is real; the books' use is a metaphor for audience adaptation, not a finding.
- **"Wired by evolution to distrust outside information and favour what originates inside us"** — just-so story. The observable effect (people overvalue self-generated ideas) is real and studied; the origin story adds nothing checkable. Keep the effect, drop the story.
- **"A spike in the superior temporal gyrus produces the feeling of certainty when an idea is our own; re-create it and you trigger Inception"** — OVERREACH. Insight-related temporal activity appears in the insight literature, but no published method targets that region through conversation and nothing links it to buyer certainty. Decorative garnish on [FRAME:BUYERFORM].
- **"Idea receptors", including "pre-wired at birth: threats, rewards, fairness"** — unnamed sources, no studies; a packaging metaphor with no neural referent. Threat salience, reward sensitivity and fairness concerns are genuine research areas, but no discrete receptor exists for social concepts and the three-item hierarchy is the author's. Keep "Pre-Wired Ideas" as a framework *name* only ([FRAME:BIGTHREE]); the usable residue is ordinary — familiar, well-structured narratives are easier to process and recall.
- **"Your brain built an idea receptor for stock characters"** — the hardware story is invented; demote [FRAME:ARCHETYPE] to a behavioural heuristic.
- **"Slot machines, drugs, pornography and sugar hijack pre-existing circuitry, and the same route works in a pitch"** — rhetorical analogy; equating a sales opener with reinforcement-schedule stimuli is unsupported and, taken literally, is the ethically loaded part of that chapter.
- **Cosmides and Tooby's cheater-detection work cited as confirming a pre-wired fairness system** — the authors and selection-task work are real, the strong modular interpretation is contested, and [FRAME:SKIN] needs no evolutionary claim at all.
- **Berlyne's exploratory-behaviour work and the novelty inverted-U** — real mid-century research, largely on rodents; extrapolating a small animal's approach-avoidance cycle to an investment committee is the author's leap. Related: **"preference for novelty is imprinted on our DNA over millions of years"** — untestable adaptationist storytelling; demote to assertion.
- **"That hit the fear center"** — there is no single fear centre; read as ordinary alarm, not neuroanatomy.
- **Evolutionary framing in the status chapters** — "a quarter of all human males killed every year", status determined purely by physical dominance for a million years, a new hierarchy appearing around the first millennium. Unsourced and inconsistent with paleodemographic estimates; the Ötzi passage misstates the specimen's age by more than an order of magnitude (roughly 70,000 years claimed against roughly 5,300 actual). Preserve only the operational point — people read status from small verbal and visual cues — and discard the prehistory.
- **"Programmed to respond to the immediate demands of any social situation"**, attributed vaguely to unnamed cognitive psychologists on the author's payroll. Situational behavioural adaptation is real; the claim that it produces five fixed sales personas in a fixed order is the author's typology, not a research finding ([FRAME:PERSONAAUDIT]).
- **"Built to seek out relationships that offer consistency in character"** — overstated folk-evolutionary framing; the practical guidance (do not switch personas mid-call) survives without it.
- **"The less cognitive strain you place on the buyer, the better your chances"** — directionally supported by processing-fluency work outside the book, which cites nothing; remains `assertion` as delivered.
- **Pitch rhetoric about humans living far past what nature "planned"** (life expectancy 84 against a supposed natural 32, with loose population framing) — a persuasion line from a script, not a research claim. Likewise **"you lose eighty percent of your body heat through your head"**, stated in-narrative and uncontradicted — a well-known myth, listed for completeness.
- **"Buyers pull away under pressure; psychological reactance"** — this one SURVIVES demotion as a construct: reactance is an established finding (Brehm), though the books cite it loosely and attribute supporting studies to unnamed manufacturers. Use the construct, never the claimed evidence.

**Quarantined numbers — never re-transmit as fact:** ~90% of a message discarded before higher processing; dopamine acting in ~1/20 second; the 22× affective-information figure attributed to Zajonc; the ~7-second pre-awareness prediction; "100 years" of time-pressure research; the unnamed jaywalking-conformity study; the claimed proportion of global private money held in ~350 family offices; at least $1T of annual US deals attributed to the half-and-half payment split; a sub-5-second voice-identification claim against 5m17s of caller questioning.

## What this tradition debunks in others (usable, but note the evidence)

- **The legacy sequence** — rapport → features and benefits → trial close → fight objections — discarded wholesale, along with the pressure scripts built on artificial scarcity and discount urgency. Grounds: reactance. The claim that manufacturers' own studies found high-pressure scripts net-harmful names no study.
- **Icebreaker rapport.** Shared surface interests produce warmth without rank alignment. Note the counter-position: this is the tradition's most contested claim and runs against mainstream trust-building research.
- **Trial closes and the standard closing questions** — "what do you think?", "any questions?", "does this make sense?" — read as handing control to the buyer, manufacturing objections and signalling that discounts exist.
- **Answering technical interrogation to build certainty.** Rapid-fire questions are read as a widening certainty gap, not as interest.
- **The novelty pitch** — new, different, disruptive, "the Uber of X" — wins meetings and loses decisions; the larger the decision, the more conservative the criteria. Improvements below roughly 2X are claimed not to motivate switching (self-reported; an 80% improvement is called insufficient and a 35% one unsellable).
- **The law of large numbers school of selling** (100 calls → 2 sales) — read as hiding a weak social position behind volume.
- **"Never take no for an answer"** and pressure closing — badgering occasionally converts and reliably produces buyer's remorse; pressure closing is reframed as a *symptom* of an already lost position.
- **Framing money as the prize** — named as a common and often fatal error.
- **"Charisma without conflict"** — an entirely pleasant meeting is named as the author's most common cause of failed pitches.
- **Long biographies** — more background is claimed to make you look more average, not more credible.
- **"More information wins pitches"** and "a better way of organising information wins deals" — the problem is selection, not accumulation.
- **"The biggest, most credentialed team wins by default"** — supported by exactly one counter-example, with the concession that the larger team could plausibly have won.
- **Charisma as looks + brains + energy**, and **modelling the natural top performer** — enthusiasm is singled out as actively repelling buyers; imitating a star produces a persona buyers see through.
- **The rational decision picture** (identify problem → evaluate options → decide) — argued to be reversed in practice, with data serving post-hoc justification. Asserted, not demonstrated.
- **The comforting post-mortem "they didn't get it"** — the failure is placed in your own signalling.

## Documented failure modes of these techniques

Named by the books against themselves, and binding on this lens:

- **Abusing control once you have it**, and the humourless version of the method getting you escorted out of the building.
- **Too many frames at once**, and practising on high-risk targets.
- **Coming on too strong triggers defensiveness** — the only stop rule offered for [FRAME:PLOW] and the status sequence, and it is vague.
- **Time pressure reduces decision quality** — the 2011 book's own concession, an argument against [FRAME:TIME-TRAIN] and [FRAME:STACK-4].
- **The strongest self-contained failure evidence in the corpus:** the four-frame stack produced a confident yes in the author himself on a transaction that left a competitor down 15% two years later. The method drove a bad decision, in the author's own account.
- **Novelty Chunking is a disclosure-shaping technique.** In the source's own example the grouped items included weak-credit tenants, one-year leases, cash-only trade and no curb appeal, and the theme label suppressed disclosure.
- **[FRAME:PREEMPTFLAW] can be used as a decoy** — a small disclosure buying credibility for a larger concealment.
- **The Flash Roll's stated design goal is credibility, not comprehension.** The author concedes he did not fully understand a script he wrote for a client and that the executives might not parse it either. This manufactures certainty independent of the buyer's understanding — the technique's genuine ethical edge, not a stylistic quibble.
- **[FRAME:BUYERFORM] depends on remaining invisible.** Once the buyer perceives they are being taught what to question, they withdraw. Invisibility as an operating requirement is also the ethical problem.
- **[FRAME:REDIRECT] and [FRAME:BUYERFORM] are aimed at price comparison and absent co-deciders.** In a committee sale with a scorecard, a security review and a champion who must sell internally, that is suppressing the real evaluation, and it loses deals rather than winning them.
- **Its own opening move failed.** An abstract principle ("make it harder to buy") delivered to a non-abstract audience produced blank stares and zero adoption until rewritten into concrete scripts — the most transferable lesson in the 2019 book, outranking any individual script in it.

## Self-reported numbers that must not be quoted as fact

All figures in both books are the author's own, unaudited, with no comparison case. They may be cited as *claims about one operator's practice*, never as evidence a technique works.

- **Capital raised and deal sizes** — ~$2M/week; a holding company reported from $100M to ~$400M in about four years; career totals above $400M raised; $250M AUM in a publisher bio; a $1B raise mandate with ~$25M in fees; a raise at a $14M valuation; $10M software and $10M Geneva deals; a $42M purchase against a $50M target resale.
- **Outcome claims with no baseline or control** — "sales more than doubled the following quarter and doubled again the quarter after" on a ~$75K/week floor; a claimed quarterly revenue doubling after a concessions policy; over $5M closed with one group over two years after one conference episode.
- **Volume claims** — one banker at 600–800 pitches/year and 10,000 deals seen; "thousands of people" trained; *Pitch Anything* at 1,000,000+ copies.
- **Timing figures presented as craft constants** — the ~20-minute pitch and its 5/10/2/3 split; the 60–90 second, ~250-word Flash Roll at double speaking pace; the ~30-second status alignment; the three-sentence Tip-Off; the ~90-word pre-stress statement. These are one operator's habits, not measured optima.
- **Switching thresholds** — the 2X trigger, the "80% is insufficient" and "35% is unsellable" claims: all self-reported observation, no dataset.
- **Third-hand or decorative figures** inside illustrations (market sizes, product specs, corporate write-offs, historical counts) — used in the books to make a point vivid, not to support the technique. Never carry them forward as facts about a market.

**Standing rule for this lens:** when a move's justification is a number from these books, the number is evidence about the author's narration, not about the world. Say so, or leave it out.
