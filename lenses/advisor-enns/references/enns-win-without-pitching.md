# The Win Without Pitching Manifesto — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens's mechanism for **power in the sale**: the buyer's leverage is a function of available substitutes, so the seller's only real lever is being non-substitutable (narrow expertise, provable) and willing to walk. Everything else — no presentations, no free thinking, no written proposals, minimum fee floors — is downstream plumbing of that one idea.
Where it beats other sources: it treats the *sales process itself* as the thing to be redesigned rather than optimized, and it gives concrete refusal scripts for the expert-services deal (agency, consultancy, studio, solo practitioner) where volume playbooks do not apply.
It is weakest exactly where it is loudest: absolutes are the author's stance, not measured outcomes, and the whole system presumes an expert who already has proof to point at.

## Core model

Twelve proclamations, four load-bearing blocks:

1. **Positioning (I, VII)** — three steps: choose a focus ("What business are we in?" — *The Difficult Business Decision*), compress it into a public claim of expertise, then build the skills, documented processes and written thought leadership that make the claim provable. Claim without proof pushes you back to pitching, because free problem-solving becomes your only remaining evidence.
2. **Conversations, not presentations (II)** — presenting casts the buyer as judge and the seller as auditioning talent; conversation makes both parties weigh fit. Two fixed anchors per interaction: *mission* = be positioned as the expert practitioner; *objective* = determine whether fit warrants a next step. Convincing is out of scope.
3. **Diagnose before prescribe (III, IV, V)** — engagements run diagnose → prescribe → apply → reapply. Competitive pitches structurally destroy the diagnostic phase. Selling is facilitation of change: the buyer moves unaware → interested → intent, and the seller shifts help → inspire → reassure. The costliest recurring error is mistaking interest for intent. Proposals are spoken; the document is the contract that follows agreement.
4. **Selectivity and money (VI, VIII–XII)** — few high-quality clients turning over slowly; churn is normal, business development is managing that turnover upward. Four money rules stack: do not solve problems before being paid, raise money early (Minimum Level of Engagement, deposit), refuse unprofitable work, charge more. Positioning decays advisor → partner → supplier, so refusal is the only lever the seller permanently owns.

## Moves

### Stage — Positioning and demand creation (before any opportunity)

### [WWP:FOCUS3] Three-step positioning sequence
**Move:** Pick one focus and close the door on the alternatives; compress it into a one-or-two-sentence public claim of who you help and how; then deliberately acquire the skills, capabilities and processes that make the claim defensible.
**APPLY WHEN:** firm is undifferentiated, RFPs and free pitches dominate the pipeline, price pressure is chronic.
**AVOID WHEN:** the firm has no delivery history at all in the chosen field (nothing to substantiate the claim yet — see WWP:PROBONO), or a single dominant client currently funds everything and the focus would strand it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from first principles about substitutes and power; no dataset or named study.

### [WWP:NARROWCLAIM] Claim narrower than capability
**Move:** State expertise deliberately narrower than the full range of what you can do; let the narrow claim earn the assumption of surrounding competence. Aim at the small target and accept the wider market that comes with it.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing website/positioning copy; first contact with a prospect who is self-selecting.
**AVOID WHEN:** the narrow claim would exclude the only segment currently able to pay you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — claim about buyer inference from breadth; no supporting data.

### [WWP:PRICETEST] Positioning test — win more AND charge more
**Move:** Judge your positioning by two things measured together: do you win more often than not where you choose to compete, and do you win while charging a premium rather than by cutting price. Failing the second means you are either outside your focus or never made the focus decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** periodic review of whether specialization is real; after a run of won deals, to check how they were won.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is one or two deals, or wins came from an existing-client relationship rather than competitive selection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning from price elasticity and substitutes, stated without measurement.

### [WWP:WRITEDEEP] Write long-form on the focus area
**Move:** Publish substantive writing on the narrow territory — not restatements of the field's clichés. It does three jobs at once: gets you found, proves expertise, and deepens your own understanding by forcing the arguments to tighten.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect is unaware of a problem; the pipeline needs a source that does not depend on chasing meetings.
**AVOID WHEN:** you expect near-term deals from it — this is a farmer's timeline, not a hunter's.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a rule ("experts write") with no measured outcomes.

### [WWP:PROCESSDOC] Document and defend your methodology
**Move:** Write down how you work, keep refining it, and use it late in the buying cycle as the reassurance asset: low variability in process implies low variability in outcome, which answers the buyer's real closing question — how do I know I get their best work?
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage buyer asking detailed, small, sequential questions; buyer trying to impose his own process.
**AVOID WHEN:** the process is invented for the sale and cannot survive being examined.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — drawn from the author's advisory work with creative firms; no external validation.

### [WWP:LEARNCULT] Mandatory continuous-learning culture
**Move:** Hire for skill, train on the documented methods, have each person write a professional development plan you approve and fund, hold them to it, and set the pace yourself. Move out those who will not keep up.
**APPLY WHEN:** after the firm commits to a focus and needs the claim substantiated quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo practice, or no defined processes yet — there is nothing to train on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — normative prescription about firm culture.

### Stage — First contact, qualification, triage

### [WWP:MISSIONOBJ] Mission position / objective fit
**Move:** Enter every interaction with two fixed intents: be seen as the expert practitioner (never trade this for a short-term gain), and establish only whether the fit between need and expertise justifies a next step. Not to sell, convince or persuade.
**APPLY WHEN:** every buying-cycle touch; useful as a pre-call checklist.
**AVOID WHEN:** cash pressure makes short-term revenue the actual goal — then the model is being used dishonestly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — doctrinal statement of purpose.

### [WWP:3MODES] Match the mode to buying stage
**Move:** Diagnose which of three states the buyer is in and act accordingly: help the unaware (education, no push), inspire the interested (show what is possible), reassure those who have formed intent (calm, sequence, detail).
**APPLY WHEN:** every opportunity, from first contact onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot yet read the stage — misreading it produces the failure modes listed below.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the model is the author's construction, presented without evidence.

### [WWP:PURSUENO] Try to kill the opportunity early
**Move:** When an opportunity appears, actively test whether it should die — put money, the nature of the need, the client's willingness to be led, geography and depth of your experience on the table straight away. Retreat slightly and see whether the buyer follows; his following is the measure of how much he values your expertise.
**APPLY WHEN:** new opportunity, before any real resource is committed.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as theatre by a firm with no differentiated expertise — the retreat is then simply accepted and the deal is gone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the retreat-and-follow dynamic is asserted with a Confucius aphorism, not evidence.

### [WWP:PREEMPTOBJ] Raise the objection before the buyer does
**Move:** Put the likely objection on the table yourself so the other party carries the burden of answering it — for example, voicing concern about whether an organization of that size can afford you, instead of waiting to be told you look expensive. If an objection is going to kill the deal, kill it early.
**APPLY WHEN:** early discovery, especially around budget, fit and control.
**AVOID WHEN:** deployed as manipulation to trigger a defensive "of course we can afford you" — the book frames it as honest early filtering, and the tactic's power over a buyer's self-image is exactly why it can be misused.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — describes an objection dynamic, with a sample line, no supporting data.

### [WWP:MLE] Minimum Level of Engagement
**Move:** Set an annual fee minimum per client and state it in the first discussion of any new opportunity: you take on few new clients a year, so a new client needs to be at or above this level over a year. Ask for readiness to commit, not an iron-clad promise.
**APPLY WHEN:** soon after a need is identified with a new prospect; also used to escalate a small tactical request into a strategic conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied rigidly — the book keeps the right to waive it; and waiving it silently, without ever stating it, forfeits the whole mechanism.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the ~10% sizing rule is given without derivation.

### [WWP:MLEWAIVE] Keep the minimum in play while waiving it
**Move:** If a prospect falls below the minimum but still looks worthwhile, keep the obstacle standing while you investigate — before I say no, let me ask you a few questions — and make removing the minimum the very last thing you do before accepting, so it is never traded for another round of RFPs or firm interviews.
**APPLY WHEN:** attractive but under-sized opportunity; project work you may accept opportunistically.
**AVOID WHEN:** the minimum has already been waived out loud — the leverage is spent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a negotiation sequencing rule, no supporting evidence.

### [WWP:NOCHASE] Do not chase a meeting after a no
**Move:** If the prospect says he sees no fit, do not request a meeting to change his mind. Be able to describe who you help and how, concisely, by phone; then put him on the thought-leadership list and follow up over time.
**APPLY WHEN:** outbound contact meets a clean no; prospect shows no awareness of a problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** the no came from a gatekeeper rather than the person with the problem.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — asserts that meeting-chasing creates buying resistance; no measurement.

### [WWP:GAPHONEST] Name the expertise gap and let the client bridge it
**Move:** For work inside your capability but outside your stated expertise, say plainly that you can do it but it is not what you are usually hired for, restate your claim, and let the buyer decide whether to bridge the gap. Take the work if he bridges it and it makes sense; decline if the offer comes attached to an invitation to compete for it, and refer him elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** adjacent-scope inbound requests; existing clients extending into new territory.
**AVOID WHEN:** you lack the ability or capacity, or the work cannot be done profitably — then the answer is simply no.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented with explicit conditions (ability, capacity, profitability, no claim inflation), no outcome data.

### Stage — Diagnosis

### [WWP:SELFDIAG] Validate the self-diagnosis without contradicting it
**Move:** When the buyer arrives already naming the solution he wants, neither accept it nor argue. Acknowledge he may well be right and propose finding out for certain — then run your diagnostic before any prescription.
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound request scoped as a deliverable ("we need a new site / a campaign"); RFP with a predetermined solution.
**AVOID WHEN:** the item is genuinely tactical reapplication for an existing client where diagnosis is already done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — grounded in the author's repeated observation that clients ask for X and need Y; no counts given.

### [WWP:DIAGPROC] Formalize the diagnostic and insist on using it
**Move:** Map your own diagnostic method, name it, and when a buyer or procurement tries to dictate process, argue that the consistency of your outcomes depends on this process, so it must be the one used.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer or procurement is dictating steps; you need a legitimate reason to push back on the pitch format.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real repeatable method — an invented process collapses under questioning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — from advising firms; effectiveness asserted.

### [WWP:FOURPHASE] Four-phase engagement frame
**Move:** Structure and describe every engagement as diagnose → prescribe → apply → reapply, and use the sequence to explain why work cannot start mid-stream. It later doubles as the pricing skeleton (see WWP:BIGROUND) and the phased-sale skeleton (see WWP:PAIDDIAG).
**APPLY WHEN:** scoping conversations; explaining why a proposal cannot be written for free.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already completed a credible diagnosis with another party and is buying execution knowingly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a model borrowed by analogy from medicine.

### [WWP:NOFREETHINK] Draw the free-work line at diagnosis
**Move:** Collect preliminary diagnostic information during the buying cycle to assess fit, but do not share your diagnosis, and never prescribe strategy, before being hired and paid.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery calls that start turning into consulting; RFPs asking for approach and recommendations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a small, deliberate demonstration is your chosen positioning play — the book rejects this, but the trade-off is real for firms without a reputation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as an absolute (free pitching is free thinking, full stop).

### [WWP:PAIDDIAG] Get paid to write the proposal
**Move:** When the situation is too complex to quantify a solution without investigation, sell a paid diagnostic as the first phase of a phased engagement. Its deliverable has two parts — findings, and recommendations including timeline and budget — so the proposal is produced inside paid work.
**APPLY WHEN:** technical or complex challenge; buyer wants a costed plan; you would otherwise do free discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer sees no value gap to investigate and the scope is genuinely routine.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented as standard practice among the firms the author advises, by analogy to MRIs, audits and legal discovery.

### Stage — Running the buying cycle (meetings instead of pitches)

### [WWP:NOPITCHMEET] Run the meeting as if presenting were banned
**Move:** Before any buying-cycle meeting, ask what you would do in that room if you were not allowed to present. What remains is conversation — talking and listening without one side performing. Move credentials and firm background into that conversation instead of a deck.
**APPLY WHEN:** invited to "present capabilities"; early buying-cycle meetings.
**AVOID WHEN:** a formal process you have decided to enter still mandates a presentation slot — then negotiate its content (see WWP:INSIDETRACK) rather than refuse outright.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a reframing exercise, offered without results.

### [WWP:INSPIRE] Inspire only the interested
**Move:** With a buyer who recognizes a gap but has not committed, use portfolio, examples, competitor work and outside best practice to show what the other side of the problem looks like. Aim at his intent to solve the problem, not at his intent to hire you.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer is gathering information, weighing pros and cons, no date attached.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is unaware — inspiration aimed at someone who denies the problem produces defensiveness; or when he has already formed intent and needs reassurance instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated with a psychological rationale, not a source.

### [WWP:INTENTTEST] Test intent before treating a request as real
**Move:** Check whether the buyer's decision to act is anchored to a future date or event, and whether the work has moved from wish list to to-do list. If not, it is inspiration he needs, not a proposal — offer examples or a small paid discovery instead.
**APPLY WHEN:** a proposal request arrives with vague timing; enthusiastic meeting with no next date.
**AVOID WHEN:** procurement calendars force artificial dates that do not reflect real intent — the date alone can mislead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — named by the author as the most common and costly mistake he sees across firms.

### [WWP:REASSURE] Answer the motive behind the free-work request
**Move:** When an intent-stage buyer asks for a written proposal with free recommendations or spec work, read the request as fear of making a mistake and offer other risk-reducers instead: phased engagement, pilot project, money-back guarantee, case studies framed inside your defined method.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-cycle request for free thinking; detailed small questions signalling remorse-driven doubt.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's constraint is genuinely procedural (regulated procurement) rather than emotional.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to how Win Without Pitching firms respond; no comparative data.

### [WWP:NOOVERINVEST] Keep visible investment in the sale low
**Move:** Deliberately limit the effort you visibly sink into winning, and invite the buyer to say no early and often — no is the second-best answer. Heavy visible investment signals need, hands over power, and provokes stalling and strings of maybes instead of honesty.
**APPLY WHEN:** any opportunity where the temptation is to show how much you want it.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuinely strategic account where a costly, deliberate investment is a considered bet — the book does not cover that case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — mechanism described (buying resistance from obvious investment), no data.

### [WWP:PASSIONHOLD] Keep passion off the table as a differentiator
**Move:** Do not lead with enthusiasm, chemistry or price. Treat them as tiebreakers only; leaning on them implicitly concedes that on expertise you are interchangeable, and visible eagerness invites the buyer to take control.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive situations, chemistry meetings, any moment the instinct is to show how much you want it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly weights cultural fit and you have already established expertise — then passion is a legitimate tiebreak.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — an interpretation of buyer inference, unsupported.

### Stage — Competitive processes and RFPs

### [WWP:4PRIORITIES] Four priorities of winning new business
**Move:** Work the hierarchy in order — (1) win before a competitive process exists, (2) derail the process into an alternative first step, (3) if it cannot be derailed, get the inside track, (4) walk away. The goal is to win; not pitching is the preferred means, not the point.
**APPLY WHEN:** any opportunity that arrives already inside a selection process.
**AVOID WHEN:** applying it as rigid doctrine — Enns explicitly warns against replacing the client's rigidity with your own.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a prioritization framework offered without outcome data.

### [WWP:INSIDETRACK] Trade participation for concessions
**Move:** Assume every competitive bid already has a favored party. If you enter anyway, extract concessions as the price of participating: access to decision makers, negotiated limits on what you will write or show. Judge by the buyer's behavior — his willingness to treat you differently — rather than his words.
**APPLY WHEN:** the process cannot be derailed but the buyer shows signs of valuing expertise.
**AVOID WHEN:** no concession is granted — the book's read is that you are the stalking horse; go to priority four.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "somebody always has the inside track" is the author's working assumption.

### Stage — Proposal, agreement, close

### [WWP:ORALPROP] Propose in speech, paper only as contract
**Move:** Deliver the proposal verbally — the work, the timeframe, the price — and reach agreement in conversation covering scope, timing, budget and basic terms. Produce a document only after agreement, and call it the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer at intent stage who recognizes your expertise; owner-led or few-stakeholder deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** committee or public-sector buying where a written submission is legally required; or when the buyer has never had the substantive conversation, in which case there is nothing to write up.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — supported by the author's retrospective claim that documents rarely swayed decisions.

### [WWP:POLICYLANG] Say "it is our policy"
**Move:** Convert your requirements into written policy statements and deliver them with that framing — it is our policy that we do not begin solving clients' problems before we are engaged — rather than as preferences or hopes.
**APPLY WHEN:** any request to work before engagement; deposit and payment requirements.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "policy" is fabricated for one deal and you would abandon it under mild pressure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author reports less resistance with this framing; no comparison data.

### [WWP:DEPOSIT] Payment before work, no exceptions for new clients
**Move:** Treat the engagement as unstarted until money moves, and ask for it plainly: work begins when the deposit arrives, as policy for all new clients. Take one third to one half of the fee portion, or the entire fee for the first phase of a phased engagement.
**APPLY WHEN:** every new client, at the moment intent is verbalized or a contract is signed.
**AVOID WHEN:** enterprise procurement where payment terms are fixed by the buyer's finance function — then the phased first-fee variant is the realistic form.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the thresholds are given as appropriate practice, with no basis stated.

### Stage — Pricing and negotiation

### [WWP:BIGROUND] Price thinking in round numbers, not hours
**Move:** Price everything up to the first creative deliverable as large round figures ending in zeros, so the price visibly has nothing to do with elapsed hours. Reserve hourly billing for reapplication work later in the engagement.
**APPLY WHEN:** quoting strategic/diagnostic phases; any moment the buyer asks for a rate.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's procurement mandates rate cards — then the phased fixed-fee framing is the workaround, and the book offers none beyond that.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from commoditization logic, no pricing data.

### [WWP:SIZEPRICE] Price to client size and to commitment
**Move:** Charge larger organizations more for equivalent work, on two grounds: they capture more financial value from it, and the price must carry enough pain that the advice is acted on rather than shelved.
**APPLY WHEN:** same offering sold across very different company sizes.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer can compare across your accounts and the difference cannot be explained by value delivered.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the General Motors vs. a single dealership contrast is illustrative, not evidential.

### [WWP:NODISCOUNT] Exhaust alternatives before cutting price
**Move:** Before discounting, work the two substitutes: guarantee the first phase (diagnosis and prescription) when the buyer doubts value — never guarantee ROI or the whole engagement — and offer payment terms over time when the buyer sees value but cannot pay now. Otherwise hold ground, which favors whoever is most comfortable discussing money.
**APPLY WHEN:** price objection from a buyer who otherwise fits.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is simply below your minimum — that is a walk-away, not a negotiation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — mechanisms described, outcomes not measured.

### [WWP:DISCRULES] Two rules if you do discount
**Move:** Make the price cut the last concession — clear every other objection first, and ask whether, if you agreed to this price, anything else would stop you working together right now. Then record the discount explicitly on contracts, estimates and invoices so the full value stays visible and the reduced number does not become the new baseline.
**APPLY WHEN:** a negotiated close where you have decided a discount is acceptable and still profitable.
**AVOID WHEN:** the cut would take the engagement below profitability — the book treats that as disqualifying.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as rules; the "client forgets the real price" claim is unsupported.

### [WWP:PRICELOSS] Use lost deals as a pricing gauge
**Move:** Raise prices as expertise deepens and read the results: occasionally losing business on price is the signal the level is right; never losing on price means you are underpriced, while needing to win on price means the expert positioning has failed.
**APPLY WHEN:** periodic pricing review after a run of deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** pipeline volume is too thin for the signal to mean anything.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a heuristic with no threshold or data behind it.

### [WWP:PROBONO] Separate charity from business development
**Move:** Choose pro bono clients by cause alignment and need, commit fully, and take no commercial benefit — no upsell path, no boardroom networking. Use that clean separation as the licence to require profit from every commercial engagement. Early in a firm's life, free or deeply discounted work inside the chosen focus is legitimate for building expertise, provided you say openly that this is the trade.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-stage firm with no proof in its new focus; charity work decisions.
**AVOID WHEN:** the discount is quiet — undisclosed cheap work is competing on price, and prolonged, it cements the price positioning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** assertion — normative, with the honesty condition stated but no time limit beyond "for a period of time."

### Stage — Delivery and account management

### [WWP:STRATFIRST] Strategy agreed before any creative, and restated before every reveal
**Move:** Refuse to develop or show creative of any kind before the problem is diagnosed and the strategy agreed. Immediately before showing any work, walk back through the agreed strategy so the discussion is measured against it rather than against taste.
**APPLY WHEN:** any delivery engagement where subjective reaction can override intent; recurring rounds of concepts.
**AVOID WHEN:** purely executional/production scope where no strategy layer was bought.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a firm policy, no outcome data.

### [WWP:EXECFREEDOM] Input on strategy, freedom on execution
**Move:** Explicitly trade: invite the client's input on strategy, and in exchange ask for freedom to explore executions. Frame the boundary out loud — feedback tied to the strategy is welcome (this doesn't deliver on strength), instruction on execution (make it darker) is not.
**APPLY WHEN:** kickoff of an engagement, while rules of collaboration are being set.
**AVOID WHEN:** the client has already been granted control and the engagement is mid-flight — retrofitting the rule reads as defensiveness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — policy proposal.

### [WWP:FEWOPTIONS] Fewer options, one recommendation
**Move:** Show as few options as practical and always recommend one; never ask which one the client likes. Route every reaction back to whether the strategy is being met.
**APPLY WHEN:** presenting any work product where a menu invites the buyer to act as designer.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer contractually purchased a set number of concepts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the stated inverse relation between option count and confidence in quality is asserted, not measured.

### [WWP:WEPRESENT] Our work is only presented by us
**Move:** Make it a rule that findings, strategy and solutions are never carried into the client organization without someone from your firm in the room; the client contact may assist but not substitute.
**APPLY WHEN:** work must travel to executives or committees the seller has not met.
**AVOID WHEN:** the client's internal politics or security rules make outside presence genuinely impossible — then treat it as a risk to price, not a rule to enforce.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the value of an unbiased outside perspective.

### [WWP:CHURNPLAN] Manage turnover deliberately
**Move:** Accept that client relationships end, and set the business development goal as replacing departing clients with larger opportunities rather than accumulating accounts. Use that goal to justify saying no to volume.
**APPLY WHEN:** annual planning; deciding whether to take marginal work.
**AVOID WHEN:** cash position cannot support the gap between an exit and a bigger replacement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the observed slide from advisor to supplier; no figures.

## Where this book stops working

- **No proof yet.** The whole system runs on a provable expertise claim. A new firm, a career-changer, or anyone entering a focus with no delivery history has nothing to substantiate the claim, so refusal reads as arrogance rather than selectivity. The book's own concession here is WWP:PROBONO (openly discounted work inside the focus to build proof) — that is a slow, cash-hungry bridge, not a sales tactic.
- **Regulated and public-sector procurement.** Oral proposals (WWP:ORALPROP), refusing written recommendations (WWP:NOFREETHINK), deposit-before-work (WWP:DEPOSIT) and round-number pricing (WWP:BIGROUND) all collide with mandated written submissions, fixed payment terms and rate cards. The book offers the phased-fee workaround and nothing beyond it.
- **Committee and enterprise buying.** Moves gated at stakeholders=solo/few (WWP:PURSUENO, WWP:MLE, WWP:MLEWAIVE, WWP:ORALPROP, WWP:DEPOSIT, WWP:INTENTTEST, WWP:SELFDIAG) assume a single owner-level buyer who can be retreated from and followed. With a buying committee, the retreat has no single addressee and the deal simply ends.
- **Low-ACV and volume motions.** A per-client annual fee floor set near 10% of target income presumes a small book of large engagements. Micro-ticket, transactional or high-volume selling has no room for a minimum, a paid diagnostic, or "no is the second-best answer."
- **Cash pressure.** Every refusal move (WWP:MISSIONOBJ, WWP:NOOVERINVEST, WWP:CHURNPLAN, WWP:NODISCOUNT) presumes you can afford the deal to die. Under runway pressure the model is either abandoned or used dishonestly — the book itself names cash pressure as the invalidating condition for WWP:MISSIONOBJ.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Existing dominant client.** Committing to a focus (WWP:FOCUS3) can strand the one account currently funding the firm; the manifesto treats this as a decision to make anyway, without addressing the transition.
- **Genre limit.** Absolutes ("free pitching is free thinking, period") are the author's stance, aimed at persuading creative-firm owners to change behavior, and the author sells training built on the same material. Treat every rule as a strong default with the stated exceptions, not as measured practice.

## What the book debunks

- **"Full service / we do everything."** Read by buyers as a low-integrity claim, because broad expertise is known to be hard to amass. Breadth is an integrity problem, not a capability advantage.
- **Differentiating on personality, process or price** is treated as non-differentiation; passion and chemistry are tiebreakers at best (WWP:PASSIONHOLD).
- **"The customer is always right"** — a retail idea wrongly imported into expertise work. Accepting the buyer's self-diagnosis is the root of bad engagements: a dominant client directs the work from an unexamined diagnosis, then the "we only did as we were told" spiral into mutual blame. Doctors, lawyers and accountants are named as the worst clients precisely because taking control is trained into them.
- **Winning a pitch is still a loss.** The judge-vs-auditioner dynamic established in the sale persists into delivery and caps what you can deliver and charge. Sellers rationalize client-imposed processes because those processes feed their own addiction to the reveal.
- **"We don't do spec creative"** is false comfort while strategy and diagnosis are still given away — free thinking, not just free design, is the actual leak.
- **"Thin margins now, better margins later."** Rejected outright: margin and power only decay. The recovery plan is called naive, a false agreement, or a hunt for hidden profit. Superior service does not create profit; profit creates the room to fix mistakes, so the least profitable clients receive the worst service (the ringing-phone test).
- **A signature is not commitment.** Commitment escalates private decision → said aloud → signature → payment, and only payment counts.
- **Mistaking interest for intent** — the book's named costliest error: a buyer asking for a proposal before intent has formed is still shopping for inspiration.
- **The euphoria/remorse cycle.** After committing, the buyer's high fades within hours into doubt; the seller's instinct (more excitement, big-picture, organic language) is exactly wrong where calm, sequence and specifics are needed.
- **Change orders end relationships.** Firms are fired over small tactical invoices, not large strategic ones.
- **Compensating for weak clients by adding more of them** is named as the standard error — the roster problem is quality, not quantity.
- **Waiving an unstated boundary is not selectivity** — it is simply not having parameters (WWP:MLEWAIVE).
- **"The one who eddied out."** Early success from a boom or one benefactor client removes the pressure to specialize; the reckoning arrives later and is misread as bad luck or a bad market.
- **Written proposals** are decoded as a way for the buyer to avoid saying no, and RFP motives are read as: keeping the crowd at bay, comparing like-for-like, measuring value, gathering inspiration, stalling, price-shopping. Price-shopping is the one motive the book grants no answer to.
- **Circularity warning (the digest's own read).** Whenever a proclamation fails, the book attributes the failure to an earlier proclamation — which makes the system difficult to falsify.

## Numbers that matter

- **12 proclamations**; positioning = **3 steps** (focus → claim → proof); **4 engagement phases** (diagnose → prescribe → apply → reapply); **3 selling modes** (help / inspire / reassure); **4 priorities of winning new business**; **2 steps** in the weaning-off-presentations sequence (existing clients first, buying cycle second); **5 rules of collaboration**; **4 money proclamations** (VIII–XI).
- **Minimum Level of Engagement ≈ 10% of the year's total target fee income**, expressed as an annual per-client fee floor. No derivation given.
- **Deposit: one third to one half** of the fee portion, or the **entire fee of the first phase** in a phased engagement. Given as appropriate practice, no basis stated.
- **Skill ranking for a differentiated creative firm:** consulting first, writing second, artistry third (artistry treated as the commoditizing element).
- Strategic pricing expressed as **large round numbers ending in zeros**; hourly reserved for reapplication.
- Buyer's implicit equation the pricing must survive: **Value = Quality / Price**.
- **"No is the second-best answer"** (best being yes).
- Publication **2010**, ISBN-13 **978-1-60544-004-0**. Circulation of **>100K copies is self-reported by the publisher** — not independently verified, and not evidence that the method works.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No measurement anywhere.** Nearly every move is an assertion or a practitioner anecdote from the author's advisory practice; there are no win rates, no price realization figures, no control group. The evidence ceiling for this lens is practitioner-anecdote, and most moves sit below it.
- **Author incentive.** Enns sells training built on this material. The absolutes are a persuasion device aimed at firm owners, and the digest should be read with that discount applied.
- **Unfalsifiable by construction.** Failure of any proclamation is explained by failure of an earlier one, so the system cannot be disconfirmed from inside.
- **Assumes the expert already exists.** Almost nothing on the cold-start problem: how to build the first proof, how long WWP:PROBONO discounting may run, what to do in year one with no reputation.
- **Buying committees, procurement and multi-stakeholder politics** are treated as obstacles to route around rather than a system to work. There is no map of internal champions, blockers, or consensus building.
- **Demand generation is thin.** Beyond writing on the focus area (WWP:WRITEDEEP) and a thought-leadership follow-up list, the book has no pipeline mechanics — no channel selection, no outbound system, no measurement of source.
- **Firm scale and delivery economics** are barely modelled: hiring, utilization, capacity, subcontracting, what happens when the expert cannot be in every room (WWP:WEPRESENT does not scale).
- **Not products.** The model is built for expert services sold by their owner. Product, SaaS, marketplace and productized offers get no treatment; the offer slot in most Deal-gates is service/consulting for that reason.
- **No macro sensitivity.** Written in 2010 for creative firms; it says nothing about downturn behavior, buyer consolidation, in-housing, or how price floors survive a market where clients cut first and specialize second.
- **The retreat dynamic is asserted, not tested.** "People follow what withdraws" carries WWP:PURSUENO, WWP:PREEMPTOBJ, WWP:NOOVERINVEST and WWP:MLE, and rests on an aphorism.
- **Ethics of the softer manipulations are undertheorized.** Pre-empting an objection to trigger a defensive "of course we can afford you" (WWP:PREEMPTOBJ) works precisely because it engages the buyer's self-image; the book frames it as honest filtering and does not examine the misuse case.
