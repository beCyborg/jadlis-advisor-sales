# The Four Conversations: A New Model for Selling Expertise — digest

## What this book is for the lens

Mechanism claim: in expertise sales the sale itself is a working sample of the engagement, so the seller's conduct during the sale assigns the role (expert or vendor) that persists through delivery — roles are not renegotiated after signature. From that premise the book derives a staged sequence (Probative → Qualifying → Value → Closing) whose defining rule is that price guidance is set only after value metrics are agreed and before any solution is designed, which disconnects price from cost.

Where it beats other sources: it is the only source in the lens that gives a full operational script for the value-then-price ordering, including the anchor exchange, the guaranteed-case ceiling probe, and the mechanics of building and delivering a three-option one-page proposal. It also supplies a power model (P = Db/D) that makes concession-seeking a measurement instrument rather than a negotiation tactic.

Weighting caution to carry everywhere: same author and same training business as The Win Without Pitching Manifesto. Agreement between the two is continuation of one practitioner's system, not independent corroboration. Evidence ceiling is practitioner-anecdote; no trials, no call data, no measured lift anywhere in the book.

## Core model

Four conversations, each with an objective, principles (non-negotiable behavior) and frameworks (swappable structure):

1. **The Probative Conversation** — prove expertise and move from vendor to expert in the client's mind. Uniquely, it usually happens without the seller present, through thought leadership and referrers. Its pivotal event is *The Flip*: the moment the power dynamic inverts and the client starts mining you for knowledge. Aim for The Flip before first contact. Tools: The Ladder of Lead Generation (selling → marketing → educating, rising status, falling immediacy), specialization (discipline × market), ideology/perspective as the final differentiator, P = Db/D as the power law.
2. **The Qualifying Conversation** — vet the lead for a right-fit opportunity and set the next step, while holding the expert position. Framework: a five-drawer variation on BANT (attributed to IBM, 1950s) — Context, Desired Future State, Decision Makers, Timeframe, Budget. Context opens; order of the rest is free. "Need" is deliberately renamed Desired Future State because the expert is obliged to transcend the client's self-diagnosis. Timeframe and Budget are read as diagnostics of interest-versus-intent, not as logistics.
3. **The Value Conversation** — determine the value that could be created and what share of it you can command. Four questions, all about the client: what they want; what will be measured; what those metrics are worth; what they would pay. Four steps: confirm desired future state (vision, personal outcomes, obstacles, assets) → set metrics collaboratively → quantify economic value only → give pricing guidance as a range, high end first, with solutions still untouched.
4. **The Closing Conversation** — help the client select and commit while holding the expert position. Three principles: closing is a conversation, closing is facilitating, closing is reassuring. Seven-point agenda: timecheck, roll call, state the objective, recap the Value Conversation, share the options, facilitate the choice, drive the next step. Output is agreement in principle; commitment escalates verbal → written → paid.

Cross-cutting: the two positions (expert / vendor); Principles of Navigating the Sale (Expert's Mantra, leading with visible frameworks, detachment, conversation over presentation, closing the gap between thought and speech, racing to object, silence, saying/hearing/inviting no, assuming an advantaged player and testing for it with behavioral concessions). The book states outright that the four-stage linearity is a construct, defended as useful rather than true.

## Moves

### Stage: Probative — positioning, lead generation, standing posture

### [WWP:SAMPLE-CONDUCT] Sell as the sample of the engagement
**Move:** Before each sales interaction, decide which behaviors you want the client to expect in delivery — composure, preparedness, discernment, efficiency — and perform exactly those, treating persuasion tactics imported from product selling as off-limits.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any expertise sale where you will personally advise or practice, from first contact onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** Transactional product/SaaS/retail sales, where buyer tolerance for aggressive selling is high and post-sale roles are not set during the sale.

### [WWP:THREE-ORIENT] The three orienting questions
**Move:** At any point in a live deal, silently answer: which of the four conversations is this, what is that conversation's objective, and which principles and framework navigate to it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-call disorientation; deals that blur across stages; coaching a rep through a stalled opportunity.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as rigid — the author concedes the conversations blur and the boundaries are not clean.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:LADDER-CLIMB] Balance the lead generation ladder over time
**Move:** Audit the current lead-gen mix against the three rungs, keep lower-rung activity for immediacy, and start the education-based activity (a book, a serious speaking slot) whose payoff arrives years later, shifting weight upward as expertise deepens.
**APPLY WHEN:** Planning pipeline for a firm whose deals routinely open in the vendor position; early or lean stages where immediacy is still required.
**AVOID WHEN:** Revenue is needed this quarter and education-based work is treated as a substitute rather than an addition — the tradeoff is immediacy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own model, illustrated by his practice; no measured lift.

### [WWP:ARMOR-PIERCE] Armor-piercing outreach
**Move:** In cold outreach, lead with the single narrow thing you do better than anyone rather than a service menu: acknowledge the incumbent relationship explicitly, state the one wedge capability, and ask whether a need exists, leaving the client free to refuse or redirect you to an adjacent need.
**APPLY WHEN:** Outbound into accounts with existing agency/consulting relationships and you hold a defensible narrow claim.
**AVOID WHEN:** No genuine wedge exists, or you would revert to a battering-ram list of everything you do.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — sample phrasing and the breadth/depth triangle from the author's practice.

### [WWP:INBOUND-TRIAGE] Sort inbound by origin to predict the sale
**Move:** For every inbound inquiry, ask how they found you and why they reached out; read referral or deep content engagement as evidence The Flip has already happened, and untracked search/ad arrivals as evidence you are one of several bidders and must engineer The Flip inside the call.
**APPLY WHEN:** Triaging inbound volume; deciding how much process to concede on a given deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to dismiss SEO/paid entirely — the book holds these are valuable, just status-neutral.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — pattern recalled from the author's and clients' inbound, no counts.

### [WWP:P-EQ-DBD] Modulate expressed desire against expressed desirability
**Move:** Read the client's expressed interest in you and calibrate your own expressed enthusiasm to sit just under it — damping excitement with a subdued buyer, raising warmth with an enthusiastic one — instead of leaning on passion to win.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any direct client conversation, especially when you notice yourself selling hard.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pushed to feigned indifference; the book explicitly warns against reading as aloof or arrogant.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a formula given as a first principle, with no measurement behind the ratio.

### [WWP:SPECIALIZE] Narrow to create depth
**Move:** Define focus explicitly as discipline-for-market and narrow one or both until your expertise is deeper than the alternatives the client perceives; accept lost breadth as the price.
**APPLY WHEN:** Positioning work; when deals routinely turn on price because competitors look interchangeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** Depth already exists and only awareness is missing — then the fix is communication, not further narrowing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — restates the first proclamation of the author's own Win Without Pitching Manifesto (same author, same training; not independent corroboration).

### [WWP:IDEOLOGY] Make the final battle one of ideology
**Move:** Articulate a belief about how your discipline should be practiced in your market that your closest specialist competitors do not hold, then build content and your way of working through that lens, polarizing in proportion to competitive density.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mature, dense markets where specialization alone no longer separates you.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early or burgeoning markets (a perspective is not required there); and whenever the ideology would target people or organizations rather than ideas or conventions — the book calls that toxic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative

### [WWP:JOURNEY-MAP] Show a way of working that embodies the perspective
**Move:** Keep a map of the engagement journey — diagnosis, then prescription/planning, then execution or oversight — that visibly carries your perspective, ready for the moment a convinced client asks how the engagement will work.
**APPLY WHEN:** A buyer has bought the point of view and asks about mechanics; also as an alternative next step to a client-run process.
**AVOID WHEN:** The map is indistinguishable from competitors' — that is the moment the client learns your perspective was bait.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — illustrated by a hypothetical speaking-to-hire scenario.

### [WWP:PROBATIVE-AUDIT] The four probative prompts
**Move:** Periodically answer four sets of questions in writing — current focus and whether it must narrow; the problems you solve and the consistent words for them; whether an unspoken perspective underlies your methodology; and how you will climb the ladder long-term while generating leads today.
**APPLY WHEN:** Annual or quarterly positioning review; onboarding a new seller into the firm's claim.
**AVOID WHEN:** Substituted for live selling work — these are upstream strategy prompts, not conversation tools.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a checklist offered with a pointer to the author's paid resources.

### Stage: Cross-stage — mindset and navigating principles

### [WWP:MANTRA] Recite the Expert's Mantra before contact
**Move:** In a quiet minute before a call or meeting, say the four lines to yourself — I am the prize, I am on a mission to help, I can only help if you let me lead, I accept that not all will follow — to leave vendor mindset before applying any framework.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any direct client interaction, especially with a history of deferential selling.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a swagger prompt by someone whose bias is the need to be respected — that path ends in arrogant know-it-all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's training device; the claim that belief follows recitation is asserted.

### [WWP:DETACH-PERSONAL] Name and park the personal need
**Move:** Identify which of three needs is yours — to be liked, to win, to be respected — and set it aside before the call: push relationship out to a reward earned after value creation; refuse convince mode; or pair the need for respect with silence and urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** You notice a recurring behavioral tell across deals (over-accommodating, over-arguing, over-talking).
**AVOID WHEN:** Read as permanent suppression of your natural self — the book warns that depletes energy and is unfair to demand.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a self-diagnosis typology from the author's coaching, no instrument or validation.

### [WWP:LOW-SUNK-COST] Keep sale investment low to stay detached
**Move:** Deliberately withhold travel, unpaid external research and large time blocks early in a deal, so objectivity is not eroded by escalating commitment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early-stage opportunities before fit is established; competitive processes with heavy unpaid requirements.
**AVOID WHEN:** A concession has already proven you are the advantaged player and investment is now warranted.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — invokes sunk-cost reasoning informally, no cited study.

### [WWP:CONVERSE-NOT-PRESENT] Replace brief-and-present with conversation
**Move:** When a client proposes an arm's-length brief-then-present process, name it and push back for a two-way conversation; the harder they lean on process, the harder you press for talk, and if the process is institutional, seek the senior decision maker with the political capital to bend it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Formalized selection processes, procurement-led buying, RFP-shaped opportunities.
**AVOID WHEN:** Regulated or government procurement where the process cannot lawfully be bent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reasoning about why clients default to process; the cost is framed as lower margin and vendor status.

### [WWP:WHY-US-DEFLECT] Never answer "why should we hire you" directly
**Move:** When invited to sell yourself, decline the frame with a smile and offer instead to describe the reasons clients typically hire you, leaving the client to judge whether those reasons are theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client flips into interviewer mode mid-qualification.
**AVOID WHEN:** Delivered with condescension — the book pairs the firmer version explicitly with humility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two sample phrasings from the author's practice.

### [WWP:CLOSE-GAP] Say what you are thinking, immediately
**Move:** The moment you notice yourself nodding yes while thinking no, stop and voice the disagreement with a permission-seeking opener — asking to push back, to challenge an assertion, to test the assumptions beneath it, or to raise a process concern — pausing only long enough to find kind words.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any live conversation where the client's framing of problem, solution or process seems wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The impulse is venting rather than substance; the standard is ruthless about the gap, kind in the words.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — causal story about blow-ups arising from accumulated unspoken disagreement.

### [WWP:RACE-OBJECT] Win the race to object
**Move:** As soon as you sense an objection forming — price, category experience, team size or location — state it plainly as a gap between what they want and what you offer, then stop and leave silence for the client rather than pre-emptively rebutting yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualifying, whenever a concern is visibly present but unspoken.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot resist bridging the gap yourself — self-rebuttal signals neediness and an intent to argue with every concern.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three scripted openers plus two illustrated client replies.

### [WWP:RFP-DECLINE] Raise your own process objection to an RFP
**Move:** When an RFP is announced, say you don't typically respond to them, keeping the hedge word so discussion stays open; if pressed, give the plain reason (built for procuring commodities, creates work, keeps you at arm's length) and offer the alternative — talk, exchange criteria both ways, then scope and price for discussion.
**APPLY WHEN:** A qualifying call that is going well ends with an RFP handoff.
**AVOID WHEN:** You need a hard line — then drop the qualifier or invoke policy instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — includes reading the client's reaction as a power measurement.

### [WWP:POLICY] Invoke policy against policy
**Move:** Frame an objection as a pre-made decision (for example, that as a matter of policy you don't write proposals for people you have never met) when you want a hard line or expect a policy counter; when two policies collide, propose a human compromise — asking the gatekeeper to secure a short call with one senior decision maker, with a stated agreement to withdraw gracefully if it fails.
**APPLY WHEN:** Gatekeepers block access to decision makers; large or bureaucratic buyers with professional purchasers.
**AVOID WHEN:** The objection benefits from remaining a preference (the RFP case), where a hard line closes off discussion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked dialogue, plus the argument that a policy need not be written to be real.

### [WWP:SILENCE] Embrace silence after anything contentious
**Move:** After raising an objection, saying no, asking for a concession or floating a price, stop talking and let the client speak next however long the pause runs.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every contentious moment in qualifying and pricing.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication given; the failure mode is your own discomfort producing unprompted concessions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — claims comfort grows from three seconds to much longer with practice; self-reported.

### [WWP:SAY-NO] Say no, and reserve the right to retract
**Move:** Decline poor fits, arduous demands and inappropriate next steps out loud — offering a referral elsewhere where appropriate — treating every no as potentially "not now," since any obstacle you place can be withdrawn later.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualifying, when fit criteria are not met or the process is unacceptable.
**AVOID WHEN:** Overplayed into performative discernment; the book wants the possibility of no felt, not brandished.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the claim that neediness repels and a yes without a possible no is worthless.

### [WWP:HEAR-NO] Work a no instead of fearing it
**Move:** Treat no as the second-best answer (better than maybe, "we'll get back to you," or silence) and respond by mirroring the word back as a question, asking directly about the obstacle, or setting it aside explicitly to return to after further questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any stage; especially early, to kill unfit opportunities before sunk costs accrue.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your discomfort traces to unchecked desire or over-investment — then the fix is upstream, at P = Db/D.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three sample responses; the claim that good fits are robust is asserted.

### [WWP:INVITE-NO] Preface awkward requests with permission to refuse
**Move:** Before a request that might be awkward — access to a CEO, a change of process — say explicitly that they are free to refuse; if they do, follow with a question about what would need to happen for them to become comfortable.
**APPLY WHEN:** You sense the client hedging, softening or withholding.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used mechanically on trivial asks, where it manufactures weight rather than removing it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed emotional mechanism (you are the source of the weight), no measurement.

### [WWP:MAGIC-EMAIL] The Closing the Loop email
**Move:** When a client goes dark after a positive proposal response, send a two-line note titled Closing the Loop stating that, having not heard back, you assume they went another direction or priorities changed, and inviting future contact — no chasing, no apology, no check-in tone.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-proposal silence where earlier neediness likely made honest refusal hard.
**AVOID WHEN:** Sent before the client has had reasonable time, or used as a manipulation lever rather than a genuine release.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two typical reply patterns reported; no response rates. Origin unknown, taught to the author as a voicemail script over 20 years ago.

### [WWP:ADVANTAGED] Assume an advantaged player exists
**Move:** In any competitive sale, assume someone already holds the inside lane — inside information, an internal advocate, or a decision already made — and treat your task as finding out whether that position is yours or available, rather than assuming 1/n odds.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-bidder processes, procurement-driven shortlists, three-bid compliance situations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely level, high-volume transactional selling where 1/n strategy is sound — the book grants this case for products.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the shift from below 1/n to better than 1/2 is offered without derivation or data.

### [WWP:CONCESSION-LADDER] Seek behavioral concessions as a fit test
**Move:** Ask the client to treat you differently and read compliance as signal, escalating roughly in this order: moving a meeting's date or place; sharing information withheld from others; substituting an existing credentials document for a questionnaire; completing a written needs assessment; having all decision makers complete it; assembling all decision makers to discuss the findings; increasing budget; granting access to seniors you were told were off-limits; changing scope; paying for a first-phase diagnostic.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive processes where you need an early read on your true position.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pushed too far — the client's reconciliation then becomes "demanding and annoying" rather than "different."
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a ranked list from practice; the causal claim (behavior precedes changed thinking) is asserted.

### [WWP:REVEALED-PREF] Test stated preference with a small ask
**Move:** When a client says you are the one they want but the process must run, do not accept the statement — request a concrete concession instead: substituting a credentials document for the questionnaire, or a short interview with their boss before you write the proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Verbal assurance of preferred status in a process-bound deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The ask would breach a regulated or public-interest process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two sample asks; the rationale that revealed preferences validate and stated ones stay suspect.

### [WWP:BID-LAST] Offer to bid only after the others bid
**Move:** When the client is compelled to collect competing bids, note that you will not be lowest and propose they gather the others first, then talk about whether your bid makes sense at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** Three-bid compliance situations with a client who has discretion and political capital.
**AVOID WHEN:** Government, regulated industries, or any setting where this contravenes regulation, public interest or ethical norms — the book states this carve-out itself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented with its own exception.

### [WWP:PAID-DIAGNOSTIC] Convert a reframing into a paid first phase
**Move:** Once the client has rethought the problem or the solution, propose setting their selection process aside for a small paid step: take a slice of budget and test the hypothesis about the underlying issue.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have already shifted the client's framing and there is acknowledged uncertainty about root cause.
**AVOID WHEN:** No reframing has occurred — this concession is described as the payoff of having changed their thinking first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — listed as the most significant concession on the ladder.

### Stage: Qualifying — objective, structure and questions

### [WWP:STATUS-FIRST] Refuse the money-for-status trade
**Move:** When a client offers a good fee conditional on you dropping to order-taker — their diagnosis, their process, their scope — decline the condition rather than the money, and renegotiate the terms of leadership before the terms of price.
**APPLY WHEN:** A lucrative brief arrives fully self-diagnosed and self-scoped with no room to lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** Survival economics genuinely dominate; the book treats this as a resolve test and does not price the exception.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued by hypothetical (a stack of money on the table), no case data.

### [WWP:QUAL-Q-GEN] Generate qualifying questions by the junior-qualifier exercise
**Move:** Imagine an eager junior repeatedly bringing you unfit meetings, then write out the answers you would demand before attending another; supplement with prompts about target-client variables, absolute requirements, red flags and absolute nos.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building or refreshing a qualification standard; onboarding someone into the qualifying role.
**AVOID WHEN:** The output gets recited as a script — these are selectable tools, never all used at once.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a training exercise presented as the author's method.

### [WWP:CDFSDMTF] Organize questions in the five-drawer toolbox
**Move:** File every qualifying question under Context, Desired Future State, Decision Makers, Timeframe or Budget, keep one key question per category, always open with Context, then navigate the remaining four in whatever order the conversation suggests.
**APPLY WHEN:** Structuring a first person-to-person conversation with a new lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-engineering placement — asking the right question matters more than filing it correctly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an adaptation of BANT, which the book attributes to IBM in the 1950s.

### [WWP:CONTEXT-FLIP-PROBE] Ask how they found you
**Move:** Inside context questions, ask how they found you or why they came to you and read the answer as evidence of whether The Flip has already happened; pair it with basic organizational context (revenue size, customers served, geography) or your discipline's equivalent.
**APPLY WHEN:** Opening minutes of a qualifying call with any inbound lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** Delivered as interrogation rather than an opener — the model is a walk-in clinic's intake.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — clinic-intake analogy plus a financial-planner example (net worth).

### [WWP:FIVE-WHYS] Five Whys, with a stated limit
**Move:** Chain "why" questions inward from the stated request — new website, poor performance, fewer leads, falling traffic and conversion — branching when more than one cause surfaces.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client's stated need is concrete and you need the layer beneath it quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** You would otherwise stop before the fundamental issue; the book prefers going straight to the client's own definition of success.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — described as standard sales-training fare and reproduced as sample dialogue.

### [WWP:THREE-YEAR-Q] The three-year desired-future-state question
**Move:** Acknowledge the stated request, ask permission to back up to the bigger picture, then place the client three years out at a named date in a named shared setting, pleased with their progress, and ask what happened to make that so; add detail deliberately, because the detail level of your question sets the detail level of their answer. Take visible notes, repeat their words back, and keep asking what else until the answers thin out.
**APPLY WHEN:** Desired Future State questioning in Qualifying, especially with a client who arrived self-diagnosed with a tactical brief; and again as the opening step of a Value Conversation, including with a cool, time-constrained senior where The Flip has not happened.
**AVOID WHEN:** You contaminate it with context (for example, assuming the project succeeded) — its power depends on the client supplying the inference that they hired you; or the contact cannot see three years ahead, in which case defer per WWP:JUNIOR-FALLBACK.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — credited to Dan Sullivan's The Dan Sullivan Question; one extended worked dialogue; the emotional-benefit mechanism is asserted.

### [WWP:PREMORTEM-Q] Pre-mortem to surface obstacles
**Move:** After capturing the three-year vision, invert it: ask them to imagine the same date with the vision unrealized and the two of you dissecting the failure, then ask for the most likely causes. Treat every answer as an objection to be handled before any close.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client has articulated a desired future state and is talking freely; early-to-mid qualifying or the DFS step of a Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is a process-runner with no stake in outcomes, or under roughly 15 minutes remain and the vision itself is not yet on the table.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's dialogue examples, no dataset.

### [WWP:PERSONAL-MANDATE] Probe the individual's personal stake
**Move:** Alongside organizational goals, ask what achieving this means for them personally and how their own role changes if it lands; record personal outcomes separately from corporate ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** B2B with multiple players and a decision maker who has visible career exposure to the outcome.

### [WWP:JUNIOR-FALLBACK] Handle a contact who cannot produce a vision

**APPLY WHEN:** The qualifying call landed on a coordinator or middle manager.

### [WWP:DM-OPEN-NARROW] Open-then-narrow decision-maker mapping

### [WWP:PROCESS-Q] Uncover the selection process

### [WWP:TF-INTENT] Timeframe as intent detector
**Move:** Ask when they need to start or when they need it finished, then always ask why. A named date backed by a real event signals intent; absence of a date means they are still contemplating, however enthusiastic they sound.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every qualifying conversation, before allocating senior resource to the deal.

### [WWP:TF-BUILD] Build a timeframe backwards from an event
**Move:** If the answer is "as soon as possible," find the real forcing event (board meeting, review cycle), work backwards — propose that a solution be identified and underway by that event, subtract contracting time, and ask what the earliest realistic decision date is.
**APPLY WHEN:** Urgency is emotional and triggered by something already past, but no forward date exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client has no institutional calendar to anchor against, or you would be manufacturing urgency they do not feel.

### [WWP:ALLOCATED] Ask whether funds are allocated, not "what's your budget"
**Move:** Replace the standard budget question with allocation variants — has a budget been allocated, are funds budgeted, is there a line item, is it in the current fiscal year — because you are testing progress through the buying cycle, not extracting a number.
**APPLY WHEN:** Budget quadrant of qualifying, any B2B deal with a fiscal process.
**AVOID WHEN:** Domains where fees are never separately budgeted (architecture folded into project cost, advisory folded into return) — there, use total project budget or total investable assets as an affordability surrogate.

### [WWP:RANGE-5X] Give a wide range, high number first, then silence
**Move:** If asked to size the spend, state a broad range whose top is roughly five times its bottom, deliver the larger figure first, then stop talking. Optionally open with whether this is budgeted or whether they want your help setting the number.
**APPLY WHEN:** No budget set, the client explicitly asks what it will cost, and date-anchored intent already exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are tempted to narrow into scope and pricing detail — this is explicitly not the pricing conversation.

### [WWP:MINIMUM] Deploy a price minimum as a showdown

**APPLY WHEN:** Signals point to a client who cannot afford you and you are willing to lose the deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early, or reflexively — the book calls it a jarring hurdle and warns against using it just because you have one.

### Stage: Qualifying — disposition and next step

### [WWP:DECLINE-FIT] Decline on fit, keep the door open
**Move:** Thank them for the answers, say plainly that it is not a fit at this time, give the honest reason kindly, and offer names of firms that do serve their level.
**APPLY WHEN:** Budget, discipline, market or capacity mismatch is clear.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not actually explored a third way — the book warns against falling in love with disqualifying.

### [WWP:RETREAT-ADVANCE] Decline in order to make the client advance
**Move:** Where the objection is to their buying behavior (an arduous RFP, a budget far below minimum) rather than to them, state the reason honestly and withdraw, leaving room for them to move toward you.
**APPLY WHEN:** You genuinely accept the deal ending and the client has shown they are underfunding or over-processing a real opportunity.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are bluffing — the book asks directly what happens when they call it.

### [WWP:REFERRAL-DECLINE] Decline a referred lead without damaging the referrer

### [WWP:THIRD-WAY] Look for a third way before disqualifying on process

### [WWP:REGROUP] Book the Value Conversation with the missing seniors
**Move:** Summarize surface fit and comparable prior engagements, then request a short specific block (around 30 minutes) with the named missing decision makers to understand success metrics and quantify value, framing the proposal as the output; close with an alternative-date question rather than an open ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** Value creation sits above your current contact, or your own qualifier is too junior to run a Value Conversation with an executive.
**AVOID WHEN:** The person in front of you is genuinely the value owner.

### [WWP:MODEL-WALK] Walk the client through your codified visual model
**Move:** Right after qualifying, ask permission to spend a few minutes showing how you work with clients so they can see what an engagement looks like; the model implies that deviating from it lowers the odds of the outcome they want.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have genuinely codified delivery into a visual model; competitive situations where you want control early and want to resist a pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** No real codified process exists — an improvised model reads as theater.

### [WWP:NEEDS-ASSESS] Needs assessment to corral early-stage and split committees

**APPLY WHEN:** Early-stage (interest, not intent) opportunity; siloed or conflicted stakeholder group; a contact frustrated at being unable to align peers.

### Stage: Value Conversation — outcomes, metrics, value, pricing guidance

### [WWP:FOUR-Q] The four Value Conversation questions
**Move:** Run the client through four questions, all about them: what they want; what will be measured to prove they got it; what hitting those metrics is worth; what they would be willing to pay to get it. Discuss no solutions until all four are answered.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualified opportunity, executive or senior decision maker present, customized delivery and/or pricing available.
**AVOID WHEN:** Delivery and pricing are both standardized — then the fourth question is skipped and you go straight to a templated proposal and the Closing Conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the book's core framework, exposited through one worked dialogue.

### [WWP:DAY-TO-DAY] Make the vision personal via daily reality
**Move:** After the corporate vision, ask what has changed in their day-to-day reality; record personal outcomes (morale, freed-up time) alongside the corporate one, and stop probing at signs of diminishing relevance (watch-glancing, dismissal of the specifics).
**APPLY WHEN:** Rapport is opening; an executive with personal exposure to the outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** Signals of impatience appear — the book stops the inquiry there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:ASSETS] Return to optimism by cataloguing assets
**Move:** After the pre-mortem, deliberately change tack and ask what is already in place that makes them optimistic — team, standing with the board, brand equity — and note them aloud; obstacles and assets both feed solution design later.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after obstacle work, to repair the emotional tone before metrics.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication given.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:DFS-RECAP] Recap and confirm before moving on
**Move:** Summarize vision, personal outcomes, obstacles and assets in one pass and ask whether you got it right; repeat the same pattern after metrics and after value.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every step boundary of the Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never skipped in the book's model.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:METRIC-WE] Collaborative metric-setting
**Move:** Ask how *we* would measure that rather than supplying the metric. For a hard outcome, get baseline and target explicitly; for a soft outcome with no obvious measure, work toward at least one candidate (an employee NPS for morale, for instance), and let the client reset a metric's timeframe if they prefer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Step 2 with any outcome, including soft ones.
**AVOID WHEN:** Inventing metrics unilaterally — metric ownership belongs to the client.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:SELL-DIAGNOSTIC] Sell a paid diagnostic when baseline data is missing
**Move:** Where neither baseline nor target exists, propose carving a defined amount out of budget for a baseline analysis and target-setting, framed as helping them make the internal case for the investment; it also gives you the data to scope and price.
**APPLY WHEN:** Agreed metric but no data; complex sale; internal case-building needed.
**AVOID WHEN:** Metrics and baselines already exist — it becomes an unnecessary delay.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:ORDER-MAG] Get order-of-magnitude economics without demanding proprietary data
**Move:** Convert the metric to topline (market size × points of share), then push for margin; if refused, say you are not after trade secrets, offer a category-average margin yourself, do the arithmetic aloud, propose a rounded figure and ask if you can work with it. Read the non-verbal response as ratification rather than forcing a confirmation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Step 3 with a quantifiable commercial metric and a client who withholds margins.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your guessed category average would be visibly wrong — a scoff means recalibrate.

**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:LEAVE-UNPRICED] Leave non-economic value unquantified
**Move:** When the client names value that is a feeling — an enjoyable workplace, effectiveness, a high-performing culture — paraphrase it into your notes as-is rather than forcing a dollar conversion; do the economic arithmetic only where a threshold of quantified value has not yet been reached.
**APPLY WHEN:** Economic value is already large enough to carry the pricing conversation, or the domain is inherently non-economic (residential architecture, counseling, healthcare, financial advice where peace of mind dominates return).
**AVOID WHEN:** No economic anchor has been established at all and pricing guidance would have nothing to stand on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "value is a feeling" is asserted; the interior-design five-forms figure is illustrative.

### [WWP:ANCHOR-CLOSED] Anchor high with a closed question tied to value
**Move:** After recapping value, name a large figure inside a closed question — would you invest X to generate Y. If asked what you would do for that fee, answer honestly that you have not thought about solutions yet and return to the question. Anchor magnitude follows three factors in order: value to be created, your contribution to creating it, likelihood of creation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Step 4, executive present, value quantified to the right order of magnitude.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not transcended solutions — the reference point then reverts to competitors' market price or your input costs and the anchor fails. Also inapplicable where your contribution to the value is minor (a small piece of a larger campaign), where a fifth-of-value ask invites ridicule.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — anchoring is cited to endnotes without a verifiable named source in the text.

### [WWP:GUARANTEE-ANCHOR] Anchor against guaranteed value
**Move:** When the anchor is rejected outright, first reclaim the right to think creatively about how the value gets created, then pose a hypothetical: if the return were guaranteed, would they invest the anchor amount. Keep restating that this is hypothetical and that you are not committing to a guarantee. Their guaranteed-case number is the theoretical ceiling and encodes their estimate of your contribution.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client pushes back hard on the anchor and you want the true maximum without committing to performance terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** You would be read as promising a guarantee — the book resets that expectation repeatedly inside the same exchange.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:PERMISSION-TRADE] Trade budget-compliance for permission to think expansively
**Move:** When the client refuses to consider anything above their approved budget, commit to showing what you can do at their number and, in exchange, ask permission to brief your team to think expansively about maximum certainty of value, with the right to present those ideas; name the goal — putting them in the position of wanting to bring their boss in — while leaving the budget option available.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sub-executive contact, budget-locked, The Flip has not happened.
**AVOID WHEN:** They refuse the trade — the book says such a client should have been qualified out earlier.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:LOW-END] Let the client set the floor of the range
**Move:** Ask directly whether there is a number they would like you to hit at the low end; if they will not name one, set it yourself using your minimum level of engagement, and accept a wide range as desirable.
**APPLY WHEN:** No allocated budget surfaced during qualifying.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication stated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:OPTIONS-PROMISE] Close the Value Conversation by promising options
**Move:** State that you will return with several ways to engage at different price points — a top option (possibly guaranteed) near the ceiling figure, an option at their budget, and at least one in between. If they arrived with tight specifications, promise to price those too, unless you regard the spec as a gross misdiagnosis. Name the trade explicitly: they get their price and their spec priced; you get to transcend both and be heard out.
**APPLY WHEN:** End of every Value Conversation where customization is available.
**AVOID WHEN:** Standardized service and standardized price — skip to the Closing Conversation with a templated proposal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### Stage: Pricing and proposal construction

### [WWP:ONE-PAGE] One-page proposal as constraint
**Move:** Force every proposal onto one page: strip out contract terms and persuasion material, leaving solutions, outcomes and price standing alone; be prepared to speak to what is not written and to formalize the rest in the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every proposal in this model.
**AVOID WHEN:** Not stated — treated as general discipline; claimed side-effects are lower cost of sale and higher closing ratio.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — closing-ratio and cost-of-sale benefits stated without data.

### [WWP:THREE-OPT] Three-option proposal
**Move:** Build exactly three options — an expensive anchor, an inexpensive one, and a middle. Four can work but less cleanly; four to five is where choice overload begins; two provides context but forfeits extremeness aversion.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any customized engagement after a Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Any option you would consider wrong — if you would not honor it, it does not belong on the page.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — extremeness aversion and the paradox of choice invoked via endnotes with no verifiable named source in text; the "75% positive outcomes" argument is arithmetic on outcome labels, not evidence.

### [WWP:WALK-DOWN] Walk the anchor down when pricing options
**Move:** Set the top option below the client's guaranteed-case ceiling if you are not offering guarantees, preserving the pattern of improving prices; price the budget option at exactly the client's number with no upward creep; place the middle by judgment — the midpoint is a workable start and is the price most likely to move after scoping.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pricing a three-option proposal off Value Conversation guidance.
**AVOID WHEN:** Raising the client's budget figure — the book treats that as breaking the bargain; the middle option exists for that purpose.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked example $2m → $1m → $750k anchor, $425k middle, $100k budget.

### [WWP:PROFIT-OFF-TABLE] Take profit off the table before scoping
**Move:** Deduct an explicit gross profit from the anchor price and hand only the remainder to the scoping team as a cost budget; treat that deduction as a measure of selling and pricing skill, distinct from operational profit realized at utilization thresholds.
**APPLY WHEN:** Firms that price and sell time with rates built loosely on salary plus markup.
**AVOID WHEN:** Firms that conflate price with cost will brief the full price as a spend budget — named as the mistake.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $750k price, $250k profit removed, $500k to scoping; utilization threshold roughly 60–70%.

### [WWP:PRICE-ABOVE] Price above the anchor (narrow exception)
**Move:** Go above the Value Conversation anchor only when you can still make the ROI case — you left money on the table, discovered additional value, or are deliberately looking past a budget-limited manager to senior decision makers; almost always this requires some form of performance pay with a guarantee element.
**APPLY WHEN:** Confident ROI case at the higher number and a path to the senior decision makers.
**AVOID WHEN:** It contradicts the improving-price principle and requires the gatekeeping manager's consent to reach the seniors — described as a delicate game.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:PERF-PAY] Performance pay structures
**Move:** Structure a small base for a minimum deliverable set plus incentives tied to lagging indicators (units sold, revenue share) or leading ones (leads generated, channel partners created, purchase-intent change); consider waiving the fee entirely for pure incentives, and consider removing the cap when the risk you absorb outstrips the capped reward.
**APPLY WHEN:** The anchor option where you are willing to take risk off the client and measurable KPIs exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** Reward and risk are misaligned; and never use incentive structuring to make the headline number look smaller.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:BIG-NUMBER] Put the full KPI-hit price at the foot of the anchor column
**Move:** Show the price the client pays when KPIs are met as one large figure at the bottom of the anchor column, with the earning mechanics above it in the option detail and payment cadence there or in the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** The anchor option uses performance pay.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are tempted toward a palatable blended figure — the anchor is not meant to be palatable; its job is to make the middle look reasonable and push the client past their initial brief.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion.

### [WWP:SCOPE-ANCHOR] Scope the anchor against the cost constraint
**Move:** With the cost budget fixed, revisit Value Conversation notes and ask what the most is you can do for this client at that cost; list value drivers (inputs, outputs, processes, tools), assemble the anchor from them, define how each is packaged and contained, put the most obvious details on the page and be ready to speak to the rest.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-Value-Conversation scoping with customized delivery.
**AVOID WHEN:** No caveat given; the book notes it may push you past your own business model (outside partners, new hires, even acquisitions) with no obligation to take that on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — management-consultancy and marketing-agency value-driver examples.

### [WWP:BUDGET-HONEST] Scope the budget option ruthlessly
**Move:** Show what you can profitably do for their stated budget, stripping out value drivers they hoped to get; you owe them an option at their number, not their full specification at that number.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client stated an allocated budget.
**AVOID WHEN:** Optimistic scoping — overoptimism at the low end is named a business-killer, and client disappointment at this tier is acceptable because what they want lives in the middle option.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — "destroyed untold businesses" stated without cases.

### [WWP:PHASE-BELOW] Offer a phased first step below their budget
**Move:** As the cheapest option, offer a small audit or diagnostic as phase one of the anchor engagement, letting them test the relationship and build an internal case for the remaining spend; their budget can then sit in the middle.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-anchor engagement, risk-averse client, internal approval needed for the larger number.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication given.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $750k anchor, $100k middle, $50k phased step.

### [WWP:SPEC-MIDDLE] Put their own specification in the middle at your price
**Move:** When the client came with detailed specs, show the stripped version at their budget and their full specification as the middle option priced by you; iterate, checking the middle against both your cost floor and the gravity of the anchor, and strip value drivers from the budget option if it starts to look like overdelivery.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client arrived with a specified solution and a budget below its real price.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their spec is a gross misdiagnosis — you are not obliged to price it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — ecommerce site example: $100k budget, $225k cost floor, $350k judgment middle, $750k anchor.

### [WWP:TERMS-LEVER] Use payment terms to make expensive options affordable
**Move:** Attach the most generous terms to the most expensive option, fewer instalments to the middle, and none — possibly payment in advance — to the cheapest.
**APPLY WHEN:** You suspect the client is choosing down on affordability rather than preference.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cash-flow exposure you cannot carry — not addressed in the book.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $60k over 12 × $5k, $22.5k over 3 × $7.5k, $10k prepaid.

### [WWP:NAME-EXPLAIN] Name options to explain, not to persuade
**Move:** Choose names conveying differences in focus, inputs, outputs, value or engagement model, escalating through three expressions: differently sized buckets of the same inputs (lowest form), three levels of problem solving (Site Refresh / Full Ecomm Rebuild / Full Rebuild and Integrated Campaign), or three ways of doing business (Performance Pay / Deliverable / Retainer). Standardized businesses frame this once and revisit periodically; customized businesses do it per sale; some blend the two by keeping a custom high-end option for larger clients.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every multi-option proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Good/Better/Best or Gold/Silver/Bronze naming — judgment-laden names are rejected as a late lapse into persuasion after a whole sale spent not persuading.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:FINAL-SWEEP] Final relational check on the page
**Move:** Review solution, price, cost, terms and names together and confirm that at a glance the differences are legible and no obvious question is begged — notably why one option costs so much more; treat client selections, prices paid and questions asked as feedback that tunes the next proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before sending or presenting any multi-option proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Not applicable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the claimed upward march of closing ratio and average proposal value is unmeasured.

### Stage: Closing Conversation — principles and agenda

### [WWP:PROCESS-REASSURE] Answer "how will this work" as reassurance, not information
**Move:** Treat process questions as requests for reassurance that you are not out of your depth and answer them in detail, ideally by sharing a codified visual model of the journey, from which the client can infer that low process variability means low outcome variability.
**APPLY WHEN:** Closing Conversation, any "how" question, especially in large or emotionally costly sales.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no real process to describe — the surgeon analogy shows improvisation reads as danger.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — surgeon analogy, asserted mechanism.

### [WWP:REFOCUS-CALM] Acknowledge the investment and refocus on their vision
**Move:** Name the size of the investment, restate that your objective is the value they enumerated, say there are different ways to get there, and move straight into laying out the options and asking which fits best — calm and clinical rather than argumentative.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client shows late-stage anxiety about cost or risk.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as persuasion — the decision stays with the client.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:TIMECHECK] Open with a timecheck
**Move:** Confirm at the start how much time everyone actually has, including anyone who must leave early, and adjust granularity accordingly; aim for agreement in principle within the available time, then agree next steps while everyone is still present.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every Closing Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion.

### [WWP:ROLL-CALL] Roll call before proceeding
**Move:** Check who is present, who is missing and who has appeared unexpectedly — the surprise attendee is the decision maker you failed to find in qualifying; if someone essential is absent, use the brevity of the one-page proposal as leverage to proceed anyway and drive a repeat conversation with the missing person, rather than letting the client read them in.
**APPLY WHEN:** Start of every Closing Conversation; committee sales with spending-authority ceilings.
**AVOID WHEN:** Assuming you can close without the full decision set — proceeding is a tactic, not a close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:STATE-OBJ] State the objective and invite no
**Move:** Say up front that you will share three ways to engage and they will decide which, if any, makes sense; the explicit "if any" heads off the "leave it with me, I'll get back to you" outcome and preserves conversational dynamics.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every Closing Conversation, after roll call.
**AVOID WHEN:** No contraindication given.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:VC-RECAP] Recap the Value Conversation before showing options
**Move:** Walk the whole group back through desired future state, obstacles and assets, metrics with baselines and targets, value including what could not be quantified, and finally the pricing-guidance exchange — including that you asked for a large figure, they declined, they named a guaranteed-case ceiling and an approved budget, and you traded budget-compliance for creative latitude. Use notes; rehearse only for brevity.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every Closing Conversation, especially where attendees were absent from the Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Reading from or memorizing a script — the book says that instantly demotes you from expert to vendor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### Stage: Closing Conversation — sharing options

### [WWP:OPTPREVIEW] Announce a broad-strokes pass before details
**Move:** Open by telling the client you will walk all options at a high level first so they can be discussed as a set, and that questions come afterwards.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-option proposal, client live in the meeting, you want the set compared rather than one option litigated.
**AVOID WHEN:** Single-option engagement, or the client has already stated a choice and wants to sign.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's framework as taught in training; no data.

### [WWP:PRICEBEFOREPAPER] Say the anchor price aloud before sharing the document
**Move:** State the anchor option's price out loud first; only then put the proposal on screen or table, and pause a few seconds while the client scans names and prices.
**APPLY WHEN:** Live Closing Conversation with the economic buyer present.
**AVOID WHEN:** The proposal was emailed ahead (the anchoring order is already lost), or procurement-mediated processes where nobody in the room reacts to price.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted anchoring mechanism applied to the author's own sequence.

### [WWP:GUARANTEEUPFRONT] Handle the guarantee question inside the anchor's framing
**Move:** If a guarantee came up during the Value Conversation, address it in the same breath as the anchor — say plainly there is no guaranteed solution, then present the anchor as a novel option beyond the originally defined project, aimed at the transformative vision, with the price attached.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client raised risk or guarantees earlier and the anchor is performance- or transformation-flavored.
**AVOID WHEN:** No guarantee concern was voiced — raising it introduces doubt that was not there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one illustrative scripted scenario.

### [WWP:ANCHORSHORT] Keep the anchor overview to a few minutes
**Move:** Describe the anchor in broad strokes only, a few minutes at most, covering how it hits the client's DFS elements, metrics and value; resist front-loading detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** First delivery of a bold, above-brief option.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client explicitly asks for mechanics before anything else.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the book concedes practitioners overrun this at first and improve with practice; no measurement.

### [WWP:BUDGETLITERAL] Show what the stated budget literally buys
**Move:** Introduce the cheap option as a walk-through of what their own stated number covers and let the gap to the anchor be visible without arguing it — this is the option for someone checking a box, not pursuing transformation.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client gave a budget in Qualifying or the Value Conversation, possibly as an optimistic or negotiating figure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The cheap option was not scoped to be profitable on its own — never show a version you would resent delivering.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's stated practice.

### [WWP:MIDSAFEZONE] Frame the middle as the new safe zone
**Move:** Position the middle option verbally as sitting between the twin fears of overpaying and underbuying — a blend of fiscal responsibility and ambition — then give its price and broad strokes.
**APPLY WHEN:** Three-option structure with a client showing both ambition and budget anxiety.
**AVOID WHEN:** Only two options exist, or the middle is cosmetic filler you cannot actually deliver differently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — restates the extremeness-aversion rationale developed earlier in the book.

### Stage: Closing Conversation — facilitating the choice

### [WWP:GUTREACTION] Ask for the initial reaction before questions
**Move:** Before taking any questions, ask which option is most appealing on first impression; that answer routes the whole discussion and reveals whether the client is open to spending above their original budget and scope.
**APPLY WHEN:** Options have just been laid out and the client has had a moment to scan.
**AVOID WHEN:** Multiple stakeholders are present who would be forced into a public position prematurely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — typical replies from the author's experience.

### [WWP:UNATTACHEDFACILITATE] Facilitate rather than advocate
**Move:** Answer questions as a neutral facilitator, spelling out what is gained and given up in each option, and do not push toward the expensive one.
**APPLY WHEN:** The client is comparing options and asking substantive questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client has explicitly asked you to recommend — then recommend.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the principle is stated, no support offered.

### [WWP:ASKTORECOMMEND] Offer a recommendation only by invitation
**Move:** If the client stalls or seems paralyzed by choice, ask whether it would help for you to make a recommendation — then wait for permission.
**APPLY WHEN:** Visible decision paralysis; the client cannot converge.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client is progressing on their own; volunteering a recommendation early collapses the facilitator position into selling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's scripted line.

### Stage: Closing Conversation — driving the next step

### [WWP:NOUPSELLONYES] When they pick the cheap option, go to signature — not to an upsell
**Move:** Treat a yes on the budget option as a yes and move straight to contract instead of relitigating scope upward.
**APPLY WHEN:** The cheap option was scoped to be profitable and easy to deliver and the client has budget authority for it.
**AVOID WHEN:** The cheap option was priced as a loss-leader or decoy you cannot deliver at margin — the failure is upstream, in scoping.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — framed as the engagements that keep the lights on.

### [WWP:SAMEDAYPAPER] Commit to a contract on a named short deadline
**Move:** Close the meeting by naming when the contract arrives — end of day, for instance — and hold to it; standardized delivery means a contract is ready or hours away, customized work takes longer, but momentum with late-stage buyers is treated as the scarce resource.
**APPLY WHEN:** Agreement in principle is reached and the delivery model is standardized enough to paper quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** Scope is genuinely unresolved — a fast contract on unsettled scope buys a dispute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — the momentum claim is stated without evidence.

### [WWP:RESOLVEINROOM] Settle open issues on the cheaper options during the meeting
**Move:** Try to close out remaining questions on the two cheaper options inside the same conversation; expect the elaborate option and any performance-pay structure to need further rounds.
**APPLY WHEN:** The middle or budget option is chosen and the details are small.
**AVOID WHEN:** A large corporate buyer whose procurement and vendor-onboarding steps impose their own paperwork regardless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the book reports scope back-and-forth clusters on the expensive options and on performance pay.

### [WWP:INSISTONJOINTPITCH] Use the one-pager's brevity to get into the senior conversation
**Move:** When a budget-constrained manager offers to take the bigger options to senior decision makers alone, push back by arguing the one-page proposal is only a catalyst for the kind of conversation you just had, and ask them to agree the same conversation should happen with their colleagues.
**APPLY WHEN:** The champion is willing to spend political capital upward and the economic buyer was absent from the Closing Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Hard-lining it would burn a nervous champion — the book says the minimum obligation is to make the request, not to win it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — scripted line, no outcome data.

### [WWP:CONDITIONALJOINT] Bank a conditional commitment when refused
**Move:** If they still insist on going alone, secure agreement that any interest short of straight approval on the larger options triggers a joint conversation with all parties.
**APPLY WHEN:** The direct ask was declined but the champion is still escalating.
**AVOID WHEN:** No larger option is realistically in play — the conditional then just adds friction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's fallback script.

### Stage: Model-level — running and adapting the system

### [WWP:CLOSEAGENDA7] Run the seven-point Closing agenda
**Move:** Structure the closing meeting as timecheck, roll call, state the objective, recap the Value Conversation, share the options, facilitate discussion and choice, drive the next step.
**APPLY WHEN:** A Value Conversation has already agreed metrics and value, and a multi-option proposal exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** Value has not been established or the economic buyer is absent — running the agenda then closes on the wrong person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:BENDFRAMEWORKKEEPPRINCIPLE] Adapt frameworks, hold principles
**Move:** Rewrite, shorten or swap any of the four frameworks to fit your business, but keep the behavioral principles intact as the standard for how an expert behaves.

**AVOID WHEN:** The adaptation would drop a principle (dropping facilitation and reverting to presenting, for instance) — that is not adaptation, it is reversion to vendor behavior.

## Where this book stops working

- **Transactional and product motions.** The founding premise (the sale is a working sample; roles set during the sale persist) is explicitly scoped to expertise. In retail, SaaS self-serve and product sales the book itself grants that buyer tolerance for aggressive selling is high and 1/n competitive strategy is sound. WWP:SAMPLE-CONDUCT and WWP:ADVANTAGED name this boundary.
- **Regulated, government and public-interest procurement.** WWP:BID-LAST carries the book's own carve-out; WWP:CONVERSE-NOT-PRESENT and WWP:REVEALED-PREF break for the same reason. Where the process cannot lawfully be bent, most of the process-objection repertoire is unusable and the deal reverts to vendor terms by law.
- **Low-ACV and high-velocity work.** The value-then-price sequence assumes an executive who will spend an hour on vision, metrics and economics. Below the band where a Value Conversation pays for itself, WWP:FOUR-Q, WWP:ORDER-MAG and WWP:ANCHOR-CLOSED cost more than they return, and the book's own answer is standardized delivery plus standardized pricing, which skips step four entirely.
- **Standardized offers and rate cards.** The book treats a product catalogue or rate card as a handicap; firms whose economics require them get little from the pricing chapters beyond WWP:THREE-OPT and WWP:NAME-EXPLAIN.
- **Contributions that are a small slice of the value.** WWP:ANCHOR-CLOSED states its own failure case: where your contribution is minor, a value-share ask invites ridicule.
- **Sub-executive and process-manager buyers.** A Value Conversation with a pure process-runner is described as fruitless; WWP:PREMORTEM-Q, WWP:PERSONAL-MANDATE and WWP:JUNIOR-FALLBACK all bend around the fact that value creation is an executive remit.
- **Domains with no economic anchor.** WWP:LEAVE-UNPRICED works only when either large quantified value already exists or the domain is inherently non-economic; between those, pricing guidance has nothing to stand on.
- **Independence limit.** Because this is the same author and same training business as the Manifesto, its agreement with the Manifesto adds no independent weight. Two ideas are also acknowledged as borrowed with unknown origin (the "sale is the sample" phrasing and the Magic Email), and the Transtheoretical Model is imported by analogy from health behavior change, which the book concedes was not built to describe buying.

## What the book debunks

- Generic sales playbooks (software, cars, mobile plans, timeshares) do not transfer to selling expertise.
- The "win as vendor, get promoted to expert later" plan: roles are assigned during the sale and status, once traded for money, rarely returns.
- "The customer is always right" is treated as a service-sector import that is destructive in expertise work.
- "Broad expertise" is called an oxymoron; a perspective that resonates with everyone is meaningless, and first-mover advantage from a perspective decays as it goes mainstream.
- Ideology built for provocation or political advantage, or aimed at people and organizations rather than ideas and conventions, is called poison.
- Extrapolating P = Db/D into suppressing or hiding desire: minimizing expressed desire reads as aloof and arrogant.
- Scripts: memorizing lines is treated as evidence you are preparing a monologue; frameworks are visible structures, scripts are vendor behavior.
- Rebutting your own objection after raising it — a self-inflicted signal of neediness.
- Standard "overcoming objections" training is subordinated to keeping the dynamic two-way.
- Blaming the disappearing client: post-proposal silence is attributed to the seller's own neediness making honest refusal hard.
- Interest read as intent — the common, costly error of throwing expensive resource at "that's interesting, tell me more."
- Believing what people say about their own authority: they understate the approvals above them, so the Decision Makers quadrant is where information is most often missed. No access during the sale predicts no access during delivery.
- The idea that the seller's job is to explain their value: even nominally economic value is personal, and three people who agree on outcomes, metrics and economics can still prefer different vendors and prices for personal reasons each will rationalize as organizational. Financial advisors are singled out as chronically misreading the value their clients seek.
- The proposal-as-Hail-Mary (throw everything in) is a symptom of being in the vendor position, not a cause of losing; conflating proposal, contract and persuasion deck is its mechanical form.
- Overoptimistic scoping of the cheap option is named as widely destructive; using incentive framing to shrink the visible anchor defeats the anchor's purpose.
- Good/Better/Best and Gold/Silver/Bronze naming is rejected as persuasion smuggled in at the end.
- Treating the close as do-or-die: the book argues overweighting the close is what produces mistakes, and that the anxious buyer at closing is the same optimist from qualifying, so inspiration is the wrong tool there.
- Steering the client toward your preferred option is out of bounds; you may recommend only when asked or when they are visibly stuck.
- Closing speed as a proxy for deal quality: the cheap option closes faster precisely because it raises fewer questions.
- Procurement's hard-nosed posture after an option is selected is described as leverage-free theater.
- Assuming a one-page proposal can persuade in your absence — an elegant document will not rescue a champion nervous about asking for money.
- Rigorous qualifying degenerating into intellectually lazy refusal; poor fit today is not poor fit forever.
- The model's own linearity: the book concedes the four conversations are neither reliably linear nor discrete, so the sequence is a lens for organizing a sale, not a description of what happens.
- Where the book cites "research" loosely (anchoring, extremeness aversion, paradox of choice), it is best read as assertion — the endnotes are not resolved to verifiable named sources in the text.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No outcome data of any kind.** Every mechanism — anchoring order, three options, the Magic Email, concession-seeking, the one-page proposal — is supported by worked dialogue or the author's recollection. There is no win-rate, no price lift, no A/B, no cohort. Claimed effects (higher closing ratio, lower cost of sale, rising average proposal value) are asserted.
- **Single-firm, single-market origin.** The system grew out of one consultant's practice with creative and marketing firms and was then generalized; the "12+ fields" claim is self-reported by the training business that sells it.
- **Survivorship and selection.** The buyers described are ones who tolerated the posture. The book has nothing to say about the deals lost to firms that simply answered the RFP, and no way to compare.
- **The seller's own market position is assumed.** Almost every move presumes a defensible narrow claim and enough demand to walk away. For a practitioner without either, the advice is not merely hard but inverted, and the book prices this exception only as a resolve problem.
- **Team and scale.** Written for the principal who sells. Little on hiring, compensating or managing sellers, on handoffs between qualifier and expert beyond WWP:REGROUP, on CRM discipline, forecasting or pipeline math.
- **Buying committees beyond a handful.** Roll call and the joint-conversation plays assume a small named set. Six-to-ten-stakeholder enterprise buying, internal politics, and multi-quarter procurement cycles are largely out of frame.
- **Post-sale reality.** The premise is that the sale sets delivery, but delivery, renewal, expansion and churn are outside the book. Whether the anchored price survives contact with the work is untested here.
- **Borrowed psychology, thinly sourced.** Anchoring, extremeness aversion and the paradox of choice are used as load-bearing mechanisms via endnotes without verifiable named sources in the text; the Transtheoretical Model is applied to buying by analogy, which the book itself flags as outside its design.
- **Ethics of the anchor left implicit.** The anchor is defended as an invitation the client is free to decline, but the book does not examine when a deliberately non-palatable top number crosses from framing into pressure, nor what happens to a buyer who lacks the sophistication to read the structure.
- **No account of the client's own economics.** Value is quantified through the client's numbers as they report them, with the seller supplying category-average margins when refused. Systematic client overstatement is not treated as a risk.
