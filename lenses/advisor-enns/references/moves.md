# advisor-enns — Moves

Layer: **услуги: позиция эксперта** (expert position in expertise services). Citation prefix `[WWP:]`.

Sources merged here: *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto* (2010, canon) and *The Four Conversations* (2023, second-source). **Same author, same training business** — where both back a move, that is one practitioner's system stated twice, never independent corroboration. Evidence ceiling for the whole lens is `practitioner-anecdote`; most entries sit one rung below it at `assertion`.

Nine entries below are merges of moves that appeared in both books with the same action; the Manifesto code is kept as primary and the second-source name is noted in Evidence. Where the two books genuinely disagree (written vs. spoken proposal, minimum stated early vs. late, always recommend vs. recommend only when asked), both moves are kept and the contradiction is documented in `myths-and-demotions.md`.

## Stage — Positioning, ideology and demand creation (before any opportunity)

### [WWP:FOCUS3] Three-step positioning: focus, claim, proof
**Move:** Pick one focus expressed as discipline-for-market and shut the door on the alternatives; compress it into a one-or-two-sentence public claim of who you help and how; then deliberately acquire the skills, documented processes and written arguments that make the claim defensible. Narrow one or both axes until your depth exceeds the alternatives the client can perceive, and accept lost breadth as the price.
**APPLY WHEN:** the firm is undifferentiated, RFPs and free pitches dominate the pipeline, deals routinely turn on price because competitors look interchangeable.
**AVOID WHEN:** no delivery history exists at all in the chosen field (nothing substantiates the claim yet — see WWP:PROBONO); a single dominant client funds everything and the focus would strand it; or depth already exists and only awareness is missing, where the fix is communication rather than further narrowing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from first principles about substitutes and power; the second source restates the same proclamation, which is continuation of one author's system, not confirmation.

### [WWP:NARROWCLAIM] Claim narrower than capability
**Move:** State expertise deliberately narrower than the full range of what you can do, and let the narrow claim earn the buyer's assumption of surrounding competence. Aim at the small target and take the wider market that follows.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing positioning and website copy; first contact with a prospect who is self-selecting.
**AVOID WHEN:** the narrow claim would exclude the only segment currently able to pay you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a claim about how buyers infer competence from breadth, with no supporting data.

### [WWP:IDEOLOGY] Make the final battle one of ideology
**Move:** Articulate a belief about how your discipline should be practiced in your market that your closest specialist competitors do not hold, then build content and your way of working through that lens, polarizing in proportion to competitive density.
**APPLY WHEN:** mature, dense markets where specialization alone no longer separates you.
**AVOID WHEN:** early or thin markets where a perspective is not required; and whenever the ideology would aim at people or organizations rather than at ideas and conventions — the source calls that toxic.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own positioning practice; the polarization claim is asserted, never tested.

### [WWP:PRICETEST] Positioning test — win more AND charge more
**Move:** Judge positioning on two things measured together: do you win more often than not where you choose to compete, and do you win while charging a premium rather than by cutting price. Failing the second means you are outside your focus or never made the focus decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** periodic review of whether specialization is real; after a run of won deals, to check how they were won.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is one or two deals, or the wins came from existing relationships rather than competitive selection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning from price elasticity and substitutes, stated without measurement.

### [WWP:WRITEDEEP] Write long-form on the focus area
**Move:** Publish substantive writing on the narrow territory rather than restatements of the field's clichés. It does three jobs at once: gets you found, proves expertise, and tightens your own arguments.
**APPLY WHEN:** the prospect is unaware of a problem; the pipeline needs a source that does not depend on chasing meetings.
**AVOID WHEN:** you expect near-term deals from it — this is a farmer's timeline, not a hunter's.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a rule about what experts do, with no measured outcomes.

### [WWP:LADDER-CLIMB] Balance the lead-generation ladder over time
**Move:** Audit the current lead-gen mix against three rungs — selling, marketing, educating — where status rises and immediacy falls as you climb; keep lower-rung activity for immediacy while starting the education-based work (a book, a serious speaking slot) whose payoff arrives years later, shifting weight upward as expertise deepens.
**APPLY WHEN:** planning pipeline for a firm whose deals routinely open in the vendor position; early or lean stages where immediacy is still required.
**AVOID WHEN:** revenue is needed this quarter and education work is treated as a substitute rather than an addition — the trade-off is immediacy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own model, illustrated by his practice; no measured lift.

### [WWP:ARMOR-PIERCE] Armor-piercing outreach
**Move:** In cold outreach, lead with the single narrow thing you do better than anyone rather than a service menu: acknowledge the incumbent relationship explicitly, state the one wedge capability, ask whether a need exists, and leave the recipient free to refuse or redirect you to an adjacent need.
**APPLY WHEN:** outbound into accounts that already have agency or consulting relationships, and you hold a defensible narrow claim.
**AVOID WHEN:** no genuine wedge exists, or you would revert to listing everything you do.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — sample phrasing and a breadth/depth argument from the author's practice.

### [WWP:JOURNEY-MAP] Keep a map of the engagement journey
**Move:** Maintain a visible map of how an engagement runs — diagnosis, then prescription and planning, then execution or oversight — that carries your perspective on the page, ready for the moment a convinced client asks how the work will actually go.
**APPLY WHEN:** a buyer has bought the point of view and asks about mechanics; also as an alternative next step to a client-run selection process.
**AVOID WHEN:** the map is indistinguishable from competitors' — that is the moment the client learns your perspective was bait.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — illustrated with a hypothetical speaking-to-hire scenario.

### [WWP:FOURPHASE] Four-phase engagement frame
**Move:** Structure and describe every engagement as diagnose → prescribe → apply → reapply, and use the sequence to explain why work cannot begin mid-stream. It doubles later as the pricing skeleton (WWP:BIGROUND) and the phased-sale skeleton (WWP:PAIDDIAG).
**APPLY WHEN:** scoping conversations; explaining why a proposal cannot be written for free.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already completed a credible diagnosis with another party and is knowingly buying execution.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a model borrowed by analogy from medicine.

### [WWP:PROCESSDOC] Document the methodology and use it as reassurance
**Move:** Write down how you work, keep refining it, and deploy it late in the buying cycle as the reassurance asset — ideally as a codified visual model — so the buyer can infer that low variability in process means low variability in outcome. Treat every late "how will this work" question as a request for reassurance, not information, and answer it in detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage buyer asking small, detailed, sequential questions; buyer trying to impose his own process; large or emotionally costly purchases.
**AVOID WHEN:** the process was invented for the sale and cannot survive examination — improvisation in front of an anxious buyer reads as danger.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's advisory work plus a surgeon analogy in the second source; same author, so the two accounts are one source.

### [WWP:DIAGPROC] Formalize the diagnostic and insist on using it
**Move:** Map your own diagnostic method, name it, and when a buyer or procurement tries to dictate process, argue that the consistency of your outcomes depends on this process, so it must be the one used.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer or procurement is dictating steps; you need a legitimate reason to push back on a pitch format.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real repeatable method — an invented process collapses under questioning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — from advising firms; effectiveness asserted, never measured.

### [WWP:PROBATIVE-AUDIT] The four probative prompts
**Move:** Periodically answer four sets of questions in writing — your current focus and whether it must narrow; the problems you solve and the consistent words you use for them; whether an unspoken perspective underlies your methodology; and how you will climb the lead-gen ladder long-term while still generating leads today.
**APPLY WHEN:** annual or quarterly positioning review; onboarding a new seller into the firm's claim.
**AVOID WHEN:** substituted for live selling work — these are upstream strategy prompts, not conversation tools.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a checklist offered with a pointer to the author's paid resources.

### [WWP:LEARNCULT] Mandatory continuous-learning culture
**Move:** Hire for skill, train on the documented methods, have each person write a professional development plan you approve and fund, hold them to it, set the pace yourself, and move out those who will not keep up.
**APPLY WHEN:** after the firm commits to a focus and needs the claim substantiated quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo practice, or no defined processes yet — there is nothing to train on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a normative prescription about firm culture.

### [WWP:PROBONO] Separate charity from business development
**Move:** Choose pro bono clients by cause alignment and need, commit fully, and take no commercial benefit — no upsell path, no boardroom networking — then use that clean separation as the licence to require profit from every commercial engagement. Early in a firm's life, free or deeply discounted work inside the chosen focus is legitimate for building proof, provided you say out loud that this is the trade.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-stage firm with no proof in its new focus; decisions about charity work.
**AVOID WHEN:** the discount is quiet — undisclosed cheap work is competing on price and, prolonged, it cements price positioning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** assertion — normative, with the honesty condition stated but no time limit given beyond "for a period."

## Stage — Mindset and cross-stage navigating principles

### [WWP:SAMPLE-CONDUCT] Sell as the working sample of the engagement
**Move:** Before each sales interaction, decide which behaviors you want the client to expect in delivery — composure, preparedness, discernment, efficiency — and perform exactly those, treating persuasion tactics imported from product selling as off-limits.
**APPLY WHEN:** any expertise sale where you will personally advise or practice, from first contact onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** transactional product, SaaS or retail sales, where buyer tolerance for aggressive selling is high and post-sale roles are not set during the sale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the second source's founding premise, illustrated by counterexample (a rude clerk selling good coffee), with no data.

### [WWP:MISSIONOBJ] Mission position / objective fit
**Move:** Enter every interaction with two fixed intents: be seen as the expert practitioner and never trade that for a short-term gain, and establish only whether the fit between need and expertise justifies a next step. Selling, convincing and persuading are out of scope.
**APPLY WHEN:** every buying-cycle touch; usable as a pre-call checklist.
**AVOID WHEN:** cash pressure makes short-term revenue the actual goal — the model is then being used dishonestly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a doctrinal statement of purpose.

### [WWP:THREE-ORIENT] The three orienting questions
**Move:** At any point in a live deal, silently answer three questions: which of the four conversations is this, what is that conversation's objective, and which principles and framework navigate toward it.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-call disorientation; deals that blur across stages; coaching someone through a stalled opportunity.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as rigid — the author concedes the conversations blur and the boundaries are not clean.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — offered explicitly as a utility claim rather than a truth claim.

### [WWP:MANTRA] Recite the Expert's Mantra before contact
**Move:** In a quiet minute before a call or meeting, say the four lines to yourself — you are the prize, you are on a mission to help, you can only help if they let you lead, not all will follow — to leave the vendor mindset before applying any framework.
**APPLY WHEN:** any direct client interaction, especially with a history of deferential selling.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a swagger prompt by someone whose bias is a need to be respected — that path ends in an arrogant know-it-all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's training device; the claim that belief follows recitation is asserted.

### [WWP:DETACH-PERSONAL] Name and park the personal need
**Move:** Identify which of three needs is yours — to be liked, to win, to be respected — and set it aside before the call: push relationship out to a reward earned after value creation; refuse convince mode; or pair the need for respect with silence and urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** you notice a recurring behavioral tell across deals — over-accommodating, over-arguing, over-talking.
**AVOID WHEN:** read as permanent suppression of your natural self, which the source warns depletes energy and is unfair to demand.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a self-diagnosis typology from the author's coaching, with no instrument or validation.

### [WWP:P-EQ-DBD] Modulate expressed desire against expressed desirability
**Move:** Read the client's expressed interest in you and calibrate your own expressed enthusiasm to sit just under it — damping excitement with a subdued buyer, raising warmth with an enthusiastic one — rather than leaning on passion to win. The power relation is stated as P = Db/D: your power rises with their desire for you and falls with your expressed desire for the deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** any direct client conversation, especially when you notice yourself selling hard.
**AVOID WHEN:** pushed into feigned indifference — the source explicitly warns against reading as aloof or arrogant.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a formula given as a first principle, with nothing measured behind the ratio.

### [WWP:PASSIONHOLD] Keep passion off the table as a differentiator
**Move:** Do not lead with enthusiasm, chemistry or price. Treat them as tiebreakers only; leaning on them concedes that on expertise you are interchangeable, and visible eagerness invites the buyer to take control.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive situations, chemistry meetings, any moment the instinct is to show how much you want it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly weights cultural fit and expertise is already established — then passion is a legitimate tiebreak.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — an interpretation of buyer inference, unsupported.

### [WWP:NOOVERINVEST] Keep visible investment in the sale low
**Move:** Deliberately limit what you visibly sink into winning — no travel, no unpaid external research, no large time blocks early — so that neither your objectivity nor your power erodes with escalating commitment, and invite the buyer to say no early and often.
**APPLY WHEN:** any opportunity where the temptation is to show how much you want it; early-stage deals before fit is established; competitive processes with heavy unpaid requirements.
**AVOID WHEN:** a concession has already proven you are the advantaged player and investment is now warranted; or a genuinely strategic account where a costly, deliberate bet is a considered decision — a case the sources do not cover.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — both books describe the mechanism (visible investment signals need; sunk cost erodes judgment) and neither measures it.

### [WWP:CLOSE-GAP] Say what you are thinking, immediately
**Move:** The moment you notice yourself nodding yes while thinking no, stop and voice the disagreement with a permission-seeking opener — asking to push back, to challenge an assertion, to test the assumptions underneath it, or to raise a process concern — pausing only long enough to find kind words.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live conversation where the client's framing of problem, solution or process seems wrong.
**AVOID WHEN:** the impulse is venting rather than substance; the standard is ruthless about the gap and kind in the words.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a causal story about relationship blow-ups arising from accumulated unspoken disagreement.

### [WWP:SILENCE] Embrace silence after anything contentious
**Move:** After raising an objection, saying no, asking for a concession or floating a price, stop talking and let the client speak next, however long the pause runs.
**APPLY WHEN:** every contentious moment in qualifying and pricing.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication is given; the documented failure mode is your own discomfort producing unprompted concessions.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the claim that tolerance grows from about three seconds to much longer with practice is self-reported.

### [WWP:SAY-NO] Say no, and reserve the right to retract
**Move:** Decline poor fits, arduous demands and inappropriate next steps out loud, offering a referral elsewhere where appropriate, and treat every no as potentially "not now" — any obstacle you place can be withdrawn later.
**APPLY WHEN:** qualifying, when fit criteria are not met or the process is unacceptable.
**AVOID WHEN:** overplayed into performative discernment — the source wants the possibility of no to be felt, not brandished.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the claims that neediness repels and that a yes without a possible no is worthless.

### [WWP:HEAR-NO] Work a no instead of fearing it
**Move:** Treat no as the second-best answer — better than maybe, "we'll get back to you," or silence — and respond by mirroring the word back as a question, asking directly what the obstacle is, or setting it aside explicitly to return to after further questions.
**APPLY WHEN:** any stage, especially early, to kill unfit opportunities before sunk costs accrue.
**AVOID WHEN:** your discomfort traces to unchecked desire or over-investment — the fix is then upstream, at WWP:P-EQ-DBD and WWP:NOOVERINVEST.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — three sample responses; the claim that good fits survive a no is asserted.

### [WWP:INVITE-NO] Preface awkward requests with permission to refuse
**Move:** Before a request that might be awkward — access to a CEO, a change of process — say explicitly that they are free to refuse; if they do, follow with a question about what would need to happen for them to become comfortable.
**APPLY WHEN:** you sense the client hedging, softening or withholding.
**AVOID WHEN:** used mechanically on trivial asks, where it manufactures weight instead of removing it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an attributed emotional mechanism (you are the source of the weight), with no measurement.

### [WWP:PREEMPTOBJ] Win the race to object
**Move:** As soon as you sense an objection forming — price, category experience, team size, location, fit — state it plainly yourself as a gap between what they want and what you offer, then stop and leave the silence to the client instead of rebutting yourself. If an objection is going to kill the deal, kill it early.
**APPLY WHEN:** early discovery and qualifying, whenever a concern is visibly present but unspoken, especially around budget, fit and control.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot resist bridging the gap yourself — self-rebuttal signals neediness; and when deployed as manipulation to trigger a defensive "of course we can afford you," which is precisely where the tactic's leverage over the buyer's self-image gets misused.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — both books describe the objection dynamic with sample openers; neither reports outcomes.

### [WWP:POLICYLANG] Frame requirements as policy, and compromise when two policies collide
**Move:** Convert your requirements into written policy statements and deliver them in that framing — as a matter of policy you do not start solving a client's problems before being engaged, or do not write proposals for people you have never met — rather than as preferences or hopes. When the buyer answers with a policy of their own, propose a human compromise: ask the gatekeeper to secure a short call with one senior decision maker, with a stated agreement to withdraw gracefully if it fails.
**APPLY WHEN:** any request to work before engagement; deposit and payment requirements; gatekeepers blocking access; bureaucratic buyers with professional purchasers.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "policy" is fabricated for one deal and you would abandon it under mild pressure; or the objection is better left as a preference so discussion stays open (see WWP:RFP-DECLINE).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author reports less resistance with this framing and supplies worked dialogue; no comparison data.

### [WWP:3MODES] Match the mode to the buying stage
**Move:** Diagnose which of three states the buyer is in and act accordingly: help the unaware with education and no push, inspire the interested by showing what is possible, reassure those who have formed intent with calm, sequence and detail.
**APPLY WHEN:** every opportunity, from first contact onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot yet read the stage — misreading it produces the documented failure modes (inspiration aimed at the unaware creates defensiveness; excitement aimed at the committed feeds remorse).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the author's own construction, presented without evidence; the underlying behavior-change model is imported by analogy from health research that was not built to describe buying.

### [WWP:BENDFRAMEWORKKEEPPRINCIPLE] Adapt frameworks, hold principles
**Move:** Rewrite, shorten or swap any of the four frameworks to fit your business, but keep the behavioral principles intact as the standard for how an expert behaves.
**APPLY WHEN:** adapting the model to a different field of expertise or a different deal shape.
**AVOID WHEN:** the adaptation would drop a principle — reverting from facilitation to presenting, for instance — which is not adaptation but a return to vendor behavior.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a universal standard with no support.

## Stage — First contact, triage and qualification

### [WWP:INBOUND-TRIAGE] Sort inbound by origin to predict the sale
**Move:** For every inbound inquiry, ask how they found you and why they reached out; read a referral or deep content engagement as evidence the power dynamic has already flipped, and untracked search or ad arrivals as evidence you are one of several bidders and must engineer that flip inside the call.
**APPLY WHEN:** triaging inbound volume; deciding how much process to concede on a given deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to dismiss SEO or paid acquisition entirely — the source holds these are valuable, merely status-neutral.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a pattern recalled from the author's and clients' inbound, with no counts.

### [WWP:CONTEXT-FLIP-PROBE] Open with context, including how they found you
**Move:** Open the qualifying call with context questions — how they found you or why they came to you, plus basic organizational facts such as revenue size, customers served and geography, or your discipline's equivalent — modeled on a walk-in clinic's intake rather than an interview.
**APPLY WHEN:** the opening minutes of a qualifying call with any inbound lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered as interrogation instead of an opener.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — clinic-intake analogy plus a financial-planner example using net worth.

### [WWP:PURSUENO] Try to kill the opportunity early
**Move:** When an opportunity appears, actively test whether it should die: put money, the nature of the need, the client's willingness to be led, geography and the depth of your experience on the table straight away. Retreat slightly and watch whether the buyer follows — his following measures how much he values your expertise.
**APPLY WHEN:** a new opportunity, before any real resource is committed.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as theatre by a firm with no differentiated expertise — the retreat is then simply accepted and the deal is gone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the retreat-and-follow dynamic rests on an aphorism, not evidence.

### [WWP:QUAL-Q-GEN] Generate qualifying questions by the junior-qualifier exercise
**Move:** Imagine an eager junior repeatedly bringing you unfit meetings, then write out the answers you would demand before attending another; supplement with prompts about target-client variables, absolute requirements, red flags and absolute nos.
**APPLY WHEN:** building or refreshing a qualification standard; onboarding someone into the qualifying role.
**AVOID WHEN:** the output gets recited as a script — these are selectable tools, never all used at once.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a training exercise presented as the author's method.

### [WWP:CDFSDMTF] Organize questions in the five-drawer toolbox
**Move:** File every qualifying question under Context, Desired Future State, Decision Makers, Timeframe or Budget, keep one key question per category, always open with Context, then navigate the remaining four in whatever order the conversation suggests. "Need" is deliberately renamed Desired Future State because the expert is obliged to transcend the client's self-diagnosis.
**APPLY WHEN:** structuring a first person-to-person conversation with a new lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** over-engineering placement — asking the right question matters more than filing it correctly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an adaptation of BANT, which the source attributes to IBM in the 1950s.

### [WWP:DM-OPEN-NARROW] Open-then-narrow decision-maker mapping
**Move:** Open with a question that grants the contact authority while assuming it is not total — who else besides you needs to be involved in hiring a firm like ours — and if told nobody, keep probing: name the specific role you suspect, ask whether anyone must approve their decision once made, ask about outside consultants and about procurement's role.
**APPLY WHEN:** any qualifying conversation before committing resources, especially where a partner, a board or procurement is plausibly hidden.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skipped, but soften the tone if the contact already volunteered a full map — interrogation without acknowledgment reads as distrust.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an architect-and-married-couple story plus a "why are we even considering this" derailment.

### [WWP:PROCESS-Q] Uncover the selection process
**Move:** Lead with an open question about how the organization hires firms like yours, then close it down with direct yes/no follow-ups: is there a three-bid requirement, what is the direct-award ceiling above which tender or procurement kicks in, which other firms are they speaking to.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where a formal process, RFP or procurement gate may exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** true solo consumer buyers with no process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a question list given without outcome data.

### [WWP:INTENTTEST] Timeframe as intent detector
**Move:** Ask when they need to start or need it finished, then always ask why. A named date backed by a real event signals intent; absence of a date means contemplation however enthusiastic they sound. Check equally whether the work has moved from wish list to to-do list — if not, what they need is inspiration or a small paid discovery, not a proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** every qualifying conversation before allocating senior resource; a proposal request that arrives with vague timing; an enthusiastic meeting with no next date.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating a date alone as sufficient — it must survive the "why," and procurement calendars manufacture dates that do not reflect real intent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — named by the author as the most common and costliest mistake he sees; both books state it, one author.

### [WWP:TF-BUILD] Build a timeframe backwards from an event

### [WWP:ALLOCATED] Ask whether funds are allocated, not "what's your budget"
**Move:** Replace the standard budget question with allocation variants — has a budget been allocated, are funds budgeted, is there a line item, is it in the current fiscal year — because you are testing progress through the buying cycle, not extracting a number.

### [WWP:RANGE-5X] Give a wide range, high number first, then silence
**Move:** If asked to size the spend during qualifying, state a broad range whose top is roughly five times its bottom, deliver the larger figure first, then stop talking. Optionally open by asking whether this is budgeted or whether they want your help setting the number.
**APPLY WHEN:** no budget is set, the client explicitly asks what it will cost, and date-anchored intent already exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are tempted to narrow into scope and pricing detail — this is explicitly not the pricing conversation.

### [WWP:MLE] Minimum Level of Engagement stated early

Ask for readiness to commit, not an iron-clad promise. The sizing heuristic given is roughly 10% of the year's total target fee income.
**APPLY WHEN:** soon after a need is identified with a new prospect; also to escalate a small tactical request into a strategic conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied rigidly — the source keeps the right to waive it; and waiving it silently, without ever stating it, forfeits the mechanism entirely.

### [WWP:MINIMUM] Deploy a price minimum as a late showdown

**APPLY WHEN:** signals point to a client who cannot afford you and you are willing to lose the deal.

### [WWP:MLEWAIVE] Keep the minimum in play while waiving it

**APPLY WHEN:** an attractive but under-sized opportunity; project work you may accept opportunistically.
**AVOID WHEN:** the minimum has already been waived out loud — the leverage is spent.

### [WWP:STATUS-FIRST] Refuse the money-for-status trade

**APPLY WHEN:** a lucrative brief arrives fully self-diagnosed and self-scoped with no room to lead.
**AVOID WHEN:** survival economics genuinely dominate — the source treats this as a resolve test and does not price the exception.

### [WWP:WHY-US-DEFLECT] Never answer "why should we hire you" directly
**Move:** When invited to sell yourself, decline the frame with a smile and offer instead to describe the reasons clients typically hire you, leaving the client to judge whether those reasons are theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client flips into interviewer mode mid-qualification.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivered with condescension — the firmer version is paired explicitly with humility.

### [WWP:NOCHASE] Do not chase a meeting after a no

**AVOID WHEN:** the no came from a gatekeeper rather than from the person with the problem.

### [WWP:GAPHONEST] Name the expertise gap and let the client bridge it

**APPLY WHEN:** adjacent-scope inbound requests; existing clients extending into new territory.
**AVOID WHEN:** you lack the ability or capacity, or the work cannot be done profitably — then the answer is simply no.

### [WWP:DECLINE-FIT] Decline on fit, keep the door open

**APPLY WHEN:** budget, discipline, market or capacity mismatch is clear.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not actually explored a third way — the source warns against falling in love with disqualifying, and notes that poor fit today is not poor fit forever.

### [WWP:RETREAT-ADVANCE] Decline in order to make the client advance
**Move:** Where your objection is to their buying behavior — an arduous RFP, a budget far below your minimum — rather than to them, state the reason honestly and withdraw, leaving room for them to move toward you.
**APPLY WHEN:** you genuinely accept the deal ending and the client has shown they are underfunding or over-processing a real opportunity.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are bluffing — the source asks directly what happens when they call it.

### [WWP:REFERRAL-DECLINE] Decline a referred lead without damaging the referrer

**APPLY WHEN:** a good client refers someone below your threshold.
**AVOID WHEN:** taking the poor-fit client to protect the relationship — the source argues that is not what the referrer actually wants.

### [WWP:REGROUP] Book the Value Conversation with the missing seniors
**Move:** Summarize surface fit and comparable prior engagements, then request a short specific block — around 30 minutes — with the named missing decision makers to understand success metrics and quantify value, framing the proposal as the output; close with an alternative-date question rather than an open ask.
**APPLY WHEN:** value creation sits above your current contact, or your own qualifier is too junior to run a Value Conversation with an executive.
**AVOID WHEN:** the person in front of you is genuinely the value owner.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:MODEL-WALK] Walk the client through your codified visual model
**Move:** Right after qualifying, ask permission to spend a few minutes showing how you work with clients so they can see what an engagement looks like; the model itself implies that deviating from it lowers the odds of the outcome they want.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have genuinely codified delivery into a visual model; competitive situations where you want control early and want to resist a pitch.
**AVOID WHEN:** no real codified process exists — an improvised model reads as theater.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise

### [WWP:NEEDS-ASSESS] Needs assessment to corral early-stage and split committees

**AVOID WHEN:** late-stage deals where problem, solution and procurement path are already fixed; or when the assessment is poorly constructed — the value depends on it being genuinely useful to them.

### [WWP:JUNIOR-FALLBACK] Handle a contact who cannot produce a vision

**AVOID WHEN:** the junior contact is in fact the economic buyer, as in solo-buyer sales.

## Stage — Diagnosis and desired future state

### [WWP:SELFDIAG] Validate the self-diagnosis without contradicting it
**Move:** When the buyer arrives already naming the solution he wants, neither accept it nor argue. Acknowledge he may well be right and propose finding out for certain — then run your diagnostic before any prescription.
**APPLY WHEN:** an inbound request scoped as a deliverable ("we need a new site / a campaign"); an RFP with a predetermined solution.
**AVOID WHEN:** the item is genuinely tactical reapplication for an existing client where diagnosis is already done.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — grounded in the author's repeated observation that clients ask for X and need Y; no counts given.

### [WWP:FIVE-WHYS] Five Whys, with a stated limit
**Move:** Chain "why" questions inward from the stated request — new website, poor performance, fewer leads, falling traffic and conversion — branching whenever more than one cause surfaces.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client's stated need is concrete and you need the layer beneath it quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would otherwise stop before the fundamental issue; the source prefers going straight to the client's own definition of success (WWP:THREE-YEAR-Q).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — described as standard sales-training fare and reproduced as sample dialogue.

### [WWP:THREE-YEAR-Q] The three-year desired-future-state question
**Move:** Acknowledge the stated request, ask permission to back up to the bigger picture, then place the client three years out at a named date in a named shared setting, pleased with their progress, and ask what happened to make that so. Add detail deliberately — the detail level of your question sets the detail level of their answer. Take visible notes, repeat their words back, and keep asking what else until the answers thin out.
**APPLY WHEN:** desired-future-state questioning in qualifying, especially with a client who arrived self-diagnosed with a tactical brief; and again as the opening step of a Value Conversation, including with a cool, time-constrained senior.
**AVOID WHEN:** you contaminate it with context — assuming the project succeeded, for instance — since its power depends on the client supplying the inference that they hired you; or the contact cannot see three years ahead, where WWP:JUNIOR-FALLBACK applies.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — credited to Dan Sullivan's question; one extended worked dialogue; the emotional-benefit mechanism is asserted.

### [WWP:PREMORTEM-Q] Pre-mortem to surface obstacles
**Move:** After capturing the three-year vision, invert it: ask them to imagine the same date with the vision unrealized and the two of you dissecting the failure, then ask for the most likely causes. Treat every answer as an objection to be handled before any close.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client has articulated a desired future state and is talking freely; early-to-mid qualifying or the desired-future-state step of a Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is a process-runner with no stake in outcomes, or roughly 15 minutes or less remain and the vision itself is not yet on the table.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — dialogue examples, no dataset.

### [WWP:ASSETS] Return to optimism by cataloguing assets
**Move:** After the pre-mortem, deliberately change tack and ask what is already in place that makes them optimistic — team, standing with the board, brand equity — and note them aloud; obstacles and assets both feed solution design later.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after obstacle work, to repair the emotional tone before moving to metrics.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication is given in the source.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:DAY-TO-DAY] Make the vision personal via daily reality
**Move:** After the corporate vision, ask what has changed in their day-to-day reality; record personal outcomes such as morale or freed-up time alongside the corporate one, and stop probing at signs of diminishing relevance — watch-glancing, dismissal of the specifics.
**APPLY WHEN:** rapport is opening and the executive has personal exposure to the outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** signals of impatience appear — the source stops the inquiry there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:PERSONAL-MANDATE] Probe the individual's personal stake
**Move:** Alongside organizational goals, ask what achieving this means for them personally and how their own role changes if it lands; record personal outcomes separately from corporate ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** B2B with multiple players and a decision maker who has visible career exposure to the outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** rapport is thin or the counterpart is junior — mistimed, it reads as an improper breach of the professional/personal line.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — upside and downside stated from the author's experience.

### [WWP:DFS-RECAP] Recap and confirm before moving on
**Move:** Summarize vision, personal outcomes, obstacles and assets in one pass and ask whether you got it right; repeat the same pattern after metrics and again after value.
**APPLY WHEN:** every step boundary of the Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** never skipped in the source's model.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:NOFREETHINK] Draw the free-work line at diagnosis
**Move:** Collect preliminary diagnostic information during the buying cycle to assess fit, but do not share your diagnosis, and never prescribe strategy, before being hired and paid.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery calls that start turning into consulting; RFPs asking for approach and recommendations.
**AVOID WHEN:** a small, deliberate demonstration is your chosen positioning play — the source rejects this outright, but the trade-off is real for a firm without a reputation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as an absolute, that free pitching is free thinking, full stop.

### [WWP:PAIDDIAG] Sell the diagnostic as a paid first phase
**Move:** When the situation is too complex to quantify a solution without investigation, or once you have already shifted the client's framing of the problem, propose setting the selection process aside for a small paid step: take a slice of budget and test the hypothesis about the underlying issue. Its deliverable has two parts — findings, and recommendations including timeline and budget — so the proposal itself is produced inside paid work.
**APPLY WHEN:** a technical or complex challenge; the buyer wants a costed plan; you would otherwise do free discovery; or you have reframed the problem and there is acknowledged uncertainty about root cause.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer sees no value gap to investigate and the scope is genuinely routine; or no reframing has occurred, since the second source describes this concession as the payoff of having changed their thinking first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented in both books as standard practice among the firms the author advises, by analogy to MRIs, audits and legal discovery; one author, so no independent weight.

## Stage — Competitive processes, RFPs and concessions

### [WWP:4PRIORITIES] Four priorities of winning new business
**Move:** Work the hierarchy in order — win before a competitive process exists; derail the process into an alternative first step; if it cannot be derailed, get the inside track; otherwise walk away. The goal is to win, and not pitching is the preferred means rather than the point.
**APPLY WHEN:** any opportunity that arrives already inside a selection process.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied as rigid doctrine — the source explicitly warns against replacing the client's rigidity with your own.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a prioritization framework offered without outcome data.

### [WWP:INSIDETRACK] Assume an advantaged player and find out whether it is you
**Move:** Assume every competitive bid already has someone holding the inside lane — inside information, an internal advocate, or a decision already made — and treat your task as discovering whether that position is yours or available rather than assuming even odds. If you enter anyway, extract concessions as the price of participating: access to decision makers, negotiated limits on what you will write or show. Judge by the buyer's behavior, not his words; if no concession is granted, read yourself as the stalking horse and go to priority four.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-bidder processes, procurement-driven shortlists, three-bid compliance situations where the buyer shows signs of valuing expertise.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely level, high-volume transactional selling, where a share-of-field strategy is sound — the sources grant this case for products.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — "somebody always has the inside track" is the author's working assumption in both books; the odds framing is given without derivation.

### [WWP:CONCESSION-LADDER] Seek behavioral concessions as a fit test
**Move:** Ask the client to treat you differently and read compliance as signal, escalating roughly in this order: moving a meeting's date or place; sharing information withheld from others; substituting an existing credentials document for a questionnaire; completing a written needs assessment; having all decision makers complete it; assembling all decision makers to discuss the findings; increasing budget; granting access to seniors you were told were off-limits; changing scope; paying for a first-phase diagnostic.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive processes where you need an early read on your true position.
**AVOID WHEN:** pushed too far — the client's reconciliation then becomes "demanding and annoying" rather than "different."
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a ranked list of 10 from practice; the causal claim that behavior precedes changed thinking is asserted.

### [WWP:REVEALED-PREF] Test stated preference with a small ask
**Move:** When a client says you are the one they want but the process must run, do not accept the statement — request a concrete concession instead: substituting a credentials document for the questionnaire, or a short interview with their boss before you write the proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** verbal assurance of preferred status in a process-bound deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** the ask would breach a regulated or public-interest process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two sample asks; the rationale is that revealed preferences validate while stated ones stay suspect.

### [WWP:RFP-DECLINE] Raise your own process objection to an RFP
**Move:** When an RFP is announced, say you don't typically respond to them — keeping the hedge word so discussion stays open; if pressed, give the plain reason (built for procuring commodities, creates work, keeps you at arm's length) and offer the alternative: talk, exchange criteria in both directions, then scope and price for discussion.
**APPLY WHEN:** a qualifying call that is going well ends with an RFP handoff.
**AVOID WHEN:** you need a hard line — then drop the qualifier or invoke policy instead (WWP:POLICYLANG).
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — includes reading the client's reaction as a power measurement.

### [WWP:BID-LAST] Offer to bid only after the others bid
**Move:** When the client is compelled to collect competing bids, note that you will not be the lowest and propose they gather the others first, then talk about whether your bid makes sense at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** three-bid compliance situations with a client who has discretion and political capital.
**AVOID WHEN:** government, regulated industries, or any setting where this would contravene regulation, public interest or ethical norms — the source states this carve-out itself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — presented with its own exception.

### [WWP:CONVERSE-NOT-PRESENT] Push back on brief-and-present
**Move:** When a client proposes an arm's-length brief-then-present process, name it and push for a two-way conversation; the harder they lean on process, the harder you press for talk, and if the process is institutional, seek the senior decision maker with the political capital to bend it.
**APPLY WHEN:** formalized selection processes, procurement-led buying, RFP-shaped opportunities.
**AVOID WHEN:** regulated or government procurement where the process cannot lawfully be bent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — reasoning about why clients default to process; the cost is framed as lower margin and vendor status.

### [WWP:NOPITCHMEET] Run the meeting as if presenting were banned
**Move:** Before any buying-cycle meeting, ask yourself what you would do in that room if presenting were not allowed. What remains is conversation — talking and listening without one side performing. Move credentials and firm background into that conversation instead of a deck.
**APPLY WHEN:** invited to "present capabilities"; early buying-cycle meetings.
**AVOID WHEN:** a formal process you have decided to enter still mandates a presentation slot — then negotiate its content (WWP:INSIDETRACK) rather than refuse outright.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/outbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a reframing exercise offered without results.

### [WWP:INSPIRE] Inspire only the interested
**Move:** With a buyer who recognizes a gap but has not committed, use portfolio, examples, competitor work and outside best practice to show what the other side of the problem looks like. Aim at his intent to solve the problem, not at his intent to hire you.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer is gathering information, weighing pros and cons, with no date attached.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is unaware — inspiration aimed at someone who denies the problem produces defensiveness; or he has already formed intent and needs reassurance instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — stated with a psychological rationale and no source.

### [WWP:REASSURE] Answer the free-work request with risk-reducers
**Move:** When a buyer asks for a written proposal with free recommendations, spec work or an audition, read the request as fear of making a mistake: state your policy and its client-side rationale, then ask whether they would consider another route that gives them the same assurance without you working unpaid — a phased engagement, a pilot, an opt-out clause, a money-back guarantee on the first phase, case studies framed inside your defined method.
**APPLY WHEN:** a late-cycle request for free thinking; detailed small questions signalling remorse-driven doubt; creative firms facing pitch or audition demands where the standoff is about risk reduction rather than fit.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's constraint is genuinely procedural, as in regulated procurement, rather than emotional; and when the "third way" is only splitting the difference — the source distinguishes creative reframing from compromise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to how the author's client firms respond, with agency dialogue in the second source; no comparative data, one author.

### [WWP:MAGIC-EMAIL] The Closing the Loop email
**Move:** When a client goes dark after a positive proposal response, send a two-line note titled Closing the Loop stating that, having not heard back, you assume they went another direction or priorities changed, and inviting future contact — no chasing, no apology, no check-in tone.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-proposal silence where earlier neediness likely made honest refusal hard.
**AVOID WHEN:** sent before the client has had reasonable time, or used as a manipulation lever rather than a genuine release.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two typical reply patterns reported, no response rates; origin unknown, taught to the author as a voicemail script decades ago.

## Stage — Value conversation: metrics, economics, pricing guidance

### [WWP:FOUR-Q] The four Value Conversation questions
**Move:** Run the client through four questions, all about them: what they want; what will be measured to prove they got it; what hitting those metrics is worth; what they would be willing to pay to get it. Discuss no solutions until all four are answered.
**APPLY WHEN:** a qualified opportunity with an executive or senior decision maker present, and customized delivery and/or pricing available.
**AVOID WHEN:** delivery and pricing are both standardized — then the fourth question is skipped and you go straight to a templated proposal and the closing conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise

### [WWP:METRIC-WE] Collaborative metric-setting
**Move:** Ask how *we* would measure that rather than supplying the metric. ### [WWP:SELL-DIAGNOSTIC] Sell a paid baseline study when data is missing
**Move:** Where neither baseline nor target exists, propose carving a defined amount out of budget for a baseline analysis and target-setting, framed as helping them make the internal case for the investment; it also gives you the data to scope and price.
**APPLY WHEN:** an agreed metric but no data; a complex sale; internal case-building needed.
**AVOID WHEN:** metrics and baselines already exist — it becomes an unnecessary delay.

### [WWP:ORDER-MAG] Get order-of-magnitude economics without demanding proprietary data
**Move:** Convert the metric to topline — market size times points of share — then push for margin; if refused, say you are not after trade secrets, offer a category-average margin yourself, do the arithmetic aloud, propose a rounded figure and ask whether you can work with it. Read the non-verbal response as ratification rather than forcing a confirmation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the value step with a quantifiable commercial metric and a client who withholds margins.
**AVOID WHEN:** your guessed category average would be visibly wrong — a scoff means recalibrate.

### [WWP:LEAVE-UNPRICED] Leave non-economic value unquantified
**Move:** When the client names value that is a feeling — an enjoyable workplace, effectiveness, a high-performing culture — paraphrase it into your notes as-is rather than forcing a dollar conversion; do the economic arithmetic only where a threshold of quantified value has not yet been reached.
**APPLY WHEN:** economic value is already large enough to carry the pricing conversation, or the domain is inherently non-economic — residential architecture, counseling, healthcare, advice where peace of mind dominates return.
**AVOID WHEN:** no economic anchor has been established at all and pricing guidance would have nothing to stand on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:ANCHOR-CLOSED] Anchor high with a closed question tied to value
**Move:** After recapping value, name a large figure inside a closed question — would you invest X to generate Y. If asked what you would do for that fee, answer honestly that you have not thought about solutions yet and return to the question. Anchor magnitude follows three factors in order: value to be created, your contribution to creating it, likelihood of creation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the pricing-guidance step, executive present, value quantified to the right order of magnitude.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not transcended solutions — the reference point then reverts to competitors' market price or your input costs and the anchor fails; and where your contribution is a minor slice of the value, a large value-share ask invites ridicule.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — anchoring is cited to endnotes without a verifiable named source in the text, so treat the psychology as asserted.

### [WWP:GUARANTEE-ANCHOR] Anchor against guaranteed value
**Move:** When the anchor is rejected outright, first reclaim the right to think creatively about how the value gets created, then pose a hypothetical: if the return were guaranteed, would they invest the anchor amount. Keep restating that this is hypothetical and that you are not committing to a guarantee. Their guaranteed-case number is the theoretical ceiling and encodes their estimate of your contribution.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client pushes back hard on the anchor and you want the true maximum without committing to performance terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be read as promising a guarantee — the source resets that expectation repeatedly inside the same exchange, and a buyer who cannot track the hypothetical will hear a promise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one worked dialogue.

### [WWP:PERMISSION-TRADE] Trade budget-compliance for permission to think expansively
**Move:** When the client refuses to consider anything above their approved budget, commit to showing what you can do at their number and, in exchange, ask permission to brief your team to think expansively about maximum certainty of value, with the right to present those ideas; name the goal out loud — putting them in a position of wanting to bring their boss in — while leaving the budget option available.
**APPLY WHEN:** a sub-executive contact, budget-locked, where the power dynamic has not flipped.
**AVOID WHEN:** they refuse the trade — the source says such a client should have been qualified out earlier.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:LOW-END] Let the client set the floor of the range
**Move:** Ask directly whether there is a number they would like you to hit at the low end; if they will not name one, set it yourself using your minimum level of engagement, and accept a wide range as desirable.
**APPLY WHEN:** no allocated budget surfaced during qualifying.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication is stated in the source.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:OPTIONS-PROMISE] Close the Value Conversation by promising options
**Move:** State that you will return with several ways to engage at different price points — a top option, possibly guaranteed, near the ceiling figure; an option at their budget; and at least one in between. If they arrived with tight specifications, promise to price those too, unless you regard the spec as a gross misdiagnosis. Name the trade explicitly: they get their price and their spec priced, you get to transcend both and be heard out.
**APPLY WHEN:** the end of every Value Conversation where customization is available.
**AVOID WHEN:** standardized service at a standardized price — skip to the closing conversation with a templated proposal.

## Stage — Proposal construction and pricing

### [WWP:ORALPROP] Propose in speech, paper only as contract
**Move:** Deliver the proposal verbally — the work, the timeframe, the price — and reach agreement in conversation covering scope, timing, budget and basic terms. Produce a document only after agreement, and call it the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** an intent-stage buyer who recognizes your expertise; owner-led or few-stakeholder deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** committee or public-sector buying where a written submission is legally required; or the buyer has never had the substantive conversation, in which case there is nothing to write up. Note that the author's later book replaces this with a written one-page proposal (WWP:ONE-PAGE) — pick one, and know you are choosing between two versions of the same author.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — supported by the author's retrospective claim that documents rarely swayed decisions.

### [WWP:ONE-PAGE] One-page proposal as constraint
**Move:** Force every proposal onto one page: strip out contract terms and persuasion material, leaving solutions, outcomes and price standing alone; be prepared to speak to what is not written and to formalize the rest in the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** every customized proposal in the four-conversations model.
**AVOID WHEN:** not stated by the source, which treats it as general discipline; the claimed lower cost of sale and higher closing ratio are unmeasured, so do not present them as reasons to the client.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — closing-ratio and cost-of-sale benefits stated without data.

### [WWP:THREE-OPT] Three-option proposal
**Move:** Build exactly three options — an expensive anchor, an inexpensive one, and a middle. Four can work but less cleanly; four to five is where choice overload is said to begin; two provides context but forfeits the pull toward the middle.
**APPLY WHEN:** any customized engagement after a Value Conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** any option you would consider wrong — if you would not honor it, it does not belong on the page.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — extremeness aversion and the paradox of choice are invoked via endnotes with no verifiable named source in the text; the "three options give 75% positive outcomes" argument is arithmetic on labels, not data.

### [WWP:WALK-DOWN] Walk the anchor down when pricing options
**Move:** Set the top option below the client's guaranteed-case ceiling if you are not offering guarantees, preserving a pattern of improving prices; price the budget option at exactly the client's number with no upward creep; place the middle by judgment, starting from the midpoint, and expect it to be the price most likely to move after scoping.
**APPLY WHEN:** pricing a three-option proposal off Value Conversation guidance.
**AVOID WHEN:** raising the client's budget figure — the source treats that as breaking the bargain; the middle option exists for that purpose.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one worked example, $2m → $1m → $750k anchor with a $425k middle and $100k budget option; author-supplied figures.

### [WWP:PROFIT-OFF-TABLE] Take profit off the table before scoping
**Move:** Deduct an explicit gross profit from the anchor price and hand only the remainder to the scoping team as a cost budget; treat that deduction as a measure of selling and pricing skill, distinct from operational profit realized at utilization thresholds.
**APPLY WHEN:** firms that price and sell time with rates built loosely on salary plus markup.
**AVOID WHEN:** the firm conflates price with cost and would brief the full price as a spend budget — named as the mistake this move exists to prevent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $750k price with $250k profit removed leaving $500k to scoping; utilization threshold given as roughly 60–70%.

### [WWP:SCOPE-ANCHOR] Scope the anchor against the cost constraint
**Move:** With the cost budget fixed, revisit your Value Conversation notes and ask what the most is you can do for this client at that cost; list value drivers — inputs, outputs, processes, tools — assemble the anchor from them, define how each is packaged and contained, put the most obvious details on the page and be ready to speak to the rest.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-Value-Conversation scoping with customized delivery.
**AVOID WHEN:** no caveat is given; the source notes it may push you past your own business model — outside partners, new hires, even acquisitions — with no obligation to take that on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — management-consultancy and marketing-agency value-driver examples.

### [WWP:BUDGET-HONEST] Scope the budget option ruthlessly
**Move:** Show what you can profitably do for their stated budget, stripping out value drivers they hoped to get; you owe them an option at their number, not their full specification at that number.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client stated an allocated budget.
**AVOID WHEN:** optimistic scoping — over-optimism at the low end is named a business-killer, and client disappointment at this tier is acceptable because what they want lives in the middle option.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the claim that this has destroyed many businesses is stated without cases.

### [WWP:SPEC-MIDDLE] Put their own specification in the middle at your price
**Move:** When the client came with detailed specs, show the stripped version at their budget and their full specification as the middle option priced by you; iterate, checking the middle against both your cost floor and the gravity of the anchor, and strip value drivers from the budget option if it starts to look like overdelivery.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client arrived with a specified solution and a budget below its real price.
**AVOID WHEN:** their spec is a gross misdiagnosis — you are not obliged to price it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — ecommerce example with a $100k budget, $225k cost floor, $350k middle and $750k anchor; all author-supplied.

### [WWP:PHASE-BELOW] Offer a phased first step below their budget
**Move:** As the cheapest option, offer a small audit or diagnostic as phase one of the anchor engagement, letting them test the relationship and build an internal case for the remaining spend; their budget can then sit in the middle.
**APPLY WHEN:** a high-anchor engagement, a risk-averse client, internal approval needed for the larger number.
**AVOID WHEN:** no contraindication given in the source.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $750k anchor, $100k middle, $50k phased step.

### [WWP:PRICE-ABOVE] Price above the anchor (narrow exception)
**Move:** Go above the Value Conversation anchor only when you can still make the ROI case — you left money on the table, discovered additional value, or are deliberately looking past a budget-limited manager to senior decision makers; almost always this requires some form of performance pay with a guarantee element.
**APPLY WHEN:** a confident ROI case at the higher number and a path to the senior decision makers.
**AVOID WHEN:** it contradicts the improving-price principle and requires the gatekeeping manager's consent to reach the seniors — the source itself calls this a delicate game.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:PERF-PAY] Performance pay structures
**Move:** Structure a small base for a minimum deliverable set plus incentives tied to lagging indicators (units sold, revenue share) or leading ones (leads generated, channel partners created, purchase-intent change); consider waiving the fee entirely for pure incentives, and consider removing the cap when the risk you absorb outstrips the capped reward.
**APPLY WHEN:** the anchor option, where you are willing to take risk off the client and measurable KPIs exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** reward and risk are misaligned; and never use incentive structuring to make the headline number look smaller.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:BIG-NUMBER] Put the full KPI-hit price at the foot of the anchor column
**Move:** Show the price the client pays when KPIs are met as one large figure at the bottom of the anchor column, with the earning mechanics above it in the option detail and the payment cadence there or in the contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** the anchor option uses performance pay.
**AVOID WHEN:** you are tempted toward a palatable blended figure — the anchor is not meant to be palatable; its stated job is to make the middle look reasonable and push the client past their initial brief. That is also exactly where framing shades into pressure, which the source never examines.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion.

### [WWP:TERMS-LEVER] Use payment terms to make expensive options affordable
**Move:** Attach the most generous terms to the most expensive option, fewer instalments to the middle, and none — possibly payment in advance — to the cheapest.
**APPLY WHEN:** you suspect the client is choosing down on affordability rather than preference.
**AVOID WHEN:** cash-flow exposure you cannot carry — a case the source does not address.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — $60k over 12 instalments, $22.5k over 3, $10k prepaid.

### [WWP:NAME-EXPLAIN] Name options to explain, not to persuade
**Move:** Choose names that convey differences in focus, inputs, outputs, value or engagement model, escalating through three expressions: differently sized buckets of the same inputs (the weakest form), three levels of problem solving, or three ways of doing business such as performance pay versus deliverable versus retainer. Standardized businesses frame this once and revisit periodically; customized businesses do it per sale.
**APPLY WHEN:** every multi-option proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Good/Better/Best or Gold/Silver/Bronze naming — judgment-laden names are rejected as a late lapse into persuasion after a whole sale spent not persuading.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote.

### [WWP:FINAL-SWEEP] Final relational check on the page
**Move:** Review solution, price, cost, terms and names together and confirm that at a glance the differences are legible and no obvious question is begged — notably why one option costs so much more; treat client selections, prices paid and questions asked as feedback that tunes the next proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** before sending or presenting any multi-option proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable — but the claimed upward march of closing ratio and average proposal value is unmeasured and should not be repeated as fact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion.

### [WWP:BIGROUND] Price thinking in round numbers, not hours
**Move:** Price everything up to the first creative deliverable as large round figures ending in zeros, so the price visibly has nothing to do with elapsed hours. Reserve hourly billing for reapplication work later in the engagement.
**APPLY WHEN:** quoting strategic or diagnostic phases; any moment the buyer asks for a rate.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's procurement mandates rate cards — the phased fixed-fee framing is the only workaround the source offers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from commoditization logic, with no pricing data.

### [WWP:SIZEPRICE] Price to client size and to commitment
**Move:** Charge larger organizations more for equivalent work, on two grounds: they capture more financial value from it, and the price must carry enough pain that the advice is acted on rather than shelved.
**APPLY WHEN:** the same offering sold across very different company sizes.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer can compare across your accounts and the difference cannot be explained by value delivered.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the large-manufacturer-versus-single-dealership contrast is illustrative, not evidential.

### [WWP:NODISCOUNT] Exhaust alternatives before cutting price
**Move:** Before discounting, work the two substitutes: guarantee the first phase — diagnosis and prescription — when the buyer doubts value, never guaranteeing ROI or the whole engagement; and offer payment terms over time when the buyer sees value but cannot pay now. Otherwise hold ground, which favors whoever is most comfortable discussing money.
**APPLY WHEN:** a price objection from a buyer who otherwise fits.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is simply below your minimum — that is a walk-away, not a negotiation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — mechanisms described, outcomes not measured.

### [WWP:DISCRULES] Two rules if you do discount
**Move:** Make the price cut the last concession — clear every other objection first, and ask whether, if you agreed to this price, anything else would stop you working together right now. Then record the discount explicitly on contracts, estimates and invoices so the full value stays visible and the reduced number does not become the new baseline.
**APPLY WHEN:** a negotiated close where you have decided a discount is acceptable and still profitable.
**AVOID WHEN:** the cut would take the engagement below profitability — the source treats that as disqualifying.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as rules; the claim that the client forgets the real price is unsupported.

### [WWP:PRICELOSS] Use lost deals as a pricing gauge
**Move:** Raise prices as expertise deepens and read the results: occasionally losing business on price is the signal the level is right; never losing on price means you are underpriced, while needing to win on price means the expert positioning has failed.
**APPLY WHEN:** periodic pricing review after a run of deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** pipeline volume is too thin for the signal to mean anything.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — a heuristic with no threshold or data behind it.

## Stage — Closing conversation and commitment

### [WWP:CLOSEAGENDA7] Run the seven-point Closing agenda

### [WWP:VC-RECAP] Recap the Value Conversation before showing options

**AVOID WHEN:** reading from or memorizing a script — the source says that instantly demotes you from expert to vendor.

**AVOID WHEN:** a single-option engagement, or the client has already stated a choice and wants to sign.

### [WWP:PRICEBEFOREPAPER] Say the anchor price aloud before sharing the document

**APPLY WHEN:** the client raised risk or guarantees earlier and the anchor is performance- or transformation-flavored.

### [WWP:ANCHORSHORT] Keep the anchor overview to a few minutes

**APPLY WHEN:** first delivery of a bold, above-brief option.
**AVOID WHEN:** the client explicitly asks for mechanics before anything else.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:BUDGETLITERAL] Show what the stated budget literally buys
**Move:** Introduce the cheap option as a walk-through of what their own stated number covers and let the gap to the anchor be visible without arguing it — this is the option for someone checking a box, not pursuing transformation.
**APPLY WHEN:** the client gave a budget in qualifying or the Value Conversation, possibly as an optimistic or negotiating figure.
**AVOID WHEN:** the cheap option was not scoped to be profitable on its own — never show a version you would resent delivering.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:MIDSAFEZONE] Frame the middle as the new safe zone
**Move:** Position the middle option verbally as sitting between the twin fears of overpaying and underbuying — a blend of fiscal responsibility and ambition — then give its price and broad strokes.
**APPLY WHEN:** a three-option structure with a client showing both ambition and budget anxiety.
**AVOID WHEN:** only two options exist, or the middle is cosmetic filler you cannot actually deliver differently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative

### [WWP:GUTREACTION] Ask for the initial reaction before questions

### [WWP:STRATFIRST] Strategy agreed before any creative, and restated before every reveal
**Move:** Refuse to develop or show creative of any kind before the problem is diagnosed and the strategy agreed. Immediately before showing any work, walk back through the agreed strategy so the discussion is measured against it rather than against taste.
**APPLY WHEN:** any delivery engagement where subjective reaction can override intent; recurring rounds of concepts.
**AVOID WHEN:** purely executional or production scope where no strategy layer was bought.

### [WWP:EXECFREEDOM] Input on strategy, freedom on execution
**Move:** Trade explicitly: invite the client's input on strategy and, in exchange, ask for freedom to explore executions. Frame the boundary out loud — feedback tied to the strategy is welcome, instruction on execution is not.
**APPLY WHEN:** kickoff of an engagement, while the rules of collaboration are being set.
**AVOID WHEN:** the client has already been granted control and the engagement is mid-flight — retrofitting the rule reads as defensiveness.

### [WWP:FEWOPTIONS] Fewer options, one recommendation

**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer contractually purchased a set number of concepts; and note that in the sale itself the author's later position is the opposite — three priced options, and no recommendation unless asked (WWP:THREE-OPT, WWP:UNATTACHEDFACILITATE).

### [WWP:WEPRESENT] Our work is only presented by us
**Move:** Make it a rule that findings, strategy and solutions are never carried into the client organization without someone from your firm in the room; the client contact may assist but not substitute.
**APPLY WHEN:** work must travel to executives or committees the seller has not met.
**AVOID WHEN:** the client's internal politics or security rules make outside presence genuinely impossible — treat that as a risk to price, not a rule to enforce; and note the rule does not scale beyond the principal who sells.

### [WWP:CHURNPLAN] Manage turnover deliberately

**APPLY WHEN:** annual planning; deciding whether to take marginal work.
**AVOID WHEN:** the cash position cannot support the gap between an exit and a bigger replacement.
