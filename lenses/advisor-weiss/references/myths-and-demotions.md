# advisor-weiss — myths and demotions

What this tradition debunks, what must be demoted *inside* it, and which of its own numbers must never be quoted as fact. Source: Alan Weiss, *Value-Based Fees* (2021), `[VBF:]`.

---

## A. Debunked by the source (usable as the lens's negative claims)

**Pricing basis**
- **The lifestyle-needs rate formula** (income target ÷ available billable hours). Rejected on four counts: wrong basis entirely; available hours systematically overestimated; utilisation above roughly 80% destroys flexibility; and the resulting rate bears no relation to client value. Worked ceiling: 40 weeks × 80% utilisation = 1,280 hours — at a realistic rate and 60% utilisation the whole model tops out near $240,000. `[VBF:NOHOURS]`
- **"Raise fees when demand exceeds supply."** Rejected because expertise is not consumed by serving one client. *Demote the stronger version* — see section B.
- **"Something is better than nothing"** utilisation thinking. Named as the mechanism that drove architects' incomes down, together with taking unprofitable work while waiting for the prestige project. `[VBF:NO-LOWBALL-ENTRY]`
- **Contingency / gainshare as the smart alternative to hourly.** Same error plus four conflicts: big results get attributed to the client, escalating payments breed resentment, symmetry forces you to cut on a miss, and your advice is distorted by your stake. `[VBF:NOCONTINGENCY]`
- **Formulas as safety.** The source concedes formulas exist, supplies one `[VBF:BACKWARDROI]`, then instructs you to abandon it, claiming formulas usually depress fees. That internal contradiction is left standing.

**What buyers buy**
- **Deliverables** — called a consultant-invented shibboleth: easy to describe and produce, low ultimate value, instantly comparison-shopped. Selling your own time is named the worst deliverable of all. `[VBF:NODELIVER]`, `[VBF:TASK2OUT]`
- **Enlarging a project by adding people, sites or duration** — labour intensity dressed as value.
- **Options built by multiplying units** (twice the training, three times the coaching contacts) — the documented failure mode of `[VBF:OPTIONS3]`: quantity presented as value tiers.
- **Defending a failed project by the quality of your process** — failure regardless.

**Objections**
- **"No money" as a primary objection** — usually a proxy for absent trust, need or urgency; consulting budgets rarely pre-exist and are reallocated. `[VBF:FOUR-FILTERS]`
- **"The timing isn't right"** — a priority claim misread as a resource claim.
- **The elevator pitch as an answer to no-need resistance** — pitching methodology at a buyer who sees no need is firing at a moving target.
- Named self-inflicted failure modes: taking rejection personally, answering the objection you are comfortable with rather than the live one, panicking, and secretly agreeing with the buyer.

**Practice**
- **"Low fees get you in the door and you raise them later"** — the central debunk. Clients never ask you to raise them; the anchor propagates through referrals and through your own self-image; increments compound from a bad base. `[VBF:NO-LOWBALL-ENTRY]`
- **"Arrival" at the word-of-mouth or brand phase** — the success trap; stability reframed as drifting without propulsion. `[VBF:PHASE-LOCATE]`
- **Being the indispensable lone expert** — even a successful project is paid grudgingly if the buyer feels excluded. `[VBF:SHAREDWIN]`
- **Compliance mistaken for agreement**; delegation to HR, finance or legal read as a buying signal. `[VBF:COMMITGATE]`
- **Letting production capacity drive what you sell** — the training firm that sold boxes of material and seats in a room, then ran year-end fire sales clients learned to wait for.
- **Rationalizations that keep bad business alive**: they were there when we needed them; the work is easy; it is fun; we are friends; we are waiting on promised exposure; we have spare time; what if other work dries up; we are trapped by accumulated scope creep; losing a client feels like personal failure. Plus a flat rule against business with friends and family. `[VBF:ABANDON9Q]`

**Retainers and remote**
- **Guilt-driven activity to "earn" an unused month** — erodes the access premise; so does a free early project, which sets a precedent. `[VBF:ACCESS-IS-VALUE]`
- **Extending an under-used retainer for free** — refused via the insurance frame.
- **Long retainers as pure upside** — they turn you into a single-viewpoint insider and can decay into the bottom-15% business the source says to shed.
- **"Remote means less value"** — the Ch.11 core debunk `[VBF:NO-REMOTE-DISCOUNT]`; likewise the belief that a speech is worth less because its content overlaps a published book.
- **Bureaus as pure upside** — failure mode is a fixed published schedule silently capping your ceiling. Note the source's own 50%-deposit criticism of bureaus is self-interested. `[VBF:BUREAU-TERMS]`
- **"Nobody will pay to be advised by phone"** and **"coaching requires certification"** — both dismissed.

**Ethics**
- **"A law mandates equal fees for equal services."** The Robinson-Patman Act concerns commodities in interstate commerce, not value-priced services.
- **"Charging for work not yet performed is unethical."** Deposits and advances are normal. `[VBF:TERMS-FIRST]`
- **"Nonprofits are entitled to lower fees"** and **"you must match a competitor's lower quote."** Both refused.
- **Flatly forbidden**: billing expenses not actually incurred (frequent-flyer airfare, an unused hotel, a cab not taken), and undeclared cash or unreported barter — named as tax crimes, not clever billing.
- **"Loss leaders build the product business"** — rejected; moving $40,000 of product for $2,000 of profit is activity, not business.
- **Crisis gouging and hoarding** — explicitly ruled out.

## B. Demoted inside the tradition (the lens must not assert these)

1. **"There is no law of supply and demand in consulting"; "no limit to perceived value, hence no limit to fees"; "you control your fee — it's the only variable."** Rhetoric, not mechanism. The absolutist register is the source's dominant stylistic tic and its biggest overreach. Keep the mechanisms, discard the absolutes.
2. **"Money can always be found."** `[VBF:MONEY-IS-PRIORITY]` is defensible as *budgets are reallocated, not pre-existing*; the universal form is false and reads as tone-deaf against a genuine spending freeze or insolvency.
3. **The specialization contradiction.** Ch.7 attacks "specialize or die" while the rest of the book presumes a differentiated, named practice. Both cannot hold. Practitioner consensus runs the other way — value-based fees generally require a defensible specialization. This lens should side with the rest of the book, not with Ch.7.
4. **Committee and consensus buying is simply absent.** The answer to multi-stakeholder decisions is "find the one real buyer". Where no such person exists the method has no mechanism and does not concede the gap. Any council question involving a buying committee needs another lens.
5. **Manipulation is under-flagged.** Three places specifically: the embarrass-the-buyer cost-comparison library `[VBF:SMACK-COMPARISON]`; the manufactured-urgency levers, above all the buyer's personal career stake `[VBF:CREATE-URGENCY]`; and the always-one-option-above-budget rule `[VBF:OVERBUDGET]`. All are presented as reframing with almost no discussion of when they cross into working the buyer's interest against the organization's. Operating rule for this lens: do not offer a move the user could not state openly to the buyer.
6. **Self-serving ethics.** Every ethics answer resolves in the consultant's financial favour and several rest on analogy rather than argument. The single constraint accepted against interest is `[VBF:COACHCONTRACT]`.
7. **No risk pricing for the practitioner.** `[VBF:WALK-AWAY]`, `[VBF:ABANDON9Q]`, `[VBF:NO-LOWBALL-ENTRY]`, `[VBF:STOPWORK]` and refusing procurement all presuppose a pipeline and a cash buffer that are never modelled. Revenue concentration risk is never quantified. Advising a solo practitioner with one client to walk away is malpractice this lens will commit if unsupervised.
8. **Dated and jurisdiction-bound specifics.** Tax, withholding, contract enforceability, the FAR procurement claim in `[VBF:RFPBYPASS]`, card processing and publishing economics are all US-centric and time-stamped (2021). Re-verify or omit.
9. **Internal inconsistencies left standing.** Two conflicting US attorney income figures in one chapter (~$165,000 and barely above $100,000); overlapping scoring bands in the conversion instrument behind `[VBF:CONVERTTRIAGE]` (23–30 / 14–22 / 0–21); a chapter that denies formulas, supplies one, then discards it; and a sample retainer letter granting the freeze the surrounding text says never to grant.
10. **Coaching's 90-day cap** `[VBF:COACHVSADVISOR]` is an experience claim presented as a behavioural finding. Do not state it as a fact about how people change.

## C. Self-reported numbers — never quote as fact

All of the following are the author's own recollection of his own practice. No denominators, no control, no failure sample. Use them only as illustration, always attributed as self-reported.

- **Close rates.** Proposals preceded by proper conceptual agreement claimed to close over 80% versus under 5% without; post-formula close rate 60–80%. No method, no counted base.
- **Option selection.** Career-long split: cheapest option chosen under 10% of the time, most expensive over 35%. The attached "offering options lifts acceptance by at least 50%" is bare assertion with no method at all — the weakest number attached to the lens's strongest mechanic `[VBF:OPTIONS3]`.
- **Word of mouth.** "Well over 80% of business" (sourced to trade press) and "~4% web-based" (sourced to a private remark). Both unverified. `[VBF:REFERRAL-EXPECTATION-PRICING]`, `[VBF:LATERALBUYERS]`
- **ROI multiples.** 20:1 recommended, 10:1 "just fine", 5:1 still good, 3:1 significant in manufacturing consulting, annualized returns "up to 100:1". Only the 3:1 datapoint is externally sourced, and it *contradicts* the recommended floor. `[VBF:ROIFLOOR]`
- **Construction constants.** +20% minimum option step; uniqueness uplift +20%/+10%/0; final sanity adjustment ±15%; the halving heuristic. Round numbers, no validation. `[VBF:BACKWARDROI]`, `[VBF:NOMAGICNUM]`
- **Terms.** TDTC bands 30–45 good / 45–60 fair / 60–90 poor; 50% on acceptance + 50% at 45 days; 5–10% prepayment discount; 30-day proposal validity; 90-day and six-month delay clauses; solo-delivery premium 10%; phase rebate ~25%; in-retainer project discounts ~20% (quarterly) / ~33% (annual); "local tax withholding unjustified in over 90% of cases". `[VBF:TERMS-FIRST]`, `[VBF:PHASE-REBATE]`, `[VBF:RETAINER-PROJECT-DISCOUNT]`
- **Retainer economics.** $10,000/month → $27,000/quarter → $50,000/six months; access caps at 3–4 named people; 90-minute call response; five-year longest retainer (called an aberration); a $600M subsidiary CEO who unilaterally raised his own retainer to $130,000 in year four. Single cases. `[VBF:PREPAY-LADDER]`, `[VBF:RETAINER-10]`
- **Mentor program.** Launched at $3,500, 800+ through the core program, ~30% re-enrolled, ~200 through $17,000 elite coaching, fees unchanged since 1996, ~75% of participants consultants. Sales figures for the author's own products, reported by the author. `[VBF:CHARGE-THE-FREE]`
- **Speaking and publishing.** Keynote bands ($3,500 low-end association / $20,000–40,000 typical non-celebrity / $75,000–200,000 celebrity); the illustrative $40,000+ stacked menu; bureau commission 25–30%; self-publishing ~$8,000 for 2,000 copies at ~$25 margin. Market colour from one career, in one country, at one time. `[VBF:KEYNOTE-OPTION-STACK]`, `[VBF:PRODUCT-PRICE-HIGH]`
- **Practice cadences.** Bottom-15% pruning every 18–24 months; fee raises every two years and base fees +10%; ~70% repeat business at going-concern; ~3 years to reach going concern; 38 simultaneous active clients. Rules of thumb without outcome data. `[VBF:PORTFOLIO-HYGIENE]`, `[VBF:ABANDON9Q]`
- **The worst one.** "Install two fee tactics a month and double your profits in a year, 4–5× if you also grow." `[VBF:TWO-TACTICS-MONTH]` — no supporting data of any kind. Never repeat this figure.
- **Unsourced comparison** used to argue budgets exist: large organizations "each spend $100,000+ on misting plants, plowing lots, breakage and theft". No source. `[VBF:MONEY-IS-PRIORITY]`

## D. Documented failure modes of the techniques themselves

- **Options that are quantity, not value** — `[VBF:OPTIONS3]` degrades into a menu of tasks when objectives were never agreed.
- **A high number with no conceptual agreement behind it** — `[VBF:QUOTE-HIGH-REDUCE-VALUE]` becomes sticker shock, not anchoring. Illustrated by the buyer expecting $5,000 against a $55,000 floor option.
- **The allocation question asked into thin trust** — `[VBF:ALLOCATION-ASK]` and `[VBF:STICKER-SHOCK-SCREEN]` read as extractive; the author's own script needs a fallback range for exactly this.
- **The value bank fired as an interrogation** — `[VBF:Q-VALUE]` and `[VBF:Q-ECONOMIC-BUYER]` both fail when run as rapid checklists rather than conversation.
- **Metric-building that becomes an unpriced second project** — `[VBF:METRICAGREE]`.
- **Value figures invented to please you** — `[VBF:VALUEDENSITY]`, `[VBF:PERIPHERALVALUE]`: a number the buyer will not defend in writing is not stipulated value and collapses at proposal time.
- **The bridge activity against a soft no** — `[VBF:START-NOW-BRIDGE]` extends unpaid exposure when the delay was actually a refusal.
- **Access retainers sold into a project-shaped need** — `[VBF:ACCESS-IS-VALUE]` degenerates into unlimited unpaid demands.
- **Converting the whole client base at once** — explicitly named as the way `[VBF:CONVERTTRIAGE]` fails.
- **Performing the brand phase early** — `[VBF:ULTIMATE-CONDUCT]` scarcity you cannot back; the source concedes few reach this phase.
