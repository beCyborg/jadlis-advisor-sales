# advisor-weiss — moves

Layer: **консалтинг: цена от ценности** (pricing a professional-services engagement off buyer-stipulated outcome value rather than off inputs).
Citation prefix: `[VBF:]`. Single canon source: Alan Weiss, *Value-Based Fees* (2021). Evidence ceiling for this lens: `practitioner-n-many` — nothing here rises above the author's own repeated practice, and much of it sits lower.

Codes are unique within this file. Grouped by deal stage A→M.

## Stage A — Practice positioning before any deal

### [VBF:WATERTIGHT] Audit which prosperity stage you actually act from
**Move:** Place yourself on the survive → alive → arrive → thrive progression, then check whether your habits (self-talk, spending on your own development, choice of affiliations, image) still belong to an earlier stage; replace the ones that do, so backsliding is closed off.
**APPLY WHEN:** Your practice is healthy by revenue but you keep discounting, over-delivering, or refusing to invest in yourself.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuine cash-flow crisis — this is a mindset reset, not a liquidity plan, and no substitute for actual demand.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated observation across the author's coaching clients; no measurement offered.

### [VBF:HALFTIME] Reprice instead of adding volume
**Move:** When delivery volume is the source of exhaustion, do not optimise the schedule — double the fee and cut the number of engagements so income holds and life improves. Treat "nobody would pay that" as a self-esteem symptom rather than market data.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-volume, low-price delivery practices (speakers, trainers, per-diem consultants) with visible burnout.
**AVOID WHEN:** There is no differentiation or brand to justify the new price — blind doubling on a commodity offer loses the deal, as the source concedes elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one poolside conversation with a speaker's spouse ($1,000/day, 120 dates, ~240 travel days).

### [VBF:BRANDLIFT] Build the personal-name brand as a fee instrument
**Move:** Invest in being known by name in a narrow field so buyers ask for you specifically rather than for a category; use past success publicly instead of letting it sit unused. Target state: a buyer who tells staff not to shop the work or issue an RFP.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already have publishable results and want to reduce fee resistance structurally, over quarters rather than weeks.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a substitute for relationship work on a live deal — brand shortens the relationship phase without removing it; also weak for undifferentiated generalists.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — brand-effect claims illustrated by McKinsey, Ferrari and best-selling-author analogies, not measured.

### [VBF:PASTFUTURE] Three-column past → interventions → client future, and price only column three
**Move:** Write three columns: your unique background and strengths; the mechanisms that transfer it (workshops, coaching, interviews, models, observation); and the client's future results. Check which column your current pricing actually reflects — if it reflects the first two, you are undercharging. Price only the third, built jointly with the buyer. Grow column one after each engagement and prune column two deliberately.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building a pricing rationale, or when you catch yourself estimating fees from days, sites, retreats, or survey size.
**AVOID WHEN:** The engagement genuinely is a commodity deliverable with no articulable downstream result; also when used instead of buyer-stipulated value — it calibrates you, not the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing template plus a prescribed self-assessment exercise.

### [VBF:UNBUNDLE20] The 20-then-12 offering inventory
**Move:** List at least twenty services, products, advice types and other value you can deliver — including bundled, never-delivered and never-requested items — then cull to the strongest dozen. That set becomes the raw material for option ladders and conversion offers.
**APPLY WHEN:** Preparing conversion strategies, or when your proposals keep looking identical.
**AVOID WHEN:** The list becomes a menu shown to buyers — it is an internal artifact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — prescribed practicum exercise.

### [VBF:ALUMSURVEY] Harvest unrecognized results from past clients
**Move:** Systematically ask former clients what the partnership actually changed. Clients routinely name side effects the consultant never counted; record and codify them, since one party's fringe benefit is another's primary benefit and they enlarge the results column for future pricing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Between engagements, or while building the value case for a similar buyer profile.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship ended badly and asking would reopen an unresolved dispute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own client-survey habit and quoted client reactions.

### [VBF:ADVISOR-DEMAND-GEN] Market the advisory relationship as a named service
**Move:** Put retainer-specific testimonials and typical results on the site; mention advisory engagements in talks and articles; include an advisory option inside proposals, most naturally as the third option or as post-project implementation counsel; structure pro bono work as advisory rather than delivery; build authority on the topic itself.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already have several completed advisory relationships to reference.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no track record — the source concedes retainers are far easier for established brands than for newcomers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own practice-building path.

### [VBF:NO-DEAL-VIBES] Remove the behaviors that signal you will discount
**Move:** Stop the four tells: accepting last-minute rescheduling of initial meetings, agreeing to be delegated down to non-buyers, tolerating interrupted or truncated meetings, and giving free consulting during the sales process. Diagnostic: if most prospects ask for a deal, the cause is your behavior, not the market.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discount requests have become the norm across your pipeline.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rigidity would break a genuinely constrained buyer's calendar — this is a signal-management heuristic, not a scheduling law.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a behavioral checklist with no measurement behind the causal claim.

## Stage B — Qualification and access to the economic buyer

### [VBF:Q-ECONOMIC-BUYER] Qualify who the real economic buyer is
**Move:** Work out whose budget supports the initiative; whose operation is most affected by the outcomes; who should set the objectives; who will be evaluated on results; who is the most important sponsor; who has the most at stake in investment and credibility; who chose this direction; whose support is vital; who people look to in order to judge whether this is real; to whom your contact turns for approval on options; and who makes the final decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** First contact, or any time you suspect you are talking to a feasibility buyer or gatekeeper.
**AVOID WHEN:** Fired as a rapid checklist — asked in sequence these read as an interrogation; the intent is to surface one or two answers naturally.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard qualification set across his practice.

### [VBF:COMMITGATE] Gate the proposal on commitment, not on interest
**Move:** Before writing anything, test whether the buyer will co-own the project — name their own outcomes, put their people in, take public credit, argue with you honestly. Treat easy agreement and fast delegation to HR/finance/legal as a red flag and keep building the relationship instead of accelerating to a fast sale.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer is agreeable but passive; buyer wants to hand you to a technical evaluator; you feel tempted to close quickly.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely transactional, small, well-specified work where partnership has nothing to attach to; also when stalling reads as an inability to propose.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's repeated deal experience; the supporting quadrant chart is his own estimate, not data.

### [VBF:SEQUENCE] Run values → relationship → conceptual agreement before fees exist
**Move:** Establish shared business beliefs, then a trusting peer relationship, then agreement on objectives, measures and value. Only after all three does any number appear. Treat the sequence as ordered, not as a checklist to be reshuffled.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any consulting sale you intend to price on value rather than scope.
**AVOID WHEN:** The contact is not the economic buyer (the sequence then binds someone who cannot fund it); procurement-mandated RFP with no buyer access.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the author's standard operating sequence across his practice.

### [VBF:EXPERTBOUNDARY] Decline to be told how you will do your work
**Move:** When a buyer specifies your method, schedule and required domain credentials, say plainly that you do not work that way and that doing so would not help them — split the domains explicitly: they own their industry expertise, you own how consulting is done. Let the fee conversation wait until after the proposal is agreed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Authoritarian or highly specified buyer; multi-stage vendor selection where you are being commoditised against a specification.
**AVOID WHEN:** You lack the brand or track record to survive the pushback; formal RFPs where non-compliance means disqualification rather than differentiation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single Mercedes-Benz North America selection process.

### [VBF:RFPBYPASS] Answer the RFP and attach your own proposal
**Move:** Default to not responding to RFPs at all — they pre-determine the solution and are scored by people rewarded for saving money. If you must respond, comply with the required format, then attach a separate proposal exceeding the stated scope with value-based fees. Aim at sole-source status via a book, intellectual capital, language skills or unique experience; note the claim that US federal FAR rules permit choosing the highest-value bid rather than the cheapest.
**APPLY WHEN:** Government or large-institution procurement you cannot route around.
**AVOID WHEN:** The process mechanically disqualifies non-conforming attachments.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing policy; the FAR reference is a factual claim worth re-verifying against current regulation.

### [VBF:INBOUNDWEIGHT] Treat inbound contact as latent urgency and price accordingly
**Move:** When the prospect initiated contact, assume the urgency and need are greater than admitted; probe for the trigger and build on it rather than defusing it with reassurance. Weight inbound prospects as inherently more valuable than ones you chased.
**APPLY WHEN:** Prospect reached out and a deadline, incident, or mandate is hinted at.
**AVOID WHEN:** The inbound is a price-shopping RFP or a mandated three-bid exercise — the inbound signal is noise there.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — asserted asymmetry with no supporting data.

## Stage C — Relationship and trust

### [VBF:TRUST-BUILD] Manufacture and read trust signals before asking about money
**Move:** Create trust by delivering value inside the meeting itself (a sharp question about a competitor's pricing move), using self-deprecating humor rather than jokes, and requiring an uninterrupted meeting. Read it from observable behavior: the buyer blocks interruptions, asks your advice on the spot and takes it, shares confidential information, uses humor, holds eye contact, asks for proving examples, discloses personal discomfort, and takes pushback without defensiveness. If those are absent, invest in relationship before pushing for value figures.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deciding whether to move from rapport to objectives/measures/value questions; early meetings, especially cold or referral-thin entries.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are not with the economic buyer (trust built through intermediaries does not transfer); remote or asynchronous contact where cues are unreadable; culturally different signalling norms — the list is Western-executive-shaped.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — an observed-signal checklist from repeated buyer meetings; no study or counted base.

### [VBF:PEERPUSH] Demonstrate value by disagreeing early
**Move:** In early meetings, question the buyer's premises, decline impossible expectations, and push back where you genuinely disagree — treat willingness to disagree as part of the value being sold. Align image, materials and web presence with the price level you intend to charge.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer is testing you; buyer has pre-decided the solution; you are being positioned as a supplier rather than a peer.
**AVOID WHEN:** The disagreement is manufactured for effect, or aimed at the buyer's genuine domain expertise rather than at the problem framing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "Mercedes-Benz syndrome" argument plus the author's own war stories.

### [VBF:PROSPECTED] Run the prospect-education checklist in early meetings
**Move:** Ten guardrails: no fee before objectives and their value are agreed; no time-unit basis; explain why a single value fee serves the client; deflect competitor comparison by noting the buyer's own firm also operates unlike its competitors; never commit to a fixed quantity of time; talk results not tasks; refuse assumptions of constant on-site presence or being on call; minimise deliverables; refuse contingency; and deliver some value inside the meeting itself.
**APPLY WHEN:** First and second meetings with an economic buyer, before any proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has already been contracted on a different basis mid-engagement; applying all ten rigidly to small transactional work reads as posturing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a diagnostic checklist refined over the author's practice.

### [VBF:DIAGNOSE-TOGETHER] Make the buyer a co-diagnostician
**Move:** Instead of prescribing a method, put a simple process visual or profiling grid in front of the buyer and have them place their own organization on each factor. Perceived value rises because the buyer co-owns the diagnosis and therefore the treatment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer is engaged but has not yet accepted that a real need exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer wants a fast prescriptive answer and has no appetite for a workshop-style exchange.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — analogy-driven, illustrated by one figure.

## Stage D — Conceptual agreement: objectives, measures, value

### [VBF:CONCEPTAGREE] Objectives / measures / value, in that order, with the economic buyer only
**Move:** Before proposing, get the buyer to state the business outcomes, how progress and completion will be measured, and what achieving them is worth. Take the value numbers from the buyer's mouth, not your model. Only then set fees, and never run this with a non-buyer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any project you intend to price on outcomes.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot reach the check-writer, or the outcome is unmeasurable and the buyer will stipulate nothing — the source's own limits imply a fallback to time-based work here.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core of the author's long practice; the attached acceptance rates are self-reported.

### [VBF:Q-OBJECTIVES] Establish business objectives in the buyer's language
**Move:** Ask how conditions would ideally improve; what the buyer wants to accomplish; what would be different on success; how the customer would be better served; how the buyer's boss and employees would notice; which aspects are most troubling; the three must-do priorities if forced to choose now; the intended impact on ROI/ROE/ROS/ROA and shareholder value; expected market-share, profitability or productivity gain; and how the buyer personally will be evaluated on the project's results.
**APPLY WHEN:** In the conceptual-agreement conversation, before any number is mentioned.
**AVOID WHEN:** With a buyer who cannot commit budget — objectives elicited from a non-buyer produce a proposal nobody owns.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — appendix question bank drawn from the author's practice.

### [VBF:QGTRIHF-BROADEN] Open on objectives, then widen them
**Move:** Open discovery with what the buyer wants to accomplish as a result of the project, then systematically widen each stated objective (regional share → national share; one executive's delegation skills → the receiving side plus the surrounding culture). Wider objectives carry higher value and therefore higher fees. Drop unprompted references to adjacent services so you are not stereotyped into one competency.
**APPLY WHEN:** First substantive meeting with the economic buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** The widening is not genuinely wanted — inflating scope the buyer does not value converts into sticker shock later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a habitual questioning pattern.

### [VBF:WHYLADDER] Ask "why" until the requested input becomes an outcome
**Move:** When the buyer names a deliverable (sales training, coach my VP, a two-day retreat, a mentoring program), ask why, take the stated reason, and keep asking until the underlying business condition surfaces — training requested to cover churn points at customer service; a delegation-coaching request points at an authoritarian culture; a mentoring request points at flawed hiring criteria. Reframe the project around the deeper need before any fee talk.
**APPLY WHEN:** Inbound request arrives pre-specified as a deliverable or format; buyer arrives with a pre-formed solution.
**AVOID WHEN:** The want is a compliance or board-mandated programme with a fixed form; the contact is a procurement or low-level party who cannot re-scope; you have no relationship credit yet, so repeated "why" reads as obstruction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a short dialogue pattern the author uses routinely, with multiple reframing examples.

### [VBF:PROFITLADDER] Unpack an abstract objective into downstream uses
**Move:** When the buyer names something generic like "more profit", ask what achieving it would let them do — maximum bonuses, more hiring, debt paydown, R&D, dividends, an acquisition. Each answer is another value statement, and collectively they justify a much larger fee than the bare objective did.
**APPLY WHEN:** Objectives come back vague or single-line.
**AVOID WHEN:** The downstream uses are speculative enough that the buyer will not own them in writing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked question sequence from the author's practice.

### [VBF:Q-MEASURES] Establish measures of success before proposing
**Move:** Ask how the buyer will know the objective is met; who tracks progress and how that person is held accountable; what information is needed from customers, vendors and employees, and in what form; how the buyer's boss will know; how environment, culture and structure will be improved; impact on ROI/ROE/ROA/ROS; how attrition, retention, morale or safety will be determined; how frequently progress must be assessed and by what method; what counts as acceptable versus ideal improvement; and how the buyer would prove the improvement to others.
**APPLY WHEN:** Immediately after objectives, before value.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's answers are being invented to satisfy you — a metric neither party finds reasonable is worse than none.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — appendix question bank from the author's practice.

### [VBF:METRICAGREE] Nail down the measuring instrument, and build it if it is missing
**Move:** Agree explicitly which report, review or data source will measure the outcome. Where the client believes they measure objectively but actually relies on anecdote, evaluate the metric and help construct a sound one — and treat that metric-building as value delivered that justifies a higher fee.
**APPLY WHEN:** Objectives are agreed and you are moving to measures; the client's existing reporting is soft or self-reported.
**AVOID WHEN:** No one on the client side owns the data, so the metric will never be produced; also when metric-building quietly becomes a separate project you did not price.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as standard practice with sales-reporting examples.

### [VBF:QUALMETRIC] Build defensible qualitative measures
**Move:** Where the outcome is subjective (image, teamwork, stress, communication), construct measures against six parameters: the buyer personally judges; judgment rests on observed behaviour and factual evidence; success is graded rather than binary; there is a reasonable time bound; employees comment favourably; customers comment favourably. Then review progress with the buyer on a regular cadence.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-value outcomes that resist quantification; a buyer who says they will know it when they see it.
**AVOID WHEN:** The judge is not the economic buyer, or the criteria are set only after work begins — both leave you exposed at payment time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — parameters generalised from the author's projects.

### [VBF:BOARDTEST] Convert "I'll know it when I see it" into evidence
**Move:** Ask how the buyer would prove the improvement to their board, or to a journalist writing about it, then capture the concrete indicators the answer produces — fewer and shorter meetings with fewer cross-department disputes, customers no longer reporting conflicting advice or duplicate billing, talent moving between departments without CEO adjudication, the issue dropping down the annual employee-survey list, fewer private escalations from direct reports.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer names an intangible objective and resists specifying measures.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer reads the framing as a challenge to their judgment; also when the resulting indicators all lag by more than the project horizon.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one CEO teamwork engagement at a $600M operation.

### [VBF:NOMAGICNUM] Commit to direction, not to a target number
**Move:** Phrase the commitment as maximising, reducing toward a benchmark, or improving a direction rather than hitting a specific figure. If the buyer insists on a number, ask what a conservative result would look like and halve it; if they give a range, take the bottom and halve that, so the ROI ratio is unarguable.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer is uncomfortable with ambiguity and pushes for a guaranteed figure.
**AVOID WHEN:** Markets where committing to a number is the norm and the seller genuinely controls the lever; systematic halving also risks understating value in a well-instrumented account.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a stated rule with a worked heuristic, no outcome data.

### [VBF:Q-VALUE] Work the value bank, including the cost of inaction
**Move:** Get the buyer to state the value themselves: what is different at the end; what happens if nothing is done and what that costs annually; what happens if the project fails; what happened when past attempts failed; what becomes possible; effect on revenue, share, profit, productivity; effect on image, morale, safety, reputation; the three biggest impacts; the board's or boss's reaction; what it means to the buyer personally; secondary and knock-on value; what they will be proudest of; the legacy; impact on ROI/ROA/ROE/ROS. Keep the list written but deliver it as conversation, not interrogation.
**APPLY WHEN:** After objectives and measures are agreed, as the direct basis for the fee.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship is thin (a rapid-fire version reads as an audit); the contact is not the economic buyer or has no P&L visibility, so annualized cost answers are numbers nobody will defend later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's core diagnostic set, expanded in the appendices; the "no limit to perceived value, hence no limit to fees" conclusion drawn from it is assertion.

### [VBF:VALUE-COLLAB] Set value with the buyer live, then reuse their language
**Move:** In a live conversation rather than email, walk the buyer through both quantitative outcomes (retention, share) and qualitative ones (reduced stress, image, safety) until they visibly agree, then reuse that agreed language verbatim as the value section of the proposal. Where a buyer calls an outcome priceless or invaluable, accept that and stop probing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pre-proposal conceptual agreement with an economic buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are with a lower-level buyer — the lower the buyer, the more you must force quantifiable, written measures instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the core repeated mechanism of the source.

### [VBF:VALUEDENSITY] Three value statements per objective, half of them monetized
**Move:** For each agreed objective, elicit at least three distinct value statements and make sure roughly half carry a money figure. Mix hard costs with soft ones (employer-of-choice effects, media coverage, reduced training cost) so the total dwarfs the fee.
**APPLY WHEN:** During conceptual agreement, while the buyer is still supplying numbers.
**AVOID WHEN:** The figures are being fabricated to please you — a number you invent and they nod at is not stipulated value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as the key to high-fee, high-ROI proposals, with worked examples.

### [VBF:PERIPHERALVALUE] Recommend value the buyer missed
**Move:** Review the agreed objectives and proactively name second-order value the buyer did not raise: cross-selling reducing the need for a specialized field force, retention savings on search-firm fees, better technical capability cutting rework. Add these to the value side before dividing by the fee.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer's own value list is thin or single-dimensional.
**AVOID WHEN:** The added items are ones the buyer will not stand behind — imputed value the buyer disowns weakens the whole case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as routine practice with concrete examples.

### [VBF:TASK2OUT] Rewrite every activity as a client result, and keep the conversation on their future
**Move:** For each activity, write the paired outcome and lead with it — focus groups become identifying the causes of top-performer turnover; executive coaching becomes making that executive a full partner during an M&A; a strategy retreat becomes a two-year strategy meeting the board's growth targets; exit interviews become retention; a performance-review redesign becomes new-hire retention; a feasibility analysis becomes maximising return on an acquisition or preventing a bad one. Position your experience as input and your methodology as a device — neither is what merits the fee.
**APPLY WHEN:** Building proposals, website copy and early-meeting language; the buyer is comparison-shopping deliverables or asking about methodology.
**AVOID WHEN:** The outcome asserted is not actually within your influence — the reframe then over-promises; also when the buyer is a technical evaluator who legitimately must assess method feasibility, where refusing to discuss approach reads as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter's task/outcome table and Appendix F, drawn from the author's engagements.

### [VBF:NODELIVER] Refuse the deliverables negotiation, redirect to the outcome
**Move:** When asked how many reports, sessions or site visits you will provide, do not answer with a number and do not bounce the question back — state that the artifacts are not the point and the measurable improvement is, and reserve the right to add, drop or change artifacts as the work reveals what is needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer or staff score proposals on deliverable counts; procurement-style comparison is forming.
**AVOID WHEN:** Contractual or regulated environments where specified artifacts are the deliverable; a buyer who needs a report for internal governance has a legitimate requirement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a standing rule with supporting engagement examples.

### [VBF:METHODLINE] Share the kind of method, withhold the quantities
**Move:** Tell the buyer what class of approach you may use and its practical implications (focus groups need their people's time; phone interviews do not; classroom work needs scheduling; on-site observation changes the behaviour observed), while declining to commit to counts, schedules or fixed meeting attendance.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer reasonably wants to know what working with you looks like operationally.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer needs firm resourcing commitments to secure internal participation — total vagueness there costs you the project.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's drawn line between acceptable and excessive disclosure.

## Stage E — Setting the fee

### [VBF:NOHOURS] Never introduce a time unit into the conversation
**Move:** Do not quote hourly, daily, per-participant or headcount-based numbers at any point, including "for reference"; if pressed, explain that a single value-based fee protects the client from paying more for slower work and from re-deciding every time they want help. Identical work for two clients can carry very different fees when the outcomes differ in size (coaching a VP over a $200M division versus a manager over a $350K centre). Discard the client's fee history as a constraint and do not discount for remote delivery.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any first fee conversation; especially when the buyer or procurement frames the ask as days, sessions, or seats.

### [VBF:NOCONTINGENCY] Decline contingency, pay-for-performance and percentage-of-result deals
**Move:** Refuse to tie payment to a share of the client's gain, and say why: you do not control the variables, it makes you a party to the outcome rather than an advisor, and it corrupts your judgment between short-term numbers and long-term client interest. Take a high-margin flat fee and rely on follow-on work if results are dramatic.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer proposes success fees, gainshare, or equity-style upside as a way to de-risk your price.
**AVOID WHEN:** Business models where risk-sharing is the market norm and the seller genuinely controls execution (search, brokerage, some agency work) — the source's own examples show these markets exist and clear.

### [VBF:WHYME3] The why-me / why-now / why-this-way audit
**Move:** Before pricing, answer in writing: is the buyer talking to a few consultants or many; do you hold unique expertise, industry history, authorship, or prior work with this buyer; were you referred by a trusted source; are you known in the industry; are you conveniently available; could any competent speaker or trainer do this. Then: what happens if they do nothing, is there a closing window, has urgency spiked, is there funding that expires. Then: why is this not being done internally, have they tried and failed, what happened with previous consultants, why is this buyer the sponsor, what is unique about your relationship and the trust in it, and what is your unique added value.
**APPLY WHEN:** After conceptual agreement, before fixing option prices; also when justifying a premium against a comparison-shopping buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** The honest answers are weak — you are then one of many indistinguishable bidders, there is no uplift, and the source's own logic says the engagement may not be yours.

### [VBF:BACKWARDROI] The eight-step backward fee calculation
**Move:** (1) Establish value with the economic buyer during conceptual agreement. (2) Assess your uniqueness. (3) Draft options, cumulative or exclusive, ordered by rising value. (4) Work backward from stipulated value at a conservative return multiple — $2M annualized savings at 20:1 implies a $100,000 first option. (5) Step remaining options up by at least 20% each ($120K, then $144K). (6) Add 20% across all options if uniqueness is high, 10% if moderate, nothing if low. (7) Sanity-check against total client value and your own margin, adjusting by no more than 15% either way. (8) Submit and stop second-guessing.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are new to value pricing and need scaffolding, and the value figure is monetized and buyer-stipulated.
**AVOID WHEN:** Value is entirely qualitative (no monetized anchor to divide); low-ACV or transactional work where a 10–20x divisor produces a fee no buyer will entertain — the arithmetic only holds when stipulated value is large.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own scaffold, offered explicitly as temporary science before intuition.

### [VBF:ROIFLOOR] Overcharging test via conservative return multiple
**Move:** Check the fee against the buyer-stipulated conservative value. A 10:1 return is treated as very defensible. If the resulting ROI would make the investment look questionable, either the fee is too high or you have underestimated the value — determine which before cutting anything.
**APPLY WHEN:** Final fee sanity check before submitting.
**AVOID WHEN:** Value is unmonetized — the multiple has no denominator then.

### [VBF:ROIPHRASE] Use one standing sentence to frame the fee

**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer asks about price, or objects to the size of a number after value has been stipulated.
**AVOID WHEN:** Value has not yet been established by the buyer — the sentence then asserts a return nobody has quantified.

### [VBF:QUOTE-HIGH-REDUCE-VALUE] Open aggressive; adjust downward only by removing value
**Move:** Set the fee at the aggressive end on the assumption that you systematically underestimate your own value, and if you must move, lower it by taking value out of scope rather than discounting the same scope. Low fees are treated as effectively unraisable no matter how much value you later demonstrate.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are competing against larger firms whose overhead forces high prices anyway.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's perceived value has not been established — a high number with no conceptual agreement behind it is sticker shock, not anchoring.

### [VBF:NO-LOWBALL-ENTRY] Never discount to get in the door

### [VBF:SOLO-PREMIUM] Charge more, not less, for doing it all personally

**APPLY WHEN:** Client raises staffing composition or asks for a senior-only team.
**AVOID WHEN:** The work genuinely requires a team you cannot supply.

### [VBF:PRICE-PRESENTATION] Non-round numbers, matter-of-fact delivery, rehearsed

**APPLY WHEN:** Every fee conversation; especially useful for consultants whose fees have recently jumped.
**AVOID WHEN:** The flat confident delivery is not backed by agreed value — it then reads as bluff.

### [VBF:ALLOCATION-ASK] Ask for the allocation right before proposing

### [VBF:NO-REMOTE-DISCOUNT] Refuse to cut fees for not being physically present

Concede in-person advantages (body language, movement, easier breakouts) as conveniences, not value.

### [VBF:GOODDEALFACTORS] Check the non-fee good-deal variables and amplify them

## Stage F — Proposal and options

### [VBF:OPTIONS3] Build a graduated choice of yeses
**Move:** Never present a binary go/no-go proposal. After establishing a value-anchored fee range, spread it across three or more options of visibly increasing value — some cumulative, some standalone add-ons that combine with any base — so the buyer's internal question shifts from whether to proceed to which version to buy. Keep meaningful distance between them, make each add genuinely distinct value rather than more of the same, keep the set simple, deliberately include a richer option beyond the minimum needed to hit the objective, and be ready to explain the incremental value or risk reduction at each step. Name the section something like "Methodology and Options".
**APPLY WHEN:** Conceptual agreement exists and value has been stipulated; the buyer has real budget discretion; also usable tactically for scheduling choices.
**AVOID WHEN:** Objectives are not yet agreed (options then become a menu of tasks); you cannot construct genuinely differentiated value tiers, so the extra options are padding; a fixed scope is mandated by an RFP.

**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — long-standing practice with worked pricing examples and a self-reported career-long option-selection split; the acceptance-lift figure attached is unsupported.

### [VBF:UNBUNDLE] Unbundle instead of stuffing everything into one price
**Move:** Resist packing every capability you have into a single go/no-go proposal. The plumber fixing a leak does not throw in re-caulking and a new heater at the same price. Separate the capabilities so they can be tiered.
**APPLY WHEN:** Your draft proposal has one price and a long deliverables list.
**AVOID WHEN:** Components are genuinely inseparable for the outcome to hold.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as the common consultant error the author corrects.

### [VBF:OVERBUDGET] Place one option above the stated budget
**Move:** When a budget has been named, quote two or more options inside it and one clearly superior option above it. A client who found $175,000 can usually find another $50,000 if the added value warrants it; no buyer will ever invite you to exceed their budget, so the options structure is what makes the ask legitimate. Nothing is risked, because compliant choices remain available.
**APPLY WHEN:** A budget is known and you believe the value ceiling is higher.
**AVOID WHEN:** The budget is a hard-capped approved allocation with no discretionary authority, or procurement rules formally disqualify over-budget bids.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — asserted from practice, including one deal lost to a competitor who bid above budget with more value.

### [VBF:NOFEESYET] Never attach fees to options during conceptual agreement
**Move:** It is fine to discuss possible options while agreeing concepts, but assign no numbers — just signal that the buyer will have choices differing in value and risk protection. In the written proposal, list options under methodology and put all fees in a separate terms section, so the buyer forms a value preference before seeing price.
**APPLY WHEN:** Conceptual agreement conversations and proposal drafting.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer requires an indicative budget to keep the process alive — then give a range, not per-option pricing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative

### [VBF:PROPOSAL-AS-CONFIRMATION] Proposals confirm agreement, they do not explore it
**Move:** Do not send a proposal before conceptual agreement on objectives, measures and value; otherwise it becomes the opening position for a downward negotiation. Attach a validity window (30 days) after which a new proposal is required.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer asks for "something in writing" early.
**AVOID WHEN:** An RFP process requires a written submission before any buyer access — the author's answer there is to question whether to bid at all.

### [VBF:PROPPROTECT] Protect the proposal from being shopped
**Move:** Stipulate in the proposal that it is for the buyer's use only and may not be shown outside the organization without written consent, and add a copyright notice whenever you suspect it may be circulated to competing firms.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive situations; buyers known to run bake-offs.

### [VBF:CANCELTERMS] Proposal language for delay, cancellation and non-cancelability
**Move:** Put in the proposal that the client may cancel and reschedule without penalty subject to mutually agreeable dates, that you cannot be accountable for the original objectives beyond a 90-day delay, and that all obligations end after a six-month delay unless agreed in writing. **APPLY WHEN:** Drafting proposals, especially where timing windows matter or buyer turnover is plausible.
**AVOID WHEN:** Local contract law or the client's standard terms override; suing is impractical and the clause is your only leverage.

### [VBF:PHASE-REBATE] Rebate part of phase one to lock the next phase
**Move:** When a client insists on phasing a complex project, commit in the phase-one proposal to rebating a share of the phase-one fee (the author caps around 25%) if phase two is accepted within a set window such as 30 days of phase-one completion. Size the percentage against the later phase's value, not the earlier one. Skip or token-ize it when continuation is already near-certain.
**APPLY WHEN:** Genuine sequential dependency exists and the client will not commit to the whole program.

### [VBF:START-NOW-BRIDGE] Invent a bridge activity to start now and secure the deposit
**Move:** When an accepted proposal is about to be postponed for operational reasons, propose a low-intrusion parallel activity that begins immediately (observing the busy period, staying out of the way, debriefing periodically) and feeds the later main work — then close by stating you will send the paperwork and return next week. Use whoever in the room has voiced regret about waiting as the lever.
**APPLY WHEN:** The proposal is already approved, only timing is open, and the deposit is tied to start date.
**AVOID WHEN:** The delay is a soft no in disguise — a bridge activity then just extends unpaid exposure.

## Stage G — Objections and negotiation

### [VBF:FOUR-FILTERS] Diagnose which resistance is actually operating
**Move:** Before rebutting, classify the resistance as no trust, no need, no urgency, or no money, and respond only in that lane — adding trust-building for a no-urgency buyer, or cutting price for a no-need buyer, addresses your discomfort rather than theirs. Expect one or two to be live at a time, rarely all four.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any pushback in discovery or after the proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The objection is a genuine disqualifier (no economic buyer access, no fit).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the source's central objection taxonomy, applied throughout.

### [VBF:FEE-DEFLECTION-SET] Refuse the early fee conversation, with a 24-hour promise
**Move:** Keep several deflections ready and combine as needed: you cannot quote without knowing the need or whether you are even right for it; quoting now would be unfair to them; quoting now would be unethical given unknown competency fit; a deliberately absurd range ("$5,000 to $1,000,000 — seriously, I don't know") followed by a pivot to exploration; turning the question back as "what's your budget?" and noting neither side can answer yet; and asking whether they can wait 24 or 48 hours for full options in their hands. Hold the line through repeated pressure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any premature price question, including friendly ones, before conceptual agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** Speaking-engagement inquiries and unfamiliar novice buyers, where the author himself asks about budget early; the buyer's culture reads evasion as gamesmanship; or the deal is small enough that a range would qualify faster than a discovery cycle.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a scripted rebuttal set the author reports using routinely, recurring across chapters with a case study.

### [VBF:FIVE-GATES] Five preconditions before any fee discussion
**Move:** Discuss fees only after all five are true: you have met the economic buyer; a trusting relationship exists; the four resistance areas have been cleared; objectives, measures and value are agreed; and the buyer has agreed to receive a written proposal with a choice of options.
**APPLY WHEN:** As a self-check before naming any number.
**AVOID WHEN:** Speaking engagements and novice or infrequent buyers — the author himself carves these out for an early budget question.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as an unconditional sequencing rule.

### [VBF:CREATE-URGENCY] Manufacture a reason to act now
**Move:** Against "the timing isn't right", use one or more of: an impending competitor action; a closing window with a quantified cost of lost opportunity; evidence the damage is larger than perceived; ROI larger than expected; evidence the situation is declining rather than flat; and a personal stake for the buyer (visibility, reputation for innovative leadership). Name the underlying driver — fear of disruption, weak perceived return, "everyone lives with this", or intellectual-but-not-emotional buy-in.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer accepts the need and trusts you but defers.
**AVOID WHEN:** The urgency claim is fabricated — the personal-stake lever in particular shades into appealing to the buyer's career interest over the organization's, which the source does not caveat. Ethical contour: do not use a lever you would not state openly to the buyer.

### [VBF:MONEY-IS-PRIORITY] Treat "no budget" and "no time" as allocation, not scarcity
**Move:** When the buyer claims no money after trust, need and urgency are established, do not fold and do not cut price — shift to collaboratively locating funds: split across two fiscal years, draw from an unrelated budget line (hold the annual convention locally instead of overseas), or accept partial non-cash compensation. Reframe time and money as priorities rather than resources, supported by the observation that operations are visibly still being funded even in a downturn.
**APPLY WHEN:** A budget or bandwidth objection arrives late, after conceptual agreement, including in a downturn.
**AVOID WHEN:** The first three resistance areas have not actually been satisfied, so the money objection is a symptom; or the constraint is a hard external gate (bankruptcy, absolute spending freeze) where the reframe reads as tone-deaf.

### [VBF:STICKER-SHOCK-SCREEN] Red-flag list plus a budget question with three prepared replies
**Move:** Watch for investment-naivety flags: never used consultants; used only cheap ones; cost-control culture with no ROI thinking; short-term focus; company losing money; owner weighing business spend against personal spend; sees you as unproven and therefore cheap. When flags are present, ask the budget question after conceptual agreement but before writing the proposal. Prepare for three answers — refusal to disclose (offer your own range and ask if it is in the ballpark, part as friends if not); a low number (accept if workable, otherwise state your real range honestly); and "whatever is reasonable" (thank them and send the proposal).
**APPLY WHEN:** Owner-led businesses, first-time consulting buyers, or any buyer you cannot read.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trust is weak — the author's own script shows this question can be received as extractive.

### [VBF:SMACK-COMPARISON] Prepared cost comparisons and counterarguments for late objections
**Move:** Build a library of comparisons that reframe the fee against spending the buyer already tolerates: copier warranties and maintenance versus the whole project; spillage and scrap at ten times the top option; the cost of one lost customer per week; the cost of a bad hire; fully loaded cost of losing a talented employee; the cost of a recent public gaffe; savings from cutting R&D commercialization time. Source it from voluntary client disclosures, published statistics, business-press clippings, prior engagements' ROI, cost-of-inaction math, and the impracticality of doing it internally. Prepare positive counterarguments rather than defensive rebuttals, and ignore what competitors quote — the buyer's self-interest, not competitive positioning, is what dissolves the objection.
**APPLY WHEN:** A fee objection appears after conceptual agreement and proposal, from a buyer you know can afford it, or the buyer invokes competitive quotes.
**AVOID WHEN:** The objection is substantive rather than posturing; the buyer has no discretion, so the embarrassment mechanism just ends the conversation; or a formal RFP makes ignoring competitors plainly risky. Deliberately embarrassing a buyer is a relationship risk the source underweights and is manipulation dressed as reframing — use the comparison as information, never as humiliation.

**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reports reusing the same comparisons across clients; the embarrass-the-buyer mechanism itself is assertion.

### [VBF:REMOVEVALUE] Trade fee reductions for value removal, never volunteer them
**Move:** Do not build automatic volume or multi-project discounts into proposals. If the buyer asks for a slightly lower fee on a given option, agree in principle and ask which value they would like removed — drop the international leg, the post-survey debriefs, the written report — so you are never seen as having carried padded fees. If the buyer prefers option 3 but can only fund option 2, accept option 2 immediately; that is a choice, not a negotiation to reopen.
**APPLY WHEN:** Price pushback arrives after options are on the table.
**AVOID WHEN:** The pushback is about scope clarity rather than money; the value cut would break the agreed objectives, in which case decline instead; mechanical use of the line reads as a gotcha with a buyer you have thin trust with.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing scripts and consistent policy across the tactics chapter.

### [VBF:QUIDPROQUO] Never lower a fee without extracting a concession
**Move:** When the buyer offers to close immediately in exchange for a lower fee, accept only against a trade: 100% of the discounted fee paid up front, removal of some project value, referrals and introductions, a recorded session, an association introduction, products at cost, or client personnel absorbing more of the labor. Unilateral discounting invites the buyer to test how much further you will drop.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late-stage price-for-signature pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The concession you extract costs more than the discount saves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/transactional

### [VBF:NO-DEAL-UNASKED] Never volunteer a concession the buyer hasn't requested

**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-day or multi-event engagements where you feel obliged to give something back.
**AVOID WHEN:** A genuine relationship-repair situation where a gesture is warranted — the source states no exception, which is itself the gap.

### [VBF:WALK-AWAY] Be genuinely willing to leave
**Move:** At an impasse, say plainly that you do not think you can take the project on the terms offered, thank them, and propose staying in touch. Refuse troublesome, unethical, or unpleasant prospects at any fee.
**APPLY WHEN:** Terms are being ground down, or the prospect's behavior is a preview of the client relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no pipeline — the tactic depends on real alternatives, which the source does not acknowledge.

## Stage H — Terms, payment and cash

### [VBF:TERMS-FIRST] Open with the payment terms best for you, and price the prepayment
**Move:** State full payment in advance as your policy; fall back to 50% on acceptance (acceptance, not commencement) and 50% at 45 days regardless of project length. Offer a fixed courtesy discount of 5–10% for the entire fee paid on acceptance — some accounts-payable departments are obliged to take any offered discount, and the benefits to you are immediate use of the money and freedom to speak candidly without a future check hanging over the relationship. Write the proposal so the first payment constitutes acceptance in lieu of signature, and never accept payment contingent on completion conditions. Distinguish this sharply from discounting for cash in hand, which is a tax-evasion signal to refuse.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every proposal, especially any with an installment alternative.
**AVOID WHEN:** The discount would breach your margin floor, or the client's payment systems genuinely cannot prepay — negotiate the schedule, but never invert to payment-on-completion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's standing terms, including a $250,000 insurance case paid in two $125,000 tranches to stay inside an EVP's signing authority, with the proposal never signed.

### [VBF:EXPENSE-DISCIPLINE] Expenses extra, billed monthly, ideally absorbed by the client
**Move:** State orally and in writing that fees are "plus expenses"; bill client-specific materials you had to create for them; submit reimbursements every month without exception, due on presentation with roughly a 30-day grace; and where possible have the client absorb travel directly via their own hotel master bill, travel agent and car service. For overseas work, quote and collect in USD drawn on US banks and prefer wire transfer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any engagement involving travel or bespoke materials.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have deliberately quoted an all-inclusive fee.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — includes a $3,500 self-funded presentation later recognized as billable and a consultant carrying ~$100,000 in unsubmitted reimbursements.

### [VBF:STOPWORK] Stop work on non-payment
**Move:** If a client pleads financial distress or imposes 120-day vendor terms, decline the request, state that you are a small business dependent on cash flow and expect the agreement honored, and halt work until paid. Involve your attorney without embarrassment. The premise: money in a going concern is a priority, not a resource.
**APPLY WHEN:** Invoices go unpaid or the client unilaterally changes terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have contractual milestone obligations whose breach exposes you, or the relationship is strategically worth carrying — the source does not concede this case, which is itself a limit of the advice.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule with no case data.

### [VBF:BUYER-CHANNEL-DEFENSE] Keep purchasing and AP out of the negotiation
**Move:** Get explicit fee and terms agreement with the economic buyer in the room and tell them a finance handoff is fine only for paperwork. If procurement tries to renegotiate, refuse and escalate to the buyer as a shared problem. Read client boilerplate that arrives with the approval and push back on cancellation rights and 120-day expense terms. On late payments go straight to the buyer, framed as a small shared problem. If the proposal exceeds the buyer's signing authority, split it into multiple invoices. Keep legal boilerplate out of your own proposal so it never routes to counsel.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enterprise clients with formal procurement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The organization's rules genuinely require competitive procurement — refusing entirely means walking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the purchasing-agent case study (the agent was later fired) is a single, unusually favorable outcome.

### [VBF:MESSYBOX] Escalate the client's accounting objection to the revenue owner
**Move:** When the client's internal finance refuses to book a value fee because it does not fit their hourly template, do not renegotiate the fee — put the choice to the person who owns the P&L as a straight comparison between the smaller number that fits the spreadsheet and the larger number that does not.
**APPLY WHEN:** The deal is agreed with the buyer but stalls in internal accounting or procurement formatting.
**AVOID WHEN:** The obstacle is a genuine policy or audit constraint rather than convenience; escalating over the buyer's head without their consent burns the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one Arthur Andersen engagement, $400,000 value fee versus $157,000 hourly equivalent.

### [VBF:BILLINGSYSTEMFIX] Fix your own firm's billing machinery, not the client's objection
**Move:** When your own firm's accounting forces an hourly expression that horrifies clients, escalate internally with the revenue comparison rather than managing the client. In the cited case a value-priced $60,000 engagement became an unswallowable $2,500/hour; the argument to the CEO was a choice between roughly $250,000 of revenue under the old bookkeeping and over $1M for the same work under value pricing.
**APPLY WHEN:** You work inside a firm whose systems only record hours and clients accept the total but reject the implied rate.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no internal standing to escalate, or the hourly expression is a regulatory or audit requirement rather than a habit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single case study of one attorney at a large firm.

### [VBF:YEAR-END-PREPAY] Harvest the fiscal-year-end budget flush
**Move:** Near the client's fiscal year end, make clear you can accept advance payment in the current year applied to future work. Establish each client's fiscal calendar early, since it may not be the calendar year.
**APPLY WHEN:** Existing client relationships approaching a use-it-or-lose-it budget deadline.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client's finance rules prohibit prepaying for future-period services.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — described as a pattern of corporate budgeting without cited cases.

### [VBF:PRO-BONO-INVOICE] Do it free rather than cheap, and invoice the free work
**Move:** Rather than accept a low fee, do the work pro bono — never for a profit-making entity — and send an invoice showing the full normal fee with the entire amount waived, so the room learns your real price. Note this is not a tax deduction.
**APPLY WHEN:** A cause or visibility opportunity you want, at a price you refuse.
**AVOID WHEN:** The recipient is commercial, or the invoice would read as a stunt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — positioning logic, no cases.

## Stage I — Delivery, expansion and converting existing clients

### [VBF:SHAREDWIN] Engineer shared credit and shared accountability
**Move:** Use "we" language from first contact; describe partnering in your materials; write joint accountabilities into the proposal; form a mixed client-plus-you working team; give the buyer and their people the visible credit while you take the higher fee and faster payment.
**APPLY WHEN:** Implementation depends on client behaviour change; the buyer's ego is engaged in the outcome; you want the engagement to be fee-pressure resistant.
**AVOID WHEN:** Pure diagnostic or advisory work with no client-side execution; a buyer who genuinely wants an arm's-length independent assessment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as characteristic of the author's most fee-resistant projects.

### [VBF:SCOPE-CREEP-PROPOSAL] Answer scope creep with a new proposal
**Move:** Accept out-of-scope requests warmly, conditional on issuing a fresh proposal with new objectives and new fees. Treat the original objectives as fixed lines.
**APPLY WHEN:** Requests appear that fall outside the agreed objectives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The request is trivially inside scope — invoicing for it damages trust.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard response, echoed in the retainer chapter.

### [VBF:LATERALBUYERS] Reach new economic buyers inside an existing client
**Move:** Seven routes, worked during delivery: (1) ask your current buyer for recommendations and an actual introduction — the most overlooked route; if they are reluctant to admit using a coach, ask them to endorse you as an expert resource. (2) Present results at meetings and suggest adjacent groups attend. (3) Learn the names of power brokers, introduce yourself opportunistically, and after a second or third encounter request time to discuss implications for their area. (4) Get published or interviewed in the company's internal media. (5) Offer briefings to support units, internal customers and suppliers, framed as helping them help your buyer. (6) Volunteer in company charities and events. (7) Track promotions, transfers and press releases for contacts moving into buying positions. Propose next-year objectives that build on current wins, and treat every new or referred buyer as a fresh client to educate correctly — never on the legacy hourly or per-diem basis.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are onsite regularly at a mid or large organization with multiple budget holders, mid-delivery with a satisfied buyer, or receiving a referral from a time-billing colleague.
**AVOID WHEN:** Small closely-held firms (roughly under $50M revenue) with a single owner-buyer; the current buyer would read lateral outreach as poaching; or the current project is troubled, where expansion prospecting reads as opportunism.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — seven tactics with named examples (an in-house magazine feature at Merck; task-force embedding at Calgon); the "over 80% repeat business" share is self-reported.

### [VBF:CONVERTTRIAGE] Score clients before approaching any of them, and plan the abandonments
**Move:** Rate each candidate 3/2/1 on ten questions: significant long-term potential; historically accepted your fees without protest; gave you a senior buyer; has multiple buyers; one you would hate to lose; produced substantial return from your work; paid promptly without debating charges; ever said "just do it and send the bill"; has a buyer you are especially close to; serves as a reference. Work the top tier with individually customized strategies, ignore the bottom, and leave the middle on hourly until you have capacity. Decide in advance which accounts you will abandon — long-held work with no memory of why, insufficient margin, or no remaining interest.
**APPLY WHEN:** A legacy book of hourly or per-diem business is capping the practice and you want to migrate it.
**AVOID WHEN:** The entire base is transactional and single-project, so scoring adds nothing; also never attempt the whole base at once — the source explicitly says do not try to convert everyone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Exhibit 5.1 instrument from the author's practice; scoring bands are arbitrary, not validated.

### [VBF:GROUNDWORK] Seed the conversion in normal visits, don't spring it
**Move:** Lay groundwork inside routine interactions with lines the buyer recognizes as their own problem: their people hesitate to call because the meter is running; neither side can estimate how long a request will take, so stop the meter; you have a new arrangement with new clients and would be remiss not to offer it to your best ones; you want to supply IP updates automatically; you would like to run 90-day progress audits for a year; you could be available around their board schedule; remote advice becomes easier when nobody is counting minutes.
**APPLY WHEN:** High-priority conversion targets, over several visits before any formal proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship is already strained over fees, or the buyer is about to leave the role.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a set of conversational openers from practice.

### [VBF:NEWVALUEMENU] Convert by adding value the buyer would pay more for
**Move:** Pick a mechanism that makes the new basis obviously better for them: unlimited access subject to mutual scheduling, so they stop making a purchase decision every time they need you; a new service folded in with the old under one fee (360-degree feedback added to existing coaching); wider access extending proven results into adjacent functions; combined buyers pooling budgets under one capped fee so nobody argues over chargebacks; new access points (client-only web pages, a client newsletter, a dedicated line, their logo on your books); membership perks such as insider content, best-practice feeds, joint bylines, invitation-only events.
**APPLY WHEN:** Renewal, expansion, or a new project inside a converted-target account.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot service unlimited access without wrecking margin, or the perk costs real delivery time rather than near-zero marginal cost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — multiple mechanisms, with the author's 20-year unrestricted-access experience behind one.

### [VBF:CIRCUMSTANCE] Use an event as the reason to change the fee basis
**Move:** Attach the conversion to a trigger rather than slipping it in: a great year (plus end-of-budget-year funds easier to spend against a defined project than open-ended hourly billing); a bad year (a capped fixed fee cannot become part of an escalating cost problem, nor be cut as one); a new buyer (educate correctly rather than re-educate, and keep tracking the old buyer wherever they went); an acquisition (simplify everything, including billing); a divestiture (the spun-off unit already knows you); intensified competition (ambiguity makes hour estimates impossible, so offer a set fee); a new corporate initiative to attach to; heavy or international travel, where per-diem billing penalizes one side for transit time.
**APPLY WHEN:** Conversion targets of high or moderate priority, at the moment the trigger appears.
**AVOID WHEN:** The trigger is a genuine cash crisis where any new commitment will be frozen.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a catalogue of situational openings from practice.

### [VBF:RESISTLOOP] Handle refusal without burning the account
**Move:** Do not push to the point of ill will; frame the new basis as an option, leaving the current arrangement available so no bread comes off the table. Then: remind quarterly rather than persist; appeal to fair play by showing your total historical fees as a fraction of the improvement you generated; mirror the client's own pricing logic back (a carmaker does not price by build time); offer a halfway step of guaranteed minimum committed time as a bridge; supply third-party articles arguing project fees over time-and-materials; and revisit later, since buyers, conditions and finances change.
**APPLY WHEN:** A high-priority client declines conversion but the relationship is strong.
**AVOID WHEN:** The refusal is structural (procurement policy, regulated billing) rather than preferential.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a six-step sequence from practice; the "minuscule chance of losing them" claim is unsupported.

### [VBF:REFERRAL-EXPECTATION-PRICING] Price to the expectations of the person who referred you
**Move:** In the word-of-mouth phase, set fees at the level the referrer's endorsement implies rather than at your historical level; simultaneously tighten qualification criteria and be willing to walk from poor-margin work rather than accept it for exposure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Prospects arrive pre-sold, citing you without prompting.
**AVOID WHEN:** The referral chain runs through low-level or HR contacts — the expectation being set there is the wrong one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many, with an assertion-grade claim attached: the "over 80% of business comes from word-of-mouth" figure is sourced to trade press and the "~4% web-based" figure to a private remark — treat both as unverified.

## Stage J — Retainers, advisory access and remote offerings

### [VBF:ACCESS-IS-VALUE] Sell availability, not activity, and defend it as insurance
**Move:** Define the retainer as compensation for standing access to your judgment over a fixed period, with no project scope and no promised results, and state early that the value is delivered whether or not you are engaged in any given week. Never accept a retainer that includes unlimited project work. When the client points at low usage as grounds for a discount, use the insurance frame: an unused fire policy is not refunded or repriced. Refuse to manufacture activity to justify the fee, and position it as the client's right to approach you rather than your obligation to approach them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Established clients who have completed successful projects, now want ongoing counsel, and ask for more of your time.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stated need is a discrete deliverable with a deadline — that is project work at a project fee; also when you are not comfortable being paid for availability, since the arrangement then degrades into unpaid project work.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own retainer history including multiple concurrent retainers and one five-year arrangement, plus the "fire that didn't happen" coaching story.

### [VBF:RETAINER-10] Ten-condition setup checklist
**Move:** Before starting, settle in writing: access is the value; access is responsive not instantaneous (the author's own standard is calls returned inside 90 minutes, email and correspondence inside a day); named individuals only; no blackout periods; payment in advance of the period; boundaries (expenses, subcontracting, surveys, expert-witness work all cost extra); the window is calendar-based, not a usage bank; a defined renewal decision point; one or two carved-out priority topics; and framing as collaboration.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any retainer, first-time or renewal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's organization requires a procurement-drafted master agreement that overrides these terms — negotiate the terms with the buyer first, or decline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the author's standing operating conditions across retainer clients.

### [VBF:PREPAY-LADDER] Longer commitment, deeper discount, paid up front
**Move:** Quote the monthly rate, then offer longer periods at a stepped discount paid entirely in advance. The author's illustration: $10,000/month, $27,000/quarter (roughly 10% off), $50,000 for six months.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer is comfortable with the relationship and cash timing is negotiable.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's confidence is untested — a long prepaid period with a bad-fit client is hard to unwind gracefully.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single worked example, not a tested price ladder.

### [VBF:MUTUAL-RENEW] Renewal decided before the period ends
**Move:** Fix a renewal checkpoint inside the current term (mid-third-month of a quarter, week three of a month) at which both parties either agree to continue or either party ends it at term. Framing: mutual continuance, unilateral discontinuance. Never let the decision drift to the final day.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any retainer with renewal potential.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never as such — but if the client declines and later wants back in, treat that as a new arrangement priced upward.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard renewal mechanic.

### [VBF:SILENCE-PING] Intervene in the silences without begging
**Move:** Set a calendar trigger (roughly 10 days of no contact) to send a light "how are things going" note, using the pre-agreed priority topics as the pretext for reopening contact. Do not offer free work and do not ask for assignments.
**APPLY WHEN:** A retainer is running quiet and renewal is the goal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer has explicitly asked for space during a crunch, or the outreach would read as fishing for tasks.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as a repeated renewal-protection habit.

### [VBF:PROJECT-SEPARATION] Route emergent projects into separate proposals
**Move:** State up front that any discrete initiative arising during the retainer needs its own objectives, metrics and fee in its own proposal, and never blend advisory access with project deliverables in one agreement or one payment scheme. When you spot such an initiative, first offer the client ways to do it internally or with a third party; only if they still want you do you propose it. Anchor the distinction on a test: if the work needs more than your advice and counsel, it is a project. Build renewal in by overlapping periods so the relationship is continuous, and confirm the buyer's expectations match yours before starting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Retainer conversations start surfacing concrete initiatives (audits, surveys, redesigns), or the same client wants both project outcomes and ongoing access.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer's real need is a project — an access retainer with no defined outcomes then invites unlimited demands; note also that the author rejects the opposite extreme of refusing all project work during a retainer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — framed as the author's standing solution to a recurring problem, restated in the axioms chapter.

### [VBF:RETAINER-PROJECT-DISCOUNT] Discount in-retainer projects by retainer length
**Move:** Price the separate project at a visible discount that scales with the retainer commitment — roughly 20% during a quarterly retainer, roughly 33% during an annual one — so the buyer experiences the retainer as reducing project cost rather than as an upsell channel.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyer accepts the separation in principle but is price-sensitive about paying twice.
**AVOID WHEN:** The project is the real work and the retainer is a token — you are then discounting your main revenue line.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — percentages given as advice with no outcome data.

### [VBF:WORKING-AGREEMENT] One-page rules of engagement, kept away from legal
**Move:** After the proposal is accepted but before work starts, exchange a short plain-language letter covering term, who has access, prepayment and non-refundability, freeze rules, project separation, the renewal discussion date, expense billing, confidentiality, and a mutual-revision clause. Keep it deliberately simple so it stays a working document rather than a contract.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any retainer, especially longer ones where ambiguity compounds.
**AVOID WHEN:** The client's governance genuinely requires a contract — do not present an informal letter as a substitute for required legal review.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author supplies his own template (Exhibit 6.1) as routine practice.

### [VBF:RETAINER-EXIT] End a failing retainer yourself, with money back
**Move:** If you conclude the arrangement is not working, raise it first and propose ending it with a prorated refund or a credit against future work, explicitly on future-business and referral grounds.
**APPLY WHEN:** Access is unused, advice is ignored, or the fit was misjudged.
**AVOID WHEN:** The quiet period is a normal lull rather than structural failure — use `VBF:SILENCE-PING` first.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as professional ethics, no cases given.

### [VBF:COACHVSADVISOR] Separate coaching from trusted-advisor work and price them differently
**Move:** Treat coaching as a finite, behaviour-objective assignment (public speaking, temper, negotiation, conflict, promotion readiness) priced value-based against the expected performance improvement; treat trusted-advisor work as reactive access to expertise priced as a retainer for the relationship's duration. Hold coaching engagements to roughly 90 days or less, on the argument that longer breeds codependency and that failure to improve within the window predicts no improvement at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** A buyer conflates ongoing counsel with a coaching engagement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The 90-day cap is applied dogmatically — it is an experience claim, not a measured finding.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — explicitly framed as personal experience.

### [VBF:COACHCONTRACT] Fix the reporting contract when the payer is not the subject
**Move:** When a buyer pays you to coach someone else, establish up front whether the buyer wants feedback from you, feedback from the subject, or none — then disclose that arrangement fully to the subject. Never report on progress without the subject's consent. The author's preferred setup is no feedback to the buyer, who judges from observed behaviour.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sponsored coaching, executive development, any three-party engagement.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never — this is the method's one unambiguous ethical constraint, and the one place the source rules against its own financial interest.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a frequently misunderstood standard in the author's practice.

### [VBF:COACH-INTERVAL] Sell coaching as a bounded interval of access
**Move:** Price a fixed window (for example 30 days) during which the individual may call, video or email you, with contact rules stated up front — a return-call standard, an optional cap on calls per week, and different fee levels for different access levels. No in-person meetings. Payment always in advance.
**APPLY WHEN:** An individual manager, professional or entrepreneur has one issue and wants judgment, not a project.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer needs organizational change with measurable business outcomes — that is a project priced by value, not an access window.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=low · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing coaching offer, published on his site to avoid negotiation.

### [VBF:SITUATIONAL-FEE] Put a fixed price on the "quick question"
**Move:** When someone calls with an urgent, well-defined problem, say you cannot do the issue justice as an off-the-cuff answer, then quote a flat fee that buys a bounded package — up to an hour of phone time cumulatively plus unlimited email for a set number of business days.
**APPLY WHEN:** The caller wants fast judgment on a live decision (a proposal, a fee level, a legal exposure) and is not a client.
**AVOID WHEN:** It is a genuine referral-partner relationship where reciprocity is the currency, or a declared pro bono case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "Roy" story, free proposal help repaid with a paperweight, is the stated origin.

### [VBF:CHARGE-THE-FREE] Audit and price what you already give away
**Move:** At least annually, list the ongoing or periodic help you provide gratis and ask, for each, why it is unpriced. Convert whatever survives that question into a named, priced offering; treat "I have no real reason" as the default answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Requests for free advice have grown to consume meaningful time, especially after a book, talk, or visible win.
**AVOID WHEN:** It is a deliberate pro bono commitment you have chosen with open eyes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's mentor program originated this way after roughly four years of free help.

### [VBF:MENTOR-OBJECTIVE-CRITERIA] Publish objective entry criteria and refuse exceptions
**Move:** Define a single published route into your program with objective criteria, and when someone pleads special determination, hardship, or offers labor in exchange, decline in a sentence and redirect them to free public resources. State the reason plainly: the criteria exist so nobody is favored over anyone else.
**APPLY WHEN:** Someone wants your personal attention outside the paid structure, on grounds of passion or effort rather than fit.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no free tier to redirect to — the refusal then reads as pure gatekeeping.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo · offer=info-product/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one recounted two-minute phone refusal.

### [VBF:ALT-REMUNERATION] Take payment in non-cash currency when you waive the fee
**Move:** If you choose not to charge, name an alternative: a donation to a charity, a public review of your book, volunteering for your cause, referral business, help with tedious work, participation in a taping for a product, a promise of future help to someone else, or permission to test new material on the engagement.
**APPLY WHEN:** You want to keep the relationship and the goodwill but refuse to signal that your work is free.
**AVOID WHEN:** The alternative is more burdensome to collect than the fee would have been.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the author's standing menu of alternatives.

### [VBF:RESENTMENT-TRIGGER] Treat your own resentment as a pricing signal
**Move:** When you notice you dislike work you were roped into, start charging for it; if the other party refuses, that is sufficient cause to exit. Same logic that justifies charging more for difficult, fractious clients.
**APPLY WHEN:** Previously voluntary work has become an obligation you resent.
**AVOID WHEN:** The resentment is about your own overcommitment rather than the counterpart's behavior — repricing will not fix a capacity problem.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule of thumb without cases attached.

## Stage K — Speaking, products and non-consulting income

### [VBF:SPEECH-VALUE-FRAME] Position speaking on client outcomes, not on the talk
**Move:** Make all materials and conversations about what the audience's organization will be able to do afterward, treating the speech itself as the transfer point between your relevant past and their future. Name concrete outcomes rather than "a speech" or "motivation".
**APPLY WHEN:** Any speaking inquiry where the buyer frames the ask as a slot on an agenda.
**AVOID WHEN:** Dealing with a meeting planner rather than an economic buyer — the outcome frame needs someone who owns the outcome.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own speaking practice, cross-referenced to his speaking book.

### [VBF:KEYNOTE-OPTION-STACK] Turn one keynote into a five-figure engagement
**Move:** Offer the base keynote plus a menu of priced add-ons the buyer can combine: pre-interviews with selected participants, interviews with executives or the board, a comparative industry-practice survey, follow-on breakout sessions interpreting the keynote operationally, a book or product copy per attendee, a password-protected attendee page, a recording link, and time-boxed email coaching afterward.
**APPLY WHEN:** The speaking buyer has real objectives and you already work as a consultant, so you can actually deliver the add-ons.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer is a pure meeting planner with a fixed speaker line-item, or you cannot deliver the consulting-flavored components.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/info-product · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own option architecture; the specific prices are explicitly illustrative.

### [VBF:BUREAU-TERMS] Accept bureau representation only with negotiation rights intact
**Move:** Treat the bureau as a client-grade relationship and make the right to negotiate scope and options directly with the end client a condition of working together. If the bureau insists your fee is locked to its published schedule minus commission, decline rather than accept the capped economics.
**APPLY WHEN:** A third party is bringing you engagements you would not have sourced yourself and your own marketing cost for that stream is effectively zero.
**AVOID WHEN:** The bureau's restriction on client contact is non-negotiable, or you have no independent demand to walk back to.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=few · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated bureau dealings across the author's speaking career; commission percentages stated as market norms.

### [VBF:PRODUCT-SCREEN] Run every product idea through the affirmative-answers screen
**Move:** Before building a product, require a yes on each: sufficient margin per sale; sellable to both clients and non-clients; reinforces the marketing plan; cheap to create and cheap to kill if it flops; fulfillable quickly; stays relevant for at least a few years. Then set up the plumbing — card processing, an ISBN for distribution reach, a separate secure product section on the site, lost shipments budgeted as a cost of doing business.
**APPLY WHEN:** An established practice wants income that arrives without your presence, or branding lift alongside revenue.
**AVOID WHEN:** The item is low-margin merchandise (mugs, shirts, calendars, planners) — classed as clutter that dilutes positioning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=info-product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own product line plus observed consultant and speaker product failures.

### [VBF:PRODUCT-PRICE-HIGH] Price and source products for margin, not volume
**Move:** Self-publish at a premium rather than a commodity price, and buy your commercially published titles from a wholesaler rather than from the publisher, so you clear a bigger discount, an early-payment discount, and royalties on your own purchase — then always sell at full retail yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** You sell books or materials at the back of the room or into client organizations in bulk.
**AVOID WHEN:** The material is genuinely commodity content, or you are actually trying to run a fulfillment or volume business.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=info-product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own print runs and margin arithmetic.

### [VBF:SUBSCRIPTION-FLIP] Convert per-unit downloads into an annual subscription
**Move:** When a client sells discrete digital items for small amounts, restructure to an annual all-you-can-download subscription with a commitment to keep adding items (by jurisdiction, by new regulation), and price the year at a level that reflects the avoided cost of the alternative, typically an in-house hire.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers have a recurring, generic need and the alternative to your product is an expensive hire.
**AVOID WHEN:** The catalogue is static and cannot credibly be refreshed — renewal then collapses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=low · stakeholders=solo · offer=info-product/saas · market=transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single named case, a Florida attorney selling small-business legal forms.

## Stage L — Fee progression over the life of the practice

### [VBF:PHASE-LOCATE] Locate yourself on the progression table and plan the leap
**Move:** Score your practice on the six columns of Table 10.1 (business sources, qualification criteria, fee integrity, fee levels, nonconsulting fees, margins), name the phase — Entry Level, Going Concern, Word of Mouth, Brand, Ultimate Consultant — and ask three questions: what phase am I actually in, what am I doing to prepare for the next, and how would I know if I have been here too long. Then attack the specific column that lags.
**APPLY WHEN:** Annual planning, or when income has been flat while workload has not.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as self-flattery — the diagnostic only works if fee integrity and margins are scored honestly.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the phase model is the author's own taxonomy, offered without external validation.

### [VBF:FEE-PHILOSOPHY] Write a fee philosophy at the going-concern stage
**Move:** Once bills are reliably paid, answer four questions in writing: which work you will encourage, accept and phase out; how you will raise your own comfort with value pricing and with educating buyers on the single fee; where you can offer options (target: any project, any setting); and your absolute minimum fee level.
**APPLY WHEN:** Roughly 70% repeat business and referrals flowing, but fees still flex under any resistance.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no pipeline choice yet — the minimum-fee line only bites if you can afford to enforce it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — phase hallmarks presented as observed patterns across the profession.

### [VBF:PORTFOLIO-HYGIENE] Raise fees on a clock and prune the bottom
**Move:** Raise fees roughly every two years, base fees such as keynotes or retreats by at least 10%, applying increases to new clients and only to existing clients where demonstrably more value is delivered. Every two years also consider shedding the bottom 15% of accounts and referring them out. Track margin, not just revenue, and gather market fee ranges through casual client and peer conversation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Annual or biennial practice review.
**AVOID WHEN:** Raising on existing clients without added value — the author explicitly restricts that, and veteran clients who discover newer clients get better terms leave fast.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — cadence numbers given as rules of thumb without outcome data.

### [VBF:ABANDON9Q] The nine-question abandonment audit
**Move:** Every 18 months or so, between fiscal years, ask of each client: am I learning; am I still adding value others cannot; is the pay and margin as good as with new clients; am I being stretched; am I introducing new products or services; am I using this client as a referral or springboard; can I experiment and gain marketing leverage here; am I enjoying it; am I steadily reducing labor intensity. Two or more "no" answers means plan the exit. Exit constructively: give the client your honest reasoning, arrange a handover to another consultant, stay through a transition or until renewal, then leave. Do not subcontract it or take a referral fee. If they counter with more money, decline — the increase will still miss your margin goal and your time stays consumed. Keep the relationship as a reference and referral source.
**APPLY WHEN:** Periodic portfolio review, or when you cannot take on higher-value work for lack of capacity.
**AVOID WHEN:** Revenue concentration means the exit endangers solvency — the source does not price this risk; also where contractual obligations remain open.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=any · offer=consulting/service · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's long-standing and self-described controversial practice.

### [VBF:TWO-TACTICS-MONTH] Install two fee tactics per month into your standard language
**Move:** Treat fee improvement as a practice: pick two techniques a month and build them into your conversational patterns and promotional material. Named examples: deflecting early fee questions with turnaround questions rather than being pinned to a number, and introducing options very early so the buyer is prepared for a choice of yeses.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ongoing — the claim is that daily buyer conversations each contain unexploited margin.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as scripting to be recited; the source's own framing requires the language to be native to you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the "double your profits in a year, 4–5× if you also grow" projection has no supporting data and should be discounted heavily.

### [VBF:EPONYMOUS-BRIDGE] Migrate methodology brands toward your own name
**Move:** Take an existing approach or product brand and attach your surname to it as an intermediate step, then work toward the name itself being the brand. Simultaneously stretch each brand across a wider range of offerings rather than letting it fence you into a niche.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already own recognized methodology brands but buyers ask for the method, not for you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The methodology brand's value is institutional and transferable by design — the counter-example given is Accenture succeeding precisely by dropping the original name.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=consulting/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's roughly two dozen registered trademarks plus named third-party examples (Parkinson's Laws, Augustine's Laws, The One Minute Manager).

### [VBF:ULTIMATE-CONDUCT] Behave as the consultant whose fee is never discussed
**Move:** Adopt the conduct list: do not discuss fees; dress and act as a peer of senior buyers; be engaged in collaboration, never hired; be known by name; build products that trade on that name; chase margin rather than volume; cultivate the "can we even get this person?" perception; decide jointly with the buyer whether the project is worth accepting; and refuse to negotiate or build the relationship through HR or low-level staff.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers already seek you out directly, with no feasibility gatekeeper in the path.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are not there yet — performing scarcity you cannot back is the obvious misuse mode, and the source quietly admits few practitioners reach this phase.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Federal Reserve Bank case where the buyer pursued him on a single peer recommendation and fees never came up in the preliminaries.

## Stage M — Crisis and volatility

### [VBF:HOW-CAN-I-HELP] Open crisis outreach with a pure offer of help
**Move:** In a crisis, call everyone — clients, non-clients, referrers, prospects — and ask how you can help, with the likely answers pre-scripted. If things are too chaotic for them to know what to ask for, offer to share what comparable clients are doing successfully. If they ask what you are offering, ask for their greatest need and commit only if it is within your competence. If they name a need, focus immediately on your strongest suit within it. If money comes up, state plainly that you are not asking for money, you want to help.
**APPLY WHEN:** A shared shock (disaster, pandemic, recession, unrest) has hit your market and buyers are frozen.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stated need is outside your competence — the source's own rule is to end the call rather than stretch. The offer must also be genuinely unconditioned, or it reads as a discount close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 9/11 case, where three lower-Manhattan clients were offered refunds on fully prepaid, unstarted work and all three chose to proceed roughly 60 days later.

### [VBF:BRIEF-ADVISORY] If they truly cannot pay, go short and advisory
**Move:** When the client genuinely has no money, cap the engagement tight (roughly 30 days) and structure it as an advisory role rather than delivery work, so labor intensity stays low while value stays high. Accept the deferred payoff explicitly: subordinate short-term fees to be first in line when investment resumes.
**APPLY WHEN:** A relationship worth keeping is temporarily insolvent.
**AVOID WHEN:** The "cannot pay" is a negotiating posture from a buyer who has reallocated money elsewhere.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as standing crisis practice; the "you will be remembered and move to the top of the list" payoff is assertion.

### [VBF:ASK-GUIDANCE] Invite people to shape something unfinished
**Move:** Invert the help offer and ask for guidance, advice and direction on work still in draft. People respond to influence over an outcome and ignore a finished product; asking for direction frequently produces a meeting, and sometimes a project on the topic itself.
**APPLY WHEN:** You want re-entry to dormant relationships without a pitch, especially in a crisis period.
**AVOID WHEN:** The artifact is already final — the same audience that engaged with a draft produced zero response to the finished version.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=consulting/info-product · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client's two self-published books, draft circulated (feedback plus two projects) versus finished (no feedback).

### [VBF:BEST-PRACTICE-BROKER] Position yourself as the objective source of what is actually working
**Move:** Collect empirical evidence of what is demonstrably working for other organizations, convert it into transferable processes, and distribute it. Because it involves process rather than trade secrets, sharing is ethical and easy — and it makes you the reliable news source people prioritize. Use cross-industry transfer explicitly (restaurant curbside handoff → retail parking-lot fulfillment with mobile payment; clinic screening protocol → salon screening).
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers in a crisis want evidence rather than forecasts or opinions.
**AVOID WHEN:** You would be passing on client-confidential specifics rather than generalized process.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — named public examples (parking-lot fulfillment, salon and clinic screening) observed, not measured.
