# advisor-jolt — myths, demotions and failure modes

What this tradition debunks, what it demotes inside itself, and which of its own numbers must
never be quoted as established fact. Source: The JOLT Effect (Dixon & McKenna, 2022).

---

## Myths this lens refutes

**"Hesitation means the value case failed."** A buyer who already conceded the vision and then
stalls is afraid of buying wrong, not unconvinced of the need. Re-proving ROI to them adds work,
not conviction. → [JOLT:PHASE-SWITCH]

**"More fear moves buyers."** FUD aimed at an already-frightened buyer supplies fresh reasons to
postpone. Four named flavors, all failure modes: manufactured urgency, artificial scarcity,
wallowing in status-quo pain, and isolation ("your peers are moving without you") — isolation is
called the harshest, producing resentment and broken trust. → [JOLT:FEARCHOICE], [JOLT:EXACERBATOR-CHECK]

**"Status-quo bias and indecision are the same thing."** Separated in the underlying literature
(Ritov & Baron); late in a deal, fear of making a mistake is the stronger grip and takes the
opposite treatment. → [JOLT:TWOFOE]

**"Stated intent means effectively closed."** Buyers who behaved as satisficers while expressing
intent flip to maximizer behavior at signature. Verbalized intent converted at roughly 26% — the
study average, not a premium. → [JOLT:GOOD-ENOUGH-DEBRIEF]

**"Always ask, never tell."** Reactive needs-diagnosis in the face of indecision is what produces
the ask-for-more-time ending; more discovery questions add choice where the buyer wanted guidance.
Bounded: this is a late-stage claim only. → [JOLT:DIAGNOSE-THEN-REC], [JOLT:PROBE-CAP]

**"More choice is better."** Participation and conversion fall with option count. Counter-bounded
in the same breath: stripping choice *early* is also wrong, since option abundance is what draws
attention in the first place. → [JOLT:CHALK-FIELD]

**"Listen more than you talk" and "never interrupt."** Contradicted by the talk-time split (58%
in wins vs 52% in losses) and by overlap frequency (~2× in wins) — with the caveat the authors
themselves attach: the mechanism is engagement, not dominance, and interrupting does not cause
buying. → [JOLT:TALK-SHARE], [JOLT:COOP-OVERLAP]

**"Bigger deals are higher-quality deals."** Contradicted: the strongest reps sold more in total by
starting smaller. → [JOLT:START-SMALL], [JOLT:SCOPE-DOWN-PREQUOTE]

**"An opt-out signals weak confidence in the product."** Named as the belief that keeps the
highest-delta de-risking move unused. The complex-sale variant — "legal and finance would never
allow downside protection" — collapsed when experienced sellers were pushed and recalled terms
they had already granted. → [JOLT:SAFETY-NET-SURFACE], [JOLT:SAFETY-NET-ENGINEER]

**"Never concede a competitor advantage, never admit a gap, never say I don't know."** Framed as
average-performer training that feeds the principal–agent problem; claiming the product does
everything produces immediate skepticism and post-sale churn. → [JOLT:COMP-CREDIT], [JOLT:GAP-ADMIT],
[JOLT:DONT-KNOW]

**"Indecision is a complex-enterprise problem."** Behavior *frequency* varies by channel; impact
per behavior does not. → [JOLT:CHANNELPARITY]

**"Great sellers are born."** The behaviors were extracted from observed high performers, so they
already exist on most teams — and hiring is far too slow to fix a pipeline stalling this quarter.
→ [JOLT:PERSKILLDIAG]

**"Org design can route around indecision."** No territory or reporting structure avoids something
present in 87% of opportunities. → [JOLT:HUDDLE]

## Documented failure modes of these techniques

- Treating research requests and pilot demands as buying signals and feeding them — the single
  most common way information-type indecision is amplified rather than resolved.
- Misreading decision avoidance as procrastination: optimistic forecasts and months of wasted
  coverage on a deal that will never be decided. → [JOLT:DELAY-TYPING]
- Sending the specialist in early and being delegated down to the level you sound like.
  → [JOLT:OWN-INFO-FLOW], [JOLT:SME-PREPCALL]
- Demo mode suppressing objections, so silence gets read as acceptance. → [JOLT:DEMO-PAUSE]
- The candor quadrants: manipulative insincerity (agree to everything, complain later), obnoxious
  aggression (challenging from self-interest), ruinous empathy (care, say nothing, let the buyer
  spin). [JOLT:CANDOR-DECLINE] degrades into the second whenever it serves the seller, not the buyer.
- Asking for the business on a cold deal. Low ask rates (46% of calls) are a *symptom*; the authors
  explicitly refuse the "always ask" reading, since about half the conversations had not earned it.
  → [JOLT:ASK-BIZ]
- Reps solving inbound service issues out of goodwill and converting at the bottom of the range
  (16%). → [JOLT:INBOUND-TRIAGE]
- Manual call audits sampling ~1% of volume against binary criteria; reps then read the process as
  arbitrary and punitive. → [JOLT:COMPETENCY-AUDIT]
- Tonal sentiment analysis producing high false-positive rates; off-the-shelf conversation-
  intelligence categories that do not generalize across companies. → [JOLT:CI-DILIGENCE],
  [JOLT:PERSKILLDIAG]
- De-risking moves quietly skipped because comp claws back on cancellation or the quota is
  land-only — the book acknowledges this and offers nothing beyond escalating to a manager.

## Demotions inside the tradition — do not quote as fact

**The famous headline number is two numbers.** 40–60% of deals ending in no decision is an author
estimate/range that varies by organization, not a computed corpus figure. Within that stalled
bucket, 44% is status-quo preference and 56% is indecision. "56% of deals are lost to indecision"
is a false statement produced by collapsing them. → [JOLT:INACTION-BASELINE]

**Every win-rate delta is vendor-run and machine-scored.** Correlations from one vendor-assembled
corpus, classifiers hand-built by the vendor's own team, data contributed by client companies,
self-reported, not peer-reviewed, several values read off bar charts. The authors' own modelling
note concedes effects are combined and sometimes competing — so no single behavior's number is a
standalone coefficient, even though the presentation invites exactly that reading.

**The 22-point indecision scorecard threshold** has no reported validation. Heuristic only.
→ [JOLT:INDECISION-SCORECARD]

**The 40–70 information rule** is a quoted leadership maxim with no measurable referent, not a
finding from the data. → [JOLT:P4070]

**"Predicts outcomes over 85% of the time"** is a self-reported model claim about the vendor's own
classifier, not an out-of-sample validation anyone can inspect.

**The customer-effort → win-rate bridge** runs through the vendor's proprietary index (280+
variables, deep-learning proxy), not an independently validated measure.

**Cooperative overlapping** is footnoted to popular-press linguistics; the win-rate correlation is
the study's, but the concept and its cultural claims are not. → [JOLT:COOP-OVERLAP]

**Coaching the middle 60% "lifts performance up to 19%"** is a CEB/Gartner vendor claim tied to the
authors' earlier work, not to this study's data. Same for the interaction-velocity signal, sourced
to Challenger with no citation. → [JOLT:COACH-MIDDLE], [JOLT:VELOCITY-SIGNAL]

**Conversation-intelligence adoption at 26%** is secondhand from Aberdeen; the ~80%-of-deployments-
miss-ROI figure is vendor-reported. → [JOLT:CI-DILIGENCE]

## Anti-recommendations, with the reason

- **Do not run both playbooks at once.** Pain-of-inaction and fear-reduction cancel each other; the
  73%/84% relitigation finding is the closest thing this lens has to a hard prohibition.
- **Do not use this lens before a vision is agreed.** Its own advice — cap the diagnosis, recommend
  instead of asking — is actively wrong in discovery. It has nothing on prospecting, needs
  development, qualification of fit, packaging or pricing design.
- **Do not disqualify broadly on indecision.** The authors cap it at ~10–15% of potential buyers;
  wider use turns the framework into a licence to drop hard but winnable deals.
- **Do not fire the safety net reflexively.** Surfacing an escape hatch after running FUD in the
  same conversation reads as insincere and burns the trust the move depends on.
- **Do not define "must-haves" as your own differentiators, or the recommended default as your
  highest-margin option.** The lens names both misuses — and supplies no test the seller can fail.
  The guardrail is seller honesty alone, which is a genuine structural gap, not a nuance.
- **Do not treat any move here as ethically self-validating.** Advocacy, defaults and blame
  absorption work *because* they transfer decision responsibility away from the buyer. If you would
  not be willing to show the buyer exactly what you are doing and why, the move is out.
- **Do not import the measurement chapters into a team without call recording, a QA function and a
  manager layer.** They presume all three, and they converge on tooling the authors sell.
