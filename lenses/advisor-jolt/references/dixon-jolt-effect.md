# The JOLT Effect — digest

## What this book is for the lens

This is the lens for deals that die *after* the buyer agrees with you. It separates two failure causes that every other source blends together: preference for the status quo (buyer does not want to change) and indecision (buyer wants to change and cannot commit), and shows that the standard status-quo playbook actively worsens the second. Its distinctive asset is a machine-scored corpus of recorded calls, which lets it attach win-rate deltas to specific late-stage seller behaviors — proactive recommendation, information rationing, expectation setting, downside protection — where competing sources offer only conviction. Use it when intent exists and the deal will not close; do not use it to design discovery, qualification, prospecting or pricing, which it does not address.

## Core model

Two phases with opposite logics. Phase one (status quo → agreement on a vision): raise the fear of *not* buying. Phase two (vision → signature): lower the fear of *buying*. The handoff is the moment the buyer concedes the vision; carrying phase-one habits across the line is the book's central documented error.

Indecision is not status-quo bias. It is omission bias plus loss aversion: an error of commission is concrete, fast, and personally attributable, an error of omission is abstract and diffusely blamed, so buyers prefer missing out to messing up (Samuelson & Zeckhauser; Kahneman & Tversky; Ritov & Baron; Leach & Plaks).

Indecision has three sources (structure borrowed from Germeijs & de Boeck's course-choice study and reproduced in the call data): **valuation problem** (cannot rank options), **lack of information** (fear of insufficient homework), **outcome uncertainty** (belief gap on delivered results). The source selects the play.

The four behaviors — chapter names double as navigation anchors: **J**udge the indecision (ch. 3), **O**ffer your recommendation (ch. 4), **L**imit the exploration (ch. 5), **T**ake risk off the table (ch. 6). Supporting chapters: Becoming a Buyer's Agent (ch. 7, the principal–agent problem and trust bought with visible acts against self-interest), JOLT-ing Customer Loyalty (ch. 8, unresolved indecision survives signature as worrying, re-checking and cancellation), How Much Is Indecision Costing You? (ch. 9, measurement instruments), Applying JOLT in Different Sales Environments (ch. 10, inbound vs outbound), Building the JOLT-capable sales force (ch. 11, hiring and development). Judging maps the source and the depth; the other three are the treatments.

## Moves

### Stage: diagnosis, judging and pipeline triage

### [JOLT:TWOFOE] Diagnose the loss cause before choosing a playbook
**Move:** When a deal stalls, force one explicit answer before spending more effort: is this buyer committed to keeping things as they are, or sold on change and unable to commit to it? Route to status-quo work only in the first case, to fear-reduction work in the second. Treat "stalled" as two diseases with opposite treatments.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal has slipped one or more forecast periods; engagement has gone from rich to curt; the pipeline review is arguing kill-for-cause versus keep pursuing.
**AVOID WHEN:** early discovery, before any vision is agreed — no evidence yet separates the two causes; also as a substitute for basic fit/budget qualification, which the book does not cover.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the 44/56 split of inaction losses in the recorded-call corpus; the framing question itself comes from one sales leader's pipeline review.

### [JOLT:PHASE-SWITCH] Switch playbooks at agreement on a vision and retire the cost-of-inaction arsenal
**Move:** Treat the buyer's concession of the vision as a hard handoff. Before it, prove they will succeed by buying. After it, subordinate that entire toolkit and prove only that they will not fail: no more re-proving ROI, no differentiation replays, no discount/expiry/competitor-pressure framing, no darkening the picture of their current state. Silence beats pressure here. Do not run both playbooks at once.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer has agreed the current state is unacceptable and yours is the answer, yet keeps deferring, goes quiet, or asks for more time to think.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has not conceded the status quo — pain-of-same work is still the job there; when the concession is soft or came from one champion while the committee still favours the status quo; when a firm no is being misread as indecision.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — relitigating the status quo appeared on 73% of calls and correlated with a worse outcome 84% of the time; the two-playbook model is the authors' synthesis of the corpus plus high-performer interviews.

### [JOLT:SQTRIAGE] Sort status-quo objections into three flavors
**Move:** While the buyer still resists change, place the resistance from what they actually say: (a) genuine satisfaction with or sunk investment in the current way; (b) not enough daylight between you and the incumbent; (c) they accept B beats A but fear the transition — staffing, timeline, scars from a past implementation. Only (c) borders on the indecision playbook.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer has not yet stated intent to change; you are competing against do-nothing or an incumbent.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has already agreed to move — reopening this is exactly the relitigation the book warns against.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — recurring patterns in the studied call corpus, illustrated by buyer utterances.

### [JOLT:CODETECT] Read emotional register, and treat one phrase as the alarm
**Move:** Use the buyer's emotional register rather than their stated objections as the early-warning system: uncertainty, confusion, anxiety, skepticism, concern. Treat a request for more time to think as the strongest negative signal in the dataset — worse than an outright no — and answer it by opening the fear conversation, not by re-selling. Watch hardest where the buyer has already said yes in principle.
**APPLY WHEN:** any stage, decisive after vision agreement; a deal that felt won starts producing vague, delayed replies.
**AVOID WHEN:** treating one hedge word as a verdict — the detection was statistical across many utterances, and a human listener will over- and under-read individual cues.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the phrase is reported as the utterance most correlated with lost deals among tens of thousands isolated; emotion markers came from NLP scoring, not human listening.

### [JOLT:SOURCE-TRIAGE] Classify the source of indecision from buyer cues
**Move:** Score the buyer against three cue sets and let the winner select the play. Valuation problem: everything looks appealing, repeated requests to re-explain package differences, open statements of being stuck, distraction by newly found features, dithering over scope and contract length. Lack of information: above-normal appetite for demos, references and SME time, delays stated as gathering more, feeling overwhelmed or in the dark. Outcome uncertainty: pressure for ROI projections and re-projections, references to past investments that went wrong, framing your solution as unusually risky, demands for guarantees, free pilots or proofs of concept, open skepticism that results are achievable. Name the bucket before responding — recommend, limit, or de-risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** intent has been expressed but the deal keeps generating new work; late-stage churn with no new objection to the value case.
**AVOID WHEN:** the same behaviors come from legitimate procurement or compliance process rather than fear — the book gives no test to tell them apart; and as a substitute for fit/budget/authority qualification.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — cue phrases derived from co-presence patterns in the corpus; the three-bucket structure replicates Germeijs & de Boeck (2003, 174 graduating seniors).

### [JOLT:ABILITY-TO-DECIDE] Qualify on ability to decide, not only ability to buy
**Move:** Add a second qualification axis alongside use-case fit, industry and company dynamics: can this buyer actually decide? Then split the pipeline three ways — disqualify the intractably stuck, run the indecision playbook where the buyer is movable, and adjust the forecast to the level of indecision found. Deprioritize a large, perfect-on-paper account that demands endless references, unpaid long pilots and impossible guarantees; upgrade a small account whose decision-maker moves fast and declines optional validation.
**APPLY WHEN:** territory planning, pipeline and forecast reviews; any opportunity where enthusiasm is high and commitment keeps receding.
**AVOID WHEN:** too few interactions exist to read decisiveness (early strategic accounts); as licence to drop hard but winnable deals — the book frames disqualification as a post-diagnosis judgment, not a default.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — high performers' pipelines held fewer highly indecisive buyers and they disqualified them disproportionately often; the paired illustrations come from one medical-device high performer.

### [JOLT:BACKTRACK-FLAG] Treat late-stage backtracking as a kill signal
**Move:** Watch for a buyer already in pilot, proposal or contracting who suddenly reverses on newly surfaced information — a fresh analyst report, an unknown vendor they now must check off. Read it as low ambiguity tolerance, not diligence: respect their process, formally pause your side (hold contracting) and reforecast the deal out.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage reversal into earlier evaluation activity without a substantive new requirement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the new information is a genuine requirement change — regulatory, budget, reorg — rather than reassurance-seeking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep's story; the ambiguity-intolerance link is attributed to unnamed research, so treat that layer as assertion.

### [JOLT:SHORTLIST-CRITERIA-TEST] Make the buyer explain how they built the short list
**Move:** Ask which must-have criteria filtered vendors in and which they judged less important. You are not asking who else is in the deal — you are testing whether a structured, non-compensatory filter exists at all. Separately, check whether the competitors listed are actually comparable: a set spanning entirely different categories means the buyer does not yet know what they want.
**APPLY WHEN:** evaluation stage, especially with no formal RFP to impose structure.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is contractually barred from discussing process; or the questioning turns into interrogation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — high-performer interview tell, backed by the book's account of compensatory versus non-compensatory selection research (Christopher Anderson).

### [JOLT:GOOD-ENOUGH-DEBRIEF] Debrief every interaction and read the ordering, not the content
**Move:** After each call, demo, pilot or proof-of-concept, ask your main contact how it went — then analyse the shape of the answer: did they open with what went wrong or what went right, is the negative list longer, are the negatives framed as seeds of failure or as improvement items. Two identical pilots debriefed differently forecast very differently (satisficer versus maximizer).
**APPLY WHEN:** any multi-touch evaluation with a consistent point of contact.
**AVOID WHEN:** single-call transactional sales; also unreliable if you never forewarned the buyer about known rough edges, since then the complaint is simply legitimate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one interviewed rep's routine, framed against the satisficer/maximizer literature (Simon 1956; Schwartz et al.; Roets et al.).

### [JOLT:DELAY-TYPING] Separate procrastination from decision avoidance
**Move:** Type every delay by its language. Moving this week's call to next week = procrastination, intent to act survives. Now is not the right time / priorities are shifting / let us pick this up next quarter = decision avoidance, no intent to decide. Treat the second as a forecast change, not a nurture task.
**APPLY WHEN:** any slipped close date.
**AVOID WHEN:** a verifiable external event explains the pause — budget freeze, acquisition, leadership change — which is neither type.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — call-data tells plus high-performer interviews; the two-type distinction is sourced to Ferrari, Rassin and Anderson.

### [JOLT:POWERFUL-REQUEST] Test intent with a costly ask
**Move:** When the delay type is ambiguous, request something that consumes other people's time: a working session with the wider buying group, or a meeting with IT, legal, HR, finance or procurement. Agreement verifies intent and builds momentum; refusal or vagueness exposes deeper reluctance.
**APPLY WHEN:** heel-dragging without a stated cause, mid-to-late stage.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no substantive agenda for those stakeholders — a make-work meeting burns credibility; and in solo-buyer transactional deals there is nobody to convene.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — identified as a high-performer pattern in the call corpus.

### [JOLT:ASK-STRAIGHT] Probe past the stated reason for delay
**Move:** Ask directly whether something is going on that you should know about. When the answer is generic — shifting priorities — ask what the new priorities are, map your solution onto them out loud, then ask what else is causing concern. Keep going until a personal reason surfaces, often the contact's own exposure after previously championing a purchase that underdelivered.
**APPLY WHEN:** a deal repeatedly forecast to close keeps slipping with no explanation.
**AVOID WHEN:** trust is not established — the same question from a stranger reads as pressure; and the senior-to-senior version can route around your champion, so use it carefully.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a tech CEO's account of a conference coffee that unstuck a six-month forecast lie.

### [JOLT:INDECISION-SCORECARD] Score the opportunity on the indecision dimensions
**Move:** Rate the buyer 1–7 on each screen — information consumption (comfortable with ambiguity versus needing certainty), alternative comparison (structured versus unstructured), tolerance for good enough (satisficer versus maximizer), delay type (procrastination versus decision avoidance), plus the remaining screens in the book's scorecard. Higher total means more indecisive; above 22 points the opportunity becomes a candidate for disqualification or at least deprioritization and manager scrutiny.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline review, forecast calls, territory triage.
**AVOID WHEN:** early-stage deals with too little interaction data; and treat 22 as a heuristic — the book reports no validation study for the cutoff.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — scorecard and threshold presented without reported validation, though the underlying dimensions come from the call study.

### [JOLT:EXACERBATOR-CHECK] Adjust the score for consequence and time pressure
**Move:** After scoring, check two amplifiers: how consequential the decision is for the buyer personally and organizationally (price tag, blast radius of a mistake), and whether time pressure is present. Both convert an apparently decisive buyer into a stuck one, so a mid-range score under heavy amplifiers should be handled as high.
**APPLY WHEN:** large or career-visible purchases; deals where your own side is applying deadlines.
**AVOID WHEN:** not applicable as a restriction — but note it argues directly against your own discounting deadlines and exploding offers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research — Ladouceur's pill-sorting experiment (added consequence produced more hesitation and rechecking), plus the study's own data on pressure tactics.

### [JOLT:CUT-FOR-INDECISION] Disqualify on indecision alone
**Move:** Where indecision reads as extreme, deprioritize or kill the opportunity even though the buyer says they want to buy, and move the time to deals that can actually convert.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-cycle complex sale where years of pursuit are possible; standard BANT-style criteria all pass yet cold feet keep recurring.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied broadly — the authors bound it at roughly 10–15% of potential buyers at most and state indecision cannot be disqualified away wholesale.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — high-performer behavior in the study; the 10–15% ceiling is the authors' own bound, supported by a heavy-construction sales leader's account.

### [JOLT:OPP-RETRO] Run a decision post-mortem on every closed opportunity
**Move:** Won or lost, ask what this opportunity teaches you about reading decisiveness next time, and feed it back into how you score the next buyer.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal closure; weekly review.
**AVOID WHEN:** no stated restriction — the practice is low-cost, but it carries no measured effect.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a high-performer habit without supporting data.

### Stage: valuation problem — offer your recommendation

### [JOLT:CHALK-FIELD] Impose a binary must-have filter for the buyer
**Move:** When the buyer is drowning in options, convert fuzzy weighted comparison into binary elimination: name the two or three capabilities they cannot live without, cut everything lacking them, and only then compare survivors on value. This is RFP logic and e-commerce filter logic applied by hand.
**APPLY WHEN:** valuation problem present; large consideration set; no formal procurement structure to impose order.
**AVOID WHEN:** very early exploration, where a wide option set is what attracts buyers; and where "must-have" would be defined purely as your own differentiators — that is manipulation, not structure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research — Iyengar & Lepper's jam study and the Vanguard 401(k) participation data as reported; the sales application is the authors' extrapolation.

### [JOLT:PROACTIVE-GUIDE] Nudge with a default
**Move:** Volunteer directional guidance before being asked — name the most popular configuration, or say most new customers start on a given plan and expand as needs evolve.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer faces multiple packages or configurations, ideally before they voice confusion.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no honest basis for the default, or the "popular" option is simply your highest-margin one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product/info-product · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 18% win rate without the skill versus 44% with it (+144%) in the studied calls.

### [JOLT:ADVOCACY] Put your name on the recommendation
**Move:** Escalate from neutral nudge to personal position: say what you would do with your own money and your own accountability, acknowledge the other options are reasonable, and explicitly offer to absorb the blame if it disappoints. The mechanism is blame transfer — your endorsement absorbs the buyer's fear of committing an error of commission and turns an overwhelming comparison into one achievable next step.
**APPLY WHEN:** valuation-type indecision — feature comparisons, package scope, contract-length dithering, everything-looks-fine — where the buyer fears personal accountability for the choice.
**AVOID WHEN:** the stall is information fear or outcome fear; you have not yet earned credibility as a trustworthy expert (advocacy from an untrusted rep reads as a push toward a bigger deal); or the recommendation is not genuinely the buyer's best option.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — advocacy alone associated with a 74% lift and a 33% win rate when used, against the 26% study average.

### [JOLT:PREEMPT-REC] Recommend before the buyer forms a preference
**Move:** Deliver the recommendation early, ahead of expressed confusion, rather than after the buyer has already leaned toward an option.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-option deals where you can predict the likely hang-up.
**AVOID WHEN:** you genuinely lack the discovery to recommend well — a wrong early recommendation contradicts a preference the buyer already holds and makes them feel foolish.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — high performers were observed recommending before confusion surfaced more often than after.

### [JOLT:DIAGNOSE-THEN-REC] Never end a diagnosis without landing a recommendation
**Move:** Keep probing questions at the front of the conversation and close the loop with an explicit recommendation. When the buyer signals they are stumped, do not answer with more questions about what matters to them or what else you can clarify.
**APPLY WHEN:** any call where the buyer has expressed confusion about which option to take.
**AVOID WHEN:** true early discovery — there, open questions still calibrate you to what the buyer learned before contacting you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — diagnosis plus recommendation converted at 36%, open-ended diagnosis with no recommendation at 14%, against a 26% average.

### [JOLT:PROBE-CAP] Cap diagnosis once intent exists
**Move:** Stop adding discovery and diagnosis after purchase intent is established; commit to a recommendation instead of deferring to whatever the buyer says they want.
**APPLY WHEN:** highly indecisive buyer in a complex sale; you catch yourself queueing another round of questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** intent has not actually been established, or the requirement is genuinely unknown — the book is silent on early-stage discovery and is not an argument against it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — average performers probed more frequently than high performers in the outbound call set.

### [JOLT:THREE-TIER-STEER] Offer all-in / moderate / toe-dipping, then steer to the middle
**Move:** Present three sizes of commitment and actively recommend the middle one as the better way to start, rather than anchoring only on the largest proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** high-consequence decisions of the buyer's own money, where commitment size itself drives the fear.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has already settled scope and pace — reopening size reintroduces choice overload.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a wealth-management leader's account of his early career.

### Stage: information problem — limit the exploration

### [JOLT:P4070] Apply the 40–70 information rule
**Move:** Use Colin Powell's P = 40 to 70 heuristic as the yardstick for "enough": below roughly 40% of the needed information a decision is guessing, above roughly 70% you are only delaying. Say out loud when the buyer has crossed into research for research's sake.
**APPLY WHEN:** repeat demos to the same stakeholder group, serial reference calls, endless proposal iterations.
**AVOID WHEN:** genuinely novel or high-consequence technology where the buyer's diligence bar is legitimately higher; the percentages are rhetorical, not measurable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a leadership maxim quoted by the authors, not a finding from their data.

### [JOLT:OWN-INFO-FLOW] Stay the locus of expertise
**Move:** Resist reflexively handing calls to solutions engineers, product leaders or CSMs. Invest the time to run your own demos and to know the competitors and the category well enough to carry the conversation. For juniors: authority comes from knowing this product and market better than any prospect does, not from knowing the buyer's job better than they do.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout the cycle, especially the first technical conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** you genuinely do not know the answer — faking it is explicitly rejected; and where regulatory or technical accuracy demands a specialist.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — top sellers relied on internal experts less than average performers in the corpus; the rationale comes from interviews.

### [JOLT:SME-PREPCALL] Script the specialist's entry and exit
**Move:** When you must bring in a specialist, run a prep call first: tell them exactly where in the conversation to speak, and set the expectation that they answer and hand control back. The buyer should see you as the person who sourced the answer, not the one out of their depth.
**APPLY WHEN:** a specific technical question exceeds your knowledge.
**AVOID WHEN:** deep technical workshops where the specialist genuinely should lead — over-constraining them there damages credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — expert talk-time in top-performer calls was far lower than in comparable average-performer calls; the prep-call mechanic comes from a manufacturing rep interview.

### [JOLT:CURATED-READING] Hand the buyer a curated third-party reading list early
**Move:** Early in the process, name a short set of external sources — analyst explainers, a podcast, a couple of articles — that lift a novice buyer to a working level fast, and encourage them to circulate it internally. Deliberately include material that is not your own marketing.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer is new to the category and likely to self-educate chaotically online.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is already expert; and the sources must be genuinely useful or the credibility gain reverses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product/info-product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — observed on calls; high performers suggested outside reading more proactively than peers.

### [JOLT:REBUT-EVERY] Answer every stated objection
**Move:** Never let an explicit objection pass uncontested; offer at least one rebuttal.
**APPLY WHEN:** any call, especially closing calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** no blanket exception — but rebuttal quality matters; reflexive contradiction is not what was measured.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — objections appeared in 69% of calls but rebuttals in only 52%; closing calls with a rebuttal converted at 31% versus 17% without.

### [JOLT:PREBUTTAL] Pre-butt the unstated objection
**Move:** Watch for implicit non-acceptance — a hedge instead of an affirmation, a tonal shift, a pause. Stop the pitch and surface it: note that the point does not seem to have landed and invite the concern onto the table. Or name it before they do: guess aloud what they may be wondering, or say a common worry at this stage is X. Normalizing the concern makes the buyer feel less alone in it.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-to-late calls where the buyer's engagement quality drops.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would be planting an objection the buyer did not have; and it requires genuine comfort with tension — a half-hearted probe followed by plowing on is worse than not asking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — preemptive rebuttals associated with a 40% win rate, well above the 26% average.

### [JOLT:CANDOR-DECLINE] Decline the superfluous request, professionally
**Move:** When a buyer asks for another demo, reference call or data pack that will change nothing, say so directly and kindly — you do not want to waste their team's time and the extra activity will show nothing new — then pivot to the real question of why they are not ready and what would actually help, explicitly including the possibility that they buy elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** repeat requests that duplicate what has already been delivered.
**AVOID WHEN:** the request genuinely carries new information; and when done from self-interest rather than the buyer's interest it degrades into obnoxious aggression.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — observed in top-performer calls; the four-quadrant engagement framing is adapted from Kim Scott's Radical Candor.

### [JOLT:QUESTION-BEHIND-REQUEST] Ask what the request is meant to prove
**Move:** Before granting an information request, ask what specifically they are trying to validate, then test its sufficiency: if the reference confirms exactly the result they hope for, is that enough to move forward, or is something else giving them pause? Keep asking why, in the Five Whys spirit, until the underlying objection is named.
**APPLY WHEN:** any request for extra validation late in the cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes an interrogation, or a pretext to refuse everything — the intent is diagnosis, not obstruction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — repeated pattern in the studied calls; Five Whys attributed to Sakichi Toyoda.

### [JOLT:BETTER-PATH-SWAP] Substitute a better route to resolving the concern
**Move:** Once the real hang-up is identified, propose a more efficient way to settle it instead of the requested activity — say plainly that another call with the product team will not address that concern and that you know a better way to get them what they need.
**APPLY WHEN:** after the underlying objection has been surfaced.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not actually diagnosed the concern — swapping activities without diagnosis reads as dodging.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — observed rep behavior on studied calls.

### [JOLT:TALK-SHARE] Take more of the airtime when you have a point of view
**Move:** Do not ration your own speaking to satisfy the listen-more convention. Share expertise proactively instead of waiting to be asked, carrying somewhat more than half the conversation.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have genuine category expertise and the buyer needs guidance.
**AVOID WHEN:** the talk time is filler, small talk or irrelevant detail — the finding is about content, not volume; early discovery still needs listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 58% average rep talk time in won deals versus 52% in lost deals.

### [JOLT:COOP-OVERLAP] Use cooperative overlapping, not passive listening
**Move:** Keep continuous verbal acknowledgment while the buyer speaks and step into pauses — finish a thought, add an example, paraphrase, ask, redirect. Overlap signals engagement rather than rudeness; sitting silent can leave the buyer feeling unheard and alone.
**APPLY WHEN:** indecisive buyers who need an active, engaged conversation to get over the hump.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes cutting the buyer off to push a pitch — the authors deny that interrupting causes buying; and the norm is culturally variable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — roughly twice as many interruption/overtalk instances in won deals as in lost ones; the concept is from linguist Deborah Tannen (footnoted to popular-press sources).

### [JOLT:SILENCE-BAND] Keep purposeful silence inside a narrow band
**Move:** Use deliberate silence sparingly and in specific spots — after a targeted question, or after stating price or terms — keeping total silence roughly in the 8–17% range of the call.
**APPLY WHEN:** any live call, especially negotiation moments.
**AVOID WHEN:** below roughly 8% (the buyer cannot get a word in) or above roughly 30% (the buyer starts doubting you know the material); worst case is silence combined with visible confusion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — silence quartiles against win rate: 8–17% band at 30%, adjacent band 28%, outer bands lower.

### [JOLT:DEMO-PAUSE] Break the demo to read the room
**Move:** Stop mid-demonstration to check reaction and dig into any hesitation, confusion or silence, rather than running the presentation to its end.
**APPLY WHEN:** demo-centric outbound calls where the demo occupies most of the agenda and objections have gone quiet.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes a ritual pause with no follow-through — the value is in pursuing the hesitation you spot.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — top performers stopped demos to gauge reaction far more often than average performers in demo-centric calls.

### Stage: outcome uncertainty — take risk off the table

### [JOLT:SET-EXPECT] Set believable-impact expectations, not maximum-impact ROI
**Move:** State the ceiling case once, then explicitly hand the buyer a lower number you are confident in and build the business case on that number, framing anything above it as upside. Do this early, not at signature.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer shows maximizer tendencies, asks whether the ROI is real, or the internal business case is being written.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot actually clear the conservative number — used as false modesty it recreates the credibility gap it was meant to close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — 20% win rate without versus 51% with (stated as +155%), and reps set no expectation on 81% of calls; one of the largest single-behavior deltas reported.

### [JOLT:SAFETY-NET-ENGINEER] Engineer contractual downside protection
**Move:** Instead of scaring the buyer, invent concrete loss-limiters and write them into the deal: opt-out clauses, refund or plan-change clauses, contract carve-outs, additional professional-services support bundled in.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer voices outcome uncertainty — asks for guarantees, cites vendors that burned them, questions whether the ROI is achievable.
**AVOID WHEN:** the hesitation is really a valuation problem (which option) or an information problem — a safety net does not answer "which one"; and where legal or finance genuinely cannot support the term you promised.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the high-performer pattern in the corpus (downside-risk options moved win rate 22% → 46%, used on only 14% of calls); specific clause types come from interviews.

### [JOLT:SAFETY-NET-SURFACE] Name the existing escape hatch the moment hesitation appears
**Move:** Proactively surface the cancellation window, plan-change option, money-back guarantee, free trial or flexible payment terms as reassurance rather than as a concession — that is the answer to "I need more time", instead of a discount deadline or a polite promise to call back.
**APPLY WHEN:** transactional deal, buyer's own money at stake, one conversation to work with, and the company genuinely offers an opt-out.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have been running FUD in the same conversation, so the pivot reads as insincere; when it is fired reflexively at every hesitation; or where comp plans claw back on cancellation, which is why reps skip it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas/product · market=transactional
**Evidence:** field-study — observed in transactional calls but on under 15% of them; the book flags both underuse and misuse.

### [JOLT:PRE-CLOSE-PLAN] Build the post-sale plan before signature
**Move:** Weeks ahead of a signable contract, walk the buyer through milestones, named owners, target KPIs and the first three-to-six-month path — where early wins land and how much return appears when — positioned as the route derived from customers who got the best results.
**APPLY WHEN:** complex purchase with no opt-out or refund available, implementation risk is the stated worry, or the buyer is personally accountable for the outcome.
**AVOID WHEN:** the plan is generic collateral without named owners and metrics, or you cannot actually staff and deliver it — a broken guardrail converts indecision into churn.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — pattern seen in several recorded complex-sale calls and illustrated by rep language; no isolated win-rate figure for this variant.

### [JOLT:PRO-SERV] Attach professional services as purchase insurance
**Move:** Add a paid implementation/services element so the supplier is contractually obliged to walk parts of the rollout with the buyer, and sell it as shared ownership of the outcome rather than as an upsell — accepting that total price rises, because the fear is of acting alone, not of cost.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage complex deal where the blocker is outcome uncertainty, internal capability is thin, and the economic buyer is personally exposed to failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** the blocker is price or valuation rather than outcome fear; small transactional deals where added cost dominates; or when the services line is a disguised quota grab with no real joint-execution commitment behind it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — observed JOLT-seller behavior in the recorded-call dataset with a mechanism argument rather than an effect size; the framing example comes from one sales leader interview.

### [JOLT:CARVE-OUT] Ring-fence the scariest scope in creative contracting
**Move:** Split the piece of scope the buyer is most anxious about into a separate short-term, cancellable agreement while the rest stays long-term, holding the original discount level even though total contract value drops.
**APPLY WHEN:** eleventh-hour stall on a multi-year deal traced to one business unit or one implementation unknown.
**AVOID WHEN:** the anxiety is diffuse rather than localized, or you cannot actually staff a rescue if that unit goes sideways.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single tech-industry sales leader's deal story.

### [JOLT:START-SMALL] Recommend a smaller first purchase
**Move:** Deliberately shrink the initial scope so the buyer can prove value and then expand — sell less up front to sell more overall.
**APPLY WHEN:** outcome uncertainty dominates and the deal size itself is what has frozen the buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** quota math or a land-only comp plan makes the expansion motion unreal; and where the product needs full deployment to work at all.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — presented as a counterintuitive finding from the call analysis, contradicting the bigger-is-better convention.

### [JOLT:SCOPE-DOWN-PREQUOTE] Talk the buyer down before quoting the price
**Move:** When the buyer describes a maximal package, propose starting with the two or three most-used components and book a follow-up in a few months to add on — do it before the number is spoken, not after sticker shock.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer is assembling a wish list, price has not been quoted, and a repeat-purchase or add-on motion exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** the price is already on the table — removing items then feels to the buyer like settling for less.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a home/small-business maintenance services leader.

### Stage: buyer's agent — trust, candor and the ask

### [JOLT:NO-OVERBUY] Recommend the buyer spend less than they asked for
**Move:** Tell the buyer directly that the coverage, seat count or tier they requested exceeds their need, and propose the smaller configuration with the reason — avoid paying for unused capacity, generate internal demand before expanding.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer arrives asking for more than their described use case requires and you want credibility currency before the close.
**AVOID WHEN:** the smaller configuration will visibly fail their stated requirement — under-scoping to look honest creates churn.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — called one of the most impactful agency-dilemma techniques in the call set, illustrated by insurance and software calls where it produced a larger bundled sale.

### [JOLT:COMP-CREDIT] Credit — or recommend — the competitor
**Move:** Acknowledge honestly where a competitor is cheaper or leads the category, then state precisely where you are the better partner and where you are not.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer raises a competitor comparison and your differentiation is real but narrower than your marketing claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a rhetorical trick with no real concession — a fake compliment followed by a pitch reads as manipulation and burns the trust it was meant to buy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — examples from both transactional and complex studied calls; no isolated win-rate delta reported.

### [JOLT:GAP-ADMIT] Name the capability you do not have yet
**Move:** State plainly that a requested capability is missing or roadmap-only, give a realistic horizon, and say you do not want to overpromise.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyer asks for a feature at the edge of the product and a committee will verify the claim anyway.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gap is central to their use case and you have no compensating story — then the right move is disqualification, not trust-building.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — recurring top-performer behavior in the call set; the counter-pattern of claiming the product does everything is tied to skepticism and later churn.

### [JOLT:DONT-KNOW] Use a well-timed "I don't know, I'll find out"
**Move:** Admit an unanswerable question openly, note that you checked the obvious sources, and commit to returning with the answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** a narrow factual question — integration detail, edge case — where bluffing is checkable.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes the default posture; demonstrated expertise stays non-negotiable and repeated ignorance destroys the delegate role.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — inbound call example, described as small but impactful with no metric attached.

### [JOLT:ASK-BIZ] Ask for the business and make yes the default
**Move:** After the de-risking work, make an explicit forward-motion request — permission to submit the order, send the agreement, book implementation, take payment — phrased as the natural next step rather than a question about interest.
**APPLY WHEN:** you have earned it: recommendation given, exploration limited, risk taken off the table, and the buyer sounds ready.
**AVOID WHEN:** that groundwork does not exist — the authors explicitly warn against reading their data as "ask on every call", since roughly half of conversations had not earned the right.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** field-study — reps asked for the business on 46% of studied calls; the default-option mechanism is borrowed from Thaler & Sunstein.

### [JOLT:INBOUND-TRIAGE] Triage the queue in the first minute or two
**Move:** Establish fast whether the caller is a buyer at all; if the request is a service issue, route it out with a warm transfer — say you are handing them to someone who can solve it and stay on the line until that person has the context.
**APPLY WHEN:** inbound sales queue where misdirected service contacts are common.
**AVOID WHEN:** the transfer would be cold or blind — the effort cost then lands on the customer and the goodwill argument collapses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound · acv=micro/low · stakeholders=solo · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional
**Evidence:** field-study — misdirected service contacts converted at only 16%, the lowest of any call type; warm-transfer positioning rests on earlier CEB/Gartner service research.

### Stage: organizational — measuring the indecision problem

### [JOLT:INACTION-BASELINE] Set a lost-to-inaction hard deck and an age-out date
**Move:** From CRM, define an acceptable no-decision rate for your market, then set cycle-time norms per deal type and segment so there is an explicit date past which a deal counts as stuck.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline review or quarterly planning, before investing in any indecision remediation.
**AVOID WHEN:** benchmarking against a generic industry number — the book's own counterexample is a saturated-TAM vendor for whom a 40% no-decision rate would be a good outcome.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/inbound · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the 40–60% no-decision range comes from the authors' study and varies by organization.

### [JOLT:VELOCITY-SIGNAL] Read interaction frequency as a health signal
**Move:** Track whether calls and emails on a deal are getting denser or sparser; widening gaps flag drift toward no decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-cycle deals where CRM stage fields are unreliable.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as causal — forcing more touches onto a dying deal does not revive it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to Challenger research, a vendor source with no citation given.

### [JOLT:LOSS-SPLIT] Separate "said no" losses from "said yes and didn't buy"
**Move:** Report the two loss types separately in QBRs and treat the second as a seller-skill problem rather than a product, pricing, messaging or process problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** win rate is the only loss metric in review, or coaching resources are being allocated.
**AVOID WHEN:** loss reasons are rep-entered and unaudited — the split is only as honest as its inputs.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the book's framing with no metric attached.

### [JOLT:COMPETENCY-AUDIT] Score calls by competency level, and split the audit job
**Move:** Replace binary did-the-rep-say-X checklists with scorecards rating a skill from novice to mastery, and separate the auditors who score selling skill from those listening for compliance, competitor mentions and market feedback.
**APPLY WHEN:** running manual call audits, or QA is scoring at volume in tick-box mode.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sample is tiny — small samples produce false positives and negatives regardless of scorecard quality, and reps come to see the process as arbitrary and punitive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — from the authors' work overhauling call-auditing processes at numerous companies.

### [JOLT:VOC-WINLOSS] Supplement audits with post-sale surveys and win–loss interviews
**Move:** Add a voice-of-customer instrument that captures how the buyer experienced the rep, using interviews where qualitative depth matters.
**APPLY WHEN:** call audits alone are producing scores nobody can interpret.
**AVOID WHEN:** response rates are low, the survey is already contested real estate across departments, or the interview pool is mostly wins — losses are exactly where indecision hides.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the authors' experience with large-scale sales QA programs.

### [JOLT:CI-DILIGENCE] Buy conversation intelligence on categories, actionability and true TCO
**Move:** Before purchase, test three things: can it build behavior categories (phrase sets describing a concept) rather than spot keywords; does the vendor supply predictive/prescriptive modelling plus an engagement model that drives change; and price the indirect costs — custom category work, data-science or professional-services SOWs, extract licences, reprocessing fees, duplicate ingestion when each department buys its own point tool.
**APPLY WHEN:** an organization is scaling indecision measurement beyond manual audits.
**AVOID WHEN:** expecting off-the-shelf JOLT categories — the authors state no platform ships them except their own, so read this section as vendor-adjacent.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — 100+ in-depth interviews with early adopters; the adoption figure is cited secondhand from Aberdeen.

### [JOLT:COACH-MIDDLE] Aim coaching time at the core performers
**Move:** Redirect manager coaching away from the best and worst reps toward the middle of the distribution, measured on lost-to-inaction rate; the specific failure pattern there is happy ears — over-reading an eager-looking buyer as a committed one.
**APPLY WHEN:** managers self-select toward star reps or firefight the bottom; the forecast keeps slipping on deals that looked great.
**AVOID WHEN:** the bottom tail is failing on compliance or basic process — that is performance management, not a coaching-ROI question.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a CEB/Gartner claim of up to 19% performance improvement, vendor research linked to the authors' earlier work rather than to this study's data.

### Stage: organizational — hiring for JOLT

### [JOLT:PROFILEAUDIT] Reverse-engineer the profile from your own JOLT sellers, then demote it to an input
**Move:** Identify the reps who actually beat no decision, look for shared experiences and credentials, and write that into the job profile — while naming your prior assumptions first so you can tell confirmation from discovery. Never let the resulting profile alone decide who gets an interview or an offer.
**APPLY WHEN:** opening a requisition or refreshing a recruiting profile, with assessment data on the current team available.
**AVOID WHEN:** the internal sample is tiny; hiring managers cannot articulate their priors; or the profile would be wired into automated résumé screening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recommended from the authors' work with client sales teams; the bias warning is generic confirmation-bias reasoning, named but not sourced.

### [JOLT:NONSALESPOOL] Put non-salespeople on the long list
**Move:** Source candidates from customer success (whose day job is helping buyers overcome adoption fear), from your own former customers (read immediately as subject-matter experts and who have personally felt pre-purchase fear), and from professions built on motivating action — consultants, teachers, lawyers, accountants.
**APPLY WHEN:** early long-list stage, where the team keeps hiring conventional profiles and keeps losing to no decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** the role's real bottleneck is elsewhere (prospecting volume, technical depth); or as a blanket replacement for sales hiring rather than a widening of the pool.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — two client stories (a company whose assessment data favoured customer-success backgrounds, a software leader hiring former customers), no dataset behind the claim.

### [JOLT:PASTSHOPRISK] Read prior sales experience as a swing factor, including negatively
**Move:** Ask sales-experienced candidates what selling culture they come from, and treat a background at a hard-charging, pressure-selling shop as a risk signal for no-decision losses rather than a credential; use past performance only as a weak positive indicator, since a low performer elsewhere is unlikely to become your best JOLT seller.
**APPLY WHEN:** screening experienced AEs, where your own hiring history shows no-decision rates by prior employer.
**AVOID WHEN:** applied as a blanket ban on candidates from a named competitor, or when your own no-decision data is too thin to support the pattern.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one study participant's self-reported analysis that hires from aggressive-selling competitors lost more deals to no decision.

### [JOLT:INDECISIONINT] Interview specifically for indecision, not for closing
**Move:** Ask how the candidate handles a buyer who gets cold feet late; ask for stories from work or personal life about motivating an indecisive person to act; ask how they would forecast a deal showing decision dysfunction.
**APPLY WHEN:** structured interview loops needing discriminating questions beyond walk-me-through-a-deal-you-closed.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as sufficient on its own — the authors flag that interview settings cannot reproduce buyer emotion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — recommended interview content, stated without win-rate or predictive-validity data.

### [JOLT:ROLEPLAYOBS] Prefer direct observation over self-report
**Move:** Build simulated indecision conversations or role plays into the loop, record them, and review how the candidate behaved when the buyer stalled; use recorded real interactions where possible, accepting that privacy rules usually block candidates from sharing prior calls.
**APPLY WHEN:** finalist stage in an organization that already records and analyses calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** recording without candidate consent or against privacy/legal constraints; or when nobody on the panel can score the behavior consistently.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as better in principle, with no comparative hiring-outcome data.

### [JOLT:EQSCREEN] Rebalance prehire assessments toward emotional capability
**Move:** Check whether your prehire diagnostics over-weight intellectual capacity and add measures of emotional capability — the stated bar is a seller as fluent in the buyer's fears as in the product facts.
**APPLY WHEN:** an assessment is already in the process, especially as an early screen before interviews.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a hard gate — assessments of either type are positioned as inputs, not arbiters.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a stated design preference for the assessment battery, no validation data offered.

### Stage: organizational — developing sellers and managing the motion

### [JOLT:OVERLAY] Deploy JOLT as an overlay, not a replacement methodology
**Move:** Introduce the model as an added tool for the part of the sale the team's current methodology never named, explicitly keeping the existing process, training and language intact.
**APPLY WHEN:** rolling out to a team already invested in a methodology, where resistance is "we already have a sales process."
**AVOID WHEN:** used to dodge fixing genuinely broken discovery or qualification — the book is silent on those stages and cannot substitute for them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a positioning claim about methodology complementarity, not a measured comparison of rollout approaches.

### [JOLT:FEARCHOICE] Make "am I increasing or reducing fear?" the daily coaching lens
**Move:** Frame the model as a per-conversation choice rather than a trait, and use that one question to evaluate calls, coach reps and review deals — the seller's job shifts from persuasion to motivation at some point in the cycle, and that shift is visible in how they talk about the work.
**APPLY WHEN:** call reviews, one-on-ones, interviews; a rep keeps relitigating the status quo on a deal already won on merits.
**AVOID WHEN:** reduced to a slogan with no per-behavior measurement; and remember fear reduction alone is insufficient — the model still needs enough pain to move plus enough fear reduction to act.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — rests on the study's finding that behaviors amplifying failure fear reduce win rates; the coaching framing is the authors' interpretation.

### [JOLT:PERSKILLDIAG] Diagnose per behavior, coach the specific gap
**Move:** Measure each of the four behaviors separately per rep, expect uneven profiles (a seller can be excellent at limiting exploration and mediocre at de-risking), and tailor coaching to the individual's weakest behavior instead of running one generic training.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-rollout once behaviors are being scored, especially when aggregate numbers look fine but no-decision losses persist.
**AVOID WHEN:** no reliable scoring exists — off-the-shelf conversation-intelligence categories generate false positives and negatives.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — per-behavior variation is reported from the authors' measurement of frontline seller behavior across the corpus.

### [JOLT:MGRBRAKES] Have the manager pressure-test indecision in pipeline review
**Move:** In deal reviews the manager taps the brakes on deals the rep is excited about, probes the buyer's level of decision dysfunction early, makes it a factor in whether to keep spending time — and where warranted uses managerial authority to construct de-risking terms the rep cannot offer alone.
**APPLY WHEN:** forecast roll-up and pipeline review; a deal shows enthusiasm without decision progress.
**AVOID WHEN:** the manager is themselves status-quo-oriented and will simply add pressure tactics; or the review is used to kill deals wholesale rather than to reallocate rep time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a role prescription; the underlying indecision-frequency finding is field-study, the management practice itself is unmeasured.

### [JOLT:NORMALIZE] Defuse resistance by mapping the skills onto everyday behavior
**Move:** When reps object that a technique feels rude or manipulative — cooperative overlapping is the named example — anchor it in ordinary life: recommending a restaurant or a show, a parent cutting off an endless line of questions, the familiar money-back guarantee and free trial.
**APPLY WHEN:** training and enablement, where there is visible discomfort with limiting the exploration.
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection is substantive rather than stylistic — the analogy does not licence talking over a buyer who is disclosing the real blocker.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a training-framing argument by analogy, no data.

### [JOLT:HUDDLE] Institutionalize idea-sharing on de-risking tactics
**Move:** Run team huddles to surface new ways of limiting exploration and taking risk off the table, and add manager-to-manager sessions so coaches trade observations; organizational design can accelerate it (T-Mobile is cited for cross-functional account teams aligned by city with a defined account set).
**APPLY WHEN:** post-rollout, when de-risking creativity is the bottleneck — reps know the concept but lack concrete offers.
**AVOID WHEN:** substituted for individual coaching; and the cited org structure is one company's arrangement, not a validated pattern.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one named company example plus the authors' observation of manager-to-manager sessions.

### [JOLT:CHANNELPARITY] Treat channel as a frequency dial, not an applicability filter
**Move:** When reviewing a deal or coaching a rep, do not excuse a missing behavior because the motion is inbound or transactional; identify which behaviors that channel habitually under-uses (inbound teams under-recommend) and deliberately raise the rate there.
**APPLY WHEN:** auditing a book of business or a team spanning multiple channels; a rep argues the model does not fit their motion.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a substitute for the underlying skill — raising frequency without judging the indecision first just adds pressure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** field-study — the channel split showed behavior-frequency differences with comparable win-rate impact per behavior.

## Where this book stops working

- **Before intent exists.** The entire model is late-stage. It has nothing on prospecting, discovery quality, needs development, commercial qualification or pricing design, and its own advice (cap the diagnosis, recommend rather than ask) is actively wrong if applied before a vision is agreed. Pair it with a discovery source; never let it replace one.
- **When the buyer genuinely prefers the status quo.** Roughly 44% of inaction losses. Fear-reduction moves do nothing there; the phase-one toolkit the book tells you to retire is the correct one.
- **Single-call transactional motions.** Moves that need multi-touch reading — GOOD-ENOUGH-DEBRIEF, INDECISION-SCORECARD, SHORTLIST-CRITERIA-TEST, POWERFUL-REQUEST — have no data to run on when there is one 30–60 minute conversation and no relationship bank.
- **Micro-ACV / solo-buyer deals.** Committee-shaped moves (POWERFUL-REQUEST, PRE-CLOSE-PLAN, CARVE-OUT, PRO-SERV, DEMO-PAUSE) have nobody to convene and no contract surface to engineer. Conversely SAFETY-NET-SURFACE, INBOUND-TRIAGE and SCOPE-DOWN-PREQUOTE are transactional-only and read as under-serious in an enterprise pursuit.
- **Where the economics forbid the de-risking.** Opt-outs, refunds and land-small motions collide with clawback comp plans, land-only quotas, legal/finance policy, and products that need full deployment to work. The book acknowledges reps skip these moves for exactly these reasons and offers no answer beyond escalating to a manager.
- **Founder-led and professional-services selling.** The agency dilemma is structurally worse when the advice being sold *is* the withheld information, and part-time sellers get too few repetitions to build the reflexes.
- **Solo/no-recording teams.** The measurement half (COMPETENCY-AUDIT, CI-DILIGENCE, PERSKILLDIAG, COACH-MIDDLE) presumes a call-recording estate, a QA function and a manager layer. It also shades into positioning for the authors' own conversation-intelligence platform.
- **Disqualification is bounded.** Killing deals on indecision is capped by the authors at roughly 10–15% of potential buyers; used more widely it becomes an excuse to drop hard but winnable deals.

## What the book debunks

- **"Hesitation means the value case failed."** A hesitant buyer who already agreed on the vision is afraid of a wrong purchase, not unconvinced of the need.
- **"More fear moves buyers."** FUD applied to an already frightened buyer supplies more reasons to postpone. Four documented flavors, all failure modes: manufactured urgency, scarcity, wallowing in status-quo pain, and isolation (peers are moving on without you) — isolation is called the harshest, manufacturing resentment and broken trust.
- **"Status-quo bias and indecision are the same thing."** Ritov & Baron separated them; avoidance of a mistake is the stronger grip late in a deal.
- **"Stated intent means effectively closed."** Buyers who behaved as satisficers while expressing intent flip to maximizer behavior at signature, because intent is free and commitment is not.
- **"Always ask, never tell."** Reactive needs-diagnosis in the face of indecision produces the ask-for-more-time ending; more discovery questions add choice where the buyer wanted guidance.
- **"More choice is better."** Participation and conversion fall with option count — but stripping choice *early* is also wrong, since option abundance is what attracts attention in the first place.
- **"Listen more than you talk" and "never interrupt."** Contradicted by the talk-time and overlap splits, with the caveat that the mechanism is engagement, not dominance, and that silence is a real technique with diminishing returns in both directions.
- **"Bigger deals are higher-quality deals."** Contradicted: the best reps sold more over time by starting small.
- **"An opt-out signals weak confidence in the product."** Named as the belief that keeps a high-value move unused; the complex-sale variant is the reflex that legal or finance would never allow downside protection — pushed, experienced sellers recalled methods they had already used.
- **"Never concede a competitor advantage, never admit a gap, never say I don't know."** Presented as the average-performer training that fuels the agency dilemma; claiming the product does everything produces immediate skepticism and post-sale churn.
- **"Indecision is a complex-enterprise problem."** Frequency of behaviors varies by channel; impact per behavior does not.
- **"Great sellers are born."** The behaviors were extracted from observed high performers, so they exist on most teams — and hiring is too slow to fix a pipeline stalling now.
- **"Org design can route around indecision."** No territory or hierarchy avoids something present on 87% of calls.
- **Documented failure modes:** treating research requests and pilot demands as buying signals and feeding them; misreading decision avoidance as procrastination (optimistic forecasts, months of wasted coverage); sending the specialist in early and being delegated down to the level you sound like; demo mode suppressing objections so silence reads as acceptance; reps solving service issues out of goodwill and converting at the bottom of the range; manipulative insincerity (agree to everything, complain afterwards), obnoxious aggression (challenge from self-interest), and ruinous empathy (care, say nothing, let the buyer spin); asking for the business on a cold deal — low ask rates are a symptom, not the disease; manual audits sampling ~1% of volume with binary criteria; tonal sentiment analysis producing high false positives; conversation-intelligence categories that do not generalize across companies.

## Numbers that matter

All win-rate figures are vendor-run, machine-scored analysis of recorded calls (self-reported, not peer-reviewed); several were read from bar charts.

- Corpus: 2.5M sales calls, dozens of participating companies, transactional/inbound and complex/outbound; 8,300+ tagged independent variables against a closed-sale dependent variable; the model is self-reported to predict outcomes over 85% of the time. Comparison anchor: SPIN Selling — 12 years, 35,000 calls, 116 factors.
- Deals ending in no decision: 40–60% depending on organization (author estimate/range, not a computed corpus figure). Within that no-decision bucket: 44% status-quo preference, 56% indecision. **Do not collapse these two numbers** — 56% is a share of the stalled bucket, not of all deals.
- Indecision at moderate or high levels in 87% of opportunities. Study-average win rate 26%.
- Win rate by indecision level: low 45–55%, moderate 25–30%, high under 5%. Average performer vs JOLT seller: low indecision 39% vs ~69%; moderate/high 26% vs 57% (~120% delta, the largest in the dataset); highest 6% vs 31%.
- Opportunities where the buyer verbalized intent to buy converted at ~26%.
- Relitigating the status quo: present on 73% of calls, negative on outcomes 84% of the time. A JOLT approach is reported to flip this to roughly a 70% chance of a good outcome. JOLT skill was low or undetectable on about a third of calls; high on only 7%.
- Proactive guidance: 18% → 44% win rate (+144%). Advocacy: +74% lift, 33% when used. Diagnosis plus recommendation 36% vs diagnosis alone 14%. Neither skill present in ~40% of calls (13% conversion); both present in only 16% (48% win rate). Recommend-skill lift by indecision level: +240% at low, +530% at high (under 2% vs 13% absolute).
- Objections stated in 69% of calls, rebuttals offered in 52%. Closing calls with a rebuttal 31% vs 17% without. Preemptive rebuttal 40%.
- Rep talk time 58% in wins vs 52% in losses; interruptions/overtalk ~2× more frequent in wins. Silence 8–17% of call → 30% win rate, 17–31% → 28%, outer bands lower. Limiting the exploration overall: 42% conversion vs 16% when research goes unchaperoned.
- Setting expectations: 20% → 51% (stated as +155%); no expectation set on 81% of calls. Downside-risk options: 22% → 46% (+109%), used on 14% of calls. Safety-net mentions in transactional calls: under 15%. Asked for the business on 46% of calls.
- Inbound: 60–75% of calls/chats opened with clear purchase intent; the other 25–40% were largely misrouted service contacts, which converted at 16% (lowest of any type). Outbound complex sellers volunteered a personal recommendation 60–75% of the time vs 40–50% for inbound peers. Experienced JOLT sellers set guidelines and post-purchase expectations on over 60% of complex-sale interactions.
- Disqualification on indecision: capped at ~10–15% of potential buyers. Indecision scorecard: 22-point threshold (heuristic, no validation reported).
- Customer effort (Tethr Effort Index, a vendor deep-learning proxy over 280+ variables): low effort ~40–90% win rate, moderate down to ~9%, high to ~4%; perceived high effort roughly 3× typical when the seller relitigates the status quo.
- Measurement context: conversation-intelligence adoption 26% of companies (Aberdeen, secondhand); ~80% of deployments reported by the vendor as missing intended ROI; call auditing industry standard ~1% of volume; coaching the middle 60% claimed to lift performance up to 19% (CEB/Gartner).
- External anchors: loss aversion 2–3× (Kahneman & Tversky); Germeijs & de Boeck 174 seniors; Iyengar & Lepper 24 flavors → 60% stopped/3% bought vs 6 flavors → 40%/30%; Vanguard participation falls ~2 points per additional ten funds; 401(k) auto-enrolment under 50% opt-in vs over 90% opt-out; Frost & Shows indecisiveness scale (15 statements, 1993; high scorers ~14 min vs under 9 min for 50 decisions, n=15/group); ~20% of US adults chronic procrastinators; buyers ~60% through the journey before contacting a supplier (CEB/Gartner); maximizer graduates earned 20% more and were less satisfied (Roets et al.); realtors sell their own homes for ~3% more and hold them ~10 days longer (Levitt).

## Blind spots of this book

- **No causal identification.** Machine-scored correlations inside one vendor-assembled corpus, with classifiers hand-built by the vendor's own team and data contributed by client organizations. The authors' own model note concedes that effects are combined and sometimes competing, so no single behavior's coefficient is a standalone rule — yet the book's presentation invites exactly that reading.
- **Selection of the corpus.** Companies willing to hand over recorded calls, in call-recording-heavy motions. Field sales without recordings, partner/channel selling, procurement-led public tenders and RFP-bound bureaucracies are effectively absent.
- **The buyer's side is inferred, never asked.** Everything about buyer fear is read off what was said on a call and interpreted through the psychology literature; there are no buyer interviews, no post-hoc verification that the diagnosed fear was the actual one, and no test separating genuine procurement process from fear-driven stalling.
- **Silence on everything before the vision.** Discovery, qualification of fit, deal design, pricing, packaging, prospecting — the book is not merely thin here, it is absent, which makes it dangerous as a standalone methodology.
- **No treatment of multi-stakeholder politics.** The committee appears as a scoring dimension and a source of career risk, but there is no model of blockers, mobilizers, consensus mechanics or internal selling — a gap its authors' own earlier work covers and this one does not.
- **Vendor adjacency in the measurement chapters.** The recommended instrumentation converges on capabilities the authors state only their own platform ships; the effort→win-rate bridge runs through the vendor's proprietary index, not an independently validated measure.
- **Ethics of the moves is asserted, not enforced.** Advocacy, defaults, must-have filtering and blame absorption are potent precisely because they transfer decision responsibility. The book names the misuse (recommend your highest-margin option, define must-haves as your own differentiators, fake concessions) but supplies no test the seller can fail — the guardrail is the seller's own honesty.
- **Uneven citation quality.** The psychology of indecision rests on named peer-reviewed work; several actively recommended tactics (cooperative overlapping, cost-of-indecision figures) trace to popular press or unpublished vendor research.
- **No time horizon or durability data.** Nothing shows whether JOLT-won deals hold up over years, whether the behaviors survive training decay, or what happens when a whole market's sellers run the same plays.
