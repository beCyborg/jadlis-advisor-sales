# Getting to Yes — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The lens's default operating system for any negotiation with more than one movable variable. Its contribution is mechanical, not motivational: it names *why* haggling produces bad deals (positions weld ego to a number, so face-saving displaces the real interest) and supplies four replaceable parts — People, Interests, Options, Criteria — plus one measuring instrument, the BATNA. Where other sources give you a script for a moment, this gives you a decision procedure for the whole cycle: how to diagnose a stall, how to generate more than one shape of deal, how to settle a number on a basis neither side controls, and how to know what any offer is actually worth. It beats persuasion-first books on multi-stakeholder enterprise deals where the counterparty must justify the outcome upward, and it beats pure-leverage books on anything you have to live with after signature.

## Core model

Two games exist. **Positional bargaining** (soft or hard — the same game at different volumes) costs three things: unwise outcomes, inefficiency, damaged relationships. **Principled negotiation / negotiation on the merits** replaces it: hard on substance, soft on the people, decided by standards independent of either side's will.

Four elements, run three times (analysis → planning → discussion):
1. **People** — separate (the authors' own preferred word: *disentangle*) the people from the problem. Three baskets: perception, emotion, communication. Handle people problems with people techniques, never with substantive concessions.
2. **Interests** — a position is a decision; interests are what caused it. Behind opposed positions sit shared, differing and conflicting interests; the *differing* ones are trade material.
3. **Options** — invent before you decide. Four blockers: premature judgment, single-answer search, fixed-pie assumption, "their problem is their problem."
4. **Criteria** — settle disputed terms on market value, precedent, cost, expert judgment, what a court would award, reciprocity. Standards turn a concession into an act of consistency.

Around the four sit three defenses: **BATNA** (Ch. 6) for power asymmetry, **negotiation jujitsu + the one-text procedure** (Ch. 7) for a counterpart who stays positional, and **taming the hard bargainer** (Ch. 8) for tricks — where the method is applied one level up, to the rules of the game. ## Moves

**Stage A — Deal strategy and preparation**

### [NEGO:WISETEST-GTY] Three-criteria test for any deal process
**Move:** Judge how a negotiation is going against three tests, not one: does it produce a wise agreement (meets both sides' legitimate interests, durable), is it efficient in time and decisions consumed, does it leave the relationship intact or better.
**APPLY WHEN:** retrospective on a stalled or won deal; choosing between a haggling and a merits approach.
**AVOID WHEN:** pure price-only commodity buys where the relationship test is noise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a definitional standard, not measured.

### [NEGO:FOURPASS-GTY] Run People/Interests/Options/Criteria three times
**Move:** Walk the four elements through three stages — analysis (diagnose their perceptions, hostile emotion, unclear communication, both sides' interests, options and criteria already on the table), planning (rank your interests, set realistic objectives, generate more options and criteria), discussion (surface perception gaps, let each side state interests, jointly build options, agree standards).
**APPLY WHEN:** deal prep and call debrief; any multi-meeting cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** micro-deals where prep cost exceeds the spread.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — the authors' teaching framework.

### [NEGO:APPROACHTRIAGE-GTY] Five-question triage: haggle or go principled
**Move:** Score the deal on five axes before choosing an approach. How costly is an arbitrary outcome (high stakes and precedent push toward principled)? How complex are the issues (complexity demands interest analysis and invention)? How much does the working relationship matter? How entrenched are their expectations, and how long would shifting them take (ritualized adversarial histories may need a multi-negotiation timetable)? Where are you in the sequence — positional bargaining does least damage after interests, options and standards are already on the table. Weigh the reputational spillover onto how others negotiate with you later.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal-strategy prep; deciding how much process to invest in an account.
**AVOID WHEN:** trivial single-issue transactions with a competitive market on both sides — the triage costs more than the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — GM/UAW cited as needing four contracts to restructure their negotiations.

### [NEGO:LOWRISKLADDER-GTY] Adopt the method on a risk ladder
**Move:** Four-part rollout. Start small — experiment where stakes are low, your BATNA is good, favorable standards exist and the counterpart is likely receptive; add one technique at a time and raise stakes as confidence grows. Accept an investment dip, like a player rebuilding a stroke. Review after each significant negotiation (what worked, what didn't, what you'd change) and keep a negotiation journal. Prepare hard: write both sides' interests, invent options serving as many as possible, gather benchmarks that would persuade a reasonable third party, find the facts behind your arguments, work out what benchmarks *their* negotiators need to justify terms upward, decide what commitments you want from each side, sketch a framework agreement. Optionally role-play — including playing their side and hearing your own arguments from the receiving end.
**APPLY WHEN:** rep enablement; folding the method into an existing sales process.
**AVOID WHEN:** the first trial is run on a marquee deal — the dip lands where it costs most.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — training advice by analogy to athletics.

### [NEGO:GOODFAITHTEST-GTY] Self-test before using a tactic, and name the game
**Move:** Run three internal checks on any tactic you are considering: would you use it with a close friend or family member; would a published account of it embarrass you; would the conduct suit a hero or a villain in a story. These test your values, not outside opinion. Separately, consider opening the negotiation by naming the choice of game out loud — are we both after a wise agreement quickly and cheaply, or is this a stubbornness contest?
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding your own tactical line; framing the first meeting of an adversarial account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart reads the meta-question as naive or as an opening for exploitation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — normative self-check.

### [NEGO:WINDFALL-GTY] Four-question test before pocketing an unfair advantage
**Move:** When you can get materially more than you think fair, do not take it reflexively. Ask: how much is the excess worth above what you could justify as fair, and are you sure of the benefit (negotiators overestimate their own cleverness)? Will it hold — will they perform, what would enforcement or replacement cost, could a court find it unconscionable? What damage to this and other relationships, including a reputation for fair dealing that is far easier to destroy than to build? Will your conscience carry it? Consider restructuring so they feel they did you a favor rather than got taken.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart is visibly under-informed, or offers terms far above market.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "windfall" is a legitimately earned premium — over-applying the test talks you out of fair value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — moral and practical reasoning plus the Kashmir rug anecdote (payment in worthless Weimar-era currency).

### [NEGO:CHANNELPICK-GTY] Choose the channel deliberately; warm it before substance
**Move:** Route emotional or relationship-laden conversations to face-to-face; failing that, video over voice, voice over text. On remote channels invest first in personal connection — share something personal, evoke an existing relationship or shared identity, find a common connection. In email: reread before sending, make context and reasoning explicit, hunt for ambiguities, and switch channel the moment a reply signals an unexpected bad reaction. Even for email-dominated deals, open in person or by phone and schedule periodic same-mode check-ins. Add procedural agreements up front — a mutual promise to raise tentative concerns early, by voice, framed as fears rather than accusations. Counter-uses: phone and email make it easier to ask tougher questions, written arguments get closer attention without interpersonal cues, and email buys reflection time where texting rewards fast talkers.
**APPLY WHEN:** any multi-touch deal, especially remote or asynchronous.
**AVOID WHEN:** speed matters more than fidelity on a low-stakes item.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — Valley, Moag & Bazerman, *Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization* 34:211 (1998); review by Nadler & Shestowsky in Thompson, ed., *Negotiation Theory and Research* (2006).

### [NEGO:GETINSTEP-GTY] Get in step without stereotyping
**Move:** Adapt to the counterpart's values, perceptions, norms and mood across a concrete checklist: pacing fast/slow; formality; physical proximity; whether oral or written agreements bind; bluntness direct/indirect; time frame short/long; scope of relationship business-only vs all-encompassing; public vs private place of doing business; whether negotiators are status equals or the most competent people; rigidity of commitments. Know group customs but never infer an individual from them. Question every assumption, "just like me" and "totally different" alike.
**APPLY WHEN:** cross-border, cross-industry, or unfamiliar-counterpart deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** adaptation slides into mimicry, or group traits are treated as predictive — the book calls that insulting and factually risky.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — gender-difference claims hedged and footnoted to Carol Gilligan, *In a Different Voice* (1982), with the data said to show many individuals of each sex tending the other way.

### [NEGO:WHETHERBATNA-GTY] Decide whether to negotiate at all by comparing both BATNAs
**Move:** Whether and how hard to negotiate depends on how good your BATNA is and how likely talks are to beat it. Good BATNA plus unpromising talks → invest little. Bad BATNA → invest more even when talks look unpromising. Analyze *their* BATNA too, and check whether you are negotiating with the right party at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding whether to pursue an account; deciding whether the person in the room is the one whose decision you need.
**AVOID WHEN:** never — but its output is only as good as the honesty of both BATNA estimates.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a bank stuck for months with the owner-managers of a bankrupt energy company (whose BATNA was waiting for oil prices and keeping 100%); the case settled within 24 hours once the judge's interest was engaged.

### [NEGO:BACKFROMCLOSE-GTY] Design closure backward from their justification
**Move:** Before negotiating, picture the successful agreement and its implementation, then work backward: which issues must be resolved, and above all how the other side would explain and justify the deal to their own constituents, in concrete form ("top 10 percent of all electrical workers in Ontario", "less than the value given by two of three appraisers"). Ask the same of yourself. Then ask what agreement would let both sides say such things, and what it would take for each of you to accept rather than keep negotiating. Update the picture as information arrives.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals where the champion must sell internally.
**AVOID WHEN:** early exploratory calls where the shape is unknowable — premature closure design narrows options.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

**Stage B — Framing the game and the room**

### [NEGO:METAGAME-GTY] Negotiate the process before the price
**Move:** Treat every deal as two simultaneous negotiations — substance (price, terms, scope) and procedure (how we will decide). When they open with positional haggling, name the procedural question out loud and propose deciding on merits and standards instead of on who blinks.
**APPLY WHEN:** early in a deal; a demand-and-concede rhythm has started; cross-cultural or cross-functional deals with no shared process.
**AVOID WHEN:** a genuinely one-off transactional purchase where process talk costs more than the spread; late-stage deals where re-opening the frame reads as stalling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — the core insight of the Harvard Negotiation Project; no dataset offered.

### [NEGO:SIDEBYSIDE-GTY] Restructure the room as a joint task
**Move:** Say explicitly that neither side's interests get met unless both are addressed, and propose looking at the problem together; back it physically — sit on the same side, put the contract, map or blank sheet between you and the problem in front of both. If they will not agree verbally, start behaving that way and make joining attractive.
**APPLY WHEN:** adversarial framing has set in; commercial negotiation after a technical win.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty exploits collaborative framing to extract unilateral concessions; note the book concedes this must be re-done continuously, not once.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued by analogy (two shipwrecked sailors sharing rations).

### [NEGO:COJUDGE-GTY] Talk to them as a co-decider, in a small private room
**Move:** Stop arguing as if to a jury: treat the counterparty as a second judge with whom you must write one joint opinion. Establish confidential channels, shrink the meeting, and expect the real decision to happen among very few people.
**APPLY WHEN:** large committee negotiations that have turned performative; deals with press, board or internal audiences watching.
**AVOID WHEN:** excluding people who must own the outcome would trigger the participation failure (see PARTICIPATE-GTY); regulated processes requiring documented openness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 1954 Trieste talks, where progress came only after the principals dropped their delegations and met informally in a private house.

### [NEGO:PREGAME-GTY] Build the relationship before the negotiation exists
**Move:** Convert stranger to acquaintance ahead of time: learn their likes and dislikes, meet informally, arrive early and stay late around the scheduled meeting, and ask a small favor that lets them feel you owe them one.
**APPLY WHEN:** before a known-hard renewal or a first enterprise negotiation; any long-cycle account.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty's process forbids manufactured intimacy (regulated procurement); where the favor would look like an inducement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Benjamin Franklin's book-borrowing habit as the illustrative device.

### [NEGO:RULEGAME-GTY] Negotiate the rules of the game (recognize → raise → renegotiate)
**Move:** Three steps when a ploy appears. (1) Name it to yourself: deception cue, discomfort cue, or lock-in cue. (2) Raise it out loud, tentatively and non-accusingly — flag that you may be misreading, describe the pattern, offer a procedural remedy (e.g. a recess if they need to align internally). (3) Turn it into an explicit procedural negotiation: attack the problem, ask what interest the tactic serves, propose an alternative game, test the principle for reciprocity. Attack the tactic, never their integrity.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-cycle when behavior rather than terms is blocking progress; recurring buyer games (fake deadlines, mystery approvers, seat-of-power theater).
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no procedural remedy to propose; naming it publicly humiliates someone who then must defend the tactic to save face; the ploy is genuinely their institutional policy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Harvard Negotiation Project teaching and mediation across labor, legal and diplomatic cases.

### [NEGO:PROCRECIP-GTY] Reciprocity test on procedure
**Move:** Convert the tactic into a proposed rule and offer to apply it symmetrically. Seated facing the sun or in the low chair — ask what the theory is and whether you swap tomorrow. Preconditions set — ask whether you should set them too. A tactic that cannot survive being stated as a two-way rule usually gets dropped.
**APPLY WHEN:** ritual power plays around setting, sequencing, agenda control, first-offer demands.
**AVOID WHEN:** the asymmetry is legitimate (their procurement process, regulated approval flows) — insisting on symmetry there reads as pedantry.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the authors' principled-negotiation logic, no study cited.

### [NEGO:SETTINGFIX-GTY] Diagnose and renegotiate the physical setup
**Move:** Ask yourself whether you feel stress and why — noise, temperature, no room to caucus privately, sun in your eyes, hostile seating — and treat the answer as data about designed pressure. Raise it objectively as a shared problem and propose a change: swap chairs, take a break, move rooms, reschedule. Note the counter-intuitive option: meeting on *their* turf can be an advantage — it relaxes them and makes it easier for you to leave.
**APPLY WHEN:** long on-site sessions, deliberately marathoned meetings, hostile venue choices.
**AVOID WHEN:** the discomfort is incidental rather than engineered — turning ordinary friction into a procedural fight burns goodwill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern in the authors' practice; the eye-contact effect credited to unnamed student experiments.

**Stage C — People, perception and emotion**

### [NEGO:SHOES-GTY] Map their perceptions in two columns
**Move:** Before the meeting, write their view of the situation next to yours, item by item, phrased as they would phrase it — including the items that make your side look bad. Withhold judgment while doing it; the goal is to feel the force of their view, not to score it.
**APPLY WHEN:** any renewal, price increase or re-negotiation where both sides have a history to interpret; prep for a hostile stakeholder.
**AVOID WHEN:** never contraindicated as prep, but do not confuse the output with agreement — understanding is not conceding.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a tenant/landlady perception table where each line has a mirror-image counterpart.

### [NEGO:NOBLAME-GTY] Describe symptoms, then ask for their advice
**Move:** Replace an accusation with a factual incident log plus a request for help: state what failed, how often, what it cost, that you need the outcome fixed, and ask what they would do about it. Keep the person separate from the symptom even where blame would be justified.
**APPLY WHEN:** service failures, churn-risk conversations, escalations, any call you arrive at angry.
**AVOID WHEN:** a documented breach where legal or contractual notice is the required move, not a collaborative one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/saas/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the mechanics of defensiveness, illustrated with a generator-service rewrite.

### [NEGO:PERCEPTALK-GTY] Overinvest in what is cheap to you and loud to them
**Move:** Say explicitly the true things they want to hear, and do not dismiss as unimportant an issue that matters to them merely because it is easy for you to grant. Spend real time working out the practical mechanics of the easy concession — that work is what makes it credible and buys goodwill elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** an issue is low-cost on your side but symbolically central for them; multi-issue deals where you need currency for the hard issue.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "cheap" item is actually a precedent you cannot repeat across the customer base.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Law of the Sea Conference (1974–1981, ~150 nations): developed states treated technology transfer as trivially grantable and forfeited a low-cost lever on the rest of the agenda.

### [NEGO:COUNTEREXPECT-GTY] Act against the role they have cast you in
**Move:** Change a perception by taking an action their model of you cannot generate — go where an adversary would not go, concede what a hard bargainer would not concede, show up in person where a defensive party would send a letter.
**APPLY WHEN:** you are stereotyped (vendor, adversary, the-people-who-broke-it) and argument alone is not moving them.
**AVOID WHEN:** the gesture concedes substance you need; when it can be read as weakness by a counterparty already playing hard.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Sadat's November 1977 trip to Jerusalem, credited as a precondition of the 1979 treaty.

### [NEGO:PARTICIPATE-GTY] Give them authorship, not a finished product
**Move:** Involve the other side while the answer is still unfinished: ask their advice early, let them mark up drafts, credit their ideas generously rather than claiming them. Do not save the difficult stakeholder for last after you have worked everything out internally.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals, procurement, security or legal review, internal champions who must defend the deal upward.
**AVOID WHEN:** involving them leaks information you must hold; when "participation" is theater — the claim is about real influence over the product.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the insurance-commissioner scenario plus an all-white committee drafting reforms for excluded blacks in South Africa; felt participation called perhaps the single biggest determinant of acceptance, unmeasured.

### [NEGO:FACESAVE-GTY] Engineer consistency with their past words
**Move:** When they hold out on terms that look acceptable, assume the blocker is the appearance of backing down. Re-frame or re-package the same substance so it reconciles with their stated principles and prior commitments — including letting them announce it as their own decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stakeholder publicly committed to a contrary position; late-stage stalls with no substantive objection left.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as cosmetic pretense — the book insists face-saving is a real interest, not a courtesy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a mayor who rejected negotiated municipal-jobs terms and accepted identical terms once he could present them as his own campaign-promise decision.

### [NEGO:EMOAUDIT-GTY] Name the emotions, both sides', and put them on the table
**Move:** Mid-deal, check your own state, write down what you feel and what you would rather feel, do the same for them, and ask what is producing it — past grievance, spillover from another issue, something outside work. Then say it aloud as a legitimate fact, including the part you suspect is irrational, and invite them to say whether their side feels the same.
**APPLY WHEN:** repeated stalls with no new substantive argument; visible tension; post-incident renewal talks.
**AVOID WHEN:** the emotion named would signal desperation about your alternatives; counterparties or cultures where naming feelings reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — technique described from practice, no data.

### [NEGO:CORE5-GTY] Check the five core concerns
**Move:** Diagnose negative emotion against five interests: autonomy (making their own choices), appreciation (being valued), affiliation (belonging), role (a meaningful purpose), status (fair acknowledgement). Stop trampling whichever one you are stepping on, and feed it deliberately to build a working climate.
**APPLY WHEN:** an unexpectedly hostile stakeholder; champions sidelined by your going over their head; multi-threading that bruised someone's role.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a flattery script rather than a real change in how you treat them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — drawn from the authors' companion work on emotions (*Beyond Reason*) and Harvard teaching practice; not presented as a peer-reviewed result.

### [NEGO:IDENTITY-GTY] Read out-of-character behavior as identity threat
**Move:** When a counterparty reacts far out of proportion, or you feel yourself losing balance, test whether a self-image is under attack (competent, fair, good manager) rather than an interest. Adjust the framing so your point does not require them to accept being incompetent or unfair.
**APPLY WHEN:** disproportionate reactions; sudden hostility after a neutral remark; discovery that exposes a stakeholder's past decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to psychologize away a legitimate substantive objection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — described mechanism, no study cited.

### [NEGO:STEAM-GTY] Let them vent without feeding it
**Move:** Instead of interrupting or leaving when they unload grievances, sit still, do not answer the inflammatory content, and occasionally invite them to keep going until they are finished. Recognize that an angry speech may be performed for their own constituents and can buy the speaker room to settle later.
**APPLY WHEN:** an escalation call; a renewal after a failure; a stakeholder speaking in front of their team.
**AVOID WHEN:** venting escalates into a spiral rather than draining; abusive behavior you should not absorb.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/saas/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — asserted from practice; the release mechanism is stated, not measured.

### [NEGO:ONEANGER-GTY] One-at-a-time anger rule
**Move:** Agree an explicit ground rule that only one person may be angry at a time. It legitimizes the outburst, licenses the other side not to answer in kind, and makes breaking the rule a visible loss of self-control.
**APPLY WHEN:** recurring adversarial forums — steering committees, joint escalation boards, partner reviews — where blowups repeat.
**AVOID WHEN:** one-off transactional calls where imposing rules is heavier than the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to a 1950s labor-management Human Relations Committee in the steel industry.

### [NEGO:SYMBOL-GTY] Cheap symbolic gesture, including apology
**Move:** Use small, low-cost acts with high emotional return — a note of sympathy, a statement of regret, a shared meal, a gift for a family member, an apology. An apology can defuse without conceding personal responsibility or intent to harm.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-incident repair; relationship reset before a hard commercial ask.
**AVOID WHEN:** an apology carries legal liability; where the gesture substitutes for the substantive fix they actually need.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — called one of the cheapest and highest-return investments available, with no supporting count.

### [NEGO:TWOLEDGER-GTY] Build the relationship independent of agreement; never trade substance for it
**Move:** Keep two ledgers — substantive issues (terms, prices, dates, liabilities) and relationship issues (balance of emotion and reason, ease of communication, trust and reliability, acceptance vs rejection, persuasion vs coercion, mutual understanding) — and negotiate each on its own merits. Do not buy goodwill with unjustified concessions, and do not coerce concessions by threatening the relationship. If you do concede for relationship reasons, do it as a recognized favor with the expectation visible to both sides.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-horizon accounts, renewals, partner and channel relationships.
**AVOID WHEN:** the relationship is genuinely transactional and single-shot — the investment does not return.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from principle, with the Chamberlain/Sudetenland case as illustration.

### [NEGO:RELNEGOTIATE-GTY] Negotiate the relationship itself, on the merits
**Move:** When behavior keeps blocking substance, raise it as you would any substantive difference: describe your perceptions and feelings, inquire into theirs, avoid judging or attributing motive, propose external standards for how you will deal with each other, decline pressure tactics. Frame forward, not backward. Work on two assumptions — that they may not intend the effects you experience, and that they can change if they see the need. Have your BATNA thought through: sometimes they treat your concern as a shared problem only once they see your no-deal path is bad for them.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled deals where conduct, not terms, is the blocker.
**AVOID WHEN:** raising it hands a manipulative counterpart a new lever — the frame assumes a counterpart open to rational exchange.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

### [NEGO:NOMIRROR-GTY] Distinguish how you treat them from how they treat you
**Move:** Do not reciprocate unconstructive behavior. Mirroring reinforces what you dislike and confirms their belief that everyone behaves that way and they must protect themselves. Design your behavior to model and reward the conduct you want and give no payoff to the conduct you do not — without conceding substance to do it.
**APPLY WHEN:** hostile, sloppy or disrespectful counterparts you still want a deal with.
**AVOID WHEN:** unilateral good conduct is being harvested as weakness with no reciprocation over repeated rounds.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — normative claim.

### [NEGO:IRRATTRACE-GTY] Deal rationally with apparent irrationality
**Move:** Three steps. Stay purposive yourself regardless of their state. Question the assumption of irrationality — they may see the situation differently, hear your padded opening as unjustifiable, value things differently, or be victims of a communication failure; in most conflicts each side thinks it is reasonably saying no to what it heard demanded. Where the belief really is skewed (the fear-of-flying example: the perception is skewed, not the response to it), neither evidence nor punishment moves them, but empathic inquiry that traces the reasoning to its roots can surface a logical leap, a factual misperception or an old association they can then revise themselves. You are hunting the psychological interest behind the position.
**APPLY WHEN:** buyers whose objections do not track the economics; irrational-seeming risk aversion.
**AVOID WHEN:** the behavior is strategic rather than sincere — empathic excavation of a performance wastes the room.

**Evidence:** assertion — psychological reasoning with illustrations.

### [NEGO:HARDSOFT-GTY] Attack the problem while visibly supporting the person
**Move:** Push aggressively on the interest — this is where aggressive energy belongs — while giving the human being on the other side positive support in roughly equal measure: listen respectfully, thank them for their effort, state your concern for their basic needs, refuse to blame them personally. The deliberate inconsistency is the mechanism: it pulls them toward dissociating from the problem and joining you against it.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterparty is personally decent but institutionally opposed; hard commercial pushback without relationship damage.
**AVOID WHEN:** either half is delivered alone — support-only or attack-only is stated as insufficient; and when they read warmth as an opening to press harder.

### [NEGO:APPRECIATE-GTY] Acknowledge what they did for you before raising the problem
**Move:** Open a difficult conversation by explicitly naming what the other person has done for you; this separates the relationship from the merits, defuses the threat to their self-image, and creates a small stake in continuing to deserve the approval.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-standing relationship; escalation with an existing customer or partner.
**AVOID WHEN:** the praise is not true — hollow appreciation reads as setup.

**Stage D — Communication mechanics**

### [NEGO:ACTIVELISTEN-GTY] Steelman them, then answer
**Move:** Make listening — not composing your rebuttal — the task: interrupt periodically to check understanding, ask them to spell out ambiguities. Then restate their position in its strongest, most positive form, including their fear about your motives, ask what you missed, confirm you have it right, and only then raise the problems you see. Once they know you understood, they cannot dismiss your disagreement as ignorance of their situation.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery; objection handling; the counterparty is repeating themselves or escalating; any point where they suspect you have not grasped their constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a formula — mirroring without genuinely being able to state their case is transparent; when time is short and they want a number.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — being heard called the cheapest concession available; Turnbull case as the worked script.

### [NEGO:ISTATE-GTY] Describe impact on you, not their motives
**Move:** Convert any characterization of them into a statement of effect on you — a felt letdown instead of an accusation of a broken promise. A claim about your own experience is hard to dispute; a claim about their intent invites denial or anger.
**APPLY WHEN:** raising a breach, a missed commitment, or an unfair term.
**AVOID WHEN:** a factual record of what they did is what the moment requires (audits, contractual notice).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from defensiveness mechanics, no source given.

### [NEGO:PURPOSE-GTY] Say it only if you know what it buys
**Move:** Before any significant statement, decide what you are trying to communicate or learn and what that information will do. Over-communication — especially disclosing how flexible you are — can make agreement harder than silence.
**APPLY WHEN:** pricing conversations; any moment you are tempted to volunteer your flexibility; high-emotion exchanges where some thoughts should stay unsaid.
**AVOID WHEN:** it slides into the concealment the same book condemns in positional bargainers — this move sits in tension with the method's transparency claim.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise

### [NEGO:CORRECTME-GTY] "Please correct me if I'm wrong"
**Move:** Present your version of the facts as possibly inaccurate and invite correction, making the two of you colleagues establishing facts rather than opponents asserting them. Doubles as insurance: if you are wrong you lose no face, and they keep trusting your other statements.
**APPLY WHEN:** opening a confrontational conversation; raising a billing, contract or compliance discrepancy.
**AVOID WHEN:** the facts are certain and the stakes require a firm record.

### [NEGO:FACTASQ-GTY] Deliver facts as questions, and offload the sharpest one
**Move:** Turn each damaging fact into a question they can verify, accept or correct, so the same information arrives without a verdict attached; attribute the most pointed question to an absent third party (a partner, a colleague) to lower the heat further.
**APPLY WHEN:** you must surface a violation, a broken promise or an unflattering comparison.
**AVOID WHEN:** the question format reads as sarcasm or entrapment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull's rent-control question sequence.

### [NEGO:QSILENCE-GTY] Questions instead of statements, then silence
**Move:** Convert assertions into questions — statements generate resistance, questions generate answers and offer nothing to attack. After an unreasonable proposal, an unjustified attack, or a non-answer to an honest question, say nothing at all and wait; the discomfort of silence tends to produce either the answer or a new suggestion. Do not rescue them with a follow-up question.
**APPLY WHEN:** they dodge a direct question about a number or a decision path; an unreasonable demand has just landed.
**AVOID WHEN:** rapport is fragile, or the channel is asynchronous and silence reads as disengagement.

### [NEGO:LISTENPOWER-GTY] Transparent thinking, including your ambivalence
**Move:** Share your actual reasoning rather than a bare verdict — a purchaser who likes a bid but fears a new firm cannot handle peak volume should say both, because "no thank you" leaves the bidder no chance to address the real concern and leaves both worse off. Pair it with inquiry: take a refusal seriously enough to trace what sits behind it.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled objections that do not respond to more information; discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** transparency about your ambivalence hands a hard bargainer a lever on a purely distributive term.

### [NEGO:FORWARD-GTY] Convert cause-talk into purpose-talk
**Move:** Notice when an exchange has become a ritual of scoring points about the past and switch the question from why it happened to who should do what tomorrow. Talk about the destination, not last quarter's overrun or yesterday's shortfall.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-mortem-turned-blame calls; renewal after failures; any argument neither side is actually trying to win.
**AVOID WHEN:** root-cause work is genuinely required, or accountability must be established before moving on.

**Stage E — Interests: discovery and assertion**

### [NEGO:WHY-GTY] Ask why behind each stated position
**Move:** Take each position they have taken and ask what need, fear, hope or constraint made it the obvious choice — first by reasoning it out yourself, then by asking them directly, making clear you want the underlying concern, not a defense of the position.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery; any hard term stated as non-negotiable (contract length, payment schedule, seat count).
**AVOID WHEN:** the question reads as an audit of their authority; when trust is too low for an honest answer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the librarian/window story (Mary Parker Follett) and Camp David, where security vs sovereignty replaced a map-splitting fight.

### [NEGO:WHYNOT-GTY] Build their currently-perceived-choice balance sheet
**Move:** Name whose decision you need, write the decision as they think you are asking for it, then list in their voice the consequences of yes and of no. Score it against a checklist: personal political cost, colleagues' reaction, short vs long term, economic/legal/political consequences, effect on outside audiences, precedent, whether it forecloses something better, consistency with their principles, whether they can do it later. Aim for a human reading, not arithmetic.
**APPLY WHEN:** a stakeholder keeps not-deciding; the champion cannot get sign-off; a "no" you cannot explain.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real information about their internal politics and would be inventing the sheet.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked out for a hypothetical Iranian student leader during the 1980 hostage crisis, showing why holding was locally rational.

### [NEGO:MULTIINT-GTY] Write and rank the interest list; never a single "them"
**Move:** List both sides' interests as they occur to you, rank by estimated importance, and update as you learn — the list doubles as an idea generator. Refuse to collapse the other side into one actor: enumerate the individuals and factions, their constituencies (owner, board, client, spouse, colleagues) and where their interests diverge from each other.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals; deals where the negotiator's stated reasoning is obviously not their own.
**AVOID WHEN:** the list becomes a substitute for asking them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a baseball GM holding an unjustifiable salary line under secret instructions from owners in financial trouble.

### [NEGO:BASICNEEDS-GTY] Probe the five basic human needs under a money number
**Move:** When the negotiation looks purely monetary, test the number against five bedrock needs: security, economic well-being, belonging, recognition, control over one's own life. Where the money is a proxy for security or recognition, satisfy those another way and the number becomes negotiable.
**APPLY WHEN:** price fights where their stance seems disproportionate; discount demands tied to fairness or respect claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** it becomes a pretext for not moving on price when price genuinely is the issue.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the US/Mexico natural-gas case, where price pressure insulted a counterparty who needed to be treated as an equal and the gas was burned off rather than sold cheaper.

### [NEGO:ALIVE-GTY] Make your interest concrete enough to feel
**Move:** State your interest with specifics — time, place, magnitude, the actual incident — rather than as a general grievance, since detail is both credible and forceful. Assert the seriousness firmly, invite them to correct you if wrong (silence then reads as acceptance), and legitimize by asking them to imagine themselves in your position.
**APPLY WHEN:** justifying a hard requirement, a deadline, a security or budget constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** the specificity implies their interests are illegitimate; where sharing detail exposes information you should hold.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the neighborhood/construction-company illustration (a named child, a specific truck, a specific morning).

### [NEGO:ACKINT-GTY] Acknowledge their interests as part of the problem
**Move:** Before asserting yours, summarize their interests back, ask whether you have them right and whether anything is missing, and state explicitly that their interests are part of what any solution must handle — easiest where an interest is genuinely shared.
**APPLY WHEN:** the start of any negotiation on terms; whenever they act as if you are indifferent to their constraints.
**AVOID WHEN:** the summary is a rhetorical prelude you then ignore.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the reciprocity of feeling understood.

### [NEGO:REASONSFIRST-GTY] Reasoning first, proposal last
**Move:** Lay out the problem, criteria and evidence first and state the proposal only afterwards. Leading with the demand guarantees they stop listening and start assembling counter-arguments; principles delivered after the number read as post-hoc justification for an arbitrary position. Do the homework that produces quantified standards before the meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** presenting a price increase, scope change, refund or uplift request — any ask likely to trigger objection.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer explicitly wants the number first and will disengage during a preamble.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull obtained from the Rent Control Board that roughly $30,000 of improvements would be needed to justify the $268 increase.

### [NEGO:TIMEPROBE-GTY] Break a deadlock with timeline and consequence questions
**Move:** When both sides have stated incompatible positions, stop arguing the positions and ask each side two factual questions: when do you actually need this, and what specifically goes wrong for you if you do what they are demanding right now. Look for a sequencing solution where the timelines do not in fact collide.
**APPLY WHEN:** hard deadlock over an either/or; both sides' real constraints are about timing or scope, not the headline term.
**AVOID WHEN:** the conflict is genuinely simultaneous and zero-sum; interrogating under high emotion before venting has happened.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise

### [NEGO:ILLUSTRATIVE-GTY] Use illustrative specificity instead of an opening position
**Move:** Ask yourself what exactly you would want them to agree to if they said yes tomorrow, then present that as one illustration of something that would meet your interest — held explicitly as illustrative, with more than one option in mind. You get the anchoring and clarity benefits of an opening position without committing your ego to it.
**APPLY WHEN:** you must put a number or structure on the table but do not want it to become a defended position.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty treats any number as a binding anchor; procurement processes that log first offers as commitments.

**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — sports-agent illustration pairing a salary figure with a contract length, each tied to a named interest (worth, job security).

### [NEGO:ONESHOES-GTY] Negotiate with a person, not an institution
**Move:** Stop trying to persuade "the company" or "the committee". Pick one human — usually your counterpart — and work the problem from their seat, including what they must say internally. Reframe your job as strengthening their hand and supplying the arguments they need.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals, procurement, any champion-led enterprise cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** your contact has no path to the real decision-maker — then you are arming the wrong person.

**Stage F — Inventing options**

### [NEGO:BRAINSEP-GTY] Separate inventing from deciding (structured brainstorm)
**Move:** Run a dedicated invention session away from the negotiating table — define what you want to walk out with, 5–8 participants, different room, time and dress from a normal meeting, a facilitator, everyone seated side by side facing a board rather than each other, criticism outlawed, all ideas written up in full view. Afterwards star promising ideas, improve them ("what I like best about that is… might it be even better if…"), then schedule a separate decision meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deal has multiple movable variables; you are stuck on one axis; pre-call prep on a complex account.
**AVOID WHEN:** simple one-variable transaction; no time before the meeting; a group that will treat any idea aired as a commitment.

### [NEGO:JOINTBRAIN-GTY] Brainstorm with the other side, protected
**Move:** Hold a joint invention session explicitly labelled as *not* a negotiating session and off the record. Protect yourself by always advancing at least two alternatives at once and by tabling options you obviously do not want, so everything that follows reads as a possibility rather than a proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** the relationship survives the deal; both sides are stuck; you need to educate them about your constraints.
**AVOID WHEN:** confidential pricing or your floor could leak; the counterparty logs everything as an offer; adversarial or manipulative counterpart.

### [NEGO:CIRCLE-GTY] Circle Chart — shuttle specific↔general
**Move:** Take one workable idea and climb four rungs: the concrete problem → descriptive diagnosis in general terms → general prescriptive approaches → new specific actions. From each general theory generate fresh concrete moves; one good option becomes a factory for others.
**APPLY WHEN:** you already have one decent option and want a family of variants; joint problem framing.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty needs a decision now and abstraction reads as stalling.

### [NEGO:EXPERTLENS-GTY] Look through the eyes of different experts
**Move:** Re-diagnose the deal as a banker, tax expert, labour leader, real-estate speculator, economist, stockbroker or inventor would; each profession yields its own diagnosis, approach and action. Combinable with the Circle Chart.
**APPLY WHEN:** solo prep with no brainstorm group available; the option list has gone stale.
**AVOID WHEN:** purely transactional purchase where only price moves.

### [NEGO:AGRSTRENGTH-GTY] Invent agreements of different strengths
**Move:** Keep weaker fallback shapes on hand along the ladder substantive→procedural, permanent→provisional, comprehensive→partial, final→in-principle, unconditional→contingent, binding→nonbinding, first-order→second-order. If you cannot agree on substance, agree on procedure; at minimum reach second-order agreement — a shared map of exactly where you disagree.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deal is stalling and a full close is out of reach this quarter; pilot or LOI territory.
**AVOID WHEN:** a weak form would cannibalise the strong one you could still get; internal approval treats a pilot as the ceiling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a checklist of agreement types.

### [NEGO:SCOPEVARY-GTY] Change the scope — fractionate or enlarge
**Move:** Shrink the unit (edit one chapter for $300 and see how it goes; fewer parties, narrower subject, one region, limited term) or enlarge it to sweeten the pot by bringing in new subject matter or a new party with resources.
**APPLY WHEN:** budget or trust is the blocker (shrink); the pie is genuinely too small to split (enlarge).
**AVOID WHEN:** slicing destroys the value of the whole; enlarging pulls in stakeholders who add veto power.

### [NEGO:SHAREDGOAL-GTY] Convert latent shared interests into an explicit joint goal
**Move:** Interrogate the deal for latent common ground — do we share an interest in the relationship, what future cooperation is at stake, what does breakdown cost each of us, is there a common principle such as a fair price? Then make it concrete and future-oriented as a joint target, so the concession you want reads as an action toward *our* goal rather than a give from them.
**APPLY WHEN:** a price fight where both sides also depend on a continuing relationship; renewal or expansion.
**AVOID WHEN:** a pure one-shot transaction with a counterparty you will never see again; shared-goal talk reads as manipulation if you cannot name a real one.

### [NEGO:DOVETAIL-GTY] Dovetail differing interests
**Move:** Hunt asymmetries and trade across them — differences in interests (form vs substance, symbolic vs practical, precedent vs this case, prestige vs results, near vs distant future, relationship vs ad-hoc result), in beliefs (both confident → agree an arbitrator or a vote), in time discounting (installments), in forecasts (low base plus big performance bonus), and in risk aversion (low rates while their risk is high, higher after payback). Rule of thumb: find items of low cost to you and high benefit to them, and vice versa.
**APPLY WHEN:** the two sides forecast the future differently or value timing and risk differently — earn-outs, ramps, milestone pricing.
**AVOID WHEN:** a contingent structure will be gamed or is unverifiable; buyer procurement forbids variable terms.

### [NEGO:PREFPAIRS-GTY] Ask which they prefer, not whether it is acceptable
**Move:** Build two or more packages that are equally acceptable to you and ask which is *preferable*. Take the winner, generate two new variants of it, ask again — iterate until no further joint gains appear. Nobody has to decide anything along the way.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-negotiation shaping of terms; the buyer will not state priorities directly.
**AVOID WHEN:** the variants are not truly equal for you (you will be traded down); too many rounds exhaust a transactional buyer.

### [NEGO:CRITICSIM-GTY] Simulate their worst critic
**Move:** Write a sentence or two of what the counterparty's most damaging internal critic would say if they accepted your proposal — then write their defence. Redesign the option until the defence holds.
**APPLY WHEN:** the champion faces a hostile committee, procurement or a board; before sending a proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** solo owner-buyer with no constituency to answer to.

**Stage G — Legitimacy and objective criteria**

### [NEGO:PREPSTANDARDS-GTY] Prepare a menu of fair standards in advance
**Move:** Before the meeting, list several independent criteria that could decide the number and work out how each applies to your case (for a written-off car: original cost less depreciation, resale value, blue-book, replacement cost, likely court award). Prefer standards that are independent of will, legitimate, practical, and pass the reciprocity test — would they accept the same standard applied against them?
**APPLY WHEN:** any price, scope or term dispute; procurement benchmarking; renewal uplift fights.
**AVOID WHEN:** no credible external standard exists for your category — inventing one invites attack.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core Harvard method, illustrated across insurance, construction and real estate.

### [NEGO:JOINTSEARCH-GTY] Frame the issue as a joint search for the standard
**Move:** Open by naming the obvious conflict and converting it into a shared task: you want high, I want low, so let us work out what a fair number would be — which standards are relevant here? Propose one or two yourself, then invite theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** the first substantive pricing conversation; an anchoring war is starting.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty is running a scripted hard-bargaining play and will simply pocket your standard.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — one of three basic points, with a house-buying script.

### [NEGO:WHATSTHEORY-GTY] "How did you arrive at that figure?"
**Move:** When they state a number, neither accept nor reject it — ask for the theory behind it, treating them as though they too are looking for a fair number. Then use their stated standard as the lever: apply it consistently to the comparables they did not mention.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer states a budget cap, a discount demand, or a competitor's price.
**AVOID WHEN:** the number is arbitrary and they know it — expect "that's just our policy" and escalate to NOYIELD-GTY.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Tom/insurance-adjuster dialogue.

### [NEGO:PRINCIPLEFIRST-GTY] Agree on the principle before the terms
**Move:** Settle which standard applies before discussing any specific figure. Once they have named a standard, deferring to it later is an act of consistency rather than surrender, which is what makes it acceptable to them.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-round negotiation; committee buyers who need a defensible rationale.
**AVOID WHEN:** a transactional buyer wants a number now and reads process talk as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement with the comparable-houses script.

### [NEGO:FAIRPROC-GTY] Use a fair procedure when no fair standard settles it
**Move:** When substance will not yield, switch to procedure: one cuts and the other chooses (agree the shape of the arrangement before deciding who takes which role); take turns; draw lots; delegate to an expert, mediator or arbitrator; explore trade-offs across issues. A variant is last-best-offer arbitration, where the arbitrator must pick one side's final number, which pressures both toward reasonableness. Split the difference only between figures that are each backed by legitimate independent standards — splitting between arbitrary figures just produces an arbitrary result.
**APPLY WHEN:** allocation deadlocks, multi-party splits, or a last gap on a single term where both positions are defensible.
**AVOID WHEN:** used early — it converts a principled negotiation back into horse-trading; also when you would lose a coin flip you could have won on merits, and note last-best-offer can force an arbitrator to choose between two extremes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel/expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Law of the Sea site allocation (a company proposes two sites, the Enterprise picks one); baseball's last-best-offer arbitration reported as producing more settlements, source unnamed → treat that claim as assertion.

### [NEGO:OPENJUDGE-GTY] Reason and be open to reason
**Move:** Behave like a judge who happens to be predisposed to one side: insist the outcome rest on objective criteria, but not necessarily on *your* criterion. When two legitimate standards collide, look for a meta-basis (which has been used by these parties before, which is more widely applied), or split between the two results — the outcome is still independent of will. If you still cannot agree, ask a mutually respected third party to pick which standard is most appropriate, not to decide the substance.
**APPLY WHEN:** both sides have defensible benchmarks; deadlock over whose comparable set counts.
**AVOID WHEN:** "it's a matter of principle" is being used as an ideological battle cry — the counterfeit of this move, which locks people in harder.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement plus the MIT deep-seabed model illustration.

### [NEGO:NOYIELD-GTY] Yield to principle, never to pressure
**Move:** Treat bribes, threats, manipulative appeals to trust and flat refusals identically: invite them to state their reasoning, restate the criteria you think apply, and refuse to move on any other basis. Explicitly de-couple unrelated sweeteners — a job for a relative has nothing to do with how deep the foundation must be; trust is a separate matter. Never enter with a rigid bottom line; commit to interests and to the standards that will decide the number.
**APPLY WHEN:** last-minute demands, take-it-or-leave-it framings, relationship guilt-trips.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deal has real time-value to you exceeding the concession at stake — the book does not price the delay this move buys.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — contractor and insurance dialogues; the claim that the principled side "generally has an edge" is asserted, not measured.

### [NEGO:STANDOFFTEST-GTY] Test a nonnegotiable position before walking
**Move:** If they will not budge and will not give a basis, first check whether some standard you overlooked actually makes their offer fair — if so, accept on that basis. If not, weigh what accepting an unjustified position gains you against your best alternative, and against the reputational value of walking as someone who does not reward arbitrary positions.
**APPLY WHEN:** fixed-price or policy-locked counterparties; the end of a stalled cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no alternative developed — then the walk is theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning presented directly.

### [NEGO:STDWEIGH-GTY] Weigh competing fairness standards on three criteria
**Move:** When both sides bring different standards, do not fight for agreement on which is "best". Rank by directness to the point, breadth of acceptance, and immediacy in time, place and circumstance. Where that is not obvious, examine how each standard developed — is the market in transition, are two traditions converging?
**APPLY WHEN:** price and terms justification battles, benchmark wars, "your competitor charges X".
**AVOID WHEN:** no external benchmarks exist for a one-of-a-kind item — shift to interests and creative options.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus illustrations (internet pricing undercutting distributor margins; a young lawyer countering a forty-year-old $24,000 starting salary with inflation and current comparable Manhattan firms rather than Dayton or Des Moines figures).

### [NEGO:LEGITSHIELD-GTY] Standards as both sword and shield
**Move:** Use external legitimacy in two directions: as a sword to persuade, and as a shield to resist arbitrary pressure to concede — the price is firm because it is what a named comparable buyer paid last week, with documentation available. Frame your ask as no more and no less than what is paid for comparable work, and put the burden on them to show why this case differs.
**APPLY WHEN:** discount pressure; price defense; scope creep.
**AVOID WHEN:** your comparable is weak or cherry-picked and will be checked.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued by analogy to legal precedent, with scripts; convincing them you ask only for what is fair is called one of the most powerful arguments available.

### [NEGO:LEGITPRECEDENT-GTY] Dress the ask in their own precedent
**Move:** Shape the proposal so it looks fair, legal and honourable to them, and hunt for a decision or statement they already made in a comparable case; base your request on it, leaning on their desire to be consistent.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer must justify the deal upward; policy or "we don't do that" objections.
**AVOID WHEN:** the precedent you cite is embarrassing to them — consistency pressure then becomes a face threat.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — asserted from general observations about legitimacy.

**Stage H — Alternatives, leverage and power**

### [NEGO:BATNA-GTY] Judge every offer against your BATNA, not a bottom line
**Move:** Replace "the lowest I will accept" with "what will I actually do if no deal happens by date X?" — name the concrete alternative, then measure each proposal against it. Bottom lines are rejected outright: they are set arbitrarily and usually too high, they close your ears to what you learn at the table, and they suppress imaginative multi-variable packages. Because a BATNA is a real alternative rather than a number, it stays compatible with creative repackaging.
**APPLY WHEN:** every deal, before the first pricing conversation; discount pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** never avoided, but do not confuse it with a walk-away script — it is a measuring instrument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the book's central framework, taught and applied across Harvard Negotiation Project casework; no dataset.

### [NEGO:BATNADEV-GTY] Develop the BATNA in three operations
**Move:** (1) Invent a list of everything you might do if no agreement is reached. (2) Convert the most promising into real, executable alternatives — turn "maybe Chicago" into an actual Chicago offer, "we could strike" into an authorised strike vote. (3) Tentatively select the best one. Doing this raises your minimum, not just measures it.
**APPLY WHEN:** entering an asymmetric negotiation; pipeline of one; renewal where the incumbent knows you cannot switch.
**AVOID WHEN:** developing the alternative costs more than the concession at stake.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a small town vs a corporation; union strike-authorisation example.

### [NEGO:TRIPWIRE-GTY] Set a trip wire above the BATNA
**Move:** Identify one distinctly imperfect package that is still better than your BATNA, and treat crossing it as a mandatory pause to re-examine. Also a clean way to cap an agent's authority, and it leaves reserve room for a mediator to work with.
**APPLY WHEN:** delegating to a rep or broker; long negotiations where fatigue erodes judgement.
**AVOID WHEN:** it hardens into a de-facto bottom line and re-imports the rigidity the method rejects.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as a supplementary test.

### [NEGO:BATNADISCLOSE-GTY] Disclose your BATNA selectively
**Move:** Reveal the alternative only when it is genuinely strong or when they underestimate it (another buyer literally waiting); stay silent when your true alternative is weaker than they imagine.
**APPLY WHEN:** competitive deals; multiple offers in hand.
**AVOID WHEN:** your alternative is weak — disclosure then hands them the deal; bluffing about it is not sanctioned.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a judgement call; Turnbull's non-disclosure is the illustration.

### [NEGO:THEIRBATNA-GTY] Estimate — and if necessary change — their BATNA
**Move:** Work out what they do without you; correct their overestimates by helping them think through whether the alternatives are realistic, since people aggregate alternatives they cannot actually have simultaneously. If their alternative is so good they see no reason to negotiate on merits, act to change it. If both alternatives are strong, the best outcome may be to discover quickly and amicably that there is no deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer claims "we'll just build it internally" or "we'll do nothing"; incumbent displacement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the only way to worsen their alternative is litigation or coercion — the book's own example (filing suit to revoke a construction permit) burns the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — community group vs power plant; job-interview and Mumbai brass-pot vendor illustrations.

### [NEGO:MICROBATNA-GTY] Prepare a micro-BATNA and a drafted exit line
**Move:** Beyond the overall BATNA, prepare the meeting-level one: if no agreement is reached *at this meeting*, what is the best outcome? Draft the exit line in advance — thank them for the exchange, and state that if you decide to proceed you will return, possibly with a fresh proposal. Communicating a strong BATNA is persuasive provided they believe it.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-meeting cycles; any meeting that could end without a decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** the exit line is used as a veiled threat — see WARNVSTHREAT-GTY.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — practice advice (a 20% competing salary offer as the script).

### [NEGO:BATNASHIFT-GTY] Legitimately worsen their BATNA
**Move:** Power comes from both improving your alternatives and lowering their estimate of theirs, and the second can sometimes be done legitimately. The book's example: a son who would not mow the lawn for money had a BATNA of taking cash from a wallet left out each weekend; removing the wallet and making disapproval explicit changed the alternative. Flagged candidly as dual-use — the same lever can coerce or exploit.
**APPLY WHEN:** the counterpart's stated alternative is soft, unpriced, or based on a resource you control.
**AVOID WHEN:** the only available means are coercive or deceptive — the tactic then fails the book's own reciprocity and legitimacy test.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a domestic example, explicitly flagged as dual-use.

### [NEGO:POWERBLEND-GTY] Use the power sources in harmony, not one at a time
**Move:** Do not lead with your single strongest lever. Confronting them with a strong BATNA and a walk-away threat undercuts the persuasive force of your fairness arguments. If you communicate a BATNA, do it in a way that respects the relationship, keeps two-way communication open, underscores the legitimacy of your last offer, and shows how the offer serves their interests. The seven sources — relationship, interests, options, legitimacy, BATNA, commitment, communication — compound. Also adapt the method to your own voice: as borrowed clothing it loses force.
**APPLY WHEN:** any high-leverage moment where you are tempted to play the strongest card alone.
**AVOID WHEN:** n/a — but the harmony principle assumes time and channel enough for a layered argument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — synthesis statement.

**Stage I — When they won't play: jujitsu and hard bargainers**

### [NEGO:LOOKBEHIND-GTY] Don't attack their position — mine it
**Move:** Neither reject nor accept their stated position; treat it as one option, assume it is a genuine attempt to solve the problem, and ask how it addresses each side's concerns, what trade-offs sit behind it, and what principle makes it fair. Then ask hypothetically what would actually happen if you accepted it as stated.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer opens with an ultimatum, a fixed discount demand or a non-negotiable term.
**AVOID WHEN:** the position is a scripted opening designed to consume your time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 1970 Nasser interview: asked what would happen if Golda Meir publicly promised unconditional withdrawal, Nasser laughed at his own demand's unworkability.

### [NEGO:INVITECRIT-GTY] Don't defend your proposal — invite criticism and advice
**Move:** Ask what is wrong with it rather than whether they accept it; ask which of their concerns it fails to cover; ask what they would do in your position, handing them your half of the problem. Rework the proposal in front of them using what the criticism reveals.
**APPLY WHEN:** proposal or pricing pushback; a champion who keeps relaying objections.
**AVOID WHEN:** invited criticism becomes a free list of demands with nothing offered back — the move assumes reciprocal good faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — teacher and school-board scripts, no data.

### [NEGO:RECAST-GTY] Recast a personal attack as an attack on the problem
**Move:** When attacked personally, do not defend or counterattack: let them finish, show you heard the concern underneath, then restate their attack as a shared concern about the problem and ask what you can both do about it now.
**APPLY WHEN:** blame, accusations of bad faith, emotional escalation on a call.
**AVOID WHEN:** the attack is a deliberate, repeated tactic rather than a vented emotion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — strike / children's-education script.

### [NEGO:ONETEXT-GTY] Run the one-text procedure
**Move:** Have a third party (or yourself, acting as mediator) interview both sides about interests rather than positions, publish a list of needs for criticism, then produce a deliberately imperfect draft and ask only what is wrong with it. Iterate through successive drafts, never asking anyone to concede, until the drafter judges it cannot be improved — then recommend it once, as a single yes/no, with each side's yes able to be contingent on the other's. It requires nobody's consent to start.
**APPLY WHEN:** both sides are dug in; multi-party or multi-stakeholder deals; you personally care more about closing than about the exact terms (a rep between customer and production, a manager between two subordinates).
**AVOID WHEN:** a third party will treat it as position-clarification and press for concessions — that version deepens attachment instead of dissolving it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Camp David 1978 (13 days, roughly 23 drafts, accepted by both sides); Law of the Sea prototype by Tommy Koh of Singapore; parts of the South African constitutional negotiations.

### [NEGO:FAIRSTANCE-GTY] Announce that fairness, not money, is the standard you will move on
**Move:** State plainly what would satisfy you in principle (that you paid no more than you should have), commit to sticking to that basis, and simultaneously signal you are persuadable within it — leaving them the reasoned route as the only route to their own interest. Make explicit that you are not turning this into a contest of who can do what to whom, and name the mutual costs of that route.
**APPLY WHEN:** they accuse you of leveraging a hostage position; disputes over refunds, overcharges, penalties.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no principled basis and are in fact only after the money.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull case.

### [NEGO:TRUSTAPART-GTY] "Trust is a separate issue" — and use "and", not "but"
**Move:** When trust is invoked to corner you, reaffirm the appreciation *and* restate the principle, deliberately avoiding "but" (which erases the first clause and forces an either/or) — then hand the question back by asking what considerations they think should decide the matter. Keep the framing impersonal: ask whether too much was paid, never whether they cheated you.
**APPLY WHEN:** "don't you trust me?", loyalty appeals, relationship guilt used to skip verification.
**AVOID WHEN:** never within this frame — but note it presumes they will re-engage on reasons.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull case; the "great eraser" point about "but" is asserted.

### [NEGO:PAUSE-GTY] "Let me get back to you" — a pre-planned exit
**Move:** Never take an important decision at the table; come in with a credible, decisive-sounding reason to leave (consulting your constituency, checking facts) and set the next time before you go. Distance restores perspective and resolve, so you return soft on the person and firm on the problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** pressure to sign now, on-the-spot discount demands, long draining sessions.
**AVOID WHEN:** the exit reads as inability to decide, or as a lack of authority you had claimed to have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — general negotiator practice, illustrated by Turnbull.

### [NEGO:REFRAME-GTY] Reframe a positional number into interests, options, standards or BATNA
**Move:** Against a flat positional number, pick a reframe rather than a counter-number. To interests: acknowledge it is their position, note the gap from market, ask them to explain the interest behind it (cash-flow crisis?). To options: treat their figure as one option among several including yours, then propose brainstorming jointly attractive ones. To standards: presume they had good reasons and ask how they arrived at that number — why not zero, why not double — then present the market figure and ask why less. To BATNA: acknowledge it is their call, note someone else might accept it, and ask whether an agreement that works for both is actually possible here. A softer version opens with a question they cannot answer "no" to (does your company adhere to industry safety standards?) and then applies competitor-derived standards.
**APPLY WHEN:** the conversation has collapsed into positional haggling.
**AVOID WHEN:** overuse makes you look evasive on the number; also weak against a counterpart who simply repeats the position.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — referenced to the wider reframing literature, notably Ury, *Getting Past No* (1991/1993).

### [NEGO:WALKOUT-GTY] Principled walkout on legitimate grounds
**Move:** As the last procedural resort, state that the process as run cannot produce a result, leave a contact route open, and name what you will do instead (the BATNA path). Keep it non-punitive and reversible: they are likely to call back if they actually want a deal and if your ground for leaving is clearly legitimate — proven deception about facts or about authority.
**APPLY WHEN:** documented misrepresentation, or repeated escalation after agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** your BATNA is weak or unexamined; used as theater it becomes just another lock-in and costs credibility on return.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — illustrative scripts, not measured outcomes.

### [NEGO:VERIFY-GTY] Make the deal independent of trust
**Move:** Do not extend trust you have no basis for, and do not frame verification as an accusation. Normalize it the way credit checks are normalized: routine checking of factual assertions, applied to both sides. Build verification into the process so that lying stops paying.
**APPLY WHEN:** unverified claims about volumes, incumbent pricing, competing bids, budget ceilings.
**AVOID WHEN:** verification cost exceeds the exposure, or a long verified track record makes demanding proof a signal of bad faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle, no data.

### [NEGO:AUTHCHECK-GTY] Ask about authority before any give-and-take
**Move:** Early, ask plainly how much authority the counterpart holds. If the answer is vague, either get to a real decision-maker or explicitly reserve equal freedom to reopen anything. If they suddenly reveal an approver after you thought you had a deal, insist on symmetry: treat the text as a joint draft neither side is bound by, and reserve your own overnight review. Pre-empt the ploy by establishing early that nothing is agreed until everything is agreed.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals, procurement, insurance/legal/agency counterparts, any "second bite at the apple" pattern.
**AVOID WHEN:** the champion genuinely has no authority and the question embarrasses them — then the move becomes co-planning the approval path rather than an authority audit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated pattern from the authors' advisory and mediation work.

### [NEGO:CONTINGENT-GTY] Turn their certainty into a compliance clause
**Move:** When you doubt they will perform but they insist they will, take the protestation at face value and price it into the structure: escalate their claimed certainty, then propose a contingency that costs them nothing if they are right. The divorce example: if two child-support payments are missed, the equity in the house transfers, net of what was already paid. Hard to refuse without admitting the doubt.
**APPLY WHEN:** performance risk, unproven vendor or buyer, promised volumes, migration commitments, payment reliability.
**AVOID WHEN:** the contingency is unenforceable or so punitive it reads as a trap; it also fails where the counterpart will sign anything they do not intend to honor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single worked legal scenario.

### [NEGO:CANDIDGAP-GTY] Candid non-disclosure (less than full disclosure ≠ deception)
**Move:** Good faith does not require revealing your reservation point. When asked for your maximum, decline explicitly and route the question elsewhere: name the temptation to mislead, and propose disclosing to a trusted third party who can report only whether a zone of agreement exists.
**APPLY WHEN:** "what's your real bottom number" pressure; broker or intermediary contexts.
**AVOID WHEN:** no credible neutral is available, or the ask is routine and the refusal ritual makes you look evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — normative position of the authors.

### [NEGO:ATTACKNAME-GTY] Neutralize personal and status attacks by naming them
**Move:** Recognize the family of discomfort tactics: comments on your appearance or apparent fatigue, being made to wait, interruptions for other business, implied ignorance, refusing to listen so you repeat yourself, withheld eye contact. Recognition alone blunts most of the effect; raising it explicitly usually prevents repetition. Question the behavior, not the person.
**APPLY WHEN:** status games from senior buyers or hard-bargainer procurement.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot describe the behavior without accusation, or the rudeness is habitual rather than tactical — then it is a people problem, not a procedural one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — simple student experiments on the malaise from withheld eye contact, source not named in-text; treat as thin.

### [NEGO:GOODBAD-GTY] Same principle question to both good guy and bad guy
**Move:** When one counterpart plays hardline and the partner "rescues" you with a small concession that feels like a favor, refuse the frame: thank the reasonable one and ask him the identical question you asked the hardliner — what principle makes that number fair. State you would pay the higher figure if the fairness case is made.
**APPLY WHEN:** two-person buying teams staging visible internal disagreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the internal split is real (champion vs skeptic) — treating a genuine ally as a ploy destroys your only advocate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — staged example moving $80,000 to $76,000; the concession is small but psychologically oversized.

### [NEGO:WARNVSTHREAT-GTY] Warnings instead of threats
**Move:** Avoid threats; they invite counter-threats and turn the question from "is this a good decision?" into "do we cave to pressure?" Instead communicate consequences that follow independently of your will (media interest, regulatory timing, market facts), or state your own planned protective actions framed as safeguarding your interests rather than punishing theirs — e.g. sharing the draft of a release you would issue if the contract lapses. If asked whether that is a threat, answer honestly and invite them to propose a better way for you to protect your interests.
**APPLY WHEN:** renewals, contract lapses, escalation to alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** the "warning" is a disguised threat — they will hear the disguise; also weak where the consequence is obviously within your discretion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus illustrative scripts.

### [NEGO:THREATDEFUSE-GTY] Break the communication channel a threat needs
**Move:** Threats require credible transmission, so attack transmission. Ignore them; treat them as unauthorized, hasty or irrelevant; make issuing them costly; use them against the sender publicly ("they resort to threats because the case is weak"); or answer on principle — you have countermoves prepared but prefer first to agree that threats are not productive, and your reputation is built on responding only to reason.
**APPLY WHEN:** ultimatums, threatened churn, escalation, public complaint.
**AVOID WHEN:** the threat is a real and imminent commercial fact (they truly have another vendor signed) — ignoring it then wastes the remaining time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a mediated coal-mine case where false bomb threats dropped sharply once the receptionist opened every call by announcing recording and asking for the caller's number.

### [NEGO:REFUSALPROBE-GTY] When they refuse to negotiate at all
**Move:** Read the refusal (or the precondition list) as a bargaining chip, not a wall. Communicate directly or via third parties; do not attack the refusal — probe the interest behind it: fear of conferring status, internal criticism for looking soft, fragile internal unity, or simple disbelief that agreement is possible. Offer procedural options: third-party channels, letters, private intermediaries, journalists. Then apply principle — would they want you to set preconditions, would they want others to refuse them?
**APPLY WHEN:** ghosting, "we're not evaluating vendors", procurement gatekeeping, "see you in court".
**AVOID WHEN:** their BATNA is genuinely strong and negotiating costs them real option value — persistence then reads as desperation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — diplomatic case narrative from the 1979–81 Tehran hostage crisis.

### [NEGO:EXTREMEJUST-GTY] Demand principled justification of an extreme opening
**Move:** Against an anchor obviously far off market, do not counter-anchor. Name the tactic and keep asking for the principled basis until the position looks absurd to them too. Note the tactic's costs to its user: an opening both sides know will be abandoned destroys credibility and can kill the deal if the target concludes the counterpart is not serious.
**APPLY WHEN:** lowball procurement anchors, "your competitor quoted half".
**AVOID WHEN:** in an established haggling ritual where refusing to counter forfeits range; it also fails against a counterpart happy to defend an absurd position indefinitely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — worked illustration ($175,000 offered for a house apparently worth $300,000).

### [NEGO:ESCALPAUSE-GTY] Answer escalating demands with a named pause
**Move:** When each concession you make triggers a new demand or a reopened settled issue ("agreed, but one small problem"), name the pattern explicitly and then take a break to decide whether and on what basis to continue. The pause blocks an impulsive concession and signals you have registered the conduct as serious.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage redlines that keep growing; repeated "one last thing" from a buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** the new items are genuinely newly discovered by their side (new stakeholder, new compliance finding).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Malta's premier extracting a £10 million advance and guaranteed dockyard jobs from Britain in 1971.

### [NEGO:LOCKLOOSE-GTY] Deflate a lock-in without making it the issue
**Move:** Commitment tactics work only if the commitment is communicated and believed, so interfere with the message: reinterpret it as an aspiration rather than a binding pledge, joke past it, or refuse on principle — you yield to reason, not to pressure. Above all de-emphasize it so they can climb down without losing face; never make the commitment the central question.
**APPLY WHEN:** public price pledges, "the board has mandated this number", published policy positions.
**AVOID WHEN:** the commitment is institutionally real (a published price list with legal or audit consequences) — treating it as bluff wastes cycles.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** external-research — invokes Thomas Schelling's commitment analysis (the steering wheel thrown from the truck); the negotiation application is the authors' assertion.

### [NEGO:HARDPARTNER-GTY] Route around the "hardhearted partner"
**Move:** When your counterpart agrees your request is reasonable but blames an absent boss or partner, do not argue with the messenger. Get their explicit agreement to the underlying principle — in writing if possible — and then, where feasible, speak directly to the named blocker.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'd love to, but legal/finance/my boss won't"; classic committee deflection.
**AVOID WHEN:** going over the champion's head is culturally fatal in that account — then convert the written principle into ammunition for the champion instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — described as the most common non-yielding tactic, no data.

### [NEGO:FADEOPP-GTY] Counter calculated delay with a fading opportunity and objective deadlines
**Move:** Name the stalling and negotiate about it, then change the payoff of waiting: create a genuine competing option, and anchor timing on external verifiable conditions — tax dates, an annual trustees meeting, contract expiry, end of a legislative session — so the deadline is credible rather than manufactured.
**APPLY WHEN:** deliberate stalling to reach a moment they think favors them.
**AVOID WHEN:** the deadline is invented — a discovered-fake deadline destroys the legitimacy the whole method rests on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — labor and M&A patterns cited from practice; waiting games flagged as high-cost for both sides, with labor negotiators routinely blowing past strike deadlines.

### [NEGO:TIOLPASS-GTY] Ignore "take it or leave it" first, then offer a face-saving exit
**Move:** A firm final choice is not inherently illegitimate (supermarket pricing works that way — it is simply not negotiation). Against a tactical ultimatum, first try not hearing it: keep talking, change the subject, introduce other solutions. If you do address it, make the cost of no-agreement concrete for them and supply a face-saving reason to move — a changed circumstance or a new element introduced since the "final" offer was stated.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage ultimatums, "this is our standard, non-negotiable".
**AVOID WHEN:** it truly is final and structurally fixed — persisting reads as not listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus a labor illustration (management's final hourly figure predating the joint productivity work).

### [NEGO:OFFERNOTTHREAT-GTY] Offers beat threats
**Move:** Instead of warning what happens if they refuse, work on the consequences of agreeing and make those better and more credible: what could they want that costs you little — credit for the final proposal, making the announcement, a face-saving framing?
**APPLY WHEN:** you have leverage but want the relationship intact; late-stage friction.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterparty reads generosity as weakness and simply pockets it — the book's own bias, assuming a rationally reciprocating counterpart.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — "offers are usually more effective" stated without a cited study.

**Stage J — Offers, drafting and closing**

### [NEGO:YESABLE-GTY] Draft the yesable proposition
**Move:** Decide whether you want words or performance — if performance, do not pad the ask for negotiating room, i.e. do not raise the fence you want jumped. Then draft actual agreement text early, simplest version first, and test it: could a single "yes" from them be sufficient, realistic and operational? Cut the number of approvals required and make implementation easy — it is easier for them to refrain from starting something than to stop what is running, and easier to stop than to launch something new.
**APPLY WHEN:** approaching close; multi-approver buying process; you keep hearing "come back with something better".
**AVOID WHEN:** discovery is incomplete — early drafting then anchors on the wrong shape.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as method; vending-machine and workplace-music illustrations only.

### [NEGO:OFFERTIMING-GTY] Time the first offer; anchor on a standard, not a number
**Move:** Do not assume moving first is best. Explore interests, options and criteria first, then make an offer that visibly reconciles what has been surfaced — it reads as a constructive step instead of a squeeze. You can still anchor early, but anchor the *approach or standard* favorable to you rather than a figure. If you are unprepared and do not know what is reasonable, do not let their first number set your sense of value — go research before negotiating.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-stage pricing conversations; deals where value is not commoditized.
**AVOID WHEN:** ritualized markets where refusing to name a number stalls everything; the book concedes the who-goes-first question shrinks as preparation on both sides rises.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research — reactive devaluation credited to Curhan, Neale & Ross, *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 40:142 (2004).

### [NEGO:JUSTIFIABLEHIGH-GTY] Open at the highest figure you could justify to a neutral
**Move:** When you do open, use the highest number you could state without embarrassment — operationally, the highest figure you would try to persuade a neutral third party is fair. Give the reasoning *before* the number, because a disliked number stops them listening to the rationale. Do not present it as firm: the firmer you make an early figure, the more credibility you burn when you move off it. Safer phrasing floats a standard and a comparable rate as a factor to consider, then asks how that sounds.
**APPLY WHEN:** you must open and defensible comparables exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot justify the figure to a neutral — then it is just an extreme demand and inherits all its costs.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus scripted example.

### [NEGO:ONEFAIR-GTY] "One fair solution might be…"
**Move:** Table your proposal as *a* fair option deserving joint consideration rather than as your demand or the only fair answer — specific enough to act on, framed so that rejecting it is not a rejection of you.
**APPLY WHEN:** moving from a discussion of standards to a concrete number.
**AVOID WHEN:** excessive tentativeness invites a counter-anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull case.

### [NEGO:FRAMEWORKDOC-GTY] Framework agreement and drafting as you go
**Move:** Prepare a document shaped like the final agreement with blanks for each term still to be negotiated (a standard real-estate purchase-and-sale form is the detailed archetype; sometimes a list of headings suffices). Use it as agenda and starting point so nothing important is missed. Whether or not you start from one, draft possible terms continuously during the negotiation: it focuses discussion, surfaces overlooked issues, creates a felt sense of progress, and leaves a record that prevents later misunderstanding. Where consensus is missing, draft alternative provisions side by side.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal producing a written contract; multi-issue enterprise terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** presenting a paper-shaped draft too early signals a fixed position and inhibits option generation — pair it with TENTATIVE-GTY.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard practice described from the authors' advisory work.

### [NEGO:TENTATIVE-GTY] Move to commitment gradually under an explicit "no commitments" label
**Move:** Seek a consensus proposal issue by issue; if you cannot converge, at least narrow the range and move on — a trade-off may appear later. Agree explicitly that all commitments are tentative so people can brainstorm without fearing that every floated option binds them; literally head the working draft as a tentative draft with no commitments. Tentative positions should not change without reason, but nothing is firm until the full package is visible. Expect to cycle through the issue list several times, alternating item and package view. Avoid demands and lock-ins; offer options and invite criticism.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-issue negotiations with internal approvers on both sides.
**AVOID WHEN:** a counterpart exploits tentativeness to reopen everything indefinitely — pair with ESCALPAUSE-GTY.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement with scripts.

### [NEGO:FIRMONINTEREST-GTY] Firm on interests, flexible on solutions; agree on where you disagree
**Move:** When your proposal is attacked, do not defend the proposal — restate the underlying interest and ask whether they can devise a better way to serve it alongside their own. If the conflict looks irresolvable, ask whether there is any reason one side's interest should take priority. Hold your analysis unless they make a persuasive case; when persuaded, change it and present the logic before the new conclusion. Where disagreement persists, seek second-order agreement: make each side's interests and reasoning explicit, surface the differing assumptions, and find ways to test them.
**APPLY WHEN:** objection handling on terms; deadlock on a single clause.
**AVOID WHEN:** the counterpart treats every restatement as an opening to re-litigate — then time-box it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

### [NEGO:CLOSEMOVE-GTY] Make the offer when exploration hits diminishing returns
**Move:** Once an issue or cluster is well explored, be ready to put an offer on the table; partial offers pairing two key issues are legitimate early ("June 30 closing if the down payment stays under $50,000") and can later be combined into a comprehensive proposal. The offer should not surprise — it should read as the natural outgrowth of the discussion, neither an ultimatum nor an opening position. Choose the venue: move final commitment exploration into a private one-on-one between principal negotiators even if formal closure happens publicly later.
**APPLY WHEN:** late stage; the book notes many negotiations settle as soon as a complete offer is made.
**AVOID WHEN:** exploration is still producing new interests or options.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated observation from the authors' practice.

### [NEGO:IFYESIFNO-GTY] "If we agree… if we disagree…"
**Move:** Make clear that their single yes ends the whole problem, then convey the no-deal path without threatening: attribute the alternative to an external authority, describe it as a possibility rather than a decision, state your reluctance to use it, and close by affirming confidence that a fair settlement is reachable. Withhold your own alternative if it is weaker than they believe.
**APPLY WHEN:** the close; you have real recourse but want the deal and the relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no credible alternative — the framing is then a bluff the book does not endorse.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull cited the hearing examiner's suggested remedies while concealing that his real BATNA was simply to move out.

### [NEGO:COMMITPOWER-GTY] Three commitments: what you will do, won't do, and want them to do
**Move:** (1) Make a firm, well-timed offer instead of just discussing — you trade away haggling room but simplify their decision to a single yes and counter their fear of a slippery slope. More concrete is more persuasive: written beats oral; add a fading opportunity by stating when and how the offer expires. Where useful, spell out what happens absent agreement so they see the consequences of your BATNA, including specific costs. (2) Consider a negative commitment — tying your own hands on a final offer — but only late, after interests are understood and options explored, and only with credible reasons independent of your will; early lock-in limits communication and makes them feel coerced. A true final offer works by worsening their micro-BATNA. (3) Specify exactly what you want them to do; vague or overbroad commitments fail on edge cases, and ambiguity about your demands lets them do nothing.
**APPLY WHEN:** closing; deadline design; defining next steps and mutual action plans.
**AVOID WHEN:** locking in early, or issuing a final offer you are not prepared to honor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Reagan's January 1981 inauguration as a natural expiry in the Iran hostage negotiation; a landlord facing a $250 health-department fine; the 1990 Iraq case where US goals appeared variously as withdrawal, destruction of nuclear facilities, disarmament or regime change, and UN resolutions never stated that sanctions would end upon withdrawal.

### [NEGO:ENDGENEROUS-GTY] Be generous at the end
**Move:** When you sense you are close, give something you know they value that stays consistent with the logic of your proposal — and mark it clearly as a final gesture so it does not seed expectations of further concessions. The aim is that they leave feeling satisfied and fairly treated, which pays off in implementation and in the next negotiation.
**APPLY WHEN:** final-stage closing of a relationship deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** given without the "this is final" marker, or given to a counterpart who reads any concession as proof more is available.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — practice advice, no data.

### [NEGO:CLOSECONVENIENCE-GTY] Close on their convenience and let them keep a win
**Move:** Once the principle is settled, offer to shape the remaining logistics around their preference — it satisfies a real interest of theirs, gives them a stake in the agreement, and hands them a face-saving story (they got the outcome they wanted on timing even while paying). End on an explicitly warm note so neither side wants to sabotage or ignore the deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** after a contentious settlement with a counterparty you will see again.
**AVOID WHEN:** the convenience concession quietly costs you more than the disputed amount.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led/any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull case; the observation that neither party later felt cheated is self-reported.

## Where this book stops working

- **Single-variable, one-shot haggles.** The option engine has nothing to grip when only price moves and the counterparty is a stranger you will never see again. The authors concede this directly: haggling is fine for single-issue deals among strangers where both sides are protected by competitive alternatives, and exploring interests would cost more than it returns. Low-ACV transactional motion is where METAGAME-GTY, FOURPASS-GTY and BRAINSEP-GTY are net-negative.
- **A counterparty who will not engage on reasons at all.** The whole method presumes a party who eventually responds to standards. Chapters 7 and 8 supply jujitsu and trick-taming, but each of those moves still ends in "invite them to state their reasoning". Against someone who is content to repeat a position indefinitely, who is running a scripted extraction play, or who harvests collaborative framing as one-way concession, the principled frame itself needs defending — that defense is not in this book (the lens gets it from Camp's school).
- **Deliberately manipulative or predatory counterparts.** JOINTBRAIN-GTY, INVITECRIT-GTY, LISTENPOWER-GTY and RELNEGOTIATE-GTY all hand information or leverage to the other side and assume reciprocation. Each carries its own AVOID line for exactly this reason; in a hard-distributive game they are exploitable.
- **No credible external standard.** The entire criteria layer (PREPSTANDARDS, WHATSTHEORY, LEGITSHIELD, STDWEIGH) fails on one-of-a-kind offers, brand-new categories, and pricing with no public comparables — the book's own instruction is to fall back to interests and invented options.
- **Where the power gap is real and unconvertible.** The authors are explicit that no method beats a genuinely superior alternative on the other side; no skill buys the White House. The instruction is to improve your BATNA or change theirs, not to negotiate harder.
- **Regulated or ritualized processes.** PROCRECIP-GTY, COJUDGE-GTY and PREGAME-GTY misfire where asymmetry is institutional (public procurement, regulated approval flows) rather than tactical.
- **Speed-constrained deals.** Almost every move buys quality with time. On a transactional cycle where the spread is smaller than the process cost, the triage move (APPROACHTRIAGE-GTY) should route you out of the method entirely.

## What the book debunks

- **Soft vs hard is a false choice.** Both are positional bargaining; soft loses to hard reliably, and the fix is a different game, not a midpoint. "Being nice is no answer."
- **Splitting the difference is not a solution.** It is the characteristic failure product of positional bargaining, and splitting between arbitrary figures produces an arbitrary result.
- **"Separate the people from the problem" ≠ ignore feelings.** The authors say they considered replacing "separate" with "disentangle" precisely because readers misused it; people problems often need *more* attention than substantive ones.
- **"Face-saving" as a derisive term is a grave misunderstanding** — it is a real interest, not a courtesy.
- **Inferring their intentions from your fears** feels safe and plays well to spectators but blinds you to small moves toward agreement. Assuming both sides read the same act identically is the documented error (extra responsibility given as a compliment, received as persecution).
- **Withholding acknowledgement of their case is not a good tactic** — without demonstrated understanding, your counter-argument is heard as proof you never listened.
- **Opposed positions do not imply opposed interests**, and everyone on the other side almost never shares the same interests. Committing to a position is unwise; committing to interests is wise.
- **The fixed pie is almost always false** — though the book also warns both sides can end up worse off together.
- **Asking the other side to "be more forthcoming" reliably fails**, because anything they offer becomes your new floor.
- **Bottom lines** are arbitrary, usually set too high by whoever benefits, close your ears, and suppress creative packages. Seeing your alternatives "in the aggregate" is a named cognitive error — you can only have one.
- **Negotiators are more often over-committed to agreeing than over-optimistic**; postponing the "what if no deal" question is negotiating blind.
- **Wealth, size and clout are not power** until converted into an alternative — visible wealth can actively weaken a buyer. The US being rich and nuclear-armed did not free hostages in Beirut.
- **Asking who is more powerful is harmful in both directions** — believing you are stronger breeds under-preparation, believing you are weaker breeds discouragement. Preferring to feel powerless is named as a comfortable, self-fulfilling avoidance of responsibility.
- **Standards used as ammunition for a position you already hold** is the common counterfeit; escalating a practical disagreement into "a matter of principle" is a failure mode, not a virtue. Insisting on objective criteria is not insisting on *your* criterion. "Win-win" talk is rejected as a way to hide genuinely conflicting interests.
- **Appeasement teaches stubbornness** (Chamberlain/Munich 1938, then war a year later); conceding "to improve the relationship" is specifically prohibited, and mirroring dirty tactics collapses the talk into a will contest.
- **Threats backfire** — external pressure makes groups close ranks and reframes the decision as "do we submit?" Extreme demands backfire on their user through lost credibility and killed deals. Lock-ins are gambles: a called bluff forces a humiliating explanation to the committer's own constituency.
- **Attacking the person for using a tactic** makes the tactic harder to abandon and leaves festering anger.
- **Third parties do not automatically help** — a mediator who asks about positions and presses for concessions deepens attachment. Assembling fragments of many proposals is dismissed: a camel is a horse designed by a committee.
- **"Always make the first offer" and "always let them go first" are both rejected**; preparation dominates both, and firmness on early figures is a credibility trap. Taking your sense of value from their first number is called extremely risky.
- **A clever strategy cannot compensate for weak preparation** — plan the terrain, not one path through the woods.
- **The trade-off between a good substantive outcome and a good relationship is denied.** So is the framing that you must choose between getting what you deserve and being decent. The book also disclaims being a sermon: it does not tell you to accept the first arguably-fair offer.
- **Refusing contact with terrorists is challenged** — you negotiate through actions and silence too; the question is never whether but how, decided by BATNA comparison. Negotiating is not conceding.
- **Reading does not build skill** — negotiation is trained like a sport, and "who's winning?" is the wrong question.

## Numbers that matter

- **Editions:** 1981, 1991, 2011 — roughly 30 years of use before the third edition. Four principles; three stages (analysis/planning/discussion); three judging criteria; five core concerns; five basic human needs; seven sources of power; three communication failures.
- **Communication-mode study** (Valley, Moag & Bazerman 1998): sellers with private information lied in a small minority of face-to-face interactions, about a third of written ones, more than half of phone ones; mutually beneficial agreements in almost 60% face-to-face, 22% written, 38% phone; impasse in more than half of written, 19% face-to-face, 14% phone. The only quantified external result in the book.
- **Aspiration level:** studies said to show a consistently strong correlation between aspiration and result — no source named, treat as assertion.
- **1961 test-ban talks:** USSR agreed to 3 on-site inspections, the US insisted on no fewer than 10, and nobody had defined what an inspection was.
- **Camp David 1978:** 13 days, roughly 23 drafts, accepted by both sides; follows Israeli occupation of the Sinai since the 1967 Six Day War. Law of the Sea: ~150 nations, 1974–1981; India proposed a $60M per-site initial fee against a US position of zero, resolved via the MIT deep-seabed economics model.
- **Insurance settlement (self-reported war story):** adjuster opened $13,600, moved to $15,000 on "company policy"; comparable car advertised at $14,800 with 49,000 miles against the claimant's 25,000, plus $1,650 mileage and $1,100 technology adjustments; settled at $18,024 in about half an hour.
- **House/BATNA worked example:** $300,000 asking price, $260,000 family bottom line, buyer capped near $244,000, purchase at $235,000, trip wire at $258,000, and an argued case for accepting as little as $224,000. A small town moved a factory's goodwill payment from $300,000 to $2,300,000 a year, backed by the alternative of annexation at a residential rate of about $2,500,000.
- **Turnbull vs Jones Realty:** $1,200 rent against a $932 legal maximum, a $268 monthly gap, ~$30,000 of improvements needed to justify the increase, $1,340 total overpayment.
- **Illustrative figures:** sports salary variants $8.75M×4yr, $10M×3yr, $7.5M×3yr plus a $10M bonus; baseball salary line $500,000; alimony $1,000/week; brainstorming group 5–8 people; Townsend Oil tax demand $2M → $4M with a 7-year holiday and 5 new industries in 3 years; trial edit at $300/chapter; good-guy/bad-guy $80,000 → $76,000; extreme demand $175,000 vs a ~$300,000 house; union lock-in pledge 15%; Malta 1971 £10 million advance; $24,000 forty-year-old starting salary; reframing $10,000 vs $50,000 market; $50,000 down-payment pairing; $68,000–$70,000 salary narrowing; 20% competing salary offer; $250 health-department fine; a radio-station case that saved the buyer almost $1 million once the minority owner's wish to keep managing was discovered.
- **Self-reported organizational claims:** GM/UAW needed four contracts to change their negotiation structure; the bankrupt-energy-company case settled within 24 hours once the judge was engaged; Kuwait Airways Flight 422 (1988) hostages released for incremental concessions while the core principle never moved; US–Iran hostage settlement January 1981 after more than a year, mediated by Algeria.

## Blind spots of this book

- **It has almost no data of its own.** The overwhelming majority of moves are assertions or single anecdotes, many diplomatic rather than commercial. The one measured external result is the 1998 channel study. Claims that principled negotiation "usually" dominates, that the principled side "generally has an edge", and that outcomes are better "in virtually every case" are unmeasured.
- **It assumes a counterparty available for reasoned exchange** and, in the limit, a shared notion of fairness. Where the other side's whole business model is extraction, the book's answers all route back through invitation to reason.
- **It never prices its own process.** Almost every move costs time and preparation, but the book gives no guidance on when the delay costs more than the concession — NOYIELD-GTY, STANDOFFTEST-GTY and REFUSALPROBE-GTY are the clearest gaps.
- **Selling is not its subject.** There is no pipeline, no qualification, no discovery-to-demo motion, no handling of a buyer who has not yet decided to buy anything. It starts once both parties are already at a table.
- **Little on multi-round commercial relationships as repeated games** — reputation is invoked morally rather than modelled, and there is no treatment of how a vendor's concession precedents propagate across a customer base beyond one warning line.
- **Emotion is treated diagnostically, not clinically.** The five core concerns and identity threat are frameworks for a negotiator to apply to themselves and others without training; the book acknowledges disentangling emotion from merits is not easy, then offers practice rather than method.
- **Cultural and individual variation is handled by a checklist plus a warning not to stereotype**, with the only cited research being Gilligan (1982) on gender — thin ground for the breadth of the claim.
- **It underweights the case where creativity makes things worse** — it notes in passing that both sides can end up worse off together, then does not develop it.
- **Internal-organization negotiation** (your own legal, finance, product) is present only implicitly, though most of the machinery would apply.
