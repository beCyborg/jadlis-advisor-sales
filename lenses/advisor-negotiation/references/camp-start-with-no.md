# Start with No — digest

## What this book is for the lens

The counter-canon: it treats the pursuit of agreement itself as the failure mode. Where the interest-based school optimizes for a joint solution, Camp optimizes for the negotiator's right to refuse and for the discipline that makes refusal credible — you control only your own behavior, so "no" (yours and theirs) is the only reliable instrument. It beats other sources on three mechanisms: a concrete anti-neediness protocol, a question grammar (interrogative-led) that transfers vision-building to the other side, and a budget model that names sunk cost as the real enemy of a good deal. Every move carries `-CAMP` because this digest sits beside a deliberately contradictory counterpart; on anchoring, on concession-trading, and on cooperative framing the two schools give opposite orders and must never be averaged.

## Core model

Five layers, applied per deal and per contact.

1. **Mission and purpose (M&P)** — one written sentence set in the counterpart's world, phrased as what you want them to *see and decide*. Money and titles are consequences, never the essence. Invalid M&Ps are goals in disguise ("make a killing", "renegotiate this contract"). Multiple layers: enterprise, deal, stage, sometimes single call.
2. **Goals you control** — behavior and activity only, never results. Quotas and close dates are named as dangerous even when met. Activity is split payside (on the negotiation track) versus nonpayside (overhead or avoidance). A written daily behavioral track is the monitoring instrument.
3. **Pain** — the counterpart's current or future problem, the reason they are at the table. You do not create pain and you cannot tell anyone anything; you build their vision of pain with questions ("no vision, no decision"). Price tracks the clarity of their picture, not the strength of your argument.
4. **Budget** — four currencies with illustrative weights: time 1x, energy 2x, money 3x, emotion 4x (time+energy ≈ 2x, +money ≈ 6x, +emotion ≈ 24x). Rule of four: know yours, control yours, know theirs, build theirs. Overinvestment is what produces sunk-cost decisions.
5. **Decision makers and agenda** — map the real decision path early (chapter 11, "The Shell Game"); blockers are respected and routed, not fought. Every contact, down to a one-paragraph email, carries a negotiated agenda built from five slots: problems, our baggage, their baggage, what we want, what happens next.

Behavioral layer running through all five: unokayness (Columbo effect) as public presentation while self-esteem stays private; interrogative-led questions; nurturing, reversing, connecting, 3+ as the supporting fuels; the strip line as a two-way pressure release; blank slate (no expectations, no assumptions); prep-end step so no contact closes without the next one; and "absolutely no closing". The Conclusion ("Dance with the Tiger!") compresses all of it into Thirty-three Rules and a three-stage adoption ramp.

## Moves

### Stance and self-management

### [NEGO:WINWIN-TRAP-CAMP] Read partnership language as a leverage move
**Move:** When the other side invokes fairness, good faith, partnership, or long-term relationship as a reason for you to move, treat it as a concession request with no content and answer by asking what specifically they want changed; do not reciprocate warmth with a price move.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement or a large buyer opens with collaborative framing; a professionalized purchasing function; your side is the smaller one.
**AVOID WHEN:** Low-stakes or long-standing cooperative relationships where reading hostility into ordinary courtesy poisons the deal; internal negotiations with your own team.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated pattern across the author's corporate clients facing supply-system-management buyers; GM's PICOS is named but its characterization is his interpretation.

### [NEGO:ADVERSARY-FRAME-CAMP] Label the counterpart a respected opponent
**Move:** Internally name the other side "adversary" in the sense of respected opponent — stay polite and respectful, but stop assuming they want friendship or share your interests; keep courtesy and expectation of alignment strictly separate.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any commercial negotiation, especially against a professionally trained counterpart.
**AVOID WHEN:** The label leaks into your tone or your team's language and manufactures real hostility; internal or partner-ecosystem work where the frame is corrosive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a definitional stance defended rhetorically.

### [NEGO:WANT-NOT-NEED-CAMP] Replace need with want, out loud and internally
**Move:** Before and during the negotiation, state to yourself that you want this deal and do not need it, and name concretely what happens if it dies; strike need-language from what you say to the counterpart.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal that would materially change your quarter; single-large-customer dependence; late-stage pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The dependence is real and unmanaged — pretending it away instead of fixing pipeline or runway is denial, not discipline.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reports clients ranking this as the highest-impact idea in the system (informal self-reported poll).

### [NEGO:NO-REJECTION-CAMP] Audit who can actually reject you
**Move:** Before the call, state explicitly that this counterpart has no power to reject you because you need nothing from them — only someone you genuinely depend on can — and carry that into how you take a refusal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fear of the call, avoidance of prospecting, reluctance to name a price or push back.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to dismiss real feedback — "they can't reject me" must not become "their objection means nothing".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a reframe argued from definition.

### [NEGO:NOT-OKAY-CAMP] Present slightly less than perfectly competent
**Move:** Let the counterpart feel superior — search for words, admit you are not on top of a detail, ask them to explain something, avoid displays of polish and expertise. Only one party gets to feel okay and it should be them.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings; hostile or status-conscious counterparts; any room where the other side is defensive.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your credibility is the product (technical evaluation, expert sale, regulated diligence), or the buyer is choosing on competence signals; overdone it reads as unprofessional or as technique once recognized.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — used across the client base; the Columbo, Reagan and Schwarzkopf-before-King-Fahd (1990) illustrations are rhetorical, not evidential.

### [NEGO:PROP-FUMBLE-CAMP] Engineer a small dependency on the counterpart
**Move:** Arrive without a pen or paper and borrow one, leave the cards behind for a first meeting, let a device run out of power — a small ask for help that lets the other side do you a favor.
**APPLY WHEN:** A stuck meeting where barriers have not come down; early relationship-building with a guarded counterpart.
**AVOID WHEN:** Staged clumsiness is discovered, or the setting punishes unpreparedness; this is the most openly manipulative technique in the book and the council should mark it as theatre, not honesty, despite the author's framing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a coached seller who dropped her purse in a meeting with a $35,000 commission at stake and got the deal in that moment.

### [NEGO:LET-THEM-SHINE-CAMP] Give the counterpart room to display their strength
**Move:** When the other side wants to demonstrate expertise, charm, or command of detail, encourage it and stay quiet; harvest what they reveal while they are performing. Your greatest strength is your greatest weakness — suppress the urge to display yours.
**APPLY WHEN:** Talkative or status-driven counterparts; discovery phases where information is what you need.
**AVOID WHEN:** The display consumes the only meeting you get and your decision-relevant questions go unasked.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from Emerson's line on strength and weakness, no case attached.

### [NEGO:ESTEEM-VS-OKAY-CAMP] Keep self-esteem private, hand okayness over
**Move:** Separate the two explicitly before a session: your internal appraisal of yourself is untouchable and stays with you; the visible position of being composed, expert and in control is what you concede. The first is what makes appearing unokay survivable.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any negotiation where you are tempted to defend your status, correct a slight, or prove competence.
**AVOID WHEN:** Contexts where visible competence is the product being evaluated (technical credibility gates, expert testimony) — performed unokayness there costs you the mandate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle with rhetorical examples only.

### [NEGO:PAY-FORWARD-CAMP] Pay forward as a self-esteem routine
**Move:** Deliberately give value where it cannot be repaid to you — workplace, community, family — as a daily habit, on the claim that this builds the self-image the rest of the system requires.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long sales careers with repeated rejection; periods where neediness and status-anxiety are visibly leaking into calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used instrumentally as a reciprocity lever on a specific counterpart — that is a different and manipulative move; the mechanism claimed here is internal, not transactional.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from anecdote (roses distributed to terminally ill patients; the Iowa grain-elevator owner funding Roy M. Kottman's tuition) and from Emerson, not from negotiation outcomes.

### [NEGO:FIRST-NAME-CAMP] Answer a first-name introduction with a first name
**Move:** When someone introduces themselves by full name, respond using their first name rather than title-plus-surname, so the status frame stays level from the first exchange.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings, especially with senior counterparts where deference is the reflex.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cultures, hierarchies, or industries where first-naming a senior person reads as disrespect and costs more than the status point gains.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — illustrative micro-example, no support.

### [NEGO:ABORT-SESSION-CAMP] Break or cancel when you cannot blank slate
**Move:** When excitement or emotion takes over mid-session, call a time-out — bathroom, coffee, lunch, any step back. If exhaustion, a hangover, or a family crisis makes blank slating impossible at all, cancel the session outright.
**APPLY WHEN:** You notice your own emotional pendulum swinging; anything scheduled during personal crisis.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cancellation itself signals weakness or breaks a hard deadline; the source treats cancellation as always correct and never prices its cost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as an absolute rule with no supporting case.

### [NEGO:ENERGY-STAMINA-CAMP] Manage physical endurance as a negotiating asset
**Move:** Treat stamina as part of the budget: know your endurance limits, guard against jet lag, exercise, take breaks and walks, avoid alcohol at business-social events, call a time-out without hesitation whether the session is an hour or round-the-clock.
**APPLY WHEN:** Marathon sessions, deal-closing crunches, multi-day on-site negotiations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as more than hygiene — the accompanying claim that the physically stronger side gets the better deal is not demonstrated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the 4 a.m. labor-settlement pattern is offered as folklore, not measurement.

### [NEGO:PREGAME-VISUALIZE-CAMP] Pre-call mental rehearsal of behavior, not outcome
**Move:** Before the call, run the meeting as a film in your head — you asking interrogative-led questions, taking notes, staying calm, with no expectation, no need, no fear. Rehearse conduct; never rehearse a winning ending.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ahead of any high-stakes or emotionally loaded session; when you catch yourself fantasizing about the signed deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** It slides into outcome fantasy (picturing the yes), which is exactly the neediness the system forbids; it is no substitute for research on decision makers and pain.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — reported across hundreds of students and negotiations, self-reported, no measurement.

### [NEGO:BLANK-SLATE-CAMP] Clear expectations before every session
**Move:** Enter each session with no forecast of the outcome, positive or negative. Do not let sunk travel or effort create obligation. Watch for "positive attitude" language sliding into positive expectation, which is treated as a liability.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any high-stakes meeting, especially ones carrying sunk cost (travel, long cycles, exclusive prep).
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as an excuse for not preparing — the book pairs blank slating with heavy research, not with improvisation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Ireland trip (team flew out, counterparty unavailable, flew home next day without concessions, counterparty travelled next time) plus one client stuck on a sports-derived positive-attitude habit.

### Prospecting, qualification and first contact

### [NEGO:COLDCALL-NO-FIT-OPENER-CAMP] Open cold contact by conceding you may not fit
**Move:** Start the first call by saying you do not know whether what you do is relevant to them, that it may not be, and that they should tell you so and you will go; then ask one question about how the relevant area is handled. Same shape for investor outreach: state you are unsure of fit and propose a meeting to check it in both directions, ending on a calendar question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Outbound first contact, appointment setting, any approach with no standing to assume relevance.
**AVOID WHEN:** Warm inbound where the buyer has already declared intent — manufactured uncertainty then reads as a script; also weak where the counterpart needs proof of competence fast.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author built his own client base this way and trained insurance sales teams on it; conversion figures self-reported.

### [NEGO:WARM-AS-COLD-CAMP] Treat every referral as the coldest call you ever made
**Move:** When handed a hot referral, run the cold-call discipline anyway — no assumptions about intent, same permission-to-decline opener, no mental spending of the commission.
**APPLY WHEN:** Introductions, referrals, inbound leads described as pre-sold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-applying it insults a referrer's credibility with their contact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "they're on the tee, just call and collect" handoff treated as a neediness trap.

### [NEGO:ASK-FOR-DISQUAL-CAMP] Ask the gatekeeper to disqualify you in writing
**Move:** Send your qualifying information and ask the counterpart to tell you now if you do not qualify or will not be backed, explaining that you will move to other options if so.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualification-heavy processes with many applicants or vendors; sponsor-dependent approvals; where your scarce resource is time spent on dead options.
**AVOID WHEN:** Asking for a no hands a lukewarm gatekeeper the easiest possible exit and you have no real alternatives lined up.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a recruited high school athlete who asked coaches to say no and reportedly received several acceptances.

### [NEGO:VALID-APPOINTMENT-CAMP] Refuse invalid meetings and drive-bys
**Move:** Do not travel, drive or grant a slot without a clear picture of the counterpart's pain and grounds to believe they hold time, money and emotional budget to pay for relief. Judge activity by measurable prospects, not by volume of proposals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Territory sales, outbound prospecting, any team measured on meeting counts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early market discovery where you genuinely do not yet know who has pain — the gate then screens out learning; also risks arrogance toward inbound interest that has not yet articulated itself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing rule to clients; the coupon-versus-newspaper coaching story is the illustration.

### [NEGO:PAIN-PRECHECK-CAMP] No meeting without a stated vision of their pain
**Move:** Before agreeing to any meeting, write down what you believe the counterpart's current or future problem is and how you know. If you cannot state it, treat the meeting as invalid and research first. Re-run it with a blank slate mid-deal to catch hidden pain quietly killing the deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualification, pre-meeting prep, any deal that has gone quiet or reads "logical but stuck".
**AVOID WHEN:** Trivial transactional purchases where the problem is self-evident and the gate only adds delay; also fails if your written pain is your own sales thesis in disguise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing rule across the client base; McColl's "get inside their head before the room" cited as corroboration, not data.

### [NEGO:BLOCKER-AS-GUIDE-CAMP] Recruit the gatekeeper into referring you
**Move:** Instead of evading the gatekeeper, ask for help explicitly: what criteria must be met to get an audience, who would have the principal's ear on this subject, who is that person's most competent assistant, would you be willing to tell them why I'm calling. Give them a role and let them hand you onward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stuck at reception, executive assistants, or a screening layer; early prospecting into large organizations.
**AVOID WHEN:** The gatekeeper is under explicit instruction to block, or the ask exceeds what they can grant without exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Craig Lehmkuhl's early struggle with receptionists; the accompanying White House dialogue is labelled by the author as fabricated for illustration.

### [NEGO:START-AT-TOP-CAMP] Enter high, get routed down with a hall pass
**Move:** Open at the top of the organization and expect to be sent down to the blocker — that is the point: you arrive stamped as approved, the blocker knows you can report upward, and you get time and respect.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enterprise deals with a thick blocker layer; when purchasing or an evaluator has stalled you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The culture punishes going over heads and the blocker retaliates for the rest of the cycle; also when you have nothing worth an executive's minute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a "surefire way" with no case attached.

### Mission and purpose

### [NEGO:MP-FOUNDATION-CAMP] Write a mission and purpose and consult it per decision
**Move:** Draft an explicit long-term M&P for the business or the negotiation, then run each contested decision against it — accept, refuse, or divest based on whether it serves the statement rather than on the size of the concession.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scope creep, opportunistic diversification, long negotiations where you keep losing the thread; any decision where you cannot say why you would accept.
**AVOID WHEN:** The statement is a goal in disguise ("make a million by 21") — the book calls that an invalid M&P; also useless if written and never referenced.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Midwest storyteller-artist who had accumulated a copy shop, print shop and an ISP, wrote an M&P about sharing stories sustainably, and used it to decide what to keep and sell.

### [NEGO:MP-ADVERSARY-WORLD-CAMP] Write the M&P inside the counterparty's world
**Move:** Before the deal opens, draft one sentence describing what you want the other side to see and conclude about their own business, in their benefits, not your quota. Rewrite any draft containing your revenue, commission or survival until the subject of the sentence is them — the book's example turns "renegotiate this contract" into helping their top management see a revitalized supplier that raises effectiveness for their company and industry.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal you are about to open or re-open; especially a renegotiation where you have no leverage to compel a reopening.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as self-hypnosis substituting for actual leverage or product fit — the source's own Quaker/Snapple example shows a plausible-sounding fit failing on distribution reality.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated across coaching clients (supplier renegotiation, dance-company booking, athlete recruiting, job interview).

### [NEGO:MP-SEE-DECIDE-CAMP] Phrase the M&P as "see and decide"
**Move:** Build the M&P around the verb pair see-and-decide: your job is to create a picture in their mind and let them make the call from their own vantage point. Never phrase it as get them to buy, sign, or agree.
**APPLY WHEN:** Drafting or auditing any M&P; also a diagnostic — if you cannot phrase your aim this way, the aim is a result, not a mission.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as an incantation; the wording only works if the content is genuinely in their world.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the phrasing recurs across every client M&P the chapter reproduces.

### [NEGO:MP-HYGIENE-CAMP] Written, concise, layered, revisable
**Move:** Put the M&P in writing, keep it short, and maintain more than one — enterprise level, one per negotiation, sub-M&Ps per stage, and in high-stakes deals one per phone call. On a team, negotiate the M&P rather than impose it, everyone keeping the right to refuse. Rewrite it whenever the features and benefits you actually bring change.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex multi-touch negotiations, team-run deals, any market shift that changes what you sell.
**AVOID WHEN:** Micro or fully transactional deals where per-call written M&Ps become bureaucracy; also when frozen language stops matching a changed offer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — clients plus historical and political analogies (Grant and Reconstruction, Gore 2000) offered as illustration, not data.

### [NEGO:MP-OWNERSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP] Ask whose mission you are actually serving
**Move:** Periodically test whether your current activity serves your own stated M&P or someone else's by default; if someone else's, either adopt it deliberately or rebuild your own to support it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Chronic frustration in a role; solo operators and small owners who treat their purpose as obvious.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a rationalization for quitting commitments mid-stream.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a rhetorical device the author reports using to get attention in workshops.

### [NEGO:MP-EMOTION-CHECK-CAMP] Use M&P to keep emotion out of a high-stakes personal negotiation
**Move:** Before an emotionally loaded conversation, write the specific mission for it — what you want the other party to see and decide — and let that dictate what you say and how, instead of arguing your feelings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Medical, family, or founder-level conversations where you know you are too invested to argue well; any room where being dismissed as emotional destroys your standing.
**AVOID WHEN:** It suppresses legitimate emotional information the other party actually needs to weigh.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — parents of a premature infant framing a mission of getting the neonatal head to see the transfer risk.

### [NEGO:MP-ANTI-UNBUNDLE-CAMP] Refuse to break a proposal into separately negotiable pieces
**Move:** When procurement tries to split an integrated proposal into components to negotiate each down, decline on the ground that the components were designed to work together to reduce the buyer's risk, and redirect to the real decision makers and the cost of failure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex technical proposals where component-level pricing destroys the value logic; supply-system-management buyers optimizing for up-front savings.
**AVOID WHEN:** The bundle really is separable and the refusal is margin defense dressed as principle; also weak if you cannot quantify the failure cost you point at.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a roughly $50 million proposal for chip-carrier boxes against four other global makers; the client refused to unbundle and reportedly won at full price.

### Goals, budgets and activity triage

### [NEGO:GOAL-BEHAVIOR-ONLY-CAMP] Replace result targets with controllable behavior goals
**Move:** Convert every stated goal into an action you personally execute — hold the M&P, ask interrogative-led questions, take notes, control neediness — and audit any goal by asking whether you could execute it even if the other side walks. Before a call, define what you will *do* rather than what you will get, and review the call against those actions rather than against stage movement. Kill exhortation goals ("close, close, close").
**APPLY WHEN:** Planning a deal or coaching a rep; any pipeline conversation about outcomes nobody controls; any time you notice yourself forecasting commission, quota, or a signature date.
**AVOID WHEN:** The organization genuinely needs forecast numbers — the book offers no substitute and reads as absolutist here; also risky as process-theatre if the chosen behaviors are not actually predictive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — coaching across straight sales, Fortune 500 teams, academic and service institutions; the air-force training and baseball-hitter analogies carry no measurement.

### [NEGO:PAYSIDE-TRIAGE-CAMP] Sort every activity payside versus nonpayside
**Move:** Label each planned activity: payside = directly on the negotiation track (valid appointments through final presentation); nonpayside = necessary overhead (expense forms, admin) or avoidance behavior (golf, gifts, breeze-shooting, quota-filling appointments, cold calling). Minimize nonpayside; drop a large nonpayside meeting the moment a smaller payside task appears. Begging for an appointment is nonpayside dreaming, not pipeline.
**APPLY WHEN:** Calendar and pipeline triage; reps who are busy but not advancing deals; long relationship-heavy enterprise cycles where entertaining substitutes for the hard conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early-stage founder-led or brand-new territory work where relationship-building and cold outreach genuinely are the only path in — the book concedes cold calling has disciplinary value while still calling it nonpayside.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern reported across corporate and direct-sales clients.

### [NEGO:DAILY-TRACK-CAMP] Keep a daily behavioral scorecard
**Move:** Maintain a written daily sheet scoring controllable behaviors only: neediness controlled, unokayness maintained, no said and no invited, nurturing used, reverses used, 3+ used, connection made, interrogative-led questions asked, notes taken, a bad decision corrected by the next one, emotions contained, plus your emotional state and how time was spent. Modeled on the military pilot's training folder reviewed by instructors two or three times a day.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deliberately building new negotiation habits; coaching a team where pipeline metrics hide behavioral failures.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a management compliance instrument — self-scored sheets under supervision produce flattering fiction; the author also concedes many people find the practice too rigorous to sustain and that he cannot enforce it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — required of coaching students and described as standard client practice; the pilot-training parallel is analogy only.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-TRIPLE-CAMP] Track three budgets on both sides
**Move:** For every live deal maintain a running assessment of your time-and-energy, money and emotional investment, plus a parallel estimate of the counterpart's. Money is the only numeric one; the other two are deliberate judgment calls. Keep yours low, let theirs climb.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any multi-month or multi-stakeholder deal; whenever you hear yourself justify continuing by what you have already spent.
**AVOID WHEN:** Short transactional cycles where the bookkeeping costs more than it saves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — coaching framework across the client base; the 1x/2x/3x/4x weights are admitted by the author to be non-empirical.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-BUILD-THEIRS-CAMP] Deliberately raise the counterpart's investment
**Move:** Use ordinary friction to make their side spend: do not return "urgent" calls immediately, leave messages that require a callback rather than saving them time, cancel your own meetings when appropriate, ask them to redo work that does not make sense to you, route them to a decision maker you hold back, and use short pauses to signal non-neediness and buy yourself composure.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart is dallying, non-serious, or running budget-building tactics on you; long-cycle deals where time is on your side.
**AVOID WHEN:** You actually need the deal on a clock; small vendors facing large buyers who can outspend you; against a counterpart trained in the same system, where the author concedes the tactics are pointless. Ethically this is deliberate cost imposition and corrodes trust if detected.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 15-second hold during a six-month deal; Ho Chi Minh's Paris table-shape stall used as historical analogy, not evidence.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-ASK-DIRECT-CAMP] Ask what they would budget, and offer to build it with them
**Move:** Ask outright what they would budget to solve this problem. When they say they do not know in dollars, answer that you will build the necessary budget together. Expect budget to surface indirectly as they describe pain — higher pain, higher budget.
**APPLY WHEN:** After pain is at least partly visible; consultative and enterprise cycles where budget is not pre-allocated.
**AVOID WHEN:** Asked cold before any pain vision exists — it reads as qualification theatre and invites a lowball anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — scripted exchange presented as routine coaching practice.

### [NEGO:MONEY-BUDGET-WRITE-OFF-CAMP] Set the money budget and be ready to lose all of it
**Move:** Decide in advance the cash you will spend pursuing the deal (travel, legal, engineering time) and treat it as fully expendable. If you lack reserves for the long haul, do not start — find deals you can afford to lose.
**APPLY WHEN:** Negotiating against a larger counterparty that can escalate costs (worldwide meetings, repeated rework, attrition).
**AVOID WHEN:** The "prepared to lose it all" stance becomes an excuse to skip cost discipline; it is also far easier advice for the well-capitalized than for the small supplier it addresses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — four clients flew to Ireland, were stood up, and wrote off $20,000 without emotional collapse.

### Preparation and information discipline

### [NEGO:RESEARCH-CAMP] Out-prepare on public facts
**Move:** Before negotiating, pull financial statements, annual reports, business press and web sources on the counterparty: financial position, profit and loss centers, market strengths and weaknesses, decision hierarchy, competitors' condition. Use it to test claimed numbers. Assume aggressive multinational procurement teams already hold the equivalent on you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every enterprise negotiation; specifically to check volume claims and stated urgency.
**AVOID WHEN:** At the personal-details end — the book's own illustration of applied research is wartime collection of local news about American POWs, which the author himself calls horrible. Treat opposition research on individuals as an ethical boundary, not a checklist item.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Silicon Valley client's claim of out-preparing Fortune 100 teams plus the widget-capacity case; the "Japanese always research" generalization is an unsupported stereotype.

### [NEGO:NOTES-CAMP] Take notes as the blank-slating device
**Move:** Take continuous written notes in every meeting and call, including non-verbal behavior and mood. Bring a pad rather than leading with a business card. Note names immediately. Collect pieces during the session and analyze afterwards, not live. Practice on low-stakes calls.
**APPLY WHEN:** All meetings; especially multi-party sessions where you need a record of what was said.
**AVOID WHEN:** Note-taking visibly chills a sensitive disclosure, or the setting is legally sensitive — the source addresses neither case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's repeated observation that the strongest people in the room are the ones taking notes.

### [NEGO:NO-TALKING-CAMP] Talk materially less; prefer email early
**Move:** Deliberately under-talk: ask rather than assert, leave explanations shorter than feels comfortable, listen to every word without planning your reply, without interrupting, and without answering your own questions. When emotion rises, drop pitch and slow delivery instead of speeding up. For inexperienced negotiators, prefer email over the phone to reduce emotion and accidental disclosure. Treat the urge to display knowledge as the failure mode.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery, tense sessions, junior reps prone to over-explaining; any moment you catch yourself establishing credentials, correcting the counterpart, or narrating; any moment of adrenaline.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart genuinely needs information to decide; silence used as pressure on a nervous small buyer damages trust; email also strips the tonal nurturing the rest of the system depends on, a tension the book never reconciles.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as a cardinal rule across the client base (the author admits the phrasing is an exaggeration); the mute life-insurance salesman who wrote out his questions is the illustration.

### [NEGO:BEAN-GUARD-CAMP] Guard your disclosures, harvest theirs
**Move:** Assume friendly framings ("we're partners, let us help you lower costs") exist partly to extract your cost and profit structure. Say nothing in lobbies, side conversations or over drinks that you would not say at the table. Meanwhile record what the other side volunteers — prefaces like "I shouldn't say this" or "X would kill me for saying this" mark the disclosure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enterprise deals with relationship-heavy procurement; any time you are being socialized rather than negotiated with.
**AVOID WHEN:** The posture curdles into blanket distrust of cooperative counterparties — this is the book's known bias and it can kill genuinely collaborative deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — three client-side disclosures reported in substance (penalty clauses admitted as opening theatre; a ten-percent cash-in-the-game figure admitted as invented and negotiable in kind; a principal already sold on the product) plus recurring lobby-talk patterns.

### [NEGO:TEAM-LEAK-WATCH-CAMP] Police your own team for ego-driven leaks
**Move:** Assume the counterpart will cultivate whoever on your side needs to feel liked, smart or important; brief the team that no one commits, discounts or shares internals alone, and watch for members returning with the adversary's framing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Team negotiations, long enterprise cycles with social contact (dinners, site visits, junkets), labor-management style disputes.
**AVOID WHEN:** The suspicion becomes internal surveillance that destroys trust on your own side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author claims to have seen the pattern from the other side, as the counterpart's coach, in multiple Fortune 500 negotiations.

### [NEGO:OWN-SIDE-PRENEG-CAMP] Negotiate with your own side before the joint call
**Move:** When a manager or colleague insists on joining a call to help close, make a separate agreement with them first on explicit terms — they may attend on condition of staying silent, because they do not know the system and can undo the setup. Enforce it in the room if needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager attaches themselves to a large deal late; any joint call with a colleague trained in a closing-oriented method.
**AVOID WHEN:** The colleague holds authority you actually need in the room (pricing approval, technical answers) — muzzling them then costs more than it protects; also poor politics if the manager outranks you and the constraint is not framed as a request.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the insurance story where a conventional sales manager's closing instinct nearly broke a completed decision; single case.

### [NEGO:PLANT-ASSUMPTION-CAMP] Use vague words and let them fill in
**Move:** Answer scoping questions with elastic words — "expensive", "soon", "a lot" — and let the other side supply their own definition, which may be more generous than your actual number. Conversely never accept such a word from them: ask an interrogative to pin down what "retirement", "busy" or "soon" means.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early price and timeline probes where a precise answer would anchor you low.
**AVOID WHEN:** Anywhere a stated figure will be relied upon — this is the book's most explicitly manipulative technique, deliberately inducing a false belief about your price, asymmetric by admission ("our assumptions work against us, theirs work for us"), with real reputational and, in regulated contexts, legal exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as a mechanism with an unquantified claim that people sometimes offer more than expected.

### [NEGO:NO-RISK-ANSWER-CAMP] Answer without content when you must answer
**Move:** When refusing to answer is impossible, give a low-information reply that neither commits nor concedes: cost → "a lot"; duration → "it seems like forever"; opinion request → say you respect their view, have not solidified your own, could go either way, and will keep theirs in mind.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pricing probes early in the deal; being asked to endorse a position you do not hold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trust is the binding constraint or the relationship is long-running — this is deliberate vagueness and the clearest candidate in the book for reading as evasive or dishonest once detected.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — claimed to work "most of the time" with no case behind it.

### Questions and the supporting fuels

### [NEGO:INTERROGATIVE-LED-CAMP] Lead every question with an interrogative
**Move:** Start questions with who/what/when/where/why/how/which, which cannot be answered in one word and hand talking time to the other side. Convert stock verb-led forms on the spot: "Is this the biggest issue?" → "What is the biggest issue?"; "Is it too expensive?" → "What price would you pay?"; "Do you like it?" → "What are your thoughts?"
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery, mid-deal diagnosis, any moment you need information rather than agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The phrasing is right but the intent is hostile — the source's cell-carrier example shows an interrogative-led question used accusingly and destroying the account; interrogatives can also be closed ("What time is it?").
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reproduces a client call log of roughly 18 consecutive interrogative-led questions and calls it typical.

### [NEGO:KILL-VERB-LED-CAMP] Restrict verb-led questions to two situations
**Move:** Use a verb-led question ("Can you…", "Is this…", "Do you…") only when you already know the answer, or at the very end when you must bore in. Never ask anything that reads as removing the right to say no ("Can you say yes to this?") or as a trap ("Is there any reason you wouldn't say yes?"). "Isn't this what you really want?" is worse than "Is this what you really want?" because the negation adds a rush to close.
**APPLY WHEN:** Reviewing call recordings or your own scripts for closing-flavored language.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied so rigidly you cannot confirm a fact; the book itself concedes even "Is this what you really want?" becomes acceptable when delivered with nurturing tone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated workshop observation; the law-school parallel about only asking what you know is analogy.

### [NEGO:QUESTION-DISCIPLINE-CAMP] Short, single, unanswered by you
**Move:** Cap questions at roughly nine or ten words. Ask one at a time, wait, listen, and let the answer dictate the next question rather than firing a prepared list. Never supply candidate answers ("…is it the economy or your labor problems?") — that both answers for them and converts your interrogative into a verb-led question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Live calls and meetings; especially under emotional pressure when the impulse is to stack questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely complex technical scoping where a compound question saves time and the other side is an expert peer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as a standing coaching correction; the illustrating chain runs ideal delivery date → how critical → why that month → when the production-line problem appeared.

### [NEGO:BENEFIT-IN-QUESTION-CAMP] Embed the feature or benefit inside the question
**Move:** Take the benefit named in your M&P and wrap it in an interrogative so the other side reaches the conclusion: instead of "Can you stay competitive without this technology?" ask "How can you stay competitive without this technology?" Related shapes: "How will you measure us from this point forward?"; "How long does it take to develop a great umpire?"
**APPLY WHEN:** Early discussions where you need them to articulate the gap themselves; competitive displacement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The premise is false or unproven — a "how can you survive without X" frame with nothing behind it reads as pressure and invites a real no. This is the most manipulation-adjacent question move: the softening is tonal, the leading is real.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked examples constructed by the author rather than logged calls.

### [NEGO:NURTURE-CAMP] Lower the temperature deliberately
**Move:** Manage delivery as a controllable behavior: lean back, relax neck, face and hands, avoid sudden forward movement and table-smacking, lower your voice and slow your speech when in doubt, stand lower rather than towering, use light self-deprecating humor, prepend a short warm acknowledgment to hard questions. The same six words can nurture or attack depending purely on delivery.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always, and most of all when the deal is going badly and you need it back at the table.
**AVOID WHEN:** Confused with softness — the source insists nurturing is not compromise, not rescuing the other side from a hard decision, and not touchy-feely; it is a timed stress release.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — coaching practice; the Churchill and Lieberman references are illustrative, not evidential.

### [NEGO:REVERSE-CAMP] Answer a question with a nurtured question
**Move:** When asked something, reply with a brief nurturing tag then a question that serves you: "Good question — before we get to that, what's the biggest challenge you face here?"; "That was well thought out. By the way, what are your cost constraints?"; "What am I missing here?"
**APPLY WHEN:** The other side probes for your position, opinion or numbers; discovery calls where you are being interviewed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The nurturing tag is dropped — a bare reverse sounds like cross-examination; also unsuitable when a direct factual answer is legally or contractually required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the two extended cases are the author's teenage son in college-football recruiting interviews.

### [NEGO:CONNECT-CAMP] Turn provocations into disclosure
**Move:** Never answer an unasked question, never treat a statement as a question, never respond to a random remark. Use a connector instead: a drawn-out "Aaand…?" with a shrug, "Which means…?", "Help me understand", or silence they must fill. Follow the disclosure with an interrogative question.
**APPLY WHEN:** You hear "I don't like what I see", "your price is too high", "I don't like your attitude" — vague pressure statements designed to pull a concession out of you.
**AVOID WHEN:** Overused to the point of feeling like interrogation, or with a counterparty already primed to read silence as contempt; the psychiatrist framing reads as manipulative if the tone slips.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — constructed dialogues plus the attorney's standard deposition rules; the author concedes the examples are somewhat staged.

### [NEGO:THREE-PLUS-CAMP] Confirm every agreement at least three times
**Move:** Return to any agreed point three or more times, spread over time, before treating it as real — more repetitions for more critical deals, up to twenty where needed. Wrap each repetition in nurturing or a reverse, and frame each pass as another chance for them to say no, never as a third close.
**APPLY WHEN:** Verifying a yes you distrust; long committee deals where agreement decays; anything where later reversal is expensive.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are needy — if the repetition carries any urgency it becomes pressure and inverts the purpose. The claim that 3+ is nearly impossible to overdo is unfalsifiable as stated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — long-standing coaching rule credited to a salesman acquaintance and the speech-class rule of three.

### [NEGO:STRIPLINE-NEG-CAMP] Join their negative to neutralize it
**Move:** When the other side is hard negative, step into the negative with them rather than arguing: say out loud that they are not buying this, that you do not blame them, that you would switch too — then offer a small, low-pressure next step (e.g. a free upgrade and ten days to test). Repeat hard strip lines until the emotional pendulum moves, then introduce the reframe.
**APPLY WHEN:** Churn and save conversations, hostile openings, a room that has already decided against you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The negative is factual rather than emotional — agreeing loudly with a real product failure and offering only a trial is exactly the manipulation reading; also risky in writing, where the irony does not survive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/saas/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one real cancellation call plus an extended scene from the film *Legal Eagles*, which is fiction and evidentially empty.

### [NEGO:STRIPLINE-POS-CAMP] Damp the over-excited counterparty
**Move:** When the other side runs too positive, add a light honest drawback so they validate their own enthusiasm: the black sports car looks powerful but shows dirt; "before you sign, are you sure this is what you really want?"; "I appreciate the interest, but we still have a lot of challenges to work on."
**APPLY WHEN:** Just before commitment; whenever enthusiasm outpaces the buyer's actual understanding and you expect remorse.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as risk-free — the author claims he cannot name a single instance of it backfiring, which is a self-report from a trainer with a commercial interest, not a null failure rate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — "never backfires" stated without any case or dataset.

### [NEGO:NO-TELLING-CAMP] Replace reasons with questions
**Move:** Stop supplying facts, figures and charm to make them see it your way. Convert each point you wanted to assert into an interrogative-led question that makes them articulate it. Track the shift: the version that gets them writing notes is the one that worked.
**APPLY WHEN:** You catch yourself talking more than listening; analytical counterparts; any moment you feel the urge to convince.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has explicitly asked for factual specifications, compliance answers or pricing they need to proceed — withholding then reads as evasive gamesmanship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client's informal comparison of a didactic versus question-led analyst presentation, judged by how many notes the analyst took.

### [NEGO:TALK-REVERSE-CAMP] Catch yourself talking and reverse
**Move:** The moment you notice you are doing most of the talking, cut yourself off explicitly and hand the floor back with an open question about how they see things coming together. Naming that you have said enough is part of the move — it makes the reversal natural rather than abrupt.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery and mid-deal calls; any moment you are explaining rather than asking.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has directly asked for information and needs a substantive answer — reversing there reads as evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — given as a standard adoption drill.

### [NEGO:CALM-ASK-BACK-CAMP] Absorb aggression, then ask what they want you to do
**Move:** When the counterpart attacks, name-calls, or accuses you of bad faith, do not defend or counterattack — listen, take notes, then quietly ask what they would like you to do.
**APPLY WHEN:** Trained hard-bargaining buyers who alternate hospitality with intimidation; escalation attempts.
**AVOID WHEN:** The aggression is abusive rather than tactical, or the question invites a demand you cannot refuse gracefully.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — reported as standing practice for a client team working against supply-system-management buyers.

### [NEGO:AT-YOUR-SERVICE-CAMP] Hand the next move over when conflict brews
**Move:** At the first sign of friction — a tone shift, an unnamed irritation — stop and ask what they would like you to do, adding that you are at their service. The question forces a concrete request instead of an escalating mood and keeps okayness on their side.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early tension, before any demand has been articulated; a counterpart who is annoyed but has not named anything.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart is making escalating unreasonable demands — literal service framing invites them; also risky where you cannot deliver whatever they name.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — offered as a standing drill line for new adopters.

### Pain: finding it and painting it

### [NEGO:PAIN-HUNT-NURTURE-CAMP] Hunt concealed pain with a nurturing interrogative
**Move:** Assume the real problem is camouflaged, especially inside large organizations where divisions hide pain from each other. Approach the person most likely to hold it, lower the stakes of answering ("how are you handling all of this?", "how can I help you?"), and let them talk until the private motive surfaces. Then propose a way to relieve exactly that motive.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart's stated position makes no economic sense (inflated ask, irrational refusal); committee deals with private agendas.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not established enough safety — people will not expose pain to someone they expect to exploit it; social-proximity routes (club, spouse, mutual friend) read as manipulative surveillance if surfaced later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the acquisition where the founder's widow wanted a memorial to her late husband; one case, outcome self-reported.

### [NEGO:PAIN-PERMISSION-PREAMBLE-CAMP] Ask permission to be wrong before painting a costly mistake
**Move:** When the counterpart has sunk large money into a wrong direction, open by disarming yourself: ask them to be patient, say you may be out of your mind and want them to say so, admit everything you say will sound self-serving, then ask leave to walk through what you see. Then do not describe your view — ask a sequence of interrogative-led questions, starting with where the industry is heading, and let them build the picture. Restate the frame three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart is committed to a path you believe is fatal; you have a genuine remedy; the pain is invisible to them.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their decision is cheaply reversible or the stakes do not justify challenging their competence; also when you cannot survive an honest no to your premise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting between two company presidents; outcome not quantified.

### [NEGO:PAIN-BY-DECLINING-CAMP] Paint their pain by saying you cannot proceed
**Move:** State plainly and unemotionally that you want to be their long-term supplier but cannot ship or cannot continue at current terms because you are losing money and will not survive it. Apologize for the position it puts them in. Do not spell out what they lose — let their own no do the painting — then ask an interrogative-led question about how they think the problem can be solved.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are structurally underwater on an existing contract; you are one of very few qualified suppliers; the counterpart depends on multi-vendor leverage.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are replaceable, the counterpart has ready substitutes, or you cannot actually accept the walk-away — this is a real refusal, not a bluff, and being called on it ends the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. renegotiation; single case, all figures self-reported.

### [NEGO:PAIN-CALIBRATE-CAMP] Keep the brush light — avoid the emotional pendulum
**Move:** While painting, hold the counterpart between the too-positive and too-negative poles: keep the picture of the problem clear and the picture of the remedy equally clear, never frighten, never appear to lord it over them, nurture continuously.
**APPLY WHEN:** Always while painting pain, and especially if your natural strength is eloquence, intelligence, aggression or rescuing.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes an excuse for vagueness and the pain never actually lands; over-painting in the other direction humiliates and triggers defense.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as craft with no supporting case.

### [NEGO:BUILD-THEIR-NEED-CAMP] Question the counterpart's stake instead of defending your price
**Move:** Instead of justifying the price, ask who or what the purchase is for and why it matters to them, then let them articulate the stakes; their own answer raises the price they are willing to pay.
**APPLY WHEN:** Price resistance where the purchase has personal or strategic significance to the buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** It tips into manufactured guilt — the book's own illustration is a vendor laying a guilt trip on a young buyer, which the council should flag rather than copy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/service/info-product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's 1967 Saigon market purchase, reconstructed as what a trained seller should have done to him.

### Decision makers and access

### [NEGO:DM-MAP-INTERROGATIVE-CAMP] Interrogate the decision path
**Move:** Instead of "are you the decision maker", ask a chain: who else might you want to talk with, who could be of service in this decision, who should we invite to support it, who would be upset if left out, how will the decision be reached, when, what criteria and paperwork must be in place. Keep asking yourself who is missing, and be ready to negotiate separately with every name that surfaces.
**APPLY WHEN:** First substantive contact, and again whenever a deal stalls without explanation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo-buyer transactions; also the face-saving "of course you decide, but…" phrasing grates on senior buyers who see through it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard coaching questions, illustrated by the Freeman McNeil recruiting failure (the girlfriend held the real decision weight) and an acquisition where the largest-block shareholder was never considered.

### [NEGO:BLOCKER-LADDER-CAMP] Three descending agendas with a blocker
**Move:** Offer the blocker a deal in three fallbacks. First: let me walk you through the proposal, tell me no if it won't fly and I'll leave, and if it will, let me represent it to the committee myself. If refused — second: let me coach you on it and wait in the hall for questions. If refused — third: let me coach you and wait in your office so you can phone me, and if you don't like it today just say no. If all three are refused, consult your time-and-energy budget and decide calmly whether to walk.
**APPLY WHEN:** A single blocker controls access to a committee; proposals that die in translation when relayed second-hand.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have another live route into the organization worth more than the concessions; if the blocker's own standing is threatened by your proposal, no ladder step will be accepted.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as a repeatedly coached pattern that "usually works", no outcome data.

### [NEGO:PURCHASING-OUT-OF-LOOP-CAMP] Don't negotiate with purchasing
**Move:** Treat the purchasing office as paperwork, not decisions. The more authority you concede to it, the less effective you become; work the real decision makers and let purchasing process the outcome.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large-company deals where procurement has intercepted the conversation before value is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement genuinely holds veto or the contract is a formal tender — dismissing them then loses the deal on process grounds; the author's "everyone knows this" is stated, not argued.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. re-entry, where the earlier team had been negotiating exclusively with blockers.

### [NEGO:QUALIFY-DECIDER-CAMP] Do not let an unauthorized counterpart make a decision
**Move:** Before accepting a change, cancellation or restructuring, confirm the person proposing it has the authority and the consequences in view; if not, escalate the item rather than agreeing to it in the name of the relationship.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement contacts proposing to drop service contracts or terms; changes that will be blamed on you later.
**AVOID WHEN:** Escalation over your contact's head burns the relationship you need for delivery — the book underweights this cost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/saas/product · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a field rep let a purchasing contact cancel a worldwide service contract; when parts took a week instead of a day and on-site coverage was gone, the supplier absorbed the blame.

### [NEGO:SABOTAGE-PROBE-CAMP] Name the internal-champion risk at the top level
**Move:** When you enter as challenger against an incumbent who has an internal sponsor, raise it explicitly at the highest accessible level, phrased as an interrogative: how do we keep our effort from being undermined by someone inside your company whose success is tied to our competitor's?
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive-vendor slots at large accounts; you have executive access; you suspect a middle-management liaison protects the incumbent.
**AVOID WHEN:** You lack executive access or political capital — asked at the wrong level it reads as an accusation against the person you are asking.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/channel · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client case; the reported outcome was the SVP tying an internal manager's annual bonus to the client's success.

### [NEGO:FRIENDSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP] Date-check friendships with counterparts
**Move:** When someone on your side cites a friendship with the buyer, ask how long the friendship has existed and how long that person has held the buying role; if the answers match, treat the relationship as a procurement instrument and price accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** Golf, dinners, junkets and social access appearing around renewal or negotiation time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Long-standing relationships that predate the commercial one; blanket cynicism here destroys legitimate trust-based selling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the tennis-wives dialogue, where friendship and tenure in the purchasing role were the same couple of years.

### Agendas and session control

### [NEGO:AGENDA-NEGOTIATED-CAMP] Negotiate the agenda before asking for anything
**Move:** Open by proposing the frame and getting explicit agreement: I'm not sure this is of value to you; if it isn't, say so and we stop; if it is, we move forward — fair? Confirm it as the deal. Only then ask for anything. Never ask a counterpart to act under an agenda they did not agree to, and keep no hidden agendas.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings; any request that could feel like a push to close.
**AVOID WHEN:** The ritual is repeated until it becomes a verbal tic; sophisticated counterparts recognize the permission-to-say-no pattern as technique.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core coached practice illustrated by scripted exchanges.

### [NEGO:MINI-AGENDA-CAMP] Pre-clear anything awkward with a micro-agreement
**Move:** Before a hard question or sensitive move, negotiate a one-line agenda for it: may I ask you a difficult question without your getting angry — you're sure — you won't get mad? (restated three times). On group calls, set procedural mini-agendas up front, e.g. direct all questions to one named person unless they defer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Conference calls with many parties; any question that could end the meeting if asked cold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-scaffolding trivial exchanges, which signals anxiety rather than control.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. conference call across 13 negotiators in 6 countries.

### [NEGO:AGENDA-FIVE-SLOTS-CAMP] Build every agenda from five categories
**Move:** Draft each agenda against: problems (anything either side perceives as a problem, including imagined ones), our baggage, their baggage, what we want, what happens next. When in doubt whether an item belongs, include it. Every contact gets one, down to a one-paragraph email.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every session, call and email in a live negotiation; team deals where several people meet several counterparts and answers must be comparable.
**AVOID WHEN:** Literal application to one-line emails — high overhead that is unlikely to survive real workflows, though the source states no exception.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter lists roughly a dozen problem items and a dozen baggage items drawn from coached negotiations.

### [NEGO:BAGGAGE-UPFRONT-CAMP] Put your own liability on the table first
**Move:** Name the thing you fear will be held against you — inexperience, profession, gender in a male-dominated field, your company's old reputation for poor service or high prices, a competitor's twelve-year incumbency — and ask directly what problem they see. Then confirm the answer three times, in a light register if the room suits it.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have personal history that this specific bias shows up; reputational damage predating the current relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are guessing without experience — baggage is the sole sanctioned exception to the system's ban on assumptions, and a wrong guess plants the objection yourself; it can also end the deal on the spot, which the author treats as a cheap early loss.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — several scripted exchanges plus a list of baggage items from real negotiations.

### [NEGO:WANTS-AS-DECISIONS-CAMP] Restate every want as a decision they can refuse
**Move:** List what you want at each stage, then rewrite each item as an accept-or-reject decision for the counterpart: not "we want your termite report" but "we want you to either submit or refuse to submit the report". Frame wants as means you control, never as the signed deal, which you do not.
**APPLY WHEN:** Planning any stage; teams that cannot articulate the next ask beyond "close it".
**AVOID WHEN:** Forcing a binary early invites a premature no on items that would have resolved themselves; the source states no contraindication, so this one is the lens's addition.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter converts an actual want list item by item.

### [NEGO:WANT-PROTECT-IDEA-CAMP] Secure your work before you show it
**Move:** Before presenting an idea, ask who would be involved in deciding on it and who advises them financially or legally. Then propose the sequence explicitly: I'll show it; if you like it we go together to your attorney and accountant; if you don't, we drop it — fair? And ask for broker-of-record style protection so the idea cannot be used without you being paid, confirming their comfort three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** Brokered deals, consulting concepts, anything where your value is the idea itself and can be lifted.
**AVOID WHEN:** Legal instruments would be disproportionate to the stage; asking for signatures too early reads as distrust in relationship-first markets.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a counter-example where a noncommittal counterpart took the idea to a third party and inserted himself as broker of record.

### [NEGO:NEXT-STEP-LOCKED-CAMP] Close every contact with the next step, triple-confirmed
**Move:** Never end a meeting, call or thread on "call me in a few weeks". Before anything ends, state what happens next — who does what and what triggers the next contact — get it agreed, and verify it three times. Worked shape: describe the problem, say what you want from them (that they propose the solution), and hand them the bridge, e.g. once they have an offer your team will meet them anywhere on short notice and they just call back and name the place.
**APPLY WHEN:** The end of every contact in any live deal with more than one touch; long multi-thread enterprise deals with many parallel conversations; especially when small talk has taken over the final minutes.
**AVOID WHEN:** A deal you have decided to end — use the exit move instead; over-applying it to trivial confirmations becomes bureaucratic friction in fast transactional cycles; and pressing for a commitment the counterpart cannot yet make manufactures a soft yes, precisely what the system distrusts.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — mandatory practice across coached deals; credited with holding together an engagement the author describes as roughly 500 sessions.

### [NEGO:STALL-DIAGNOSTIC-CAMP] Three questions when a deal goes nowhere
**Move:** When a negotiation stops moving, do not push on the counterpart — run the diagnostic on your own setup: is the mission and purpose actually valid, do you genuinely understand their pain, and do they themselves see that pain? Treat a stall as a defect in one of those three, never as a closing problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** Silence, repeated slipped timelines, meetings without agendas, deals stuck at the same stage.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stall has a mundane external cause (budget freeze, reorg, holidays) — the introspective loop then manufactures doubt about a healthy deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the standing troubleshooting sequence.

### "No", concessions, price and terms

### [NEGO:INVITE-NO-CAMP] Establish the right to refuse as a ground rule
**Move:** Say early and explicitly that a no is welcome at any point, that you will not take it personally, and that you would rather hear it than a maybe; repeat the invitation at each decision point, including at the end of a stalled meeting. The operating definition behind it: a negotiation is an agreement between parties all of whom hold a veto.
**APPLY WHEN:** Openings of any negotiation; stalled deals full of maybes; counterparts conditioned to avoid confrontation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as reverse-psychology theatre — if you would not actually accept the refusal it is a manipulation and gets read as one; also risky with counterparts who take any negative framing as insult (the program director who answered a plain no with a thirty-minute speech about disrespect).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring across client engagements in the US, Japan and Korea plus the author's own cold-call practice; outcomes self-reported.

### [NEGO:KILL-MAYBE-CAMP] Refuse to operate on "maybe"
**Move:** Treat a maybe as no information at all; convert it into a decision by asking what would have to be true for a no, and if you cannot get past it, disengage rather than continue investing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deals that keep advancing in tone but never in decisions; counterparts culturally or personally averse to explicit refusal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The delay is a genuine process constraint (budget cycle, legal review) rather than avoidance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as especially costly with Japanese counterparts; no data.

### [NEGO:EARLY-YES-ALARM-CAMP] Treat an early yes as a warning
**Move:** When the counterpart agrees early and easily, do not celebrate or start forecasting; assume qualifiers (an if, a but, a however) are coming and press for the specific decisions behind the yes before you move on anything.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enthusiastic early agreement, particularly from professional buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** Simple transactional purchases where the yes is exactly what it looks like and interrogating it kills momentum.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — mechanism argued (adrenaline → neediness → later compromise), no case data.

### [NEGO:NO-EARLY-COMPROMISE-CAMP] Defer every compromise until it is proven necessary
**Move:** Treat each concession as requiring a demonstrated reason you can name, not a gesture of goodwill; if you cannot state why the concession is needed now, do not make it, and hold the position while continuing to talk.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early stage, before you know the counterpart's real problem, budget, or decision process.
**AVOID WHEN:** A deadline you genuinely own is expiring; regulated collective-bargaining contexts where good-faith bargaining is legally mandated (the book explicitly exempts these).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Silicon Valley licensing negotiation where an initial $400,000 offer from a proxy buyer ended at $8 million.

### [NEGO:NO-CLOSING-CAMP] Abolish closing as an objective
**Move:** Remove closing pushes, urgency creation and trial closes from your sequence; let the deal assemble through a series of explicit decisions, and never let "get this signed" be the goal you carry into the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex, multi-month deals; sophisticated counterparts who recognize and punish closing technique.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely transactional purchases where the buyer expects a clean ask and reads its absence as incompetence; also risky where your own organization measures activity by closes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a rule with war stories, no comparative data on close rates.

### [NEGO:NO-SAVING-CAMP] Never take responsibility for the other side's decision
**Move:** Let the counterpart sit with their own hard decision — do not soften it, do not invent an easier alternative, do not concede to relieve their discomfort; silence during their deliberation is your job. Saving them transfers the responsibility, and later the blame, to you.
**APPLY WHEN:** The moment the other side must decide; team negotiations where a junior member may crack.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart genuinely lacks information you hold — withholding decision-relevant facts to preserve pressure is a different and worse act than declining to rescue them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a team member unilaterally offered 2% in a billion-dollar negotiation in Tokyo to break a silence; the concession had to be retracted the next day.

### [NEGO:NO-NURTURE-INTERROGATIVE-CAMP] Refuse, nurture, then hand the problem back
**Move:** Deliver the refusal plainly, immediately soften it with a statement of wanting to keep working together, and finish with an open interrogative that puts the search for an alternative on them — roughly: I can't do that, I really can't, but I do want to work with you, so how do we find another way? Three beats: no, nurture, interrogative-led question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discount and scope demands you intend to refuse; anywhere a flat no would otherwise end the conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** The refusal is final and no alternative exists — the trailing question then invites a negotiation you do not want; also weak if used on requests you would in fact accept, which trains the counterpart to push.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — given as a canonical drill script for new adopters.

### [NEGO:CONCESSION-SUSPICION-CAMP] Read over-concession as a competence signal
**Move:** As buyer, when the seller concedes on everything, treat the pattern as evidence they may not be able to deliver; as seller, remember that stacking concessions can lose the deal outright rather than win it.
**APPLY WHEN:** A supplier's terms improve on every axis at once; your own team is preparing a best-everything bid.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has a legitimate strategic reason for aggressive pricing (land-grab, reference account) — the inference is heuristic, not proof.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a client conceded price, terms, delivery, service and free equipment and lost the deal because the buyer's CEO read the desperation as delivery risk.

### [NEGO:REFUSE-WHIPSAW-CAMP] Exit the price-down auction by declining to bid further
**Move:** When a large buyer plays suppliers against each other for successive reductions, state that you will not participate in further cuts and are therefore probably not the right supplier — which hands them the refusal and removes you from the auction.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-vendor bake-offs with repeated concession rounds; you have differentiated capability the buyer actually needs.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are genuinely substitutable and the buyer can proceed without you — the move costs the deal outright; also poor when procurement is bound by a scored process you cannot exit and re-enter.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "Bonanza, Inc." withdrew from further reductions, the multinational began sharing information with them, and Bonanza won the project.

### [NEGO:FULL-RATE-ASK-CAMP] State full terms once, then explicitly permit refusal
**Move:** Present the whole package you actually want (rate, minimum engagement, travel, payment terms) in one pass, add that they should say no if it is too much with no hard feelings, offer to point them elsewhere — then stop talking.
**APPLY WHEN:** Underpricing driven by fear; expert, consulting and service pricing; inbound requests where you hold scarce expertise.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your terms are not defensible against market comparables — the move relies on the number being in line, not merely bold.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Japan-focused legal expert habitually billing about $100/hour asked $500/hour with a two-day minimum plus first-class travel and a car, roughly $8,000 for sixteen hours wired in advance; accepted about 45 minutes later.

### [NEGO:NUMBERS-LATE-CAMP] Treat numbers as limitations and defer them
**Move:** Hold prices and quantities back until the picture is built. As seller, do not seek the buyer's intended spend in the first meeting; as buyer, do not seek the seller's price. First wants should concern their baggage, vision, pain and budgets.
**APPLY WHEN:** Consultative deals where value is being established; complex configurations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Simple transactions, where the author himself allows the exception; and buyers who require indicative pricing to justify continuing — refusing then burns credibility. This is the sharpest anchoring conflict with the concession-trading school; keep the two apart rather than blending them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client's unit cost moved from a pre-system quote of $185 to $290 after adopting the method; single self-reported data point.

### [NEGO:PRICE-HIGH-TO-BE-SEEN-CAMP] Price high enough that value becomes visible
**Move:** Recognize that a price set too low inside the buyer's frame of reference makes value invisible, while a high price sends them looking for the value. Set price to be seen, not merely to cover margin.
**APPLY WHEN:** Premium positioning; buyers with a well-heeled reference frame; differentiated product.
**AVOID WHEN:** Commodity or procurement-led buying where price is the whole scorecard; a high price with nothing behind it just ends the conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/info-product/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Ely Callaway's Big Bertha driver launched around $400, far above industry standard; a marketing story, not a controlled result.

### [NEGO:POS-EXPECT-BAIT-CAMP] Refuse to price against an inflated volume
**Move:** When you hear encouragement plus a large number ("we'd like to give you the whole order"), do not compute the total, do not quote your best discounted price, and do not talk price at all before you understand the deal. Verify the stated volume against their real capacity or consumption. Expect the sequence: big number → your best price → shrunken volume at that price → disappointment framing if you refuse.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement-led enterprise deals; consolidation or single-supplier pitches; any time volume is dangled before terms are set.
**AVOID WHEN:** The volume is genuinely verified — the move is about unverified numbers, not about refusing volume pricing per se.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one coached case: 33,000 units dangled at roughly $1,000 each against a worldwide capacity of 22,000 and a real requirement of 3,000.

### [NEGO:NEG-EXPECT-ANCHOR-CAMP] Hold the sheet against an opening stipulation
**Move:** When the counterparty opens by stipulating a volume-discount price for a single unit, treat the number as an early number of no significance. Decline calmly, cite the absence of any mandate in your M&P to break the rate sheet, and offer a nurtured alternative framed as an interrogative (buy used from the partner who does order at volume, or add a unit to their next order) — then stop and wait.
**APPLY WHEN:** Capital-equipment and high-ACV sales where discount tiers are public or inferable; first-contact lowball anchors.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your pricing genuinely has no defensible structure, or the alternative you suggest loses you the deal to a channel partner.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single typed case, equipment at $1.7M list against a $1.3M volume price demanded for one unit.

### [NEGO:FAVOR-LEDGER-CAMP] Convert an accommodation into an explicit ask, with a refusal option
**Move:** When you have absorbed real cost to accommodate the counterpart and the need then disappears, state plainly what you did, ask for the specific compensating commitment, and add that they may decline with no hard feelings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Expedites, scope stretches, or emergency delivery you performed on request.
**AVOID WHEN:** The accommodation was standard service or you never flagged its cost at the time — retroactive invoicing of goodwill reads as bad faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few · offer=product/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a machinery maker rearranged production for a customer, the customer's need vanished, and the customer nevertheless agreed to take two older systems.

### [NEGO:PACKAGE-COMMIT-FIRST-CAMP] Secure accept/reject commitment before revealing the offer
**Move:** In senior hiring, tell the candidate you will commit to a top-of-industry package but will not disclose it until they commit to either accept or reject it, stating plainly that you do not want the number used to start a bidding war with their current employer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Executive and senior-officer recruiting where counter-offers are the standard loss mode.
**AVOID WHEN:** Candidate-scarce markets or junior roles where opacity simply loses the candidate; the source concedes companies fear exactly this, which is why they leak the number.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's stated pet peeve and standing coaching advice, no outcome data.

### Presentations

### [NEGO:DEFLECT-PRESENTATION-CAMP] Answer a presentation request with questions
**Move:** When asked to present or to respond to an RFP, say honestly you do not know how to do that yet — you would need to know where they stand, what they need, what interests them. Then ask what is driving the request: why they want your product now, how you could possibly compete with their long-standing incumbent, why they invited you at all. Keep returning to their motive rather than filling the slot.
**APPLY WHEN:** RFP-driven markets; a request to present arriving before you understand their pain or decision process.
**AVOID WHEN:** Formal procurement where non-response disqualifies you; and where the request is genuine buying intent, flat refusal reads as evasive. The author concedes some official-looking artifact may still be produced near the end.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a commercial real estate client who stopped producing RFP responses; reported to work for city zoning boards and conglomerates alike, no numbers.

### [NEGO:REVERSE-PRESENTATION-CAMP] Make them present to you
**Move:** When the counterpart finally asks for your proposal, suggest the reverse: it makes more sense for them to lay out their thinking, direction and requirements first, after which you respond with something tailored. Their exposition becomes the brief.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late in a long cycle where you have already found decision makers, painted pain and built budgets; large counterparties with internal complexity.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not earned the standing — asked early it reads as refusal to do the work; also fails against counterparts who deliberately withhold requirements as leverage.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal reported at a couple of hundred million dollars after roughly ten months of prior work; self-reported, single case.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-AGENDA-CAMP] Agenda-gated presentation with "maybe" banned
**Move:** Before agreeing to present, negotiate a written agenda for the day: what will be covered, what will deliberately not be covered, who attends, and the explicit understanding that the outcome is a yes or a no. Get that last point agreed in advance so "we'll think about it" has no landing space; a refusal to agree is itself the information you needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** A formal demo or pitch is mandated by procurement or your own management; late-stage deals where the presentation is the last remaining ritual.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early discovery, where a hard yes/no gate forces a premature no on a deal that just needs more vision work; cultures where forcing a same-day verdict reads as coercion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing coaching practice for clients forced into presentations, no dataset.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-QUALIFY-CAMP] Never present to the unqualified
**Move:** Confirm the people in the room are the actual decision makers before you show anything. Presenting your reasoning, pricing logic and differentiation to non-deciders scatters your material where it can be used against you — the same failure mode as spilling your beans in the lobby.
**APPLY WHEN:** An intermediary or champion asks you to "just present to the team first"; RFP-style processes where the audience list is vague.
**AVOID WHEN:** An internal champion genuinely needs ammunition and access to deciders is structurally impossible — flat refusal there can end the deal; also a poor fit in transactional self-serve motions where nobody is a decision maker in this sense.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — restated from the decision-maker chapter as a standing rule of the system.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-SUBTRACT-CAMP] Addition by subtraction
**Move:** Cut everything from the deck that does not address a pain the buyer has actually voiced — if the only stated concern is load capacity, say nothing about width; if the buyer never mentioned lawns, leave the fine lawn unmentioned and raise it later if it becomes relevant. Then sequence the survivors by the buyer's stated order of importance and name that order out loud.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have real discovery notes and can rank the buyer's concerns; competitive situations where a kitchen-sink deck invites objections you never needed to face.
**AVOID WHEN:** Discovery was thin — subtraction then leaves almost nothing, which the author treats as the signal that you should not be presenting yet; regulated or technical evaluations where omitting a required capability disqualifies you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the tire and house-lawn examples framed with a sports adage.

### [NEGO:EVAL-CRITERIA-CAMP] Ask how they evaluate, then build the demo to that
**Move:** Before assembling any demonstration material, ask directly how they evaluate candidates or options. Take each answer literally however narrow it seems (one coach cared mainly about vertical jump, another about speed, another about bench press, others had a hard height floor) and cut a separate version per counterpart against their stated criterion, not against what you find impressive about yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-buyer or multi-account processes where each evaluator scores differently; demos, proof-of-concept scoping, reference selection, case-study choice.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stated criteria are a smokescreen for a pre-selected vendor — building to a fake rubric wastes the whole budget; also when criteria are set by a committee and no single answer represents the group.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the football-recruit story; outcome reported by the author, not measured.

### Commitment, reversal and life after signature

### [NEGO:PUSHBACK-DECISION-CAMP] Slide the decision back before you accept it
**Move:** At the moment the counterpart commits — hands over the signed paper or the payment — push it back across the table and ask whether this is really what they want, stating you do not want them to proceed unless they are sure. Give them a clean route to no at the point most sellers would grab the yes. Aimed at the next negotiation (referrals, renewals) rather than at this one.
**APPLY WHEN:** The decision is large relative to the buyer's world, remorse is plausible, and your economics depend on referrals or renewals rather than one-off capture.
**AVOID WHEN:** Thin-margin transactional sales where re-opening a closed decision simply loses it; vulnerable or pressured counterparts for whom the theatrical re-ask reads as manipulation — the source case involves an elderly client committing a large share of her savings, exactly where the exit must be a real invitation, not a rhetorical one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product/info-product · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the life-insurance story (three checks of $100,000, a 19-year-old rep and a 67-year-old client), outcome self-reported.

### [NEGO:NEXT-DECISION-CAMP] Answer a bad decision with the next decision
**Move:** Treat any concession or agreement you should not have made as reversible: acknowledge the mistake plainly, state you cannot proceed on those terms, and reopen — replacing the negotiating team if the individual has burned credibility. A negotiation is a series of decisions, so a bad one is answered by the next one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discounts or terms agreed under pressure or without authority; contracts already losing money.
**AVOID WHEN:** The agreement is binding and reversal exposes you legally or reputationally; used habitually it makes your commitments worthless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a 28% discount agreed at near-breakeven was reopened the following week and mostly clawed back.

### [NEGO:APOLOGY-REOPEN-CAMP] Reopen a losing contract by owning your own incompetence
**Move:** To restart a contract you are losing money on, call the counterpart and credit them with having negotiated well while taking the blame for your own weak negotiating and the poor supplier position it created; ask to fix it from there.
**APPLY WHEN:** Contracts unprofitable enough to threaten the business; internal resistance framed as "it would look unprofessional to reopen".
**AVOID WHEN:** The loss is recoverable through delivery or scope changes; repeated use destroys your credibility as a counterparty.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — "Network, Inc.", losing about $100,000 per machine shipped and near bankruptcy; the counterpart later became its largest customer.

### [NEGO:REOPEN-BAD-DEAL-CAMP] Renegotiate inside the contract term when the deal is destroying you
**Move:** Do not wait for contractual expiry on a deal that is bleeding; take the renegotiation up the counterpart's hierarchy, present the replacement terms, and explicitly invite them to refuse.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-year agreements losing money annually; internal objections of the form "you cannot do that in this market or country".
**AVOID WHEN:** Relationship and reputational cost genuinely exceeds the loss, or the counterpart has leverage to enforce the original terms and punish the attempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a US company reopened a distribution deal with a Japanese giant roughly eighteen months into the effort and five years before it was contractually challengeable; accepted after a recess, relationship reportedly became profitable.

### [NEGO:SECOND-NEGOTIATION-CAMP] Treat the signature as the start of the next round
**Move:** Plan the post-signature phase as a fresh negotiation with its own M&P, behavior goals, agendas and next steps. Contracts get broken and nearly every contract creates future obligations — something must be delivered, exchanged, guaranteed or made to work. Celebrate, but do not treat signature as the end state; when the counterparty reopens terms, resume the same behaviors instead of reacting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large corporate procurement and supply-systems-management groups; implementation, delivery, renewal and expansion phases; any deal with penalty clauses or ongoing service obligations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applying adversarial framing to a delivery relationship that needs collaboration — this is where the book's systemic distrust reads as paranoia and can poison an account already won; the stance can also normalize scope creep if "done" is never defined commercially.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the nature of contracts and from how tough multinational negotiators are said to operate, without named cases.

### [NEGO:FADEAWAY-CAMP] Exit without burning the bridge
**Move:** When your budget and mission say the deal will not happen, end it calmly — a low-key "thanks, but not this time, maybe later" — instead of a grievance letter or a confrontation. Treat the urge to burn the bridge as a symptom: it appears only when you are emotionally over-invested and buys nothing but short-term self-validation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Budget (time, money, emotional investment) is exhausted; the mission clearly cannot be served; you feel ill-treated and want to say so.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart acted in bad faith in a way others in your market must be warned about, or a contractual or legal record of the breakdown is required — silence there is a real cost the book does not price.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing recommendation to clients across sales and merger-scale deals.

### [NEGO:FAIR-VALUE-TOPUP-CAMP] Ask the supplier whether the deal worked, then fix it
**Move:** After delivery, ask the other party directly whether they are satisfied with the deal they made. If they report a genuine loss (underestimated cost, mis-scoped work), offer a concrete top-up that covers the loss and leaves a fair profit, and name the figure yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the buyer or the larger party; ongoing supplier or contractor relationships where you will need goodwill for years; handshake deals with no written protection for the other side.
**AVOID WHEN:** The claimed loss is a negotiating gambit or a repeated pattern of underbidding — the move rewards it; it is also in open tension with the same book's ban on saving the adversary, so treat it as a post-delivery buyer-side move, never a mid-negotiation concession.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Go Home Lake cabin story, an $800 top-up on a handshake deal.

### Adoption drills

### [NEGO:RULE-DRILL-CAMP] Test-drive rules in risk-free situations
**Move:** Each day or week pick a small set of rules, review them in the morning, and deliberately use them in low-stakes situations during the day. Rotate the sets rather than mastering one at a time, since the rules interlock. The goal at this stage is only self-conviction that they work.
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding a rep onto this method; a seller who intellectually accepts the system but freezes when it is time to say no.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied inside a live high-stakes deal as a practice ground — the drill stage is explicitly meant to be risk-free.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard client onboarding sequence.

### [NEGO:COLUMBO-DRILL-CAMP] Practise being unokay on purpose
**Move:** In a harmless situation, engineer a small visible imperfection — pen runs dry, device battery dies — and work through it without embarrassment. Rehearsal only: lowering your own okayness so the counterpart's rises, practised where nothing is at stake.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sellers whose polish intimidates counterparts into guardedness; deliberate practice contexts.
**AVOID WHEN:** First meetings where competence is being screened, or with counterparts who read fumbling as unreliability rather than as safety.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a routine client drill drawn from the Columbo material.

### [NEGO:FIVE-STEP-START-CAMP] Five-step setup for your first system-run deal
**Move:** Pick one straightforward, non-critical live deal and set it up in five moves: (1) fix an M&P stated in the counterpart's world, aimed at letting them see and decide; (2) establish their real pain through questions that build vision; (3) assess all three budgets on both sides and keep monitoring how they shape decisions; (4) verify you are dealing with the real decision makers; (5) never make a call or send an email without a written agenda. The steps overlap in practice rather than running strictly in order.
**APPLY WHEN:** First deliberate application of the method; also usable as an audit checklist on an existing deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your most critical open deal — the author explicitly says not to start there; also mismatched to high-velocity transactional cycles where per-email agendas cost more than they return.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's prescribed client ramp.

### [NEGO:33-RULES-CAMP] The Thirty-three Rules as a pre-call checklist
**Move:** Use the deliberately unordered rule list as a fast pre- or post-call audit. Load-bearing entries: every party holds a veto so a no is always available on both sides; your job is respect and effectiveness, not being liked; results are not valid goals, only behavior and activity you control; money is irrelevant to a valid M&P; never leak information anywhere, including the lobby; never make a call without an agenda; the M&P lives in their world; maximize payside activity; you want it, you don't need it; you cannot save the counterpart; only one party gets to feel okay and it is them; action follows vision; respect the blocker; clarify every agreement point by point and seal it with 3+; the clearer the pain picture the easier the decision; value multiplies as time, energy, money and emotion are invested; no talking; always let them save face; the best presentation is the one they never see; the negotiation ends only when you decide it does; no is good, yes is bad, maybe is worse; absolutely no closing; dance with the tiger; your greatest strength is your greatest weakness; paint the pain; decisions are 100 percent emotional; interrogative-led questions drive vision; nurture; no assumptions, no expectations, blank slate only; know every decision maker; pay forward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pre-call prep, call review, coaching debriefs.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as universal law across all deal types — several rules (no closing, no talking, adversary-only okayness, "yes is bad") directly contradict the cooperative school this lens also carries, and the conflict must be surfaced per deal rather than averaged away.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a summary list restating the book's principles; no support attaches to the list itself.

## Where this book stops working

- **Transactional, self-serve and micro-ACV motions.** The system assumes a multi-month, multi-decision negotiation. "Absolutely no closing", per-email agendas, blocker ladders, 3+ confirmations and daily behavioral tracks cost more than they return below roughly mid-ACV; a buyer expecting a clean ask reads its absence as incompetence (see NO-CLOSING-CAMP, AGENDA-FIVE-SLOTS-CAMP, FIVE-STEP-START-CAMP).
- **Formal, scored procurement.** Where a non-response disqualifies you or procurement holds a real veto, DEFLECT-PRESENTATION-CAMP, PURCHASING-OUT-OF-LOOP-CAMP and REFUSE-WHIPSAW-CAMP lose the deal on process grounds rather than on merit.
- **Genuinely cooperative counterparts.** The adversary frame is the book's engine and also its failure mode: WINWIN-TRAP-CAMP, BEAN-GUARD-CAMP, FRIENDSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP and SECOND-NEGOTIATION-CAMP applied to a partner who is actually cooperating manufacture the conflict they predict. The bias is systemic, not incidental — the author blames the "weak win-win negotiator" rather than the predatory buyer for predatory outcomes, which makes the frame self-sealing.
- **Competence-gated sales.** Unokayness (NOT-OKAY-CAMP, PROP-FUMBLE-CAMP, COLUMBO-DRILL-CAMP, ESTEEM-VS-OKAY-CAMP) is unusable where credibility is the product: technical evaluation, expert testimony, regulated diligence.
- **Real dependence.** "You never need this deal" is false for founders, single-customer-dependent suppliers and undercapitalized vendors. MONEY-BUDGET-WRITE-OFF-CAMP and BUDGET-BUILD-THEIRS-CAMP are far easier for the well-capitalized side; a small supplier facing a large buyer cannot outspend them in imposed cost. The honest version is to fix the dependency, not to perform indifference.
- **Symmetric opponents.** The book concedes the toolkit exists to pry information out of untrained counterparties and would be largely unnecessary against another practitioner of the same system — an unusual admission that the method is asymmetric by design.
- **Forecasting organizations.** GOAL-BEHAVIOR-ONLY-CAMP offers no substitute for a forecast; a rep told to abandon result targets inside a quota-run org is being handed a career problem, not a technique.
- **Detection and legality.** PLANT-ASSUMPTION-CAMP and NO-RISK-ANSWER-CAMP are deliberate vagueness with reputational and, where a figure will be relied upon, legal exposure. RESEARCH-CAMP's personal-details end is an ethical boundary the book crosses in its own illustration.
- **Regulated bargaining.** The book explicitly exempts contexts where good-faith bargaining is legally mandated from NO-EARLY-COMPROMISE-CAMP.

## What the book debunks

- **Win-win as a system.** Treated as a mood, not a method: it gives no basis for judging whether 27% or 24% was the right discount, and a win-win trophy from a large customer is read as evidence of a failed negotiation. The *Getting to Yes* "wise agreement" is attacked as undefinable — who decides which interests are legitimate, or what "fair", "durable" and "community interests" mean — and as smuggling in compromise.
- **Closing.** Trial closes, urgency and "close, close, close" exhortation are named as the behavior sophisticated counterparts punish. A signed deal proves nothing about whether it was the best deal.
- **"No burns bridges."** Inverted: the inability to accept a no is what burns them. "Maybe" is claimed to protect against a wrong decision but delivers paralysis. An early, easy yes is a trap later qualified away.
- **Saving the relationship.** Reframed as the question of how much money you will pay for friendliness; the substitute goal is respect and effectiveness, not friendship.
- **Neediness.** Not merely a bad look but a buying signal trained buyers manufacture (inflated mega-orders and alliances) before introducing demands. Over-compromise can lose a deal outright, as delivery risk.
- **Warm referrals as easy.** Named as the fastest route to losing discipline.
- **Quantitative goals.** Called dangerous even when met — hitting the number early breeds coasting, missing it breeds harder work on the wrong activity. "Winning isn't everything" is corrected toward Lombardi's other line about the will to prepare.
- **"Positive attitude."** Reframed as disguised positive expectation, and therefore a liability; excitement cannot be sustained, so an excited buyer is a remorse risk. The sharpest break with conventional sales coaching.
- **Creating pain.** Called amateur hour — the negotiator builds the counterpart's vision of pain that already exists. And knowing their pain while they do not is not one-up: an unaware counterpart has no incentive to decide.
- **Presentations.** The claim is that effective negotiations survive presentations but never require them; a deck manufactures objections by putting the counterpart in critique mode, and production values cannot rescue content aimed at the wrong pain. "Maybe" allowed as a presentation outcome converts it into a free option for the buyer.
- **Topic-list and hidden agendas.** Topic lists get abandoned in practice; deals produced by ambush do not stick.
- **"They always rubber-stamp me."** Almost nobody decides alone; national stereotypes of decision structure (US vertical, Japan horizontal) are rejected as mixed and unpredictable — a janitorial staff held an effective veto over an athletic department's schedule.
- **Budget as a line-item cost sheet.** Money is one of three budgets and the cheapest to lose; sunk cost is the mechanism that corrupts decisions (NASA's pre-*Challenger* O-ring decisions cited as the extreme case).
- **Fixed self-image.** Rejected while conceding early experience matters; high self-esteem is claimed not to require diminishing the other side.
- **Documented failure modes inside the book:** a plain no triggering a thirty-minute monologue about disrespect (conceded as the most common early reaction); an internal colleague turning saboteur out of a desire for approval; a unilateral 2% concession offered to break a silence and retracted the next day; an idea spilled without an agenda and taken to a third party; a technical evaluator defending roughly $100M of installed equipment by sabotaging meetings; a sales manager's closing instinct nearly breaking a completed decision.

## Blind spots of this book

- **No comparative evidence, by construction.** Every claim rests on the author's own coached cases, reported by him, with the counterfactual never available: no control group, no win-rate comparison, no failed-client accounting. The evidence ceiling for the whole book is practitioner-n-many, and several load-bearing rules ("never backfires", "decisions are 100 percent emotional", the ~800-hour figure) are bare assertion with unnamed sources.
- **Commercial funnel inside the argument.** The diagnosis ("you cannot hold this discipline alone, you'll need a coach") routes to the author's own coaching, symposiums and team training. Selection is invisible: clients who abandoned the system do not appear.
- **Self-sealing framing.** Objections are pre-absorbed — thinking the positive strip line is dangerous is diagnosed as being stuck in closing mode; 3+ is claimed to be nearly impossible to overdo. Both immunize the technique against disconfirmation.
- **Cooperation has no model.** The system can describe exploitation and refusal but has almost no vocabulary for a counterpart who is genuinely trying to build something jointly; the one cooperative move in the book (FAIR-VALUE-TOPUP-CAMP) sits in open contradiction with its own ban on saving the adversary, and the book does not resolve it.
- **Power asymmetry is under-modelled.** Advice to write off budgets, walk away, refuse auctions and impose costs assumes you can afford the walk. The structurally weak seller — the actual protagonist of the book's opening — is given a psychology fix for a capital problem.
- **No account of trust as an asset.** Detection costs are never priced: what happens to a relationship, a reference base or a market reputation once the elastic-word technique, the staged purse drop or the manufactured callback friction is recognized.
- **Organizational fit ignored.** The book gives the individual negotiator rules that conflict with how sales organizations run (forecasts, close rates, activity metrics) and offers only "explain why they're invalid or leave".
- **Historical and cultural analogies do no evidential work.** Kosovo, Vietnam bombing pauses, the 1982 air traffic controllers, the MLB umpires, Langley's aircraft, Ho Chi Minh's table shape and a film scene from *Legal Eagles* are used as if they were negotiation data; the "Japanese always research" generalization is an unsupported stereotype.
- **Silent on modern motions.** Written in 2002: no PLG or self-serve, no multi-threaded SaaS renewal mechanics, no asynchronous or remote-first negotiation beyond a preference for email, no procurement software or scored e-sourcing.
