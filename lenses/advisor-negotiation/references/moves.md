# advisor-negotiation — moves

The negotiation layer of the deal, carried by a **deliberately contradictory pair**:
`-GTY` = the principled / interest-based school (Fisher, Ury & Patton, *Getting to Yes*, canon,
evidence ceiling `external-research`); `-CAMP` = the refusal-discipline school (Jim Camp,
*Start with No*, counter-canon, evidence ceiling `practitioner-n-many`).

Suffixes are load-bearing and never dropped. A `-GTY` move and a `-CAMP` move are never merged
even when they rhyme, and their agreement is never counted as two confirmations — it is one
layer with two opposed schools. Where they give opposite orders, see
**Where the schools collide** at the end of this file.

---

## Stage A — Deal strategy, mission and preparation

### [NEGO:WISETEST-GTY] Three-criteria test for any deal process
**Move:** Grade the negotiation on three axes rather than on the number: does it produce a durable agreement meeting both sides' legitimate interests, is it cheap in time and decisions, does the working relationship survive.
**APPLY WHEN:** Retrospective on a won or stalled deal; choosing between haggling and a merits-based approach.
**AVOID WHEN:** Price-only commodity purchases where the relationship axis is noise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a definitional standard, never measured.

### [NEGO:FOURPASS-GTY] Run People/Interests/Options/Criteria three times
**Move:** Take the four elements through analysis (diagnose perceptions, hostile emotion, unclear communication, both sides' interests, options and criteria already visible), planning (rank interests, set objectives, generate more options and standards), and discussion (surface perception gaps, exchange interests, build options jointly, agree the standard).
**APPLY WHEN:** Deal prep and call debrief on any multi-meeting cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** Micro-deals where prep costs more than the spread.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — the authors' teaching framework.

### [NEGO:APPROACHTRIAGE-GTY] Five-question triage: haggle or go principled
**Move:** Before choosing an approach, score how costly an arbitrary outcome would be, how complex the issues are, how much the ongoing relationship matters, how entrenched their expectations are and how long shifting them would take, and where you are in the sequence — plus the spillover onto how others will negotiate with you later.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deal-strategy prep; deciding how much process to spend on an account.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trivial single-issue transactions with competitive alternatives on both sides — the triage outcosts the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — GM/UAW cited as needing four contracts to restructure their negotiations.

### [NEGO:GOODFAITHTEST-GTY] Self-test a tactic, and name the game out loud
**Move:** Run three internal checks on any tactic — would you use it on a close friend, would a published account embarrass you, would it suit a hero or a villain — and separately consider opening the negotiation by asking aloud which game you are both playing: cheap wise agreement, or stubbornness contest.
**APPLY WHEN:** Setting your own tactical line; framing the first meeting of an adversarial account.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart reads the meta-question as naivety and an opening to exploit.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a normative self-check.

### [NEGO:WINDFALL-GTY] Four-question test before pocketing an unfair advantage
**Move:** When you can take materially more than you think fair, first price the excess above what you could justify, ask whether the deal will actually hold and what enforcement would cost, count the damage to this and other relationships, and check your own conscience — then consider restructuring so they feel they did you a favour.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart is visibly under-informed, or offers terms far above market.
**AVOID WHEN:** The premium is legitimately earned — over-applying the test argues you out of fair value.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — moral and practical reasoning plus the Kashmir rug anecdote.

### [NEGO:CHANNELPICK-GTY] Choose the channel deliberately; warm it before substance
**Move:** Route emotional or relationship-laden exchanges face-to-face, then video, voice, text in that order; on remote channels invest in personal connection first, make reasoning explicit in email, hunt ambiguities before sending, and switch channel the moment a reply signals an unexpected bad reaction.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any multi-touch deal, especially remote or asynchronous.
**AVOID WHEN:** Speed beats fidelity on a low-stakes item.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — Valley, Moag & Bazerman, *J. Econ. Behavior and Organization* 34:211 (1998); review by Nadler & Shestowsky (2006).

### [NEGO:GETINSTEP-GTY] Get in step without stereotyping
**Move:** Adapt to the counterpart's pacing, formality, proximity, whether oral or written agreements bind, directness, time horizon, scope of relationship, public vs private setting, status conventions and rigidity of commitments — while refusing to infer the individual from the group.
**APPLY WHEN:** Cross-border, cross-industry or unfamiliar counterparts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Adaptation slides into mimicry, or group traits get treated as predictive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — gender claims hedged and footnoted to Gilligan (1982), with the data said to cut both ways.

### [NEGO:WHETHERBATNA-GTY] Decide whether to negotiate at all by comparing both BATNAs
**Move:** Set the effort by how good your no-deal path is and how likely talks are to beat it — strong alternative plus unpromising talks means invest little; weak alternative means invest more even when talks look poor. Analyse their alternative too, and check you are talking to the right party at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deciding whether to pursue an account, or whether the person in the room owns the decision you need.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never — but the output is only as honest as both alternative estimates.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a bank stuck for months with the owner-managers of a bankrupt energy company; settled in 24 hours once the judge's interest was engaged.

### [NEGO:BACKFROMCLOSE-GTY] Design closure backward from their justification
**Move:** Picture the signed agreement and its implementation, then work backward: which issues must be resolved, and above all what concrete sentence the other side would use to justify the deal to their own constituents. Ask the same of your own side, then design terms that let both sentences be true.
**APPLY WHEN:** Committee deals where a champion must sell the outcome internally.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early exploratory calls — premature closure design narrows the option set.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

### [NEGO:MP-FOUNDATION-CAMP] Write a mission and purpose and consult it per decision
**Move:** Draft an explicit long-term mission for the business or the negotiation, then decide each contested item by whether it serves that statement rather than by the size of the concession on the table.
**APPLY WHEN:** Scope creep, opportunistic diversification, long negotiations where you keep losing the thread.
**AVOID WHEN:** The statement is a goal in disguise ("make a million by 21"), or is written and never consulted.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Midwest artist who used a written mission to decide which of several accumulated businesses to keep.

### [NEGO:MP-ADVERSARY-WORLD-CAMP] Write the mission inside the counterparty's world
**Move:** Draft one sentence about what you want the other side to see and conclude about *their* business, in their benefits; rewrite any version containing your revenue, commission or survival until the subject of the sentence is them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Opening or re-opening any deal, especially a renegotiation where you cannot compel a reopening.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as self-hypnosis in place of real leverage or product fit — the book's own Quaker/Snapple example shows a plausible fit failing on distribution reality.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated across coaching clients (supplier renegotiation, booking, recruiting, job interview).

### [NEGO:MP-SEE-DECIDE-CAMP] Phrase the mission as "see and decide"
**Move:** Build the statement around the verb pair see-and-decide — your job is to create a picture in their mind and let them call it from their own vantage point; never phrase it as getting them to buy, sign or agree.
**APPLY WHEN:** Drafting or auditing any mission; also as a diagnostic that your aim is a result rather than a mission.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as an incantation — the wording only works if the content genuinely sits in their world.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the phrasing recurs across every client mission the chapter reproduces.

### [NEGO:MP-HYGIENE-CAMP] Written, concise, layered, revisable
**Move:** Keep the mission written and short, hold several at once — enterprise, deal, stage, and in high-stakes work one per call — negotiate it with the team rather than imposing it, and rewrite it whenever what you actually sell changes.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex multi-touch negotiations, team-run deals, market shifts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Micro or transactional deals where per-call written missions become bureaucracy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — clients plus historical analogies offered as illustration, not data.

### [NEGO:MP-OWNERSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP] Ask whose mission you are actually serving
**Move:** Periodically test whether today's activity serves your own stated mission or somebody else's by default; if somebody else's, either adopt it deliberately or rebuild yours to support it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Chronic frustration in a role; solo operators who treat their purpose as self-evident.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to rationalise abandoning commitments mid-stream.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a rhetorical device the author reports using in workshops.

### [NEGO:MP-EMOTION-CHECK-CAMP] Use the mission to keep emotion out of a loaded conversation
**Move:** Before an emotionally charged conversation, write the specific mission for it — what you want the other party to see and decide — and let that dictate what you say instead of arguing your feelings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Medical, family or founder-level conversations where being dismissed as emotional destroys your standing.
**AVOID WHEN:** It suppresses emotional information the other party genuinely needs to weigh.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — parents of a premature infant framing a mission for the neonatal head.

### [NEGO:GOAL-BEHAVIOR-ONLY-CAMP] Replace result targets with controllable behaviour goals
**Move:** Convert every goal into an action you personally execute — hold the mission, ask interrogative-led questions, take notes, control neediness — and audit each by asking whether you could still execute it if the other side walked. Kill exhortation goals of the "close, close, close" family.
**APPLY WHEN:** Planning a deal or coaching a rep; any pipeline conversation about outcomes nobody controls.
**AVOID WHEN:** The organisation genuinely needs forecasts — the source offers no substitute and reads as absolutist; also risky if the chosen behaviours are not actually predictive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — coaching across straight sales, Fortune 500 teams and institutions; the training analogies carry no measurement.

### [NEGO:PAYSIDE-TRIAGE-CAMP] Sort every activity payside versus nonpayside
**Move:** Label each planned activity as payside (directly on the negotiation track, from valid appointment to final presentation) or nonpayside (necessary overhead, or avoidance dressed as work — entertaining, gifting, quota-filling meetings); minimise the second and drop a large nonpayside meeting the moment a smaller payside task appears.
**APPLY WHEN:** Calendar and pipeline triage; reps who are busy but not advancing decisions.
**AVOID WHEN:** Brand-new territory or founder-led work where outreach and relationship-building genuinely are the only route in.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern across corporate and direct-sales clients.

### [NEGO:DAILY-TRACK-CAMP] Keep a daily behavioural scorecard
**Move:** Score a written daily sheet on controllable behaviours only — neediness contained, unokayness held, no said and no invited, nurturing, reverses, 3+ confirmations, connection, interrogative-led questions, notes taken, a bad decision corrected by the next one — plus your emotional state and where time went.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deliberately building new habits; coaching a team whose pipeline metrics hide behavioural failure.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a management compliance instrument — supervised self-scoring produces flattering fiction, and the author concedes many people cannot sustain it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — required of coaching students; the pilot-training parallel is analogy only.

### [NEGO:RESEARCH-CAMP] Out-prepare on public facts
**Move:** Before negotiating, pull financials, annual reports, press and web sources on the counterparty — financial position, profit and loss centres, market position, decision hierarchy, competitors' condition — and use it to test the numbers they claim. Assume a professional procurement team already holds the equivalent on you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every enterprise negotiation; specifically to check volume claims and stated urgency.
**AVOID WHEN:** It reaches personal details — the book's own illustration of applied research is one the author himself calls horrible; treat opposition research on individuals as an ethical boundary.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Silicon Valley client's claim of out-preparing Fortune 100 teams; the "Japanese always research" line is an unsupported stereotype.

### [NEGO:PREGAME-VISUALIZE-CAMP] Pre-call rehearsal of behaviour, not outcome
**Move:** Run the coming meeting as a film in your head — you asking interrogative-led questions, taking notes, staying calm, with no expectation and no need. Rehearse conduct only; never rehearse a winning ending.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ahead of any high-stakes or emotionally loaded session; when you catch yourself picturing the signature.
**AVOID WHEN:** It slides into outcome fantasy, which is the neediness the system forbids; and it never substitutes for research on deciders and pain.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — reported across hundreds of students, self-reported, unmeasured.

### [NEGO:BLANK-SLATE-CAMP] Clear expectations before every session
**Move:** Enter each session with no forecast of the outcome in either direction, and refuse to let sunk travel or effort create obligation; watch for "positive attitude" language sliding into positive expectation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any high-stakes meeting, especially one carrying sunk cost — travel, long cycles, exclusive prep.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as licence not to prepare — the system pairs blank slating with heavy research, not with improvisation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Ireland trip where the team flew home without concessions, plus one client stuck on a sports-derived attitude habit.

## Stage B — Stance and self-management

### [NEGO:ADVERSARY-FRAME-CAMP] Label the counterpart a respected opponent
**Move:** Internally name the other side an adversary in the sense of respected opponent: stay courteous, but stop assuming they want friendship or share your interests, and keep politeness and expectation of alignment strictly separate.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any commercial negotiation, above all against a professionally trained counterpart.
**AVOID WHEN:** The label leaks into tone and manufactures real hostility; internal or partner-ecosystem work where the frame corrodes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a definitional stance defended rhetorically.

### [NEGO:WINWIN-TRAP-CAMP] Read partnership language as a leverage move
**Move:** When the other side invokes fairness, good faith, partnership or the long-term relationship as a reason for you to move, treat it as a contentless concession request: ask what specifically they want changed, and do not answer warmth with a price move.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement or a large buyer opens with collaborative framing; professionalised purchasing; your side is smaller.
**AVOID WHEN:** Low-stakes or long-standing cooperative relationships where reading hostility into ordinary courtesy poisons the deal; internal negotiations.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated across clients facing supply-system-management buyers; GM's PICOS named, but the characterisation is the author's.

### [NEGO:WANT-NOT-NEED-CAMP] Replace need with want, out loud and internally
**Move:** State to yourself before and during the negotiation that you want this deal and do not need it, name concretely what happens if it dies, and strike need-language from everything you say to the counterpart.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal that would materially change your quarter; single-large-customer dependence; late-stage pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** The dependence is real and unmanaged — performing indifference instead of fixing pipeline or runway is denial.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — clients reportedly rank this the highest-impact idea in the system (informal self-reported poll).

### [NEGO:NO-REJECTION-CAMP] Audit who can actually reject you
**Move:** Before the call, state explicitly that this counterpart holds no power to reject you because you need nothing from them — only someone you genuinely depend on can — and carry that into how you receive a refusal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fear of the call, avoidance of prospecting, reluctance to name a price or push back.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes a way to dismiss real feedback — "they can't reject me" must not become "their objection means nothing".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a reframe argued from definition.

### [NEGO:NOT-OKAY-CAMP] Present slightly less than perfectly competent
**Move:** Let the counterpart feel superior — search for a word, admit you are not across a detail, ask them to explain something, drop displays of polish; only one party gets to feel okay and it should be them.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings; status-conscious or hostile counterparts; rooms where the other side is defensive.
**AVOID WHEN:** Credibility is the product — technical evaluation, expert sale, regulated diligence — or the fumbling reads as unprofessional once recognised as technique.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — used across the client base; the Columbo, Reagan and Schwarzkopf illustrations are rhetorical, not evidential.

### [NEGO:PROP-FUMBLE-CAMP] Engineer a small dependency on the counterpart
**Move:** Arrive without a pen and borrow one, leave the cards behind, let a device run flat — a small ask for help that lets the other side do you a favour and lowers the guard in the room.
**APPLY WHEN:** A stuck meeting where barriers have not come down; early contact with a guarded counterpart.
**AVOID WHEN:** Staged clumsiness is detected, or the setting punishes unpreparedness — this is the most openly manipulative technique in the source and should be marked as theatre, not candour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a coached seller who dropped her purse with a $35,000 commission at stake.

### [NEGO:LET-THEM-SHINE-CAMP] Give the counterpart room to display their strength
**Move:** When the other side wants to show expertise, charm or command of detail, encourage it and stay quiet, harvesting what they reveal while performing; suppress the urge to display your own strength.
**APPLY WHEN:** Talkative or status-driven counterparts; discovery phases where information is the objective.
**AVOID WHEN:** The display consumes the only meeting you get and your decision-relevant questions go unasked.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from an Emerson line, no case attached.

### [NEGO:ESTEEM-VS-OKAY-CAMP] Keep self-esteem private, hand okayness over
**Move:** Separate the two before a session: your internal appraisal of yourself stays with you and is untouchable; the visible position of being composed, expert and in control is what you concede to them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any negotiation where you are tempted to defend your status, correct a slight, or prove competence.
**AVOID WHEN:** Visible competence is exactly what is being evaluated — performed unokayness there costs you the mandate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle with rhetorical examples only.

### [NEGO:PAY-FORWARD-CAMP] Pay forward as a self-esteem routine
**Move:** Deliberately give value where it cannot be repaid to you — workplace, community, family — as a daily habit that builds the self-image the rest of the discipline requires.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long sales careers with repeated rejection; periods where neediness or status anxiety is leaking into calls.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used instrumentally as a reciprocity lever on a specific counterpart — that is a different and manipulative move; the claimed mechanism here is internal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from anecdote and from Emerson, not from negotiation outcomes.

### [NEGO:FIRST-NAME-CAMP] Answer a first-name introduction with a first name
**Move:** When someone introduces themselves by full name, reply using their first name rather than title-plus-surname, keeping the status frame level from the first exchange.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings with senior counterparts where deference is the reflex.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cultures or hierarchies where first-naming a senior person reads as disrespect and costs more than the status point gains.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — illustrative micro-example, no support.

### [NEGO:ABORT-SESSION-CAMP] Break or cancel when you cannot blank slate
**Move:** When excitement or emotion takes over mid-session, call a time-out — bathroom, coffee, lunch, any step back; if exhaustion or a personal crisis makes clearing expectations impossible, cancel outright.
**APPLY WHEN:** You notice your own emotional pendulum swinging; anything scheduled during personal crisis.
**AVOID WHEN:** Cancellation itself signals weakness or breaks a hard deadline — the source treats cancelling as always correct and never prices its cost.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as an absolute rule with no supporting case.

### [NEGO:ENERGY-STAMINA-CAMP] Manage physical endurance as a negotiating asset
**Move:** Treat stamina as part of the budget — know your endurance limits, guard against jet lag, exercise, take breaks, avoid alcohol at business-social events, and call a time-out without hesitation regardless of session length.
**APPLY WHEN:** Marathon sessions, closing crunches, multi-day on-site negotiations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as more than hygiene — the claim that the physically stronger side gets the better deal is not demonstrated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the 4 a.m. labour-settlement pattern is folklore, not measurement.

### [NEGO:NOMIRROR-GTY] Distinguish how you treat them from how they treat you
**Move:** Refuse to reciprocate unconstructive behaviour — mirroring reinforces what you dislike and confirms their belief that everyone behaves that way. Design your conduct to model and reward what you want and to pay nothing for what you do not, without conceding substance to do it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Hostile, sloppy or disrespectful counterparts you still want a deal with.
**AVOID WHEN:** Unilateral good conduct is being harvested as weakness across repeated rounds with no reciprocation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — normative claim.

## Stage C — Prospecting, access and the decision path

### [NEGO:COLDCALL-NO-FIT-OPENER-CAMP] Open cold contact by conceding you may not fit
**Move:** Start the first call by saying you do not know whether what you do is relevant, that it may not be, that they should say so and you will go — then ask one question about how the relevant area is handled today. Same shape for investor outreach, ending on a calendar question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Outbound first contact and appointment setting where you have no standing to assume relevance.
**AVOID WHEN:** Warm inbound where intent is already declared — manufactured uncertainty reads as a script; also weak where the counterpart needs fast proof of competence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author built his client base this way and trained insurance teams on it; conversion figures self-reported.

### [NEGO:WARM-AS-COLD-CAMP] Treat every referral as the coldest call you ever made
**Move:** Run the cold-call discipline on a hot referral anyway — no assumptions about intent, the same permission-to-decline opener, and no mental spending of the commission.
**APPLY WHEN:** Introductions, referrals and inbound leads described as pre-sold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-applied to the point of insulting the referrer's credibility with their own contact.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the "just call and collect" handoff treated as a neediness trap.

### [NEGO:ASK-FOR-DISQUAL-CAMP] Ask the gatekeeper to disqualify you in writing
**Move:** Send your qualifying information and ask them to tell you now if you do not qualify or will not be backed, explaining plainly that you will move to other options if so.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualification-heavy processes with many applicants or vendors; sponsor-dependent approvals; when your scarce resource is time on dead options.
**AVOID WHEN:** Asking for a no hands a lukewarm gatekeeper the easiest exit and you have no real alternatives lined up.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=stage/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a recruited athlete who asked coaches to say no and reportedly received several acceptances.

### [NEGO:VALID-APPOINTMENT-CAMP] Refuse invalid meetings and drive-bys
**Move:** Do not travel or grant a slot without a clear picture of the counterpart's problem and grounds to believe they hold the time, money and emotional budget to pay for relief; judge activity by qualified prospects, not proposal volume.
**APPLY WHEN:** Territory sales, outbound prospecting, teams measured on meeting counts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early market discovery where you genuinely do not yet know who has the problem — the gate then screens out learning.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing rule to clients; the coupon-versus-newspaper coaching story as illustration.

### [NEGO:PAIN-PRECHECK-CAMP] No meeting without a written statement of their problem
**Move:** Before agreeing to any meeting, write down what you believe their current or future problem is and how you know; if you cannot state it, treat the meeting as invalid and research first. Re-run it mid-deal with cleared expectations to catch hidden pain quietly killing the deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Qualification, pre-meeting prep, and any deal that has gone quiet or reads "logical but stuck".
**AVOID WHEN:** Trivial transactional purchases where the problem is self-evident; also fails when the written pain is your own sales thesis in disguise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing rule across the client base; McColl cited as corroboration, not data.

### [NEGO:BLOCKER-AS-GUIDE-CAMP] Recruit the gatekeeper into referring you
**Move:** Rather than evading the screening layer, ask for help explicitly — what criteria get an audience, who would have the principal's ear on this, who is that person's most competent assistant, would you be willing to tell them why I am calling — and give them a role in handing you onward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stuck at reception, executive assistants, or a screening layer in a large organisation.
**AVOID WHEN:** The gatekeeper is under explicit instruction to block, or the ask exceeds what they can grant without exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one rep's struggle with receptionists; the accompanying dialogue is labelled by the author as fabricated for illustration.

### [NEGO:START-AT-TOP-CAMP] Enter high, get routed down with a hall pass
**Move:** Open at the top of the organisation and expect to be sent down to the blocker — that is the mechanism: you arrive stamped as approved, the blocker knows you can report upward, and you get time and respect.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enterprise deals with a thick blocker layer; when purchasing or an evaluator has stalled you.
**AVOID WHEN:** The culture punishes going over heads and the blocker retaliates for the rest of the cycle; also when you have nothing worth an executive's minute.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a sure route with no case attached.

### [NEGO:DM-MAP-INTERROGATIVE-CAMP] Interrogate the decision path
**Move:** Replace "are you the decision maker" with a chain — who else might you want to talk with, who could be of service in this decision, who should be invited to support it, who would be upset if left out, how will the decision be reached, when, and what criteria and paperwork must be in place — then be ready to negotiate separately with every name that surfaces.
**APPLY WHEN:** First substantive contact, and again whenever a deal stalls without explanation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Solo-buyer transactions; and the face-saving "of course you decide, but…" phrasing grates on senior buyers.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard coaching questions, illustrated by a recruiting failure where the real decision weight sat outside the room.

### [NEGO:BLOCKER-LADDER-CAMP] Three descending agendas with a blocker
**Move:** Offer the blocker three fallbacks in order — let me walk you through it and represent it to the committee myself; failing that, let me coach you and wait in the hall for questions; failing that, let me coach you and wait in your office so you can phone me. If all three are refused, consult your time-and-energy budget and decide calmly whether to walk.
**APPLY WHEN:** A single blocker controls access to a committee; proposals that die when relayed second-hand.
**AVOID WHEN:** You hold another live route worth more than the concessions; or the blocker's own standing is threatened by your proposal, in which case no step is acceptable.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeatedly coached pattern said to usually work, no outcome data.

### [NEGO:PURCHASING-OUT-OF-LOOP-CAMP] Don't negotiate with purchasing
**Move:** Treat the purchasing office as paperwork rather than decisions — the more authority you concede to it, the less effective you become; work the real deciders and let purchasing process the result.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large-company deals where procurement intercepted the conversation before value was established.
**AVOID WHEN:** Procurement genuinely holds a veto or the contract is a formal tender — dismissing them then loses the deal on process grounds.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. re-entry, where the previous team had negotiated exclusively with blockers.

### [NEGO:QUALIFY-DECIDER-CAMP] Do not let an unauthorised counterpart make a decision
**Move:** Before accepting a change, cancellation or restructuring, confirm the person proposing it holds the authority and sees the consequences; if not, escalate the item rather than agreeing to it in the name of the relationship.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement contacts proposing to drop service terms; changes that will be blamed on you later.
**AVOID WHEN:** Escalating over your contact's head burns the relationship delivery depends on — a cost the source underweights.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/saas/product · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a field rep let a purchasing contact cancel a worldwide service contract and the supplier absorbed the blame.

### [NEGO:SABOTAGE-PROBE-CAMP] Name the internal-champion risk at the top level
**Move:** Entering as challenger against an incumbent with an internal sponsor, raise it explicitly at the highest accessible level as a question: how do we keep this effort from being undermined by someone inside your company whose success is tied to our competitor?
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive vendor slots at large accounts where you have executive access and suspect a protective liaison.
**AVOID WHEN:** You lack executive access or political capital — asked at the wrong level it reads as an accusation against the listener.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/channel · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client case; the reported outcome was an SVP tying a manager's bonus to the client's success.

### [NEGO:FRIENDSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP] Date-check friendships with counterparts
**Move:** When someone on your side cites a friendship with the buyer, ask how long the friendship has existed and how long that person has held the buying role; if the two answers match, treat the relationship as a procurement instrument and price accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** Golf, dinners and social access appearing around renewal or negotiation time.
**AVOID WHEN:** Relationships that genuinely predate the commercial one — blanket cynicism destroys legitimate trust-based selling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the tennis-wives dialogue, friendship and purchasing tenure the same couple of years.

### [NEGO:PREGAME-GTY] Build the relationship before the negotiation exists
**Move:** Convert stranger into acquaintance ahead of time — learn their likes and dislikes, meet informally, arrive early and stay late around the scheduled meeting, and ask a small favour that lets them feel you owe them one.
**APPLY WHEN:** Before a known-hard renewal or a first enterprise negotiation; any long-cycle account.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their process forbids manufactured intimacy (regulated procurement), or the favour would look like an inducement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Franklin's book-borrowing habit as the illustrative device.

### [NEGO:ONESHOES-GTY] Negotiate with a person, not an institution
**Move:** Stop trying to persuade the company or the committee; pick one human, usually your counterpart, and work the problem from their seat — including what they must be able to say internally — reframing your job as strengthening their hand.
**APPLY WHEN:** Committee deals, procurement, any champion-led enterprise cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your contact has no path to the real decision-maker — then you are arming the wrong person.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a diplomat who described his job as helping his counterpart get new instructions.

### [NEGO:AUTHCHECK-GTY] Ask about authority before any give-and-take
**Move:** Ask early and plainly how much authority the counterpart holds; on a vague answer, either reach a real decision-maker or explicitly reserve equal freedom to reopen anything. If an approver appears after you thought you had a deal, insist on symmetry and treat the text as a joint draft binding neither side.
**APPLY WHEN:** Committee deals, procurement, agency counterparts, any second-bite-at-the-apple pattern.
**AVOID WHEN:** The champion genuinely has no authority and the question embarrasses them — the move then becomes co-planning the approval path instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated pattern from the authors' advisory and mediation work.

## Stage D — Framing the game, the room and the agenda

### [NEGO:METAGAME-GTY] Negotiate the process before the price
**Move:** Treat every deal as two simultaneous negotiations — substance and procedure — and when they open with positional haggling, name the procedural question aloud and propose deciding on merits and standards rather than on who blinks.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early in a deal; once a demand-and-concede rhythm has started; cross-functional deals with no shared process.
**AVOID WHEN:** A genuinely one-off transaction where process talk outcosts the spread; late stages where reopening the frame reads as stalling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — the core insight of the Harvard Negotiation Project, no dataset offered.

### [NEGO:SIDEBYSIDE-GTY] Restructure the room as a joint task
**Move:** Say explicitly that neither side's interests get met unless both are addressed, propose looking at the problem together, and back it physically — same side of the table, the contract or a blank sheet in front of both. If they will not agree verbally, behave that way anyway and make joining attractive.
**APPLY WHEN:** Adversarial framing has set in; commercial negotiation after a technical win.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterparty harvests collaborative framing for one-way concessions — and note this has to be re-done continuously, not once.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued by analogy (two shipwrecked sailors sharing rations).

### [NEGO:COJUDGE-GTY] Talk to them as a co-decider, in a small private room
**Move:** Stop arguing as though to a jury and treat the counterparty as a second judge with whom you must write one joint opinion — establish confidential channels, shrink the meeting, and expect the real decision among very few people.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large committee negotiations that have turned performative; deals with a board, press or internal audience watching.
**AVOID WHEN:** Excluding people who must own the outcome triggers the participation failure; regulated processes requiring documented openness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 1954 Trieste talks, where progress came only after the principals met informally without delegations.

### [NEGO:RULEGAME-GTY] Negotiate the rules of the game (recognise → raise → renegotiate)
**Move:** Name the ploy to yourself (deception, discomfort or lock-in cue), raise it aloud tentatively and without accusation while offering a procedural remedy, then convert it into an explicit negotiation about procedure — what interest does the tactic serve, what alternative game would work, does the principle survive reciprocity. Attack the tactic, never their integrity.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-cycle when behaviour rather than terms blocks progress; recurring buyer games such as fake deadlines or mystery approvers.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no remedy to propose; naming it publicly humiliates someone who must then defend the tactic; the ploy is genuinely institutional policy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Harvard Negotiation Project teaching and mediation across labour, legal and diplomatic cases.

### [NEGO:PROCRECIP-GTY] Reciprocity test on procedure
**Move:** Convert the tactic into a proposed rule and offer to apply it symmetrically — seated in the low chair, ask what the theory is and whether you swap tomorrow; preconditions set, ask whether you should set yours. A tactic that cannot survive being stated as a two-way rule usually gets dropped.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ritual power plays around setting, sequencing, agenda control or first-offer demands.
**AVOID WHEN:** The asymmetry is legitimate — their procurement process, regulated approval flows — where insisting on symmetry reads as pedantry.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the authors' principled-negotiation logic, no study cited.

### [NEGO:SETTINGFIX-GTY] Diagnose and renegotiate the physical setup
**Move:** Ask yourself whether you feel stress and why — noise, temperature, no room to caucus, hostile seating — treat the answer as data about designed pressure, raise it objectively as a shared problem and propose a change. Note the counter-intuitive option that meeting on their turf can favour you, since leaving is easier.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long on-site sessions, deliberately marathoned meetings, hostile venue choices.
**AVOID WHEN:** The discomfort is incidental rather than engineered — turning ordinary friction into a procedural fight burns goodwill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring pattern in the authors' practice; the eye-contact effect credited to unnamed student experiments.

### [NEGO:AGENDA-NEGOTIATED-CAMP] Negotiate the agenda before asking for anything
**Move:** Open by proposing the frame and getting explicit agreement — I am not sure this is of value to you; if it is not, say so and we stop; if it is, we move forward — confirm that as the deal, and only then ask for anything. Keep no hidden agendas.
**APPLY WHEN:** First meetings; any request that could feel like a push to close.
**AVOID WHEN:** The ritual repeats until it becomes a verbal tic — sophisticated counterparts recognise the permission-to-refuse pattern as technique.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core coached practice illustrated by scripted exchanges.

### [NEGO:MINI-AGENDA-CAMP] Pre-clear anything awkward with a micro-agreement
**Move:** Before a hard question or sensitive move, negotiate a one-line agenda for it — may I ask you a difficult question without your getting angry, you are sure, you will not get mad — restated three times. On group calls, set procedural mini-agendas up front, such as routing questions through one named person.
**APPLY WHEN:** Conference calls with many parties; any question that could end the meeting if asked cold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Over-scaffolding trivial exchanges, which signals anxiety rather than control.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. conference call across 13 negotiators in 6 countries.

### [NEGO:AGENDA-FIVE-SLOTS-CAMP] Build every agenda from five categories
**Move:** Draft each agenda against five slots — problems (including imagined ones), our baggage, their baggage, what we want, what happens next — and include an item whenever you are unsure whether it belongs. Every contact gets one, down to a one-paragraph email.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every session, call and email in a live negotiation; team deals where several people meet several counterparts.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied literally to one-line emails — overhead unlikely to survive real workflows, though the source states no exception.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter lists roughly a dozen problem items and a dozen baggage items from coached negotiations.

### [NEGO:BAGGAGE-UPFRONT-CAMP] Put your own liability on the table first
**Move:** Name the thing you fear will be held against you — inexperience, your company's old reputation, a competitor's long incumbency — and ask directly what problem they see in it; then confirm the answer three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have concrete experience that this specific bias shows up; reputational damage predating the relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are guessing — baggage is the sole sanctioned exception to the ban on assumptions, and a wrong guess plants the objection yourself.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — several scripted exchanges plus a list of baggage items from real negotiations.

### [NEGO:WANTS-AS-DECISIONS-CAMP] Restate every want as a decision they can refuse
**Move:** List what you want at each stage, then rewrite each item as an accept-or-reject decision for the counterpart — not "we want your report" but "we want you to either submit or refuse to submit it" — framing wants as means you control rather than as the signed deal you do not.
**APPLY WHEN:** Planning any stage; teams that cannot articulate the next ask beyond "close it".
**AVOID WHEN:** Forcing a binary early invites a premature refusal on items that would have resolved themselves — the source states no contraindication, so this one is the lens's addition.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the chapter converts an actual want list item by item.

### [NEGO:WANT-PROTECT-IDEA-CAMP] Secure your work before you show it
**Move:** Before presenting an idea, ask who decides on it and who advises them financially and legally, then propose the sequence explicitly — I show it; if you like it we go together to your attorney and accountant; if not we drop it — and ask for broker-of-record style protection, confirmed three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** Brokered deals and consulting concepts where your value is the idea itself and can be lifted.
**AVOID WHEN:** Legal instruments would be disproportionate to the stage, or asking for signatures early reads as distrust in a relationship-first market.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/info-product · market=consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a counter-example where a noncommittal counterpart took the idea to a third party.

### [NEGO:NEXT-STEP-LOCKED-CAMP] Close every contact with the next step, triple-confirmed
**Move:** Never end a meeting, call or thread on "call me in a few weeks" — before anything ends, state who does what and what triggers the next contact, get it agreed, and verify it three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** The end of every contact in any live multi-touch deal, especially when small talk has taken over the final minutes.
**AVOID WHEN:** A deal you have decided to end; trivial confirmations in fast transactional cycles; or when pressing for a commitment they cannot yet make manufactures a soft yes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — mandatory across coached deals; credited with holding together an engagement of roughly 500 sessions.

### [NEGO:INVITE-NO-CAMP] Establish the right to refuse as a ground rule
**Move:** Say early and explicitly that a refusal is welcome at any point, that you will not take it personally and would rather hear it than a maybe; repeat the invitation at each decision point, including at the end of a stalled meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Openings of any negotiation; stalled deals full of maybes; counterparts conditioned to avoid confrontation.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as reverse-psychology theatre — if you would not actually accept the refusal it is a manipulation and gets read as one; also risky with counterparts who take negative framing as insult.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — recurring across client engagements in several countries plus the author's own cold-call practice; outcomes self-reported.

### [NEGO:OWN-SIDE-PRENEG-CAMP] Negotiate with your own side before the joint call
**Move:** When a manager or colleague insists on joining a call to help close, make a separate agreement with them first on explicit terms — they may attend on condition of staying silent — and enforce it in the room if needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** A manager attaches themselves late to a large deal; any joint call with a colleague trained in a closing-oriented method.
**AVOID WHEN:** The colleague holds authority you actually need in the room, or the constraint is not framed as a request to someone who outranks you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an insurance case where a conventional manager's closing instinct nearly broke a completed decision.

### [NEGO:TEAM-LEAK-WATCH-CAMP] Police your own team for ego-driven leaks
**Move:** Assume the counterpart will cultivate whoever on your side needs to feel liked, smart or important; brief the team that nobody commits, discounts or shares internals alone, and watch for members returning with the other side's framing in their mouth.
**APPLY WHEN:** Team negotiations and long enterprise cycles with social contact — dinners, site visits, junkets.
**AVOID WHEN:** The suspicion becomes internal surveillance that destroys trust on your own side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author claims to have observed the pattern from the counterpart's side in multiple Fortune 500 negotiations.

## Stage E — People, perception and emotion

### [NEGO:SHOES-GTY] Map their perceptions in two columns
**Move:** Before the meeting, write their view of the situation next to yours line by line, phrased as they would phrase it, including the items that make your side look bad, and withhold judgement while doing it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Renewals, price increases or renegotiations where both sides have a history to interpret; prep for a hostile stakeholder.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never contraindicated as prep — but do not confuse the output with agreement; understanding is not conceding.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a tenant/landlady perception table where each line has a mirror-image counterpart.

### [NEGO:NOBLAME-GTY] Describe symptoms, then ask for their advice
**Move:** Replace the accusation with a factual incident log plus a request for help — what failed, how often, what it cost, that you need it fixed — and ask what they would do about it, keeping the person separate from the symptom even where blame would be justified.
**APPLY WHEN:** Service failures, churn-risk conversations, escalations, any call you arrive at angry.
**AVOID WHEN:** A documented breach where contractual or legal notice is the required move rather than a collaborative one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/saas/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the mechanics of defensiveness, illustrated with a service rewrite.

### [NEGO:PERCEPTALK-GTY] Overinvest in what is cheap to you and loud to them
**Move:** Say the true things they want to hear, and refuse to dismiss an issue that matters to them merely because it is easy for you to grant — spend real time working out the mechanics of the cheap concession, because that work is what makes it credible and buys goodwill elsewhere.
**APPLY WHEN:** An issue is low-cost for you but symbolically central for them; multi-issue deals needing currency for the hard item.
**AVOID WHEN:** The cheap item is actually a precedent you cannot repeat across the customer base.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Law of the Sea Conference (1974–1981, ~150 nations), where technology transfer was treated as trivially grantable and a cheap lever was forfeited.

### [NEGO:COUNTEREXPECT-GTY] Act against the role they have cast you in
**Move:** Change a perception by taking an action their model of you cannot generate — go where an adversary would not go, concede what a hard bargainer would not, appear in person where a defensive party would send a letter.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are stereotyped as vendor, adversary or the-people-who-broke-it, and argument alone is not moving them.
**AVOID WHEN:** The gesture concedes substance you need, or reads as weakness to a counterparty already playing hard.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Sadat's November 1977 trip to Jerusalem, credited as a precondition of the 1979 treaty.

### [NEGO:PARTICIPATE-GTY] Give them authorship, not a finished product
**Move:** Involve the other side while the answer is unfinished — ask their advice early, let them mark up drafts, credit their ideas generously — and stop saving the difficult stakeholder for last after everything is settled internally.
**APPLY WHEN:** Committee deals, procurement, security or legal review, champions who must defend the deal upward.
**AVOID WHEN:** Involvement leaks information you must hold, or "participation" is theatre — the claim is about real influence over the product.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — felt participation called perhaps the single biggest determinant of acceptance, unmeasured.

### [NEGO:FACESAVE-GTY] Engineer consistency with their past words
**Move:** When they hold out on terms that look acceptable, assume the blocker is the appearance of backing down, and repackage the same substance so it reconciles with their stated principles and prior commitments — including letting them announce it as their own decision.
**APPLY WHEN:** A stakeholder publicly committed to a contrary position; late-stage stalls with no substantive objection left.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as cosmetic pretense — the source insists face-saving is a real interest, not a courtesy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a mayor who rejected negotiated terms and accepted identical ones once he could present them as his own campaign decision.

### [NEGO:EMOAUDIT-GTY] Name the emotions, both sides', and put them on the table
**Move:** Mid-deal, write down what you feel and what you would rather feel, do the same for them, ask what is producing it, then say it aloud as a legitimate fact — including the part you suspect is irrational — and invite them to say whether their side feels the same.
**APPLY WHEN:** Repeated stalls with no new substantive argument; visible tension; post-incident renewal talks.
**AVOID WHEN:** The named emotion would signal desperation about your alternatives, or the counterpart reads emotional disclosure as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — technique described from practice, no data.

### [NEGO:CORE5-GTY] Check the five core concerns
**Move:** Diagnose negative emotion against five interests — autonomy, appreciation, affiliation, role and status — stop trampling whichever one you are standing on, and feed it deliberately to build a working climate.
**APPLY WHEN:** An unexpectedly hostile stakeholder; a champion sidelined by your going over their head; multi-threading that bruised someone's role.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a flattery script rather than a real change in how you treat them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — drawn from the authors' companion work on emotions and Harvard teaching practice, not a peer-reviewed result.

### [NEGO:IDENTITY-GTY] Read out-of-character behaviour as identity threat
**Move:** When a counterparty reacts far out of proportion, or you feel yourself losing balance, test whether a self-image is under attack rather than an interest, and adjust the framing so your point does not require them to accept being incompetent or unfair.
**APPLY WHEN:** Disproportionate reactions; sudden hostility after a neutral remark; discovery that exposes a stakeholder's past decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used to psychologise away a legitimate substantive objection.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — described mechanism, no study cited.

### [NEGO:STEAM-GTY] Let them vent without feeding it
**Move:** Instead of interrupting or walking out while they unload grievances, sit still, decline to answer the inflammatory content, and occasionally invite them to continue until they are finished — recognising that an angry speech may be performed for their own constituents and can buy the speaker room to settle later.
**APPLY WHEN:** An escalation call; a renewal after a failure; a stakeholder speaking in front of their team.
**AVOID WHEN:** Venting spirals rather than drains, or the behaviour is abusive and should not be absorbed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/saas/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the release mechanism is stated from practice, not measured.

### [NEGO:ONEANGER-GTY] One-at-a-time anger rule
**Move:** Agree an explicit ground rule that only one person may be angry at a time — it legitimises the outburst, licenses the other side not to answer in kind, and makes breaking the rule a visible loss of self-control.
**APPLY WHEN:** Recurring adversarial forums — steering committees, joint escalation boards, partner reviews — where blowups repeat.
**AVOID WHEN:** One-off transactional calls where imposing rules weighs more than the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — attributed to a 1950s labour-management committee in the steel industry.

### [NEGO:SYMBOL-GTY] Cheap symbolic gesture, including apology
**Move:** Use small low-cost acts with high emotional return — a note of sympathy, a statement of regret, a shared meal, an apology — noting that an apology can defuse without conceding personal responsibility or intent to harm.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-incident repair; relationship reset before a hard commercial ask.
**AVOID WHEN:** An apology carries legal liability, or the gesture substitutes for the substantive fix they actually need.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — called one of the cheapest high-return investments available, with no supporting count.

### [NEGO:TWOLEDGER-GTY] Keep relationship and substance on separate ledgers
**Move:** Track substantive issues (terms, prices, dates, liabilities) and relationship issues (emotion versus reason, ease of communication, trust, acceptance, persuasion versus coercion) separately and negotiate each on its own merits — never buy goodwill with unjustified concessions, never coerce concessions by threatening the relationship, and if you concede for relationship reasons make it a recognised favour.
**APPLY WHEN:** Long-horizon accounts, renewals, partner and channel relationships.
**AVOID WHEN:** The relationship is genuinely single-shot and transactional — the investment does not return.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from principle, with Chamberlain and the Sudetenland as illustration.

### [NEGO:RELNEGOTIATE-GTY] Negotiate the relationship itself, on the merits
**Move:** When behaviour keeps blocking substance, raise it as you would any substantive difference — describe your perceptions and feelings, inquire into theirs, avoid attributing motive, propose external standards for how you will deal with each other, and decline pressure tactics, framing forward rather than backward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stalled deals where conduct, not terms, is the blocker.
**AVOID WHEN:** Raising it hands a manipulative counterpart a new lever — the frame assumes a counterpart open to rational exchange.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

### [NEGO:IRRATTRACE-GTY] Deal rationally with apparent irrationality
**Move:** Stay purposive regardless of their state, question the assumption of irrationality (they may see it differently, value things differently, or have misheard a demand), and where the belief really is skewed, trace the reasoning empathically to its roots so the logical leap or old association surfaces and they can revise it themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** Buyers whose objections do not track the economics; risk aversion that looks irrational.
**AVOID WHEN:** The behaviour is strategic rather than sincere — excavating a performance wastes the room.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise

### [NEGO:HARDSOFT-GTY] Attack the problem while visibly supporting the person

### [NEGO:APPRECIATE-GTY] Acknowledge what they did for you before raising the problem

### [NEGO:AT-YOUR-SERVICE-CAMP] Hand the next move over when conflict brews

## Stage F — Communication mechanics and question grammar

### [NEGO:ACTIVELISTEN-GTY] Steelman them, then answer
**Move:** Make listening rather than composing your rebuttal the task — interrupt to check understanding, ask them to spell out ambiguities — then restate their position in its strongest form including their fear about your motives, ask what you missed, and only then raise the problems you see.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery; objection handling; the counterparty is repeating themselves or escalating.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used as a formula — mirroring without being able to state their case is transparent; also when time is short and they want a number.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — being heard called the cheapest concession available; the Turnbull case as the worked script.

### [NEGO:ISTATE-GTY] Describe impact on you, not their motives
**Move:** Convert any characterisation of them into a statement of effect on you — a felt letdown rather than an accusation of a broken promise — since a claim about your own experience is hard to dispute while a claim about intent invites denial.
**APPLY WHEN:** Raising a breach, a missed commitment, or an unfair term.
**AVOID WHEN:** A factual record of what they did is what the moment requires — audits, contractual notice.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from defensiveness mechanics, no source given.

### [NEGO:PURPOSE-GTY] Say it only if you know what it buys
**Move:** Before any significant statement, decide what you are trying to communicate or learn and what that information will do — over-communication, especially about how flexible you are, can make agreement harder than silence would.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pricing conversations; any moment you are tempted to volunteer flexibility; high-emotion exchanges.
**AVOID WHEN:** It slides into the concealment the same school condemns in positional bargainers — this move sits in tension with the method's own transparency claim.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — illustrated with an invented car-price example, not a study.

### [NEGO:CORRECTME-GTY] "Please correct me if I'm wrong"
**Move:** Present your version of the facts as possibly inaccurate and invite correction, making the two of you colleagues establishing facts rather than opponents asserting them — and insuring yourself, since being wrong then costs no face.
**APPLY WHEN:** Opening a confrontational conversation; raising a billing, contract or compliance discrepancy.
**AVOID WHEN:** The facts are certain and the stakes require a firm record.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull vs Jones Realty, reported by the authors.

### [NEGO:FACTASQ-GTY] Deliver facts as questions, and offload the sharpest one
**Move:** Turn each damaging fact into a question they can verify, accept or correct so the information arrives without a verdict attached, and attribute the most pointed question to an absent third party to lower the heat further.
**APPLY WHEN:** You must surface a violation, a broken promise or an unflattering comparison.
**AVOID WHEN:** The question format reads as sarcasm or entrapment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull's rent-control question sequence.

### [NEGO:QSILENCE-GTY] Questions instead of statements, then silence
**Move:** Convert assertions into questions, since statements generate resistance and questions offer nothing to attack — and after an unreasonable proposal, an unjustified attack or a non-answer, say nothing at all and wait, without rescuing them with a follow-up.
**APPLY WHEN:** They dodge a direct question about a number or a decision path; an unreasonable demand has just landed.
**AVOID WHEN:** Rapport is fragile, or the channel is asynchronous and silence reads as disengagement.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the two key jujitsu tools, with an illustrative stumble by a school-board chairman.

### [NEGO:LISTENPOWER-GTY] Transparent thinking, including your ambivalence
**Move:** Share your actual reasoning rather than a bare verdict — a buyer who likes a bid but fears the firm cannot handle peak volume should say both, because a flat refusal leaves the other side no chance to address the real concern — and pair it with inquiry into what sits behind their refusals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Stalled objections that do not respond to more information; discovery.
**AVOID WHEN:** Transparency about your ambivalence hands a hard bargainer a lever on a purely distributive term.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the bidder case plus the intern who found abandonment fears behind a patient's refusal.

### [NEGO:FORWARD-GTY] Convert cause-talk into purpose-talk
**Move:** Notice when the exchange has become a ritual of scoring points about the past and switch the question from why it happened to who should do what tomorrow — talk about the destination, not last quarter's overrun.
**APPLY WHEN:** Post-mortems turning into blame calls; renewals after failures; arguments neither side is actually trying to win.
**AVOID WHEN:** Root-cause work is genuinely required, or accountability must be established before moving on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=service/saas/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a distinction between two senses of "why", no data.

### [NEGO:INTERROGATIVE-LED-CAMP] Lead every question with an interrogative
**Move:** Start questions with who, what, when, where, why, how or which so they cannot be answered in one word, and convert stock verb-led forms on the spot — "is this the biggest issue" becomes "what is the biggest issue"; "is it too expensive" becomes "what price would you pay".
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery, mid-deal diagnosis, any moment you need information rather than agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The phrasing is right but the intent is hostile — the source's own example shows an interrogative used accusingly and destroying the account; interrogatives can also be closed.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a client call log of roughly 18 consecutive interrogative-led questions, called typical.

### [NEGO:KILL-VERB-LED-CAMP] Restrict verb-led questions to two situations
**Move:** Use a verb-led question only when you already know the answer, or at the very end when you must bore in; never ask anything that removes the right to refuse ("can you say yes to this") or sets a trap ("is there any reason you wouldn't").
**APPLY WHEN:** Reviewing call recordings or your own scripts for closing-flavoured language.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied so rigidly you cannot confirm a fact — the source itself concedes some verb-led forms are acceptable when delivered with a nurturing tone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated workshop observation; the law-school parallel is analogy.

### [NEGO:QUESTION-DISCIPLINE-CAMP] Short, single, unanswered by you
**Move:** Cap questions at roughly nine or ten words, ask one at a time, wait, listen, and let the answer dictate the next question rather than firing a prepared list — and never supply candidate answers, which both answers for them and collapses your interrogative into a verb-led question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Live calls and meetings, especially under emotional pressure when the impulse is to stack questions.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely complex technical scoping where a compound question saves time with an expert peer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — described as a standing coaching correction with an illustrating question chain.

### [NEGO:BENEFIT-IN-QUESTION-CAMP] Embed the feature or benefit inside the question
**Move:** Wrap the benefit named in your mission inside an interrogative so the other side reaches the conclusion — instead of "can you stay competitive without this technology", ask "how can you stay competitive without this technology".
**APPLY WHEN:** Early discussions where you need them to articulate the gap themselves; competitive displacement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The premise is false or unproven — this is the most manipulation-adjacent question form in the source, since the softening is tonal while the leading is real.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked examples constructed by the author rather than logged calls.

### [NEGO:REVERSE-CAMP] Answer a question with a nurtured question
**Move:** When asked something, reply with a brief warm tag and then a question that serves you — good question, before we get to that, what is the biggest challenge you face here; that was well thought out, by the way, what are your cost constraints.
**APPLY WHEN:** The other side probes for your position, opinion or numbers; discovery calls where you are being interviewed.
**AVOID WHEN:** The nurturing tag is dropped, making it sound like cross-examination; or a direct factual answer is legally or contractually required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the two extended cases are the author's son in recruiting interviews.

### [NEGO:CONNECT-CAMP] Turn provocations into disclosure
**Move:** Never answer an unasked question, treat a statement as a question, or respond to a random remark — use a connector instead: a drawn-out "and…?", "which means…?", "help me understand", or a silence they must fill, then follow the disclosure with an interrogative question.
**APPLY WHEN:** You hear vague pressure statements designed to pull a concession — I do not like what I see, your price is too high.
**AVOID WHEN:** Overused to the point of feeling like interrogation, or with a counterparty primed to read silence as contempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — constructed dialogues plus deposition rules; the author concedes the examples are somewhat staged.

### [NEGO:THREE-PLUS-CAMP] Confirm every agreement at least three times
**Move:** Return to any agreed point three or more times, spread over time, before treating it as real — more repetitions for more critical deals — wrapping each repetition in nurturing or a reverse and framing each pass as another chance for them to refuse, never as a third close.
**APPLY WHEN:** Verifying a yes you distrust; long committee deals where agreement decays; anything where later reversal is expensive.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are needy — any urgency in the repetition converts it into pressure and inverts the purpose; the claim that it cannot be overdone is unfalsifiable as stated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise

### [NEGO:NO-TELLING-CAMP] Replace reasons with questions
**Move:** Stop supplying facts, figures and charm to make them see it your way; convert each point you wanted to assert into an interrogative-led question that makes them articulate it, and track which version gets them writing notes.
**APPLY WHEN:** You catch yourself talking more than listening; analytical counterparts; any moment you feel the urge to convince.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has explicitly asked for specifications, compliance answers or pricing they need to proceed — withholding then reads as gamesmanship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client's informal comparison of a didactic versus question-led analyst presentation.

### [NEGO:TALK-REVERSE-CAMP] Catch yourself talking and reverse
**Move:** The moment you notice you are doing most of the talking, cut yourself off explicitly, say so, and hand the floor back with an open question about how they see things coming together.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery and mid-deal calls; any moment you are explaining rather than asking.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has directly asked for information and needs a substantive answer — reversing there reads as evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — given as a standard adoption drill.

### [NEGO:NO-TALKING-CAMP] Talk materially less; prefer email early
**Move:** Deliberately under-talk — ask rather than assert, leave explanations shorter than feels comfortable, listen without planning your reply or interrupting, and when emotion rises drop pitch and slow delivery instead of speeding up. **APPLY WHEN:** Discovery, tense sessions, junior reps prone to over-explaining; any moment of adrenaline or credential-establishing.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart genuinely needs information to decide; silence used as pressure on a nervous small buyer damages trust; and email strips the tonal warmth the rest of the system depends on — a tension the source never reconciles.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a cardinal rule across the client base, the author conceding the phrasing is an exaggeration.

### [NEGO:NOTES-CAMP] Take notes as the expectation-clearing device
**Move:** Take continuous written notes in every meeting and call, including non-verbal behaviour and mood; bring a pad rather than leading with a card, record names immediately, and analyse afterwards rather than live.
**APPLY WHEN:** All meetings, especially multi-party sessions where you need a record of what was said.
**AVOID WHEN:** Note-taking visibly chills a sensitive disclosure, or the setting is legally sensitive — the source addresses neither case.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's repeated observation that the strongest people in the room take notes.

### [NEGO:BEAN-GUARD-CAMP] Guard your disclosures, harvest theirs
**Move:** Assume friendly framings exist partly to extract your cost and profit structure; say nothing in lobbies, side conversations or over drinks that you would not say at the table, while recording what the other side volunteers — prefaces of the "I shouldn't say this" type mark the disclosure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enterprise deals with relationship-heavy procurement; any time you are being socialised rather than negotiated with.
**AVOID WHEN:** The posture curdles into blanket distrust of cooperative counterparties — the known bias of this school, and it can kill genuinely collaborative deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise

### [NEGO:PLANT-ASSUMPTION-CAMP] Use vague words and let them fill in
**Move:** Answer scoping questions with elastic words — expensive, soon, a lot — and let the other side supply a definition that may be more generous than your real number; conversely never accept such a word from them without an interrogative pinning it down.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early price and timeline probes where a precise answer would anchor you low.
**AVOID WHEN:** Anywhere a stated figure will be relied on — this is the source's most explicitly manipulative technique, deliberately inducing a false belief about your price, with reputational and in regulated contexts legal exposure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative

### [NEGO:NO-RISK-ANSWER-CAMP] Answer without content when you must answer
**Move:** When refusing to answer is impossible, give a low-information reply that neither commits nor concedes — cost becomes "a lot", duration becomes "it seems like forever", an opinion request becomes respect for their view plus an unsettled position of your own.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pricing probes early in the deal; being asked to endorse a position you do not hold.
**AVOID WHEN:** Trust is the binding constraint or the relationship is long-running — this is the clearest candidate in the source for reading as evasive or dishonest once detected.

**Evidence:** assertion — claimed to work most of the time with no case behind it.

## Stage G — Interests and pain: discovery

### [NEGO:WHY-GTY] Ask why behind each stated position
**Move:** Take each position they have taken and ask what need, fear, hope or constraint made it the obvious choice — first reasoning it out yourself, then asking them directly, making clear you want the underlying concern and not a defence of the position.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discovery; any hard term stated as non-negotiable — contract length, payment schedule, seat count.
**AVOID WHEN:** The question reads as an audit of their authority, or trust is too low for an honest answer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the librarian/window story and Camp David, where security versus sovereignty replaced a map-splitting fight.

### [NEGO:WHYNOT-GTY] Build their currently-perceived-choice balance sheet
**Move:** Name whose decision you need, write the decision as they think you are asking for it, then list in their voice the consequences of yes and of no — scoring personal political cost, colleagues' reaction, time horizon, precedent, foreclosed options, consistency with their principles, and whether they can do it later.
**APPLY WHEN:** A stakeholder keeps not-deciding; the champion cannot get sign-off; a refusal you cannot explain.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no real information about their internal politics and would be inventing the sheet.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — worked out for a hypothetical student leader during the 1980 hostage crisis to show why holding was locally rational.

### [NEGO:MULTIINT-GTY] Write and rank the interest list; never a single "them"
**Move:** List both sides' interests as they occur to you, rank them by estimated importance and update as you learn, and refuse to collapse the other side into one actor — enumerate individuals, factions, their constituencies, and where their interests diverge from each other.
**APPLY WHEN:** Committee deals; deals where the negotiator's stated reasoning is obviously not their own.
**AVOID WHEN:** The list becomes a substitute for asking them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a baseball GM holding an unjustifiable salary line under secret instructions from owners in financial trouble.

### [NEGO:BASICNEEDS-GTY] Probe the five basic human needs under a money number
**Move:** When the negotiation looks purely monetary, test the number against security, economic well-being, belonging, recognition and control over one's own life — where money is a proxy for security or recognition, satisfy that another way and the number becomes negotiable.
**APPLY WHEN:** Price fights where their stance seems disproportionate; discount demands tied to fairness or respect claims.
**AVOID WHEN:** It becomes a pretext for not moving on price when price genuinely is the issue.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the US/Mexico natural-gas case, where price pressure insulted a counterparty needing to be treated as an equal.

### [NEGO:ALIVE-GTY] Make your interest concrete enough to feel
**Move:** State your interest with specifics — time, place, magnitude, the actual incident — rather than as a general grievance, assert its seriousness firmly, invite correction so that silence reads as acceptance, and legitimise it by asking them to imagine themselves in your position.
**APPLY WHEN:** Justifying a hard requirement, a deadline, a security or budget constraint.
**AVOID WHEN:** The specificity implies their interests are illegitimate, or the detail exposes information you should hold.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the neighbourhood/construction illustration built on a named child and a specific truck.

### [NEGO:ACKINT-GTY] Acknowledge their interests as part of the problem
**Move:** Before asserting yours, summarise their interests back, ask whether you have them right and whether anything is missing, and state explicitly that their interests are part of what any solution must handle.
**APPLY WHEN:** The start of any negotiation on terms; whenever they act as if you are indifferent to their constraints.
**AVOID WHEN:** The summary is a rhetorical prelude you then ignore.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the reciprocity of feeling understood.

### [NEGO:REASONSFIRST-GTY] Reasoning first, proposal last
**Move:** Lay out the problem, criteria and evidence and state the proposal only afterwards — leading with the demand guarantees they stop listening and start assembling counter-arguments, and principles delivered after the number read as post-hoc justification.
**APPLY WHEN:** Presenting a price increase, scope change, refund or uplift request — any ask likely to trigger an objection.
**AVOID WHEN:** The buyer explicitly wants the number first and will disengage during a preamble.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull established from the Rent Control Board that roughly $30,000 of improvements would be needed to justify a $268 increase.

### [NEGO:TIMEPROBE-GTY] Break a deadlock with timeline and consequence questions
**Move:** When both sides have stated incompatible positions, stop arguing them and ask each side two factual questions — when do you actually need this, and what specifically goes wrong if you do what they are demanding now — then look for a sequencing solution where the timelines do not in fact collide.
**APPLY WHEN:** Hard deadlock over an either/or where the real constraints are timing or scope rather than the headline term.
**AVOID WHEN:** The conflict is genuinely simultaneous and zero-sum, or emotion is high and venting has not happened.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — displaced farmers versus a national oil company after 2003, where a three-year production timeline met a six-week harvest and produced coexistence.

### [NEGO:ILLUSTRATIVE-GTY] Use illustrative specificity instead of an opening position
**Move:** Work out exactly what you would want them to agree to if they said yes tomorrow, then present it as one illustration of something that would meet your interest, held explicitly as illustrative with more than one option in mind — the anchoring and clarity of an opening position without committing your ego to it.
**APPLY WHEN:** You must put a number or structure on the table but do not want it to become a defended position.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterparty treats any number as binding; procurement processes that log first offers as commitments.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a sports-agent illustration pairing a salary figure with a contract length, each tied to a named interest.

### [NEGO:PAIN-HUNT-NURTURE-CAMP] Hunt concealed pain with a nurturing interrogative
**Move:** Assume the real problem is camouflaged, especially inside large organisations where divisions hide problems from each other — approach whoever most likely holds it, lower the stakes of answering ("how are you handling all of this?"), let them talk until the private motive surfaces, then propose relief for exactly that motive.
**APPLY WHEN:** Their stated position makes no economic sense; committee deals with private agendas.
**AVOID WHEN:** Safety has not been established — nobody exposes a problem to someone they expect to exploit it; social-proximity routes read as surveillance if surfaced later.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an acquisition where the founder's widow wanted a memorial; one case, outcome self-reported.

### [NEGO:PAIN-PERMISSION-PREAMBLE-CAMP] Ask permission to be wrong before painting a costly mistake
**Move:** When they have sunk money into a wrong direction, disarm yourself first — ask for patience, say you may be out of your mind and want to be told so, admit everything you say will sound self-serving — then instead of describing your view, ask a sequence of interrogative-led questions starting with where the industry is heading, and restate the frame three times.
**APPLY WHEN:** They are committed to a path you believe is fatal, you have a genuine remedy, and the problem is invisible to them.
**AVOID WHEN:** Their decision is cheaply reversible, the stakes do not justify challenging their competence, or you cannot survive an honest refusal of your premise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one meeting between two company presidents; outcome not quantified.

### [NEGO:PAIN-BY-DECLINING-CAMP] Paint their problem by saying you cannot proceed
**Move:** State plainly and unemotionally that you want to be their long-term supplier but cannot continue at current terms because you are losing money and will not survive it, apologise for the position it puts them in, decline to spell out what they lose, and ask an interrogative about how they think the problem can be solved.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are structurally underwater on an existing contract and are one of few qualified suppliers.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are replaceable or cannot actually accept the walk-away — this is a real refusal, not a bluff, and being called on it ends the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/channel · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Network, Inc. renegotiation; single case, all figures self-reported.

### [NEGO:PAIN-CALIBRATE-CAMP] Keep the brush light — avoid the emotional pendulum

### [NEGO:BUILD-THEIR-NEED-CAMP] Question the counterpart's stake instead of defending your price

## Stage H — Inventing options and shaping packages

### [NEGO:BRAINSEP-GTY] Separate inventing from deciding (structured brainstorm)
**Move:** Run a dedicated invention session away from the table — 5–8 people, a different room and time, a facilitator, everyone seated side by side facing a board, criticism outlawed, every idea written up in full view — then star the promising ones, improve them, and schedule a separate decision meeting.
**APPLY WHEN:** The deal has multiple movable variables, or you are stuck on one axis before a complex call.
**AVOID WHEN:** Simple one-variable transactions, no time before the meeting, or a group that treats any idea aired as a commitment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/outbound/expansion · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — Harvard Negotiation Project standing practice, presented as method with no dataset.

### [NEGO:JOINTBRAIN-GTY] Brainstorm with the other side, protected
**Move:** Hold a joint invention session explicitly labelled as not a negotiating session and off the record, protecting yourself by always advancing at least two alternatives at once and tabling options you obviously do not want, so everything reads as a possibility rather than a proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** The relationship survives the deal, both sides are stuck, and you need to educate them about your constraints.
**AVOID WHEN:** Confidential pricing could leak, the counterparty logs everything as an offer, or the counterpart is adversarial.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the union/coal-mine facilitator transcript.

### [NEGO:CIRCLE-GTY] Circle Chart — shuttle specific↔general
**Move:** Take one workable idea up four rungs — the concrete problem, a descriptive diagnosis in general terms, general prescriptive approaches, then new specific actions — and generate fresh concrete moves from each general theory, turning one good option into a factory for others.
**APPLY WHEN:** You already have one decent option and want a family of variants; joint problem framing.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterparty needs a decision now and abstraction reads as stalling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/stage
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a Northern Ireland joint history workbook and the projects derived from it.

### [NEGO:EXPERTLENS-GTY] Look through the eyes of different experts
**Move:** Re-diagnose the deal as a banker, tax expert, labour leader, real-estate speculator, economist or inventor would, harvesting a distinct diagnosis, approach and action from each profession.
**APPLY WHEN:** Solo prep with no brainstorm group available; the option list has gone stale.
**AVOID WHEN:** Purely transactional purchases where only price moves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — technique with illustrative lists, no support cited.

### [NEGO:AGRSTRENGTH-GTY] Invent agreements of different strengths
**Move:** Keep weaker fallback shapes on hand along the ladder substantive→procedural, permanent→provisional, comprehensive→partial, final→in-principle, unconditional→contingent, binding→nonbinding — and if you cannot agree on substance, agree on procedure, or at minimum on a shared map of exactly where you disagree.
**APPLY WHEN:** The deal is stalling and a full close is out of reach this quarter; pilot or letter-of-intent territory.
**AVOID WHEN:** A weak form cannibalises the strong one still available, or internal approval treats a pilot as the ceiling.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a checklist of agreement types.

### [NEGO:SCOPEVARY-GTY] Change the scope — fractionate or enlarge
**Move:** Shrink the unit (fewer parties, narrower subject, one region, limited term, a paid trial slice) or enlarge it by bringing in new subject matter or a party with resources.
**APPLY WHEN:** Budget or trust is the blocker (shrink); the pie is genuinely too small to split (enlarge).
**AVOID WHEN:** Slicing destroys the value of the whole, or enlarging pulls in stakeholders who add veto power.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Indus River dispute became settleable once the World Bank joined and funded new works.

### [NEGO:SHAREDGOAL-GTY] Convert latent shared interests into an explicit joint goal
**Move:** Interrogate the deal for latent common ground — the relationship, future cooperation, what breakdown costs each side, a common principle such as a fair price — then make it concrete and future-oriented as a joint target so the concession you want reads as an action toward a shared goal.
**APPLY WHEN:** A price fight where both sides also depend on a continuing relationship; renewal or expansion.
**AVOID WHEN:** A pure one-shot transaction, or where you cannot name a real shared goal and the talk reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Townsend Oil / Pageville tax fight resolved via shared industrial-expansion goals.

### [NEGO:DOVETAIL-GTY] Dovetail differing interests
**Move:** Hunt asymmetries and trade across them — differences in interests (form vs substance, symbolic vs practical, precedent vs this case, near vs distant future), in beliefs (agree an arbitrator), in time discounting (installments), in forecasts (low base plus performance bonus) and in risk aversion (low early rates, higher after payback) — looking for items of low cost to you and high benefit to them.
**APPLY WHEN:** The two sides forecast the future differently or value timing and risk differently — earn-outs, ramps, milestone pricing.
**AVOID WHEN:** A contingent structure will be gamed or is unverifiable, or procurement forbids variable terms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=consultative/enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the deep-seabed mining treaty traded risk for revenue via low early rates.

### [NEGO:PREFPAIRS-GTY] Ask which they prefer, not whether it is acceptable
**Move:** Build two or more packages equally acceptable to you and ask which is preferable, then take the winner, generate two variants of it and ask again, iterating until no further joint gains appear — nobody has to decide anything along the way.
**APPLY WHEN:** Mid-negotiation shaping of terms; the buyer will not state priorities directly.
**AVOID WHEN:** The variants are not truly equal for you (you will be traded down), or too many rounds exhaust a transactional buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a sports-agent script pairing $8.75M×4yr against $10M×3yr, then against a bonus-loaded variant.

### [NEGO:CRITICSIM-GTY] Simulate their worst critic
**Move:** Write a sentence or two of what the counterparty's most damaging internal critic would say if they accepted your proposal, then write their defence, and redesign the option until the defence holds.
**APPLY WHEN:** The champion faces a hostile committee, procurement or a board; before sending a proposal.
**AVOID WHEN:** A solo owner-buyer with no constituency to answer to.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as a preparation exercise.

### [NEGO:MP-ANTI-UNBUNDLE-CAMP] Refuse to break a proposal into separately negotiable pieces
**Move:** When procurement tries to split an integrated proposal into components to negotiate each down, decline on the ground that the components were designed to work together to reduce the buyer's risk, and redirect to the real deciders and the cost of failure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex technical proposals where component pricing destroys the value logic; buyers optimising for up-front savings.
**AVOID WHEN:** The bundle really is separable and the refusal is margin defence dressed as principle; also weak if you cannot quantify the failure cost you point at.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a roughly $50 million chip-carrier-box proposal against four global makers; the client refused to unbundle and reportedly won at full price.

### [NEGO:PACKAGE-COMMIT-FIRST-CAMP] Secure accept/reject commitment before revealing the offer
**Move:** In senior hiring, commit to a top-of-industry package but decline to disclose it until they commit to either accept or reject it, stating plainly that you do not want the number used to start a bidding war with their current employer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Executive and senior-officer recruiting where counter-offers are the standard loss mode.
**AVOID WHEN:** Candidate-scarce markets or junior roles where opacity simply loses the candidate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few · offer=service/consulting · market=consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's stated standing coaching advice, no outcome data.

## Stage I — Presentations and proposal ritual

### [NEGO:DEFLECT-PRESENTATION-CAMP] Answer a presentation request with questions
**Move:** When asked to present or respond to an RFP, say honestly that you do not yet know how — you would need to know where they stand and what interests them — then ask what is driving the request: why now, how you could possibly compete with the incumbent, why you were invited at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** RFP-driven markets; a request to present arriving before you understand their problem or decision process.
**AVOID WHEN:** Formal procurement where non-response disqualifies you, or the request reflects genuine buying intent and refusal reads as evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a commercial real estate client who stopped producing RFP responses; no numbers.

### [NEGO:REVERSE-PRESENTATION-CAMP] Make them present to you
**Move:** When the counterpart finally asks for your proposal, suggest the reverse — it makes more sense for them to lay out their thinking, direction and requirements first, after which you respond with something tailored, their exposition becoming the brief.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late in a long cycle where you have already found the deciders and built the problem picture.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have not earned the standing — asked early it reads as refusal to do the work; also fails against counterparts who withhold requirements as leverage.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one deal reported at a couple of hundred million dollars after roughly ten months of prior work; self-reported.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-AGENDA-CAMP] Agenda-gated presentation with "maybe" banned
**Move:** Before agreeing to present, negotiate a written agenda — what will be covered, what deliberately will not, who attends, and the explicit understanding that the outcome is a yes or a no — so that "we'll think about it" has no landing space; a refusal to agree is itself the information you needed.
**APPLY WHEN:** A formal demo or pitch is mandated; late-stage deals where the presentation is the last remaining ritual.
**AVOID WHEN:** Early discovery, where a hard verdict gate forces a premature refusal on a deal that just needs more work; cultures where forcing a same-day verdict reads as coercion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/channel · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing coaching practice for clients forced into presentations, no dataset.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-QUALIFY-CAMP] Never present to the unqualified
**Move:** Confirm the people in the room are the actual deciders before showing anything — presenting your reasoning, pricing logic and differentiation to non-deciders scatters material that can be used against you.
**APPLY WHEN:** An intermediary asks you to "just present to the team first"; processes where the audience list is vague.
**AVOID WHEN:** A champion genuinely needs ammunition and access to deciders is structurally impossible; also a poor fit in self-serve motions where nobody is a decider in this sense.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — restated from the decision-maker chapter as a standing rule of the system.

### [NEGO:PRESENT-SUBTRACT-CAMP] Addition by subtraction
**Move:** Cut everything from the deck that does not address a concern the buyer has actually voiced, then sequence the survivors by their stated order of importance and name that order out loud.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have real discovery notes and can rank the buyer's concerns; competitive situations where a kitchen-sink deck invites avoidable objections.
**AVOID WHEN:** Discovery was thin — subtraction then leaves almost nothing, which is itself the signal not to present yet; or a regulated evaluation where omitting a required capability disqualifies you.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the tire and lawn examples framed with a sports adage.

### [NEGO:EVAL-CRITERIA-CAMP] Ask how they evaluate, then build to that
**Move:** Before assembling any demonstration material, ask directly how they evaluate candidates or options, take each answer literally however narrow, and cut a separate version per counterpart against their stated criterion rather than against what you find impressive about yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-buyer processes where each evaluator scores differently; demos, proof-of-concept scoping, reference and case-study selection.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stated criteria are a smokescreen for a pre-selected vendor, or a committee sets criteria and no single answer represents the group.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the football-recruit story; outcome reported by the author, not measured.

## Stage J — Legitimacy, criteria and price

### [NEGO:PREPSTANDARDS-GTY] Prepare a menu of fair standards in advance
**Move:** Before the meeting, list several independent criteria that could decide the number and work out how each applies to your case, preferring standards that are independent of either side's will, legitimate, practical, and able to survive the reciprocity test.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any price, scope or term dispute; procurement benchmarking; renewal uplift fights.
**AVOID WHEN:** No credible external standard exists for your category — inventing one invites attack.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — core Harvard method, illustrated across insurance, construction and real estate.

### [NEGO:JOINTSEARCH-GTY] Frame the issue as a joint search for the standard
**Move:** Open by naming the obvious conflict and converting it into a shared task — you want high, I want low, so let us work out what a fair number would be and which standards are relevant — proposing one or two yourself and then inviting theirs.
**APPLY WHEN:** The first substantive pricing conversation; an anchoring war is starting.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterparty is running a scripted hard-bargaining play and will simply pocket your standard.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — one of three basic points, with a house-buying script.

### [NEGO:WHATSTHEORY-GTY] "How did you arrive at that figure?"
**Move:** When they state a number, neither accept nor reject it — ask for the theory behind it as though they too were looking for a fair number, then apply their own stated standard consistently to the comparables they did not mention.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer states a budget cap, a discount demand, or a competitor's price.
**AVOID WHEN:** The number is arbitrary and they know it — expect a policy answer and escalate to refusing to move except on principle.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the claimant/insurance-adjuster dialogue.

### [NEGO:PRINCIPLEFIRST-GTY] Agree on the principle before the terms
**Move:** Settle which standard applies before discussing any specific figure, so that deferring to it later is an act of consistency rather than surrender — which is what makes it acceptable to them.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-round negotiation; committee buyers who need a defensible rationale.
**AVOID WHEN:** A transactional buyer wants a number now and reads process talk as evasion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement with the comparable-houses script.

### [NEGO:FAIRPROC-GTY] Use a fair procedure when no fair standard settles it
**Move:** When substance will not yield, switch to procedure — one cuts and the other chooses, take turns, draw lots, delegate to an expert or arbitrator, or trade across issues; and split the difference only between figures each backed by a legitimate standard, since splitting arbitrary numbers just produces an arbitrary result.
**APPLY WHEN:** Allocation deadlocks, multi-party splits, or a last gap where both positions are defensible.
**AVOID WHEN:** Used early — it converts a principled negotiation back into horse-trading; and note last-best-offer arbitration can force a choice between two extremes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=channel/expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Law of the Sea site allocation; the claim that last-best-offer arbitration produces more settlements is unsourced, treat as assertion.

### [NEGO:OPENJUDGE-GTY] Reason and be open to reason
**Move:** Behave like a judge who happens to be predisposed to one side — insist the outcome rest on objective criteria but not necessarily on yours; when two legitimate standards collide, look for a meta-basis or split between the two results, and if still stuck ask a respected third party to pick the standard rather than decide the substance.
**APPLY WHEN:** Both sides have defensible benchmarks; deadlock over whose comparable set counts.
**AVOID WHEN:** "It's a matter of principle" is being used as an ideological battle cry — the counterfeit of this move, which locks people in harder.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement plus the deep-seabed economics model illustration.

### [NEGO:NOYIELD-GTY] Yield to principle, never to pressure
**Move:** Treat bribes, threats, manipulative appeals to trust and flat refusals identically — invite them to state their reasoning, restate the criteria you think apply, refuse to move on any other basis, and explicitly de-couple unrelated sweeteners. Never enter with a rigid bottom line; commit to interests and to the standards that will decide the number.
**APPLY WHEN:** Last-minute demands, take-it-or-leave-it framings, relationship guilt-trips.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deal has real time-value to you exceeding the concession at stake — the source never prices the delay this move buys.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — contractor and insurance dialogues; the claim that the principled side generally has an edge is unmeasured.

### [NEGO:STANDOFFTEST-GTY] Test a nonnegotiable position before walking
**Move:** If they will not budge and will not give a basis, first check whether some standard you overlooked actually makes their offer fair and accept on that basis if so; otherwise weigh what accepting an unjustified position gains you against your alternative and against the reputational value of not rewarding arbitrary positions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Fixed-price or policy-locked counterparties; the end of a stalled cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no alternative developed — then the walk is theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning presented directly.

### [NEGO:STDWEIGH-GTY] Weigh competing fairness standards on three criteria
**Move:** When both sides bring different standards, stop fighting over which is best and rank by directness to the point, breadth of acceptance, and immediacy in time, place and circumstance — and where that is not decisive, examine how each standard developed.
**APPLY WHEN:** Benchmark wars and justification battles — "your competitor charges X".
**AVOID WHEN:** No external benchmarks exist for a one-of-a-kind item; shift to interests and invented options instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus illustrations, including a young lawyer countering a forty-year-old salary figure with current comparable firms.

### [NEGO:LEGITSHIELD-GTY] Standards as both sword and shield
**Move:** Use external legitimacy in two directions — as a sword to persuade and as a shield against arbitrary pressure to concede: the price is firm because it is what a named comparable buyer paid, with documentation available, and the burden sits on them to show why this case differs.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discount pressure; price defence; scope creep.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your comparable is weak or cherry-picked and will be checked.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued by analogy to legal precedent, with scripts.

### [NEGO:LEGITPRECEDENT-GTY] Dress the ask in their own precedent
**Move:** Shape the proposal so it looks fair, legal and honourable to them, hunt for a decision or statement they already made in a comparable case, and base your request on it, leaning on their desire to stay consistent.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer must justify the deal upward; policy or "we don't do that" objections.
**AVOID WHEN:** The precedent you cite embarrasses them — consistency pressure then becomes a face threat.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — asserted from general observations about legitimacy.

### [NEGO:NUMBERS-LATE-CAMP] Treat numbers as limitations and defer them
**Move:** Hold prices and quantities back until the picture is built — as seller, do not seek the buyer's intended spend in the first meeting; as buyer, do not seek the seller's price; first wants should concern their baggage, vision, problem and budgets.
**APPLY WHEN:** Consultative deals where value is still being established; complex configurations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Simple transactions, where the author himself allows the exception, and buyers who need indicative pricing to justify continuing — refusing then burns credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one client's unit price moving from $185 to $290 after adopting the method; a single self-reported point.

### [NEGO:PRICE-HIGH-TO-BE-SEEN-CAMP] Price high enough that value becomes visible
**Move:** Recognise that a price set too low inside the buyer's frame of reference makes value invisible while a high price sends them looking for it — set the price to be seen, not merely to cover margin.
**APPLY WHEN:** Premium positioning; buyers with a well-heeled reference frame; genuinely differentiated product.
**AVOID WHEN:** Commodity or procurement-led buying where price is the whole scorecard — a high price with nothing behind it just ends the conversation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/channel · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=product/info-product/service · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Big Bertha driver launched around $400, a marketing story rather than a controlled result.

### [NEGO:FULL-RATE-ASK-CAMP] State full terms once, then explicitly permit refusal
**Move:** Present the whole package you actually want — rate, minimum engagement, travel, payment terms — in one pass, add that they should say no if it is too much with no hard feelings, offer to point them elsewhere, and then stop talking.
**APPLY WHEN:** Underpricing driven by fear; expert, consulting and service pricing; inbound requests where you hold scarce expertise.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your terms are not defensible against market comparables — the move relies on the number being in line, not merely bold.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/founder-led · acv=micro/low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a legal expert habitually billing ~$100/hour asked $500/hour with a two-day minimum, accepted about 45 minutes later.

### [NEGO:NEG-EXPECT-ANCHOR-CAMP] Hold the sheet against an opening stipulation
**Move:** When the counterparty opens by stipulating a volume-discount price for a single unit, treat it as an early number of no significance — decline calmly, cite the absence of any mandate in your mission to break the rate sheet, offer a nurtured alternative as an interrogative, then stop and wait.
**APPLY WHEN:** Capital-equipment and high-ACV sales where discount tiers are public or inferable; first-contact lowball anchors.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your pricing has no defensible structure, or the alternative you suggest loses you the deal to a channel partner.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single typed case, equipment at $1.7M list against a $1.3M volume price demanded for one unit.

### [NEGO:POS-EXPECT-BAIT-CAMP] Refuse to price against an inflated volume
**Move:** When you hear encouragement plus a large number, do not compute the total, do not quote your best discounted price, and do not talk price at all before you understand the deal — verify the stated volume against their real capacity or consumption first.
**APPLY WHEN:** Procurement-led enterprise deals; consolidation pitches; any time volume is dangled before terms are set.
**AVOID WHEN:** The volume is genuinely verified — the move is about unverified numbers, not about refusing volume pricing as such.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound/expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — 33,000 units dangled at roughly $1,000 each against a worldwide capacity of 22,000 and a real requirement of 3,000.

### [NEGO:REFUSE-WHIPSAW-CAMP] Exit the price-down auction by declining to bid further
**Move:** When a large buyer plays suppliers against each other for successive reductions, state that you will not participate in further cuts and are therefore probably not the right supplier — handing them the refusal and removing yourself from the auction.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-vendor bake-offs with repeated concession rounds where you have capability the buyer actually needs.
**AVOID WHEN:** You are genuinely substitutable and the buyer can proceed without you; or procurement is bound by a scored process you cannot exit and re-enter.
**Deal-gate:** motion=inbound/outbound · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a supplier withdrew from further reductions, the buyer began sharing information, and the supplier reportedly won.

### [NEGO:NO-EARLY-COMPROMISE-CAMP] Defer every compromise until it is proven necessary
**Move:** Treat each concession as requiring a demonstrated reason you can name rather than a gesture of goodwill; if you cannot state why it is needed now, do not make it, and hold the position while continuing to talk.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early stage, before you know the counterpart's real problem, budget or decision process.
**AVOID WHEN:** A deadline you genuinely own is expiring; or regulated collective bargaining where good-faith bargaining is legally mandated — explicitly exempted by the source.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a licensing negotiation where a $400,000 opening offer from a proxy buyer ended at $8 million.

### [NEGO:CONCESSION-SUSPICION-CAMP] Read over-concession as a competence signal
**Move:** As buyer, when a seller concedes on everything, treat the pattern as evidence they may not be able to deliver; as seller, remember that stacking concessions can lose the deal outright rather than win it.
**APPLY WHEN:** A supplier's terms improve on every axis at once; your own team is preparing a best-everything bid.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart has a legitimate strategic reason for aggressive pricing — land-grab, reference account — since the inference is heuristic, not proof.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a client conceded on every axis and lost because the buyer's CEO read desperation as delivery risk.

### [NEGO:FAVOR-LEDGER-CAMP] Convert an accommodation into an explicit ask, with a refusal option
**Move:** When you absorbed real cost to accommodate them and the need then disappeared, state plainly what you did, ask for the specific compensating commitment, and add that they may decline with no hard feelings.
**APPLY WHEN:** Expedites, scope stretches or emergency delivery you performed on request.
**AVOID WHEN:** The accommodation was standard service, or you never flagged its cost at the time — retroactive invoicing of goodwill reads as bad faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/inbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few · offer=product/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a machinery maker rearranged production, the need vanished, and the customer nevertheless took two older systems.

### [NEGO:NO-NURTURE-INTERROGATIVE-CAMP] Refuse, nurture, then hand the problem back
**Move:** Deliver the refusal plainly, soften it immediately with a statement of wanting to keep working together, and finish with an open interrogative that puts the search for an alternative on them — three beats: refusal, warmth, question.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discount and scope demands you intend to refuse; anywhere a flat refusal would otherwise end the conversation.
**AVOID WHEN:** The refusal is final and no alternative exists — the trailing question then invites a negotiation you do not want; also weak on requests you would in fact accept, which trains them to push.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a canonical drill script for new adopters.

## Stage K — Alternatives, budgets, leverage and power

### [NEGO:BATNA-GTY] Judge every offer against your best alternative, not a bottom line
**Move:** Replace "the lowest I will accept" with "what will I actually do if no deal happens by date X" — name the concrete alternative and measure each proposal against it. Bottom lines are rejected outright: arbitrary, usually set too high, they close your ears and suppress multi-variable packages, while an alternative stays compatible with creative repackaging.
**APPLY WHEN:** Every deal, before the first pricing conversation; discount pressure.
**AVOID WHEN:** Never avoided — but do not confuse it with a walk-away script; it is a measuring instrument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the school's central framework, taught and applied across Harvard casework, no dataset.

### [NEGO:BATNADEV-GTY] Develop the alternative in three operations
**Move:** List everything you might do if no agreement is reached, convert the most promising into a real executable alternative rather than a hypothetical one, and tentatively select the best — which raises your minimum rather than merely measuring it.
**APPLY WHEN:** Entering an asymmetric negotiation; a pipeline of one; a renewal where the incumbent knows you cannot switch.
**AVOID WHEN:** Developing the alternative costs more than the concession at stake.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a small town versus a corporation; a strike-authorisation example.

### [NEGO:TRIPWIRE-GTY] Set a trip wire above the alternative
**Move:** Identify one distinctly imperfect package still better than your no-deal path and treat crossing it as a mandatory pause to re-examine — also a clean way to cap an agent's authority and leave reserve room for a mediator.
**APPLY WHEN:** Delegating to a rep or broker; long negotiations where fatigue erodes judgement.
**AVOID WHEN:** It hardens into a de-facto bottom line and re-imports the rigidity the method rejects.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — offered as a supplementary test.

### [NEGO:BATNADISCLOSE-GTY] Disclose your alternative selectively
**Move:** Reveal the alternative only when it is genuinely strong or when they underestimate it, and stay silent when your true alternative is weaker than they imagine.
**APPLY WHEN:** Competitive deals; multiple offers in hand.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your alternative is weak — disclosure hands them the deal, and bluffing about it is not sanctioned.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a judgement call, with non-disclosure in the Turnbull case as illustration.

### [NEGO:THEIRBATNA-GTY] Estimate — and if necessary change — their alternative
**Move:** Work out what they do without you and correct their overestimates by helping them think through whether the alternatives are realistic, since people aggregate options they cannot have simultaneously; if their alternative is so good they see no reason to negotiate on merits, act to change it, and if both alternatives are strong the best outcome may be discovering quickly that there is no deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer claims they will build it internally or do nothing; incumbent displacement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The only way to worsen their alternative is coercion or litigation — the source's own example burns the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a community group versus a power plant, plus job-interview and street-vendor illustrations.

### [NEGO:MICROBATNA-GTY] Prepare a meeting-level alternative and a drafted exit line
**Move:** Beyond the overall alternative, decide what the best outcome is if no agreement is reached at this particular meeting, and draft the exit line in advance — thank them, and state that if you decide to proceed you will return, possibly with a fresh proposal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-meeting cycles; any meeting that could end without a decision.
**AVOID WHEN:** The exit line functions as a veiled threat.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — practice advice with a competing-offer script.

### [NEGO:BATNASHIFT-GTY] Legitimately worsen their alternative
**Move:** Power comes both from improving your alternatives and from lowering their estimate of theirs, and the second can sometimes be done legitimately — by removing a resource you control and making your position explicit rather than by coercion.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart's stated alternative is soft, unpriced, or rests on a resource you control.
**AVOID WHEN:** The only available means are coercive or deceptive — the tactic then fails the school's own reciprocity and legitimacy test.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a domestic example, explicitly flagged by the authors as dual-use.

### [NEGO:POWERBLEND-GTY] Use the power sources in harmony, not one at a time
**Move:** Do not lead with your single strongest lever — confronting them with a strong alternative and a walk-away threat undercuts your fairness arguments; if you communicate the alternative, do it while respecting the relationship, keeping communication open, underscoring the legitimacy of your last offer and showing how it serves their interests. The seven sources — relationship, interests, options, legitimacy, alternative, commitment, communication — compound.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any high-leverage moment where you are tempted to play the strongest card alone.
**AVOID WHEN:** Not contraindicated, but the harmony principle assumes time and channel enough for a layered argument.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — synthesis statement.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-TRIPLE-CAMP] Track three budgets on both sides
**Move:** For every live deal maintain a running assessment of your time-and-energy, money and emotional investment plus a parallel estimate of theirs — money being the only numeric one — and keep yours low while letting theirs climb.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any multi-month or multi-stakeholder deal; whenever you hear yourself justify continuing by what you have already spent.
**AVOID WHEN:** Short transactional cycles where the bookkeeping costs more than it saves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — coaching framework across the client base; the 1x/2x/3x/4x weights are admitted to be non-empirical.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-BUILD-THEIRS-CAMP] Deliberately raise the counterpart's investment
**Move:** Use ordinary friction to make their side spend — do not return urgent calls immediately, leave messages requiring a callback, cancel your own meetings when appropriate, ask them to redo work that does not make sense, and use short pauses to signal non-neediness.
**APPLY WHEN:** The counterpart is dallying or running budget-building tactics on you; long-cycle deals where time favours you.
**AVOID WHEN:** You need the deal on a clock; you are a small vendor facing a buyer who can outspend you; or the counterpart is trained in the same system, where the author concedes the tactics are pointless. This is deliberate cost imposition and corrodes trust if detected.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a 15-second hold during a six-month deal; the Paris table-shape stall used as historical analogy, not evidence.

### [NEGO:BUDGET-ASK-DIRECT-CAMP] Ask what they would budget, and offer to build it with them
**Move:** Ask outright what they would budget to solve this problem, and when they say they do not know in dollars, answer that you will build the necessary budget together — expecting budget to surface indirectly as they describe the problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** After the problem is at least partly visible; consultative and enterprise cycles where budget is not pre-allocated.
**AVOID WHEN:** Asked cold before any problem picture exists — it reads as qualification theatre and invites a lowball anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a scripted exchange presented as routine coaching practice.

### [NEGO:MONEY-BUDGET-WRITE-OFF-CAMP] Set the money budget and be ready to lose all of it
**Move:** Decide in advance the cash you will spend pursuing the deal — travel, legal, engineering time — and treat it as fully expendable; if you lack reserves for the long haul, do not start, and find deals you can afford to lose.
**APPLY WHEN:** Negotiating against a larger counterparty that can escalate costs through repeated rework and attrition.
**AVOID WHEN:** The prepared-to-lose-it stance becomes an excuse to skip cost discipline — and note it is far easier advice for the well-capitalised than for the small supplier it addresses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — four clients flew to Ireland, were stood up, and wrote off $20,000.

## Stage L — When they won't play: hard bargainers, stalls and pressure

### [NEGO:LOOKBEHIND-GTY] Don't attack their position — mine it
**Move:** Neither reject nor accept their stated position: treat it as one option, assume it is a genuine attempt to solve the problem, ask how it addresses each side's concerns and what principle makes it fair, then ask hypothetically what would actually happen if you accepted it as stated.
**APPLY WHEN:** The buyer opens with an ultimatum, a fixed discount demand or a non-negotiable term.
**AVOID WHEN:** The position is a scripted opening designed to consume your time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the 1970 Nasser interview, where the demand's unworkability became visible to its own author.

### [NEGO:INVITECRIT-GTY] Don't defend your proposal — invite criticism and advice
**Move:** Ask what is wrong with it rather than whether they accept it, ask which of their concerns it fails to cover, ask what they would do in your position — and rework the proposal in front of them using what the criticism reveals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Proposal or pricing pushback; a champion who keeps relaying objections.
**AVOID WHEN:** Invited criticism becomes a free list of demands with nothing offered back — the move assumes reciprocal good faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — teacher and school-board scripts, no data.

### [NEGO:RECAST-GTY] Recast a personal attack as an attack on the problem
**Move:** When attacked personally, neither defend nor counterattack: let them finish, show you heard the concern underneath, restate the attack as a shared concern about the problem, and ask what you can both do about it now.
**APPLY WHEN:** Blame, accusations of bad faith, emotional escalation on a call.
**AVOID WHEN:** The attack is a deliberate repeated tactic rather than vented emotion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — strike and education scripts.

### [NEGO:ONETEXT-GTY] Run the one-text procedure
**Move:** Have a third party — or yourself acting as mediator — interview both sides about interests rather than positions, publish a list of needs for criticism, produce a deliberately imperfect draft and ask only what is wrong with it, iterating through drafts without ever asking anyone to concede, then recommend the final version once as a single yes-or-no.
**APPLY WHEN:** Both sides are dug in; multi-party deals; you personally care more about closing than about exact terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** The third party treats it as position-clarification and presses for concessions — that version deepens attachment instead of dissolving it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/channel/expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=service/consulting/saas/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Camp David 1978 (13 days, roughly 23 drafts) and the Law of the Sea prototype.

### [NEGO:FAIRSTANCE-GTY] Announce that fairness, not money, is the standard you will move on
**Move:** State plainly what would satisfy you in principle, commit to sticking to that basis, and simultaneously signal you are persuadable within it — leaving the reasoned route as the only route to their own interest — while making explicit that you are not turning this into a contest of who can do what to whom.
**APPLY WHEN:** They accuse you of leveraging a hostage position; disputes over refunds, overcharges, penalties.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no principled basis and are in fact only after the money.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Turnbull case.

### [NEGO:TRUSTAPART-GTY] "Trust is a separate issue" — and use "and", not "but"
**Move:** When trust is invoked to corner you, reaffirm the appreciation *and* restate the principle, deliberately avoiding the word that erases the first clause, then hand the question back by asking what considerations they think should decide the matter — keeping the framing impersonal.
**APPLY WHEN:** "Don't you trust me?", loyalty appeals, relationship guilt used to skip verification.
**AVOID WHEN:** Not contraindicated within this school — but it presumes they will re-engage on reasons.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Turnbull case; the point about the erasing conjunction is asserted.

### [NEGO:PAUSE-GTY] "Let me get back to you" — a pre-planned exit
**Move:** Never take an important decision at the table: arrive with a credible decisive-sounding reason to leave — consulting your constituency, checking facts — and set the next time before you go, so distance restores perspective and you return soft on the person and firm on the problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pressure to sign now, on-the-spot discount demands, long draining sessions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The exit reads as inability to decide, or contradicts authority you claimed to have.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — general negotiator practice, illustrated by Turnbull.

### [NEGO:REFRAME-GTY] Reframe a positional number into interests, options, standards or alternatives
**Move:** Against a flat positional number, pick a reframe instead of a counter-number — to interests (ask what sits behind it), to options (treat it as one of several and propose inventing more), to standards (ask how they arrived at it, then present the market figure), or to alternatives (acknowledge it is their call and ask whether a mutually workable agreement is possible at all).
**APPLY WHEN:** The conversation has collapsed into positional haggling.
**AVOID WHEN:** Overuse makes you look evasive on the number; also weak against a counterpart who simply repeats the position.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — referenced to the wider reframing literature, notably Ury, *Getting Past No* (1991/1993).

### [NEGO:WALKOUT-GTY] Principled walkout on legitimate grounds
**Move:** As the last procedural resort, state that the process as run cannot produce a result, leave a contact route open, and name what you will do instead — keeping it non-punitive and reversible, since they are likely to call back if the ground for leaving is clearly legitimate, such as proven deception about facts or authority.
**APPLY WHEN:** Documented misrepresentation, or repeated escalation after agreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your alternative is weak or unexamined — used as theatre it becomes just another lock-in and costs credibility on return.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — illustrative scripts, not measured outcomes.

### [NEGO:VERIFY-GTY] Make the deal independent of trust
**Move:** Do not extend trust you have no basis for, and do not frame verification as an accusation — normalise routine checking of factual assertions applied to both sides, and build verification into the process so that lying stops paying.
**APPLY WHEN:** Unverified claims about volumes, incumbent pricing, competing bids, budget ceilings.
**AVOID WHEN:** Verification costs more than the exposure, or a long verified track record makes demanding proof a signal of bad faith.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as principle, no data.

### [NEGO:CONTINGENT-GTY] Turn their certainty into a compliance clause
**Move:** When you doubt they will perform but they insist they will, take the protestation at face value and price it into the structure — escalate their claimed certainty, then propose a contingency that costs them nothing if they are right, which is hard to refuse without admitting the doubt.
**APPLY WHEN:** Performance risk, unproven counterparties, promised volumes, migration commitments, payment reliability.
**AVOID WHEN:** The contingency is unenforceable or so punitive it reads as a trap; it also fails against a counterpart who will sign anything they do not intend to honour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a single worked legal scenario.

### [NEGO:CANDIDGAP-GTY] Candid non-disclosure (less than full disclosure ≠ deception)
**Move:** Good faith does not require revealing your reservation point: when asked for your maximum, decline explicitly, name the temptation to mislead, and route the question elsewhere — for instance to a trusted neutral who can report only whether a zone of agreement exists.
**APPLY WHEN:** "What's your real bottom number" pressure; broker or intermediary contexts.
**AVOID WHEN:** No credible neutral is available, or the ask is routine and the refusal ritual makes you look evasive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — the authors' normative position.

### [NEGO:ATTACKNAME-GTY] Neutralise personal and status attacks by naming them
**Move:** Recognise the family of discomfort tactics — remarks on your appearance or fatigue, being made to wait, interruptions, implied ignorance, refusing to listen so you repeat yourself, withheld eye contact — since recognition alone blunts most of the effect, and raising it explicitly usually prevents repetition. Question the behaviour, not the person.
**APPLY WHEN:** Status games from senior buyers or hard-bargainer procurement.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot describe the behaviour without accusation, or the rudeness is habitual rather than tactical — then it is a people problem, not a procedural one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** external-research — simple student experiments on withheld eye contact, source not named in-text; treat as thin.

### [NEGO:GOODBAD-GTY] Same principle question to both good guy and bad guy
**Move:** When one counterpart plays hardline and the partner rescues you with a small concession that feels like a favour, refuse the frame: thank the reasonable one and ask him the identical question you asked the hardliner — what principle makes that number fair — stating you would pay the higher figure if the fairness case is made.
**APPLY WHEN:** Two-person buying teams staging visible internal disagreement.
**AVOID WHEN:** The internal split is real — treating a genuine ally as a ploy destroys your only advocate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a staged example moving $80,000 to $76,000, a small concession that lands psychologically oversized.

### [NEGO:WARNVSTHREAT-GTY] Warnings instead of threats
**Move:** Avoid threats, which invite counter-threats and convert the question into whether to cave; instead communicate consequences that follow independently of your will, or state your own planned protective actions framed as safeguarding your interests rather than punishing theirs — and if asked whether that is a threat, answer honestly and invite a better way to protect yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** Renewals, contract lapses, escalation to alternatives.
**AVOID WHEN:** The warning is a disguised threat — they will hear the disguise; also weak where the consequence is obviously within your discretion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus illustrative scripts.

### [NEGO:THREATDEFUSE-GTY] Break the communication channel a threat needs
**Move:** Threats require credible transmission, so attack transmission — ignore them, treat them as unauthorised or hasty, make issuing them costly, use them against the sender publicly, or answer on principle that you respond only to reason and prefer first to agree threats are unproductive.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ultimatums, threatened churn, escalation, public complaint.
**AVOID WHEN:** The threat is a real and imminent commercial fact — ignoring it then wastes the remaining time.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a mediated coal-mine case where false bomb threats dropped once every call was announced as recorded.

### [NEGO:REFUSALPROBE-GTY] When they refuse to negotiate at all
**Move:** Read the refusal or the precondition list as a bargaining chip rather than a wall — do not attack it, probe the interest behind it (fear of conferring status, internal criticism, fragile unity, disbelief that agreement is possible), offer procedural options such as third-party channels or private intermediaries, then apply principle by asking whether they would want you to set preconditions.
**APPLY WHEN:** Ghosting, "we're not evaluating vendors", procurement gatekeeping, "see you in court".
**AVOID WHEN:** Their alternative is genuinely strong and negotiating costs them real option value — persistence then reads as desperation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a diplomatic case narrative from the 1979–81 hostage crisis.

### [NEGO:EXTREMEJUST-GTY] Demand principled justification of an extreme opening
**Move:** Against an anchor obviously far off market, do not counter-anchor — name the tactic and keep asking for the principled basis until the position looks absurd to them too, noting the costs the tactic imposes on its own user in credibility and killed deals.
**APPLY WHEN:** Lowball procurement anchors, "your competitor quoted half".
**AVOID WHEN:** An established haggling ritual where refusing to counter forfeits range; also fails against a counterpart happy to defend an absurd position indefinitely.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — worked illustration ($175,000 offered for a house apparently worth $300,000).

### [NEGO:ESCALPAUSE-GTY] Answer escalating demands with a named pause
**Move:** When each concession triggers a new demand or a reopened settled issue, name the pattern explicitly and then take a break to decide whether and on what basis to continue — the pause blocks an impulsive concession and signals you have registered the conduct as serious.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late-stage redlines that keep growing; repeated "one last thing" from a buyer.
**AVOID WHEN:** The new items are genuinely newly discovered on their side — a new stakeholder, a new compliance finding.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Malta extracting a £10 million advance and guaranteed dockyard jobs from Britain in 1971.

### [NEGO:LOCKLOOSE-GTY] Deflate a lock-in without making it the issue
**Move:** Commitment tactics work only if the commitment is communicated and believed, so interfere with the message — reinterpret it as an aspiration rather than a binding pledge, joke past it, or refuse on principle that you yield to reason and not to pressure — above all de-emphasising it so they can climb down without losing face.
**APPLY WHEN:** Public price pledges, "the board has mandated this number", published policy positions.
**AVOID WHEN:** The commitment is institutionally real, with legal or audit consequences — treating it as bluff wastes cycles.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/investor
**Evidence:** external-research — invokes Schelling's commitment analysis; the negotiation application is the authors' assertion.

### [NEGO:HARDPARTNER-GTY] Route around the "hardhearted partner"
**Move:** When your counterpart agrees your request is reasonable but blames an absent boss or partner, do not argue with the messenger — get their explicit agreement to the underlying principle, in writing where possible, then speak directly to the named blocker where feasible.
**APPLY WHEN:** "I'd love to, but legal/finance/my boss won't"; classic committee deflection.
**AVOID WHEN:** Going over the champion's head is culturally fatal in that account — then convert the written principle into ammunition for the champion instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — described as the most common non-yielding tactic, no data.

### [NEGO:FADEOPP-GTY] Counter calculated delay with a fading opportunity and objective deadlines
**Move:** Name the stalling and negotiate about it, then change the payoff of waiting — create a genuine competing option and anchor timing on externally verifiable conditions such as contract expiry, tax dates or a scheduled meeting, so the deadline is credible rather than manufactured.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deliberate stalling toward a moment they think favours them.
**AVOID WHEN:** The deadline is invented — a discovered-fake deadline destroys the legitimacy the whole method rests on.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — labour and M&A patterns cited from practice; waiting games flagged as costly for both sides.

### [NEGO:TIOLPASS-GTY] Ignore "take it or leave it" first, then offer a face-saving exit
**Move:** A firm final choice is not inherently illegitimate — supermarket pricing works that way — but against a tactical ultimatum first try not hearing it: keep talking, change the subject, introduce other solutions; and if you do address it, make the cost of no-agreement concrete and supply a face-saving reason to move, such as a changed circumstance introduced since the "final" offer.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late-stage ultimatums, "this is our standard, non-negotiable".
**AVOID WHEN:** It truly is final and structurally fixed — persisting reads as not listening.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus a labour illustration where the "final" figure predated the joint productivity work.

### [NEGO:OFFERNOTTHREAT-GTY] Offers beat threats
**Move:** Instead of warning what happens if they refuse, work on the consequences of agreeing and make those better and more credible — what could they want that costs you little: credit for the final proposal, making the announcement, a face-saving framing.
**APPLY WHEN:** You have leverage but want the relationship intact; late-stage friction.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterparty reads generosity as weakness and simply pockets it — this school's own bias, assuming a rationally reciprocating counterpart.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — "offers are usually more effective" stated without a cited study.

### [NEGO:KILL-MAYBE-CAMP] Refuse to operate on "maybe"
**Move:** Treat a maybe as no information at all and convert it into a decision by asking what would have to be true for a refusal; if you cannot get past it, disengage rather than continue investing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Deals that keep advancing in tone but never in decisions; counterparts averse to explicit refusal.
**AVOID WHEN:** The delay is a genuine process constraint — budget cycle, legal review — rather than avoidance.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — described as especially costly with some national counterparties; no data.

### [NEGO:EARLY-YES-ALARM-CAMP] Treat an early yes as a warning
**Move:** When the counterpart agrees early and easily, do not celebrate or start forecasting — assume qualifiers are coming and press for the specific decisions behind the agreement before you move on anything.
**APPLY WHEN:** Enthusiastic early agreement, particularly from professional buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** Simple transactional purchases where the agreement is exactly what it looks like and interrogating it kills momentum.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a mechanism argued (adrenaline → neediness → later compromise), no case data.

### [NEGO:STALL-DIAGNOSTIC-CAMP] Three questions when a deal goes nowhere
**Move:** When a negotiation stops moving, do not push on the counterpart — run the diagnostic on your own setup: is the mission actually valid, do you genuinely understand their problem, and do they themselves see it? Treat a stall as a defect in one of those three, never as a closing problem.
**APPLY WHEN:** Silence, repeatedly slipped timelines, meetings without agendas, deals stuck at the same stage.
**AVOID WHEN:** The stall has a mundane external cause — budget freeze, reorg, holidays — where the introspective loop manufactures doubt about a healthy deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as the standing troubleshooting sequence.

### [NEGO:NO-SAVING-CAMP] Never take responsibility for the other side's decision
**Move:** Let the counterpart sit with their own hard decision — do not soften it, do not invent an easier alternative, do not concede to relieve their discomfort; silence during their deliberation is your job, because saving them transfers the responsibility, and later the blame, to you.
**APPLY WHEN:** The moment the other side must decide; team negotiations where a junior member may crack.
**AVOID WHEN:** They genuinely lack information you hold — withholding decision-relevant facts to preserve pressure is a different and worse act than declining to rescue.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a team member unilaterally offered 2% in a billion-dollar negotiation to break a silence; retracted the next day.

## Stage M — Offers, commitment and closing

### [NEGO:YESABLE-GTY] Draft the yesable proposition
**Move:** Decide whether you want words or performance, and if performance, do not pad the ask for negotiating room; draft actual agreement text early in its simplest form and test whether a single yes from them would be sufficient, realistic and operational — cutting the number of approvals and making implementation easy.
**APPLY WHEN:** Approaching close; multi-approver buying processes; you keep hearing "come back with something better".
**AVOID WHEN:** Discovery is incomplete — early drafting anchors on the wrong shape.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise/transactional
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as method with illustrations only.

### [NEGO:OFFERTIMING-GTY] Time the first offer; anchor on a standard, not a number
**Move:** Do not assume moving first is best — explore interests, options and criteria, then make an offer that visibly reconciles what has surfaced so it reads as a constructive step rather than a squeeze; you can still anchor early, but anchor the approach or standard favourable to you rather than a figure.
**APPLY WHEN:** Early-stage pricing conversations; deals where value is not commoditised.
**AVOID WHEN:** Ritualised markets where refusing to name a number stalls everything — the source concedes the who-goes-first question shrinks as preparation rises on both sides.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** external-research — reactive devaluation credited to Curhan, Neale & Ross, *J. Experimental Social Psychology* 40:142 (2004).

### [NEGO:JUSTIFIABLEHIGH-GTY] Open at the highest figure you could justify to a neutral
**Move:** When you do open, use the highest number you could state without embarrassment — operationally the highest figure you would argue as fair to a neutral third party — giving the reasoning before the number, and not presenting it as firm, since firmness on an early figure burns credibility when you move off it.
**APPLY WHEN:** You must open and defensible comparables exist.
**AVOID WHEN:** You cannot justify the figure to a neutral — then it is just an extreme demand and inherits all its costs.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/saas · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoning plus a scripted example.

### [NEGO:ONEFAIR-GTY] "One fair solution might be…"
**Move:** Table your proposal as one fair option deserving joint consideration rather than as your demand or the only fair answer — specific enough to act on, framed so that rejecting it is not a rejection of you.
**APPLY WHEN:** Moving from a discussion of standards to a concrete number.
**AVOID WHEN:** Excessive tentativeness invites a counter-anchor.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Turnbull case.

### [NEGO:FRAMEWORKDOC-GTY] Framework agreement and drafting as you go
**Move:** Prepare a document shaped like the final agreement with blanks for each unsettled term and use it as agenda and starting point, then draft possible terms continuously during the negotiation — which focuses discussion, surfaces overlooked issues, creates felt progress and leaves a record; where consensus is missing, draft alternative provisions side by side.
**APPLY WHEN:** Any deal producing a written contract; multi-issue enterprise terms.
**AVOID WHEN:** A paper-shaped draft too early signals a fixed position and inhibits option generation — pair it with the explicit no-commitments label.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting/product · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standard practice described from the authors' advisory work.

### [NEGO:TENTATIVE-GTY] Move to commitment gradually under an explicit "no commitments" label
**Move:** Seek consensus issue by issue and where you cannot converge, narrow the range and move on; agree explicitly that all commitments are tentative and head the working draft as such, so people can explore options without every floated idea binding them, expecting to cycle through the issue list several times.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-issue negotiations with internal approvers on both sides.
**AVOID WHEN:** A counterpart exploits tentativeness to reopen everything indefinitely — pair with the named-pause move.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement with scripts.

### [NEGO:FIRMONINTEREST-GTY] Firm on interests, flexible on solutions
**Move:** When your proposal is attacked, do not defend the proposal — restate the underlying interest and ask whether they can devise a better way to serve it alongside their own; hold your analysis unless they make a persuasive case, and where disagreement persists, aim for second-order agreement that makes both sides' reasoning and differing assumptions explicit and testable.
**APPLY WHEN:** Objection handling on terms; deadlock on a single clause.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart treats every restatement as licence to re-litigate — then time-box it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — method statement.

### [NEGO:CLOSEMOVE-GTY] Make the offer when exploration hits diminishing returns
**Move:** Once an issue or cluster is well explored, put an offer on the table — partial offers pairing two key issues are legitimate early and can later combine into a comprehensive proposal — and make sure it reads as the natural outgrowth of the discussion rather than an ultimatum, moving final commitment exploration into a private one-on-one where possible.
**APPLY WHEN:** Late stage; many negotiations settle as soon as a complete offer is made.
**AVOID WHEN:** Exploration is still producing new interests or options.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — repeated observation from the authors' practice.

### [NEGO:IFYESIFNO-GTY] "If we agree… if we disagree…"
**Move:** Make clear that their single yes ends the whole problem, then convey the no-deal path without threatening — attribute the alternative to an external authority, describe it as a possibility rather than a decision, state your reluctance to use it, and close by affirming confidence that a fair settlement is reachable.
**APPLY WHEN:** The close, when you have real recourse but want the deal and the relationship.
**AVOID WHEN:** You have no credible alternative — the framing is then a bluff this school does not endorse.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Turnbull cited external remedies while concealing that his real alternative was simply to move out.

### [NEGO:COMMITPOWER-GTY] Three commitments: what you will do, won't do, and want them to do
**Move:** Make a firm well-timed offer rather than merely discussing (written beats oral, and a stated expiry adds a fading opportunity); consider a negative commitment tying your own hands only late, after interests are understood, and only with credible reasons independent of your will; and specify exactly what you want them to do, since vague demands let them do nothing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Closing; deadline design; defining next steps and mutual action plans.
**AVOID WHEN:** Locking in early, or issuing a final offer you are not prepared to honour.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative/investor
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an inauguration date as a natural expiry, a health-department fine, and a 1990 case where the demanded outcome was never stated consistently.

### [NEGO:ENDGENEROUS-GTY] Be generous at the end
**Move:** When you sense you are close, give something you know they value that stays consistent with the logic of your proposal — and mark it clearly as a final gesture so it does not seed expectations of more.
**APPLY WHEN:** Final-stage closing of a relationship deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Given without the final marker, or to a counterpart who reads any concession as proof more is available.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=saas/service/consulting · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — practice advice, no data.

### [NEGO:CLOSECONVENIENCE-GTY] Close on their convenience and let them keep a win
**Move:** Once the principle is settled, offer to shape the remaining logistics around their preference — it satisfies a real interest, gives them a stake in the agreement and hands them a face-saving story — and end on an explicitly warm note so neither side wants to sabotage the deal.
**APPLY WHEN:** After a contentious settlement with a counterparty you will see again.
**AVOID WHEN:** The convenience concession quietly costs you more than the disputed amount.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/founder-led/any · acv=any · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the Turnbull case; the observation that neither party felt cheated is self-reported.

### [NEGO:NO-CLOSING-CAMP] Abolish closing as an objective
**Move:** Remove closing pushes, manufactured urgency and trial closes from your sequence, let the deal assemble through a series of explicit decisions, and never carry "get this signed" into the room as the goal.
**APPLY WHEN:** Complex multi-month deals; sophisticated counterparts who recognise and punish closing technique.
**AVOID WHEN:** Genuinely transactional purchases where the buyer expects a clean ask and reads its absence as incompetence; also risky where your own organisation measures activity by closes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a rule with war stories, no comparative close-rate data.

### [NEGO:PUSHBACK-DECISION-CAMP] Slide the decision back before you accept it
**Move:** At the moment they commit — signed paper or payment in hand — push it back across the table and ask whether this is really what they want, stating you do not want them to proceed unless they are sure, giving a clean route to refusal at the point most sellers would grab the yes.
**APPLY WHEN:** The decision is large relative to the buyer's world, remorse is plausible, and your economics depend on referrals and renewals.
**AVOID WHEN:** Thin-margin transactional sales where reopening a closed decision simply loses it; or vulnerable counterparts for whom a theatrical re-ask is manipulation — the source's own case involves an elderly client committing a large share of her savings, exactly where the exit must be a real invitation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=founder-led/inbound/outbound · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product/info-product · market=consultative/transactional
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the life-insurance story, outcome self-reported.

## Stage N — After signature, reversal and exit

### [NEGO:SECOND-NEGOTIATION-CAMP] Treat the signature as the start of the next round
**Move:** Plan the post-signature phase as a fresh negotiation with its own mission, behaviour goals, agendas and next steps, since nearly every contract creates future obligations — and when the counterparty reopens terms, resume the same behaviours instead of reacting.
**APPLY WHEN:** Large corporate procurement; implementation, delivery, renewal and expansion phases; any deal with penalty clauses or ongoing service obligations.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applying adversarial framing to a delivery relationship that needs collaboration — this is where the school's systemic distrust reads as paranoia and can poison an account already won.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion/any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service/consulting · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued from the nature of contracts, without named cases.

### [NEGO:NEXT-DECISION-CAMP] Answer a bad decision with the next decision
**Move:** Treat a concession or agreement you should not have made as reversible — acknowledge the mistake plainly, state you cannot proceed on those terms, and reopen, replacing the negotiating individual if their credibility is burned.
**APPLY WHEN:** Discounts or terms agreed under pressure or without authority; contracts already losing money.
**AVOID WHEN:** The agreement is binding and reversal exposes you legally or reputationally; used habitually it makes your commitments worthless.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a 28% discount agreed at near-breakeven, reopened the following week and mostly clawed back.

### [NEGO:APOLOGY-REOPEN-CAMP] Reopen a losing contract by owning your own incompetence
**Move:** To restart a contract you are losing money on, call the counterpart, credit them with having negotiated well, take the blame for your own weak negotiating and the poor supplier position it created, and ask to fix it from there.
**APPLY WHEN:** Contracts unprofitable enough to threaten the business; internal resistance framed as "it would look unprofessional to reopen".
**AVOID WHEN:** The loss is recoverable through delivery or scope changes; repeated use destroys your credibility as a counterparty.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — Network, Inc., losing about $100,000 per machine shipped; the counterpart later became its largest customer.

### [NEGO:REOPEN-BAD-DEAL-CAMP] Renegotiate inside the contract term when the deal is destroying you
**Move:** Do not wait for contractual expiry on a deal that is bleeding — take the renegotiation up the counterpart's hierarchy, present the replacement terms, and explicitly invite them to refuse.
**APPLY WHEN:** Multi-year agreements losing money annually; internal objections of the "you cannot do that in this market" form.
**AVOID WHEN:** Relationship and reputational cost genuinely exceeds the loss, or the counterpart has leverage to enforce the original terms and punish the attempt.
**Deal-gate:** motion=expansion · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a distribution deal reopened roughly eighteen months in and five years before it was contractually challengeable; accepted after a recess.

### [NEGO:FADEAWAY-CAMP] Exit without burning the bridge
**Move:** When budget and mission say the deal will not happen, end it calmly — thanks, not this time, maybe later — instead of a grievance letter, treating the urge to burn the bridge as a symptom of emotional over-investment that buys nothing but short-term self-validation.
**APPLY WHEN:** Time, money and emotional budget are exhausted; the mission clearly cannot be served; you feel ill-treated and want to say so.
**AVOID WHEN:** The counterpart acted in bad faith in a way others in your market must be warned about, or a contractual record of the breakdown is required — a real cost the source does not price.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — standing recommendation to clients across sales and merger-scale deals.

### [NEGO:FAIR-VALUE-TOPUP-CAMP] Ask the supplier whether the deal worked, then fix it
**Move:** After delivery, ask the other party directly whether they are satisfied with the deal they made, and if they report a genuine loss, offer a concrete top-up that covers it and leaves a fair profit, naming the figure yourself.
**APPLY WHEN:** You are the buyer or the larger party in an ongoing supplier relationship where you will need goodwill for years; handshake deals with no written protection for the other side.
**AVOID WHEN:** The claimed loss is a gambit or a repeated pattern of underbidding — the move rewards it; and note it sits in open tension with the same school's ban on saving the adversary, so keep it strictly post-delivery and buyer-side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid · stakeholders=solo/few · offer=service/consulting/product · market=transactional/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a cabin story with an $800 top-up on a handshake deal.

## Stage O — Adoption and drills

### [NEGO:LOWRISKLADDER-GTY] Adopt the method on a risk ladder
**Move:** Roll out in four parts — experiment where stakes are low, your alternative is good and favourable standards exist, adding one technique at a time; accept an investment dip like an athlete rebuilding a stroke; review after each significant negotiation and keep a journal; and prepare hard, writing both sides' interests, inventing options, gathering benchmarks a neutral would accept, and working out what benchmarks their negotiators need to justify terms upward.
**APPLY WHEN:** Rep enablement; folding the method into an existing sales process.
**AVOID WHEN:** The first trial runs on a marquee deal — the dip lands where it costs most.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — training advice by analogy to athletics.

### [NEGO:RULE-DRILL-CAMP] Test-drive rules in risk-free situations
**Move:** Each day or week pick a small set of rules, review them in the morning and deliberately use them in low-stakes situations, rotating sets rather than mastering one at a time since the rules interlock; the goal at this stage is only self-conviction that they work.
**APPLY WHEN:** Onboarding a rep onto this method; a seller who accepts the system intellectually but freezes when it is time to refuse.
**AVOID WHEN:** Applied inside a live high-stakes deal — the drill stage is explicitly meant to be risk-free.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard client onboarding sequence.

### [NEGO:COLUMBO-DRILL-CAMP] Practise being unokay on purpose
**Move:** In a harmless situation, engineer a small visible imperfection — a pen runs dry, a device battery dies — and work through it without embarrassment, rehearsing the lowering of your own okayness where nothing is at stake.
**APPLY WHEN:** Sellers whose polish intimidates counterparts into guardedness; deliberate practice contexts.
**AVOID WHEN:** First meetings where competence is being screened, or with counterparts who read fumbling as unreliability.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a routine client drill drawn from the Columbo material.

### [NEGO:FIVE-STEP-START-CAMP] Five-step setup for your first system-run deal
**Move:** Pick one straightforward non-critical live deal and set it up in five moves — a mission stated in their world aimed at letting them see and decide; their real problem established through questions; all three budgets assessed on both sides; the real deciders verified; and no call or email sent without a written agenda.
**APPLY WHEN:** First deliberate application of the method; also usable as an audit checklist on an existing deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** Your most critical open deal — the author explicitly says not to start there; also mismatched to high-velocity transactional cycles.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's prescribed client ramp.

### [NEGO:33-RULES-CAMP] The Thirty-three Rules as a pre-call checklist
**Move:** Use the deliberately unordered rule list as a fast pre- or post-call audit — every party holds a veto; results are not valid goals, only controllable behaviour; the mission lives in their world; maximise payside activity; you want it, you do not need it; you cannot save the counterpart; only they get to feel okay; respect the blocker; seal every agreement with repeated confirmation; the clearer their picture of the problem, the easier the decision; no talking; always let them save face; absolutely no closing.
**APPLY WHEN:** Pre-call prep, call review, coaching debriefs.
**AVOID WHEN:** Treated as universal law across all deal types — several entries directly contradict the cooperative school this lens also carries, and the conflict must be surfaced per deal rather than averaged away.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a summary list restating the book's principles; no support attaches to the list itself.

## Where the schools collide

These are the situations where GTY and CAMP give **opposite orders**. Never average them; pick a
school on the signal named, state which one you picked, and keep the suffix visible in the citation.

**1. Cooperative framing from the buyer.** [NEGO:SIDEBYSIDE-GTY] treats "let's solve this together"
as the frame to build and rebuild; [NEGO:WINWIN-TRAP-CAMP] treats the same words as a contentless
concession request. *Signal:* who benefits from the frame. A counterpart with a professionalised
purchasing function, a scored process and no personal exposure to the outcome → CAMP. A counterpart
whose own name is on the implementation → GTY.

**2. Who names the number, and when.** [NEGO:JUSTIFIABLEHIGH-GTY] and [NEGO:OFFERTIMING-GTY] say you
may open, provided the figure is justifiable to a neutral and the reasoning precedes it;
[NEGO:NUMBERS-LATE-CAMP] says numbers are limitations and are withheld until the problem picture is
built. *Signal:* whether a credible external comparable exists. Public benchmarks → GTY anchors on
the standard. No comparable, value still being constructed → CAMP defers.

**3. Transparency about your own reasoning.** [NEGO:LISTENPOWER-GTY] and [NEGO:INVITECRIT-GTY] hand
the other side your ambivalence and your draft to be criticised; [NEGO:BEAN-GUARD-CAMP],
[NEGO:NO-RISK-ANSWER-CAMP] and [NEGO:PLANT-ASSUMPTION-CAMP] treat every disclosure as extraction
material and sanction deliberate vagueness. *Signal:* whether the term is integrative or purely
distributive. Multi-variable scope and sequencing → GTY. A single distributive price line → CAMP
withholds — but note the lens marks PLANT-ASSUMPTION-CAMP as inducing a false belief and refuses to
recommend it where a figure will be relied on.

**4. Closing as an act.** [NEGO:CLOSEMOVE-GTY] and [NEGO:COMMITPOWER-GTY] say a firm, well-timed
offer with a stated expiry is what actually settles deals; [NEGO:NO-CLOSING-CAMP] abolishes closing
as an objective and reads urgency as the behaviour sophisticated buyers punish. *Signal:* whether
your offer is the natural outgrowth of explored interests (GTY: make it) or a push substituting for
missing decisions (CAMP: there is nothing to close yet).

**5. What to do with their discomfort at the decision point.** [NEGO:FACESAVE-GTY],
[NEGO:ENDGENEROUS-GTY] and [NEGO:CLOSECONVENIENCE-GTY] actively make the decision easier to take;
[NEGO:NO-SAVING-CAMP] forbids relieving it, on the ground that easing their decision transfers the
blame to you. *Signal:* whether the difficulty is face (GTY: repackage it) or genuine unresolved
doubt (CAMP: let them sit with it).

**6. Concession as a signal.** GTY's [NEGO:PERCEPTALK-GTY] spends real effort on the concession that
is cheap to you and loud to them; [NEGO:CONCESSION-SUSPICION-CAMP] reads a stack of concessions as
evidence of a supplier who cannot deliver. *Signal:* whether the concession is tied to a named
interest of theirs (GTY) or is undirected margin bleeding across every axis (CAMP).

**7. Relationship investment.** [NEGO:PREGAME-GTY] builds acquaintance ahead of the negotiation and
[NEGO:TWOLEDGER-GTY] invests in the relationship on its own ledger; [NEGO:FRIENDSHIP-AUDIT-CAMP] and
[NEGO:PAYSIDE-TRIAGE-CAMP] classify most of that activity as either a procurement instrument aimed
at you or avoidance behaviour by you. *Signal:* whether the relationship predates the commercial
role, and whether the activity is displacing a hard conversation you are avoiding.

**8. Refusal.** [NEGO:WALKOUT-GTY] treats walking as a last procedural resort requiring legitimate,
nameable grounds; [NEGO:INVITE-NO-CAMP], [NEGO:KILL-MAYBE-CAMP] and [NEGO:REFUSE-WHIPSAW-CAMP] treat
refusal as the routine instrument that keeps the process honest. *Signal:* the strength of your
alternative. Weak alternative → GTY's caution; genuinely differentiated capability and a real
walk-away → CAMP's refusal.

**Rare genuine agreement — informative because rare.** Both schools converge, from opposite premises,
on four points, and each convergence is worth flagging in a verdict:
- *Do not counter an extreme anchor with a counter-anchor.* [NEGO:EXTREMEJUST-GTY] and
  [NEGO:NEG-EXPECT-ANCHOR-CAMP] both refuse the number and demand or decline on the basis of
  structure — GTY because the standard must decide, CAMP because an early number is meaningless.
- *Do not defend under attack; ask instead.* [NEGO:RECAST-GTY] and [NEGO:CALM-ASK-BACK-CAMP] both
  answer aggression with a question rather than a counterattack.
- *Silence and questions beat assertions.* [NEGO:QSILENCE-GTY] and [NEGO:INTERROGATIVE-LED-CAMP] /
  [NEGO:NO-TELLING-CAMP] agree the assertion generates resistance while the question generates
  information.
- *Verify claims rather than trusting them.* [NEGO:VERIFY-GTY] and [NEGO:RESEARCH-CAMP] /
  [NEGO:POS-EXPECT-BAIT-CAMP] both refuse to price against unverified volumes or budget ceilings.

Even here the agreement is one layer speaking twice, not two independent confirmations.
