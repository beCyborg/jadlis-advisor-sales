# advisor-selling-with — moves

Layer: **champion / committee** (citation prefix `[SWITH:]`).
Source: *Selling With: The art of Selling with Champions* (Nate Nasralla, 2023) — single canon source for this lens, evidence ceiling `external-research`.
All codes below are unique within the lens. Where a move is cross-referenced, it is referenced by code, not repeated as a heading.

## Stage A — Account entry and enterprise physics

### [SWITH:HANDSHAKE] Count the handshakes you have actually influenced
**Move:** For each live deal, write down how many people genuinely take part in approval, what share of those pairwise interactions you have touched, and which still must happen before signature; treat the unreached handshakes as the real pipeline gap rather than the dollar number.
**APPLY WHEN:** deal reviews and forecasting on multi-stakeholder deals, especially where you have met only part of the committee.
**AVOID WHEN:** small buying groups — the arithmetic is trivial and running it is theatre.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the n(n−1)/2 formula is definitional and quoted from Seth Godin; the supporting LEGO-build experiment is attributed only to unnamed UCLA professors via a secondary source.

### [SWITH:LARGENUM] Size impact against the account's own scale
**Move:** Never open an executive conversation with an absolute revenue number; convert the impact into a share of something the company already tracks at its own scale so it survives the Law of Large Numbers filter.
**APPLY WHEN:** any executive-facing message, business case or pre-read.
**AVOID WHEN:** the account is small enough that your absolute number is already material — re-expressing it as a fraction only shrinks it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — argued through arithmetic examples (Apple; a capital-projects budget), no study.

### [SWITH:FIREPRIORITY] Attach the problem to a burning priority, not a pain
**Move:** Test whether the pain you found actually blocks a named, already-funded executive priority; if it does not, go find the priority it obstructs or accept that you are holding a distraction.
**APPLY WHEN:** after discovery surfaces user-level pain that no executive has claimed.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would have to manufacture the linkage — an invented tie collapses the first time an executive reads it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — the author's priority-versus-distraction definition, backed by a passage from Hoffman's *Blitzscaling*.

### [SWITH:MEETINGTEST] Read the deal's altitude from meeting behaviour
**Move:** Float a group meeting and read the reply: enthusiasm means you are *the* meeting; being slotted onto an agenda means you are *in* the meeting; "ran out of time, hoping for next week" means you are an aside *after* the meeting, killable in a twenty-second corridor exchange.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-cycle, when you cannot tell whether momentum is real; before committing to a forecast close date.
**AVOID WHEN:** the hedge has a mundane cause (holidays, reorg) — verify before downgrading the deal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's framing plus a comment from Kyle Asay (RVP Sales) on deals dying at the tail end of 1:1s.

### [SWITH:SENIORFIRST] Let the senior title introduce the concept
**Move:** Arrange for the most senior available sponsor to be the person who first presents the project to the committee, since executing a senior person's direction is the safest act in an enterprise and defensiveness drops accordingly.
**APPLY WHEN:** risk-averse, politically fragmented or category-new buying groups.
**AVOID WHEN:** the sponsor is not fluent enough to survive questions, or the culture punishes top-down mandates — a badly briefed exec introduction freezes the project.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — reasoned from the failure-allergy argument, with a passage from Neil Soni's *The Startup Gold Mine* on how titles change an idea's perceived quality.

### [SWITH:UPSTREAM] Start discovery as far upstream as you can
**Move:** Open the account at the highest relevant altitude — a message takes months to climb a hierarchy and days to descend it — and have the executive sponsor the downstream discovery with their own team.
**APPLY WHEN:** new-logo pursuit where you have a credible executive-level angle.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have nothing yet worth an executive's time; a wasted senior conversation is expensive to reopen.
**Deal-gate:** motion=outbound/founder-led · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — design principle carried through the FrontCountry.com worked example.

### [SWITH:OCEANMAP] Build a communication-flow account map
**Move:** Pull your last five closed deals from CRM, list every communication type they contained (discovery calls, problem summaries, demos, NDAs, forwardable emails, data requests, proof-of-value exercises, requirements, security and legal reviews), run that list down a canvas, place profiled contacts on it, and draw links representing a *specific communication between sender and receiver* rather than a reporting line — a contact may appear several times as the deal progresses.
**APPLY WHEN:** any named or strategic account with a real committee; refresh it at each deal review.
**AVOID WHEN:** small buying groups or short cycles, where the map costs more than the clarity it buys.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's process built with his teams in Miro/Mural/PowerPoint; Organization Information Theory is invoked as conceptual support, not as evidence.

### [SWITH:CONTACTPROFILE] Profile each contact on role, current priority, key metrics
**Move:** Give every mapped contact three bullets — buying role (colour-coded: champion, direct user, key stakeholder, decision-maker), the project currently absorbing their attention, and the metrics they are evaluated on — on the logic that behaviour follows information environment plus incentive.
**APPLY WHEN:** as contacts are discovered, and before deciding who carries which message.
**AVOID WHEN:** the metrics are guessed rather than heard — an invented incentive model produces confidently wrong routing.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's template, with an Upton Sinclair line supplying the incentive rationale.

### [SWITH:SHADOWORG] Find and question into the shadow org chart
**Move:** Look past titles for the informal influence network — who energises colleagues, who people go to for help and advice, who they turn to when a decision is needed — and ask directly who else the project might affect who has not weighed in, and who they count on to voice a strong opinion in a meeting; then route communications through those people regardless of seniority, prioritising contacts who are both high-influence and high-incentive.
**APPLY WHEN:** the formal chart has failed to explain who moves decisions; while assembling the committee, once trust (see SWITH:THREETS) is in place.
**AVOID WHEN:** trust is not yet established, or the questions would be put bluntly to a new contact — asked early they read as an attempt to go around the person; infer through the champion and observation instead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion + practitioner-n-many — the author's standard committee-building questions; the influence-distribution claim rests on Carta's internal employee survey (single company, self-reported, not peer-reviewed).

### [SWITH:INTERNALREQ] Route time-sensitive requests through the internal chain
**Move:** When you need work from someone inside the account — a data pull for a proof of value, say — arrange for the request to arrive from within their own reporting line with their manager looped in, rather than from another business unit or from you.
**APPLY WHEN:** deadline-bound asks that depend on a person with no stake in your deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would escalate over someone's head unnecessarily and cost your champion goodwill.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — design principle with a worked VP-to-VP example plus the doer's own manager.

### [SWITH:FWDDESIGN] Write for the forward; split broadcast from call-to-action
**Move:** Compose emails for the person who will eventually read them rather than only the addressee, so your contact can forward in one click and be the one delivering good news (you cc'd, not sending); and separately, send information to the whole group but always follow with an individual note carrying the specific request and deadline, using the broadcast as shared context.
**APPLY WHEN:** any message that must cross into the account's internal traffic, or where a group needs to act.
**AVOID WHEN:** the content is sensitive or commercially delicate enough that you need control over who receives it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — two of the author's ten communication-design principles; the claim that internal mail is prioritised over external mail is asserted.

### [SWITH:CROSSBU] Bridge business units from above, pre-wire stakeholders early
**Move:** Where the use case spans two units with different priorities and metrics, source the connecting communication from the executive who owns both rather than joining the teams laterally; in parallel, take small early steps with procurement, legal and security (vendor profile, key contract terms, data processing agreement) so late-stage contracting is an update rather than an introduction.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-unit use cases; stakeholder pre-wiring from mid-cycle onward.
**AVOID WHEN:** early procurement contact would trigger a formal vendor process before you have a sponsor — sequencing matters more than earliness.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — two of the author's ten design principles.

### [SWITH:SHADOWPLAYER] Name the silent contact out loud with your champion
**Move:** When someone consistently goes quiet, fades out or ducks meetings, raise it with the champion under a soft label — it looks like this person isn't interested, or sees it differently — and find out whether they are a power user of a rival tool or simply opposed to the change.
**APPLY WHEN:** the contact list has grown and one participant's engagement has visibly dropped.
**AVOID WHEN:** the label would land as an accusation, or would be said to anyone other than a trusted champion.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — communication-design principle from the author's practice.

## Stage B — Discovery and problem definition

### [SWITH:CURIOUSBUYER] Judge questions by the curiosity they produce
**Move:** Grade discovery on the buyer's reaction rather than on the information you extracted — aim for responses that signal they want to go find something out, which turns discovery into internal action and pulls colleagues in.
**APPLY WHEN:** throughout the cycle; discovery continues long after the "discovery call".
**AVOID WHEN:** the deal genuinely needs qualification facts (budget existence, technical blockers) — that is a different job needing a different question type.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's framing, developed with his teams.

### [SWITH:PROBQ] Three problem-focused questions most sellers skip
**Move:** Ask what it is about this issue that other people they have raised it with do not seem to grasp (cathartic, and it builds their standing); ask which projects they would prioritise over this one at the team's next meeting, phrased to assume higher priorities exist so honest ranking is easy; and ask why not simply defer, since they appear to be coping — then keep the answer and replay it when momentum slows.
**APPLY WHEN:** first and second substantive conversations with any stakeholder.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a gotcha; the deferral question only works if you can accept "yes, we'll wait" as a real answer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's high-value question set, with a rationale given per phrasing.

### [SWITH:PROCQ] Three process-focused questions
**Move:** Ask whether the team has a standard structure or template for presenting new projects, then build your narrative in *their* format; ask what must be true of a solution for it to work here, stressing "must be", to surface requirements they are already sold on; and open the budget topic by assuming spare budget does not exist — ask how they would create it if it were worth the effort.
**APPLY WHEN:** once a problem is agreed and you need to shape requirements and the approval path.
**AVOID WHEN:** the budget question would come before any problem cost is established — it then reads as a premature close.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the template question is credited to strategic AE Matt Rogers (via Nordli), who reports breezing through approvals by working in the buyer's own format.

### [SWITH:PAYOFFQ] Three payoff-focused questions
**Move:** Ask which phrases were repeated at the last all-hands or executive update and how they connect to this project (executive trigger phrases run three to five words and mark an already-prioritised initiative); ask what a successful project enables beyond the obvious benefit, to reach second- and third-order consequences; and ask lightly which metrics they personally are working toward this year.
**APPLY WHEN:** while building the executive-facing narrative and identifying potential champions.
**AVOID WHEN:** the personal-metrics question is asked without a softening frame — it then reads as "how do I sell to you".
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — with a named example trigger phrase, "Make the Mail Move", from former SendGrid CEO Jim Franklin.

### [SWITH:QCRAFT] Question-craft principles
**Move:** Identify the negative emotion the buyer lives with, then release and replace it with a question that reframes them as ahead rather than behind; prompt stories with "tell me about when…"; raise follow-up density and use 5 Whys to reach root cause; state why you are asking before sensitive questions; bridge each question to a prior answer so the buyer reasons forward Socratically; keep questions around "100 degrees" open rather than 30 (leading) or fully open (tangent-generating); ask the obvious ones you assume you already know; and pepper questions through the meeting rather than pouring them.
**APPLY WHEN:** every discovery interaction; particularly useful in coaching call reviews.
**AVOID WHEN:** the emotional release is fabricated flattery, or 5 Whys is run mechanically past the buyer's patience.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's principles plus a worked 5 Whys ladder; the 30°/100° metaphor is credited to AE Jessie Lewis.

### [SWITH:PROBFRAME] Reframe before you solution
**Move:** Treat the stated problem as one framing among several and test better framings before discussing solutions, since the framing chosen decides which solutions can look like a fit at all.
**APPLY WHEN:** early, and again whenever the committee's requirements start excluding your strengths.
**AVOID WHEN:** the reframe would be self-serving — the goal is a better problem, not one that happens to match your product.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — carried by the HBR elevator-and-mirrors example (Wedell), a practitioner article rather than peer-reviewed work.

### [SWITH:PROBSTATE] One problem statement per role, reconciled in a room
**Move:** Hold separate conversations with each affected role; after each one write a statement in the form "despite trying [failed solution], we still can't [desired outcome], because of [problem], caused by [root cause]" and have that role confirm it; then convene a meeting whose single agenda item is agreeing the problem, present each role's written statement, and ask explicitly where they agree and disagree.
**APPLY WHEN:** committees of four or more with visibly different views of the problem; more work is needed on outbound than inbound deals, where the team has usually been debating already.
**AVOID WHEN:** surfacing the disagreement would blow up a fragile political situation with no sponsor present to arbitrate.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's stated method, with a facilitated example spanning an SVP Sales, a head of new product development and a CMO.

### [SWITH:SURVEY] Run a structured survey to make disagreement visible
**Move:** With the champion, survey each team with identical questions about the problem, then display the divergent answers under a single question — the contrast becomes the conversation starter and, in the strongest form, the data source for a board-level memo.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buying group claims an alignment you doubt, or an unprecedented purchase needs external evidence to justify it.
**AVOID WHEN:** the champion cannot legitimately field a survey internally, or the results might contradict your own case — the worked example worked precisely because the data was allowed to speak.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the philanthropy case: a three-part email campaign, a Typeform survey, a Mailchimp send, a Loom to coach the champion, Excel analysis and a one-page board memo; later productised as a free assessment.

### [SWITH:LOSSFORMULA] Cost-and-consequence problem statement formula
**Move:** Write two halves — Costs: "every [frequency], at least [reach] are [pain], costing us [loss]"; Consequences: "that means [implication 1]; if it isn't addressed by [timeline], then [implication 2]." Estimate loss as reach × frequency × severity, ask the buyer for their own guess first, write it down, then chase data to harden it; lead with an active or imminent loss rather than an upside, and walk consequences up three levels — functional, strategic, personal — using questions rather than statements.
**APPLY WHEN:** once problem framing is agreed and the champion needs a number to carry internally.
**AVOID WHEN:** the number cannot survive the Law of Large Numbers test against the account's scale (see SWITH:LARGENUM), or when it would mean stating personal consequences directly — those must be reached by the buyer.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's formula with a fully worked conversion-rate example; loss aversion is named but no study is cited.

### [SWITH:KNOWLEDGEADJ] Calibrate to the buyer's experience level
**Move:** With a first-time complex buyer, ask "how" questions that expose skipped steps and underestimated timelines; with a highly experienced champion do the opposite — help them simplify by writing the internal narrative together so less-informed colleagues can follow it.
**APPLY WHEN:** early, once you know whether the champion has led a purchase like this before.
**AVOID WHEN:** the diagnosis would be voiced to the buyer — it is a calibration for you, not feedback for them.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — names Dunning–Kruger and the curse of knowledge without citing studies for the sales application.

### [SWITH:ANECFIRST] Lead with the anecdote, follow with the data
**Move:** Open with a concrete, emotionally vivid story about a named individual affected by the problem, then immediately attach the aggregate numbers so the statistics inherit the story's urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** first framing of a problem to a group; executive pre-reads; demo openings.
**AVOID WHEN:** the anecdote is invented, generic, or would embarrass a real employee in front of leadership.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — invokes salience bias by name; the worked example (a support agent stuck until 10 p.m., 48-hour slower responses, doubled ticket volume) is illustrative.

### [SWITH:REPEATPROB] Have the champion repeat one problem statement relentlessly
**Move:** Before pushing any product content, produce a single clear written problem statement and coach the champion to open every internal meeting by restating it verbatim.
**APPLY WHEN:** long committee cycles containing many meetings you will not attend.
**AVOID WHEN:** the statement has not been validated by multiple roles — repetition entrenches a wrong frame as efficiently as a right one.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to attentional bias and the frequency illusion, with no supporting data.

### [SWITH:INTERNALPAIN] Dig for internal pain and split your notes
**Move:** Ask feeling-level follow-ups — acknowledge how frustrating this sounds, ask how they feel about it and whether teammates feel the same — and keep meeting notes in two columns: functional pains that set up features, and internal pains that create urgency.
**APPLY WHEN:** discovery and every subsequent conversation; the two-column split is what makes later follow-up possible (see SWITH:JUSTTHINKING).
**AVOID WHEN:** rapport is thin enough that emotional questions read as intrusive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standard note discipline.

### [SWITH:INACTION] Clarify what inaction actually means
**Move:** With a buyer whose pain is long-standing and normalised, ask how long the problem has existed; if the answer is long, work to make the current reality concrete, since prolonged endurance breeds the apathy and burnout that make doing nothing attractive — then hand off into the written problem-statement work.
**APPLY WHEN:** long-standing, normalised pain with no compelling event.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer already owns the cost — repeating it reads as manufactured drama.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — cross-referenced to the book's problem-statement chapter.

### [SWITH:HURDLEPROBE] Measure the trust hurdle with five questions
**Move:** Ask how significant this investment is relative to others this year; how risky the team would call the project if they spoke for them; whether they have tried anything like it before and what that was like; what the outcome means for them personally; and whether anyone holds a different view.
**APPLY WHEN:** early-to-mid cycle, before setting close dates or pushing for commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** asked of a contact with no visibility into the committee — the answers will be guesses.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's diagnostic set for hurdle height.

### [SWITH:BASELINE] Establish your starting trust position
**Move:** Ask early what their impression of your company is, and account for referrals, review sites, brand reputation, marketing content and — for existing customers — NPS or experience surveys as the pre-meeting balance you inherit; treat deep trust debt as an opportunity, since the largest swings produce the most vocal advocates, and note that the inverse also holds.
**APPLY WHEN:** first interactions, especially in accounts with vendor history.
**AVOID WHEN:** the question invites a negative anchor you cannot address in that same meeting.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the −5 to +10 swing illustration is the author's model, not a measurement.

## Stage C — Champion creation and qualification

### [SWITH:CHAMPBRIEF] Co-author the champion's internal brief
**Move:** Instead of presenting yourself, write your contact's internal narrative *with* them — the exec brief, plan or memo they were already expected to deliver — and let the visible back-and-forth of comments and drafts become the deal's real selling surface.
**APPLY WHEN:** the champion has a scheduled internal forum (new-leader plan, ELT update, board packet) where a vendor pitch would be out of place.
**AVOID WHEN:** the contact has no standing to present internally, the forum genuinely wants a vendor in the room, or co-authoring with an outsider breaches account policy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the book's opening story of a six-figure deal closed in a seven-minute call because the CPO had already read the co-written memo.

### [SWITH:CHAMP3I] Qualify a champion on Incentive / Influence / Information
**Move:** Score each candidate against all three traits and label them honestly: influence plus information but no personal stake is an influencer; incentive plus information but no ability to move the outcome is a coach; only all three plus follow-through counts as a champion.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where you are about to bet the cycle on one contact; rerun whenever the buying committee expands.
**AVOID WHEN:** single-decision-maker or transactional deals — with no internal committee to be championed to, the whole construct is inert.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's core operating definition across his teams and clients; no dataset given.

### [SWITH:BELIEVE] Discount influence by internal believability
**Move:** Before relying on someone's advocacy, estimate their internal credibility as a rough percentage — being recruited by an executive, visible confidence, and a comparable project delivered end to end raise it; flakiness, exaggeration or ownership of a past failure collapse it — and treat that score as a multiplier on everything they say internally.
**APPLY WHEN:** several willing contacts exist and you must choose whom to invest in; or a "champion" advocates hard and nothing moves.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would voice the assessment to the account — it is a private filter, and getting it wrong on hearsay burns the relationship.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's mental model, explicitly analogised to Bridgewater's Baseball Cards / Dot Collector believability weighting (Dalio).

### [SWITH:TRICONTACT] Cover all three contact classes or assume the deal is lost
**Move:** Map the deal against daily users, key stakeholders (security, legal, IT, procurement — "SLIP") and the budget-holding decision-maker(s); two of three on board is treated as a loss, so name the missing class and go get it.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-cycle deal reviews and forecast calls, especially when momentum looks good from one angle only.
**AVOID WHEN:** deals with a single buyer-operator — the classes collapse into one person and the check is noise.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a rule of thumb with worked failure combinations, no data.

### [SWITH:TWOLEVEL] Run the two-level legitimacy test and pay the champion's WIIFM
**Move:** With your champion, answer both questions — what happens to *them* personally without your solution, and what happens to the *company* — then explicitly connect the buying process to their own metrics, incentives and career goals so time spent with you is not stolen from their day job.
**APPLY WHEN:** early qualification, and again whenever an engaged champion goes quiet.
**AVOID WHEN:** the personal angle would be voiced as leverage or flattery — the ask is to align real incentives, not to imply obligation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — framing credited to investor and ex-Salesforce executive Brett Queener, applied by the author across deals.

### [SWITH:CHAMPTEST] Three-probe champion commitment test
**Move:** Run three probes: change the channel — ask permission to text or open a shared Slack Connect channel, then raise your communication quality to match; ask them to lay out their own buying process end to end, with criteria, players and approval steps; and assign an action item with a binary follow-through signal (call a reference, pull data for the business case, comment on the draft memo).
**APPLY WHEN:** after two or three substantive conversations, before you invest engineering time, executive time or a POC.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would read a single miss as betrayal — one skipped item in a busy week is data, not a verdict; also avoid channel-shifting in accounts with strict external-communication policies.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a repeatable "create-test loop" the author runs on deals; each test is claimed to deepen rather than strain the relationship.

### [SWITH:COMMITTEST] Assign gap-filling tasks as a champion test
**Move:** Find gaps in your framework — a missing baseline number, say — assign a specific task to close each one, and treat follow-through between meetings as the measurement of whether this person is actually a champion.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-cycle, once a shared artifact exists and you need a commitment signal before investing more.
**AVOID WHEN:** the contact has no organisational access to the data you are asking for — you will misread inability as disinterest.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented as one of the author's more reliable champion tests.

### [SWITH:CHAMPREHEARSE] Train the champion and ask for their unfiltered version
**Move:** Ask the champion to walk you through exactly how they will describe your offering in an internal meeting and to give their real, unedited opinion of it; then coach the wording and pre-load answers to the objections their colleagues will raise.
**APPLY WHEN:** before any internal meeting you will not attend, and before executive or committee reviews.
**AVOID WHEN:** the contact resists rehearsal or dodges the feedback — that resistance is itself the result; do not force it, and do not treat a polished restatement as proof of commitment.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's fourth champion test; willingness to be coached is the stated proof of a real champion.

### [SWITH:CHAMPGLORY] Design for who the champion wants to become
**Move:** At each key moment ask who this buyer wants to be seen as — prescient, competent, the one who fixed it — and shape the next artifact or meeting so that the champion, not you, gets the visible win.
**APPLY WHEN:** any point where a champion is spending internal social capital on you.
**AVOID WHEN:** it would mean withholding information the wider committee needs, or building someone's status on claims the product cannot support.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — argued from the champion's exposure of personal capital, illustrated with the author's first-class-flight framing.

### [SWITH:THREETS] Pass the champion's trust test before asking for access

### [SWITH:MOMENTUMCHECK] Read the Build Loop's three momentum signals

### [SWITH:IKEA] Make buying collaborative, not concierge

### [SWITH:SHORTREWARD] Deliver value before the purchase, during the cycle

## Stage D — Narrative and the one-page business case

### [SWITH:MINTO] Pyramid the message (BLUF)
**Move:** Lead with the short specific answer, put three mutually exclusive supporting arguments beneath it, then sub-points; invert the natural "I learned A, B, C → therefore F" order into "Do F, because D and E, as supported by A, B, C."
**APPLY WHEN:** any written artifact aimed at a director-and-above reader; executive emails and pre-reads.
**AVOID WHEN:** exploratory discovery notes where you have no defensible recommendation yet — a headline you cannot support invites correction.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — attributed to Barbara Minto's McKinsey work, no outcome data.

### [SWITH:ZOOMIO] Zoom out, zoom in
**Move:** Frame the narrative on two horizons instead of the usual one-to-three years: what the buyer's industry looks like in ten-plus years and what company they must become to survive, then which single project in the next six months starts the shift and how progress gets measured.
**APPLY WHEN:** markets undergoing visible structural change; executive-sponsored transformation deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** commodity or tool-level purchases where a ten-year framing reads as inflated, or the buyer has explicitly scoped a tactical fix.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — credited to Budman & Garia and to unnamed tech firms; no results reported.

### [SWITH:DRIVERTREE] Model the buyer's driver tree
**Move:** Map the target outcome metric on one side and the chain of input metrics that move it on the other, so you can see which lever your product actually touches and how many competing levers exist at that altitude.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparing a senior conversation; deciding which metric the payoff section should speak to.
**AVOID WHEN:** you lack real data on how their business works — an invented tree gets corrected in the room and costs credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a standard analytical model, with no deal outcomes attached.

### [SWITH:ONEPAGER] The one-page business case skeleton
**Move:** Fill five blocks on one page: a bold title plus a byline naming the champion and key deal players (never yourself); a headline in the form "[team] should [action] by [timeline], producing [outcome] while avoiding [cost] caused by [problem]"; a problem statement carrying who is affected, frequency, cost, the link to a company-wide goal and why it worsens; the recommended approach with how it has been proven and what must be true for it to work; a before/after payoff tied to the decider's metrics with time-to-impact; and the required investment listing people, time and attention, not just budget.
**APPLY WHEN:** after discovery has produced the buyer's own words and a quantified problem; as the artifact the champion forwards internally.
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot yet name a specific problem cost or the executive metric it rolls into — the page then becomes a brochure.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author reports it working across his teams' pipelines; a downloadable version is referenced.

### [SWITH:BCEARLY] Start the business case after call one and score it by stage
**Move:** Begin drafting the account-specific business case immediately after the first conversation and strengthen it at every stage, mapping stage number to a target maturity score; each gap in the document is a live discovery gap or deal risk, and the document is reviewed with a scorecard the way a call would be.
**APPLY WHEN:** any complex deal you intend to forecast; also as a coaching instrument for a team.
**AVOID WHEN:** deals too small or too fast to justify the artifact — on a transactional deal the business case is overhead.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — drawn from 190 sales teams the author's company worked with over 12 months (vendor-collected, self-reported).

### [SWITH:BCJTBD] Run every deal activity off the one-pager
**Move:** Use the same document as the demo agenda, the executive pre-read, the anchor that protects value during negotiation, and the object champions forward when multithreading — one artifact serving many jobs instead of a new deck per moment.
**APPLY WHEN:** the business case already exists in draft; each use is also a test of whether it can stand alone.
**AVOID WHEN:** the document is still one-sided, built by you and unedited by them — forwarding vendor prose damages the champion's credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — enumerated use cases from the author's practice and client teams.

### [SWITH:CAMO] Camouflage the materials, format for scanning
**Move:** Strip your own branding and adopt the buyer's visual conventions so the document could plausibly have been made inside the buying committee; co-create it in a shared doc rather than sending a polished asset; and invest as much time in headers, bolds, bullets and breakouts as in the prose, because readers descend the page in passes.
**APPLY WHEN:** anything intended to be forwarded internally by a champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** the artifact is a formal RFP or procurement response where your corporate identity is required.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one named-role buyer interview (a champion owning a $10M product development budget at a Fortune 100 cable company) reporting that branded material rarely gets forwarded.

### [SWITH:APPROACHFIRST] Sell the approach before the product
**Move:** Get written agreement on the vendor-agnostic shift or practice being adopted — moving the lead mix from paid to organic, for example — before discussing features, then transition with language that your company was found to meet the agreed requirements.
**APPLY WHEN:** committee deals where a project must be justified before a vendor is chosen; early narrative construction.
**AVOID WHEN:** a competitive bake-off is already underway with fixed requirements, or the buyer has already committed to the approach and wants product proof.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author flags it as the hardest habit for reps to hold and the most often skipped part of his framework.

### [SWITH:THESISBRIDGE] Bridge or reshape the executive's thesis (no-bridge gate)
**Move:** Identify which priority the senior buyer has already picked among competing levers, then either align your product to that thesis or argue for reshaping it; if no logical bridge exists between their chosen priority and what you sell, stop selling rather than pushing.
**APPLY WHEN:** newly appointed executives, post-funding priority resets, any account with multiple plausible revenue levers.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have not actually heard the executive's thesis — assuming it and building the bridge on air produces a narrative nobody sponsors.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a worked example of a newly hired CRO after a Series B choosing NRR over new logos.

### [SWITH:BUILDLOOP] Start the narrative as a few sentences in a shared space
**Move:** Rather than engineering a polished document to unveil late, ask permission to draft a short recap of the contact's view of the problem before the next call and invite comments; drop a short problem statement plus notes into a shared workspace; then in follow-up calls share your screen in editor mode with existing comments visible and ask what resonates and what is missing, ideally cueing your first contact to narrate it.
**APPLY WHEN:** immediately after first discovery, before any deck exists.
**AVOID WHEN:** the account's security posture blocks external shared docs, or the contact has shown no willingness to spend time between calls.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's repeated practice, supported by the IKEA-effect framing.

### [SWITH:SOCIALIZE] Socialise the draft and hunt critical feedback
**Move:** While the narrative is still visibly a draft but has a complete storyline, recruit additional co-authors and deliberately route a share of the critique through executives, so budget-holders hear the story several times well before any decision; then merge the feedback into a single cohesive line rather than a patchwork.
**APPLY WHEN:** the storyline holds together end to end and you want commitment to harden through public advocacy.
**AVOID WHEN:** the draft is still wrong on the problem — exposing it early to an executive spends your champion's credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — plus a cited Bezos line on memos being rewritten with colleagues.

### [SWITH:WRITEMEMO] Co-write one page, and use a wrong draft to pull corrections
**Move:** Write the narrative *with* the champion instead of sending marketing collateral: one page, in their words, kept as a living document that brings new buyers up to speed and kills the telephone-game effect; and exploit the fact that people cannot leave an inaccurate written characterisation of their own beliefs alone — a deliberately imperfect draft draws corrections and additions better than a question does (Cunningham's Law).
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal where internal meetings will happen without you — which the book treats as all enterprise deals.
**AVOID WHEN:** the wrong-draft tactic would be used on a contact who reads carelessness as incompetence, or on anything customer-facing and formal.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — seven stated advantages of writing, supported by a quoted essay from Eliot Peper on writing as thinking; the co-creation effect is asserted, not measured.

### [SWITH:NARRATIVETIE] Tie your narrative into already-accepted narratives
**Move:** Find the stories the company has already approved — the CRO's net revenue retention focus, the CEO's growth target — and connect your project's narrative to them, since internal requests are approved or denied largely on that linkage.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing anything that will travel internally without you.
**AVOID WHEN:** the linkage is decorative; a forced tie to a fashionable initiative is easily spotted by the people who own it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — argued with two worked examples of tying a team's request into a superior's stated focus.

### [SWITH:COORDLANG] Assemble copy from the buyer's own words
**Move:** Record and transcribe conversations, then build the document by dropping the buyer's exact word choices into your framework — especially words that differ from how other buyers describe the same problem — so they never have to translate your jargon.
**APPLY WHEN:** writing any mid-funnel artifact for a specific account.
**AVOID WHEN:** recording consent is unavailable, or the buyer's phrasing is internally contested and adopting it takes a side.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — framed as the failsafe route to good content.

### [SWITH:RENAME] Change the language to change the category
**Move:** Coin or adopt a name for the problem and for the approach that puts you in a new mental container instead of a feature comparison — the book's cases are Clari's "Revenue Leak" versus "Revenue Precision", the Sydney Opera House sold on the single word "sail", and a Band-Aid renamed a "sticker".
**APPLY WHEN:** crowded categories where you keep getting compared feature for feature.
**AVOID WHEN:** buyers already have a working internal vocabulary for the problem — imposing a new term breaks coordination (see SWITH:COORDLANG) and reads as marketing spin.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — named company and architecture examples, no measured effect.

### [SWITH:VILLAIN] Elevate the fight by naming a villain
**Move:** Instead of battlecards, answer two questions — what dream life the customer wants and what obstacle you help remove — then name that obstacle as a shared enemy, shifting the buyer's implicit questions to who am I, what situation is this, and what would someone like me do (B2B examples: Drift versus forms, Salesforce versus installed software, Zuora versus ownership, Gong versus opinion-not-data).
**APPLY WHEN:** a loud committee member is anchoring the group on a single feature and you are losing narrative control.
**AVOID WHEN:** the villain is an incumbent your buyer personally chose, or a colleague — you then make the champion defend a personal attack. The chapter openly concedes the Domino's framing suppressed real counter-benefits; used cynically this is identity manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — cites the Heath brothers' identity decision-making model from *Switch* plus brand examples, no sales data.

### [SWITH:WHICHMEANS] Build the "which means" value thread
**Move:** Chain the problem and the value up three levels using the connector "which means": what daily users experience → what that costs directors → what goal the executive misses; then run the same chain forward for the payoff.
**APPLY WHEN:** preparing to multithread; writing anything that must land at several altitudes at once.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chain requires a leap you cannot evidence — a broken link is visible to the executive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — worked example on a lead-generation product.

## Stage E — Writing and conversation craft

### [SWITH:REWRITE] Run the seven-pass rewrite
**Move:** Pass a draft through: contrast (before/after), passive to active voice, the buyer's plain language over Victorian vocabulary, cut redundant phrases, dial up the pain with concrete counts (minutes, tabs, swear words), swap abstractions for visual comparisons, shorten sentences, add personality, state what is actually at stake, and close on the good life the change buys.
**APPLY WHEN:** editing any customer-facing prose; as a training exercise on other people's bad copy.
**AVOID WHEN:** the reader is a procurement or legal audience expecting precise, unembellished terms.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — demonstrated on a single before/after paragraph the author wrote.

### [SWITH:ETHOSLOGOSPATHOS] Balance the three rhetorical elements
**Move:** Check a persuasive piece for credibility (experience, results), logic that lets the reader reach the conclusion before you state it, and emotional stake; add whichever is missing.
**APPLY WHEN:** reviewing a near-final narrative or executive email.
**AVOID WHEN:** the credibility claims cannot be substantiated for this account.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — classical rhetoric invoked without study citations.

### [SWITH:LEADTRUTH] Lead with what they already believe
**Move:** Open by confirming a belief the buyer already holds, and only then introduce the nonobvious insight that challenges convention — sequencing matters because a closed mind rejects the challenge outright.
**APPLY WHEN:** any headline, opener, or contrarian insight delivery.
**AVOID WHEN:** the shared belief is the thing you must overturn immediately (a safety or compliance misconception).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — invokes confirmation bias plus a website-headline example (Lattice).

### [SWITH:HEADLINE] Rewrite the first sentence until it pulls
**Move:** Treat the opening line as the majority of the work: draft and redraft until a reader cannot stop, on the logic that far more people read the headline than the body.
**APPLY WHEN:** emails, memos, one-pagers, subject lines.
**AVOID WHEN:** the hook would overpromise — it then damages everything after it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — Ogilvy's 5× readership / 80-cents-of-the-dollar claim is quoted without a source study.

### [SWITH:ONETRUTH] Strip to one truth, say it three ways
**Move:** Reduce the message to the single truth the reader must understand and be able to repeat, then restate it in a few different phrasings across the piece.
**APPLY WHEN:** anything meant to survive being retold internally without you.
**AVOID WHEN:** technical documentation requiring multiple distinct points.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author demonstrates it on his own book's thesis.

### [SWITH:FRAMESWITCH] Switch the frame or flip it negative
**Move:** Either move the comparison set entirely (the Rolls-Royce move: compete with yachts and art, "commission" rather than "configure"), or hold the same facts and flip the emotional charge from gain to loss ("save $300K" becomes "avoid wasting $300K"; "collaborate" becomes "prevent duplicate work").
**APPLY WHEN:** price anchoring; re-energising a message the buyer has gone numb to.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buying culture reacts badly to fear framing, or the loss is speculative — overuse reads as manipulation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — brand examples and B2C framing pairs, no measurement.

### [SWITH:GOLDPHRASE] Keep a pocket set of copy phrases
**Move:** When stuck, reach for a stock construction — "At last", "Announcing", "You guessed it", "Should… not…", "That's right", "If… then", "Why? Because", "Get the… without the…" — and fit the account's specifics into it (Webflow's "get the site you want, without the developer time" retargeted to a product team as "the product launch you need, without all the red tape").
**APPLY WHEN:** drafting headlines fast; adapting one message across accounts.
**AVOID WHEN:** the phrasing collides with the buyer's internal tone; overuse makes the copy sound like an ad.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — copywriting convention plus a vendor example.

### [SWITH:PREOBJECT] Pre-empt the inevitable objection in writing
**Move:** Name the objection you know is coming inside the document itself, early, because unresolved objections sit in the reader's head as blockers and consume the attention you were trying to earn.
**APPLY WHEN:** predictable category objections (security, implementation load, price).
**AVOID WHEN:** the objection is not actually live for this account — you would be planting it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a rule, no support given.

### [SWITH:ONEPERSON] Write to one named reader
**Move:** Pick a single specific person in the audience and write to them by name in your head (Buffett writes his shareholder letter to his sisters); the symptom you are curing is jargon produced by writing to "the committee".
**APPLY WHEN:** any document with a mixed audience.
**AVOID WHEN:** the chosen person is unrepresentative of who must act.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a Buffett quotation plus an Economist billboard example.

### [SWITH:DRAFTEDIT] Separate drafting from editing
**Move:** Do two distinct passes — draft for creativity without judging the output, then edit for clarity: restructure bottom-line-up-front, check logical flow, and only then clean grammar.
**APPLY WHEN:** whenever writing feels slow or the output feels flat.
**AVOID WHEN:** not contraindicated — it is a process rule, though it costs a second pass on trivial notes.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — rests on a stated claim that multitasking is a myth.

### [SWITH:LONGTHENCUT] Write past 200 words, cut to 50
**Move:** If the draft is under 200 words, keep writing; once it is over 200 with no fluff, compress to about 50 — the goal being a 50-word email carrying 200 words of value.
**APPLY WHEN:** any consequential follow-up, especially to senior readers.
**AVOID WHEN:** detailed technical or contractual content genuinely requires length.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a rule of thumb with a Pascal quotation.

### [SWITH:WEIRDTEST] Run the weird test, and apply it to gifts
**Move:** Ask whether it would be strange for anyone else to read this message if it were misdelivered; if it would not be strange, it is too generic — edit and retest. Apply the same standard to gifts: send something that would only make sense for this specific person (the author's example is a make-your-own-fortune-cookies kit for a Chinese-takeout enthusiast), never mugs or notebooks.
**APPLY WHEN:** final check before sending any personalised outreach or gift.
**AVOID WHEN:** the specificity would reveal something the buyer shared in confidence, or the gift breaches their gifting policy.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=low/mid/high · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own test plus a gifting anecdote, with a management note to give reps a no-approval quarterly gift budget.

### [SWITH:BLUFEXEC] Write to a distracted executive
**Move:** Assume executives read your email mid-meeting with partial attention: bottom line first, short simple sentences, no build-up.
**APPLY WHEN:** any email to a senior contact.
**AVOID WHEN:** not contraindicated within this audience; it misfires only if the "bottom line" is a claim you cannot support.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=any · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a stated observation about executive reading habits.

### [SWITH:MVS] Speak in minimum viable sentences
**Move:** Before the conversation, fix the one point to make or question to answer, then say the shortest complete thing carrying goal, context and a call to reply — and stop to listen, building each next sentence from their reply.
**APPLY WHEN:** any conversation, and specifically with executives where "just answer the question" applies.
**AVOID WHEN:** the moment genuinely calls for storytelling or off-the-cuff rapport; this is an antidote to fluff, not a ban on narrative.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one 16-word versus 193-word worked comparison with the two divergent buyer replies.

### [SWITH:DATAOVERSTORY] Substitute a data point for an illustrative story
**Move:** When tempted to add an unsolicited anecdote to make a point clear, research a specific number and lead with it instead — for example asking how to move BDR-qualified pipeline from $32K to $50K per rep rather than narrating team stress.
**APPLY WHEN:** mid-conversation clarity problems, internal cross-functional asks, executive conversations.
**AVOID WHEN:** no credible number exists, or the point is emotional rather than analytical.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one internal AE/BDR example.

### [SWITH:ONEPOINT] Lead with one strong argument, drop the rest
**Move:** Present your single most compelling reason rather than stacking reasons, on the logic that weak arguments dilute strong ones.
**APPLY WHEN:** persuasion moments — executive meetings, business-case framing, objection responses.
**AVOID WHEN:** the audience explicitly requires comprehensive coverage (procurement, security review).
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — relayed secondhand via Adam Grant's *Think Again* quoting Rackham; no primary citation given.

## Stage F — Multithreading and the buying group

### [SWITH:PARALLELINVITE] Invite unknown stakeholders directly, with an out
**Move:** When the champion cannot introduce someone they do not know, reach out yourself with an invitation naming the internal priority and the business unit involved, and explicitly give permission to decline as not-yet-relevant; even a declined invite tends to be remembered favourably.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-business-unit accounts where stakeholders have not met each other.
**AVOID WHEN:** the champion has asked you not to contact that group — going around beats working with them only as a last resort.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — a large health-insurance deal spanning digital innovation, provider, patient and finance teams whose group calls opened with mutual introductions.

### [SWITH:SYNCFLIP] Convert "let us sync internally" into a scenario
**Move:** Agree that they will want to sync, then flip the framing: ask whether they can think of a scenario where meeting again would not make sense, probe the likelihood of that scenario as further discovery, then book the follow-up conditioned on the outcome they are personally pushing for, with permission to decline the invite if it does not happen.
**APPLY WHEN:** the deflection appears at the end of a call and you would otherwise chase for a date.
**AVOID WHEN:** the contact has already signalled no interest — the technique then keeps a dead thread alive.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a full scripted dialogue is given for an attribution-software deal.

### [SWITH:CCCHOICE] Offer two ways to loop in the boss
**Move:** Rather than going over the contact's head, offer a choice: you write the update to the VP cc'ing them and your executive, or you send it to them to edit and forward while cc'ing your side.
**APPLY WHEN:** a senior stakeholder needs visibility but the champion controls access.
**AVOID WHEN:** the champion has a political reason to keep the VP out that you have not yet surfaced.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — presented with the rationale that access mirrors access.

### [SWITH:MIRROR] Mirror the behaviour you are asking for
**Move:** Bring your own executive to meet theirs and your technical team to meet theirs, and collaborate internally on your side — the buyer's willingness to multithread tends to mirror yours, and operating in a silo produces a siloed counterpart.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled multithreading; planning which of your colleagues joins which call.
**AVOID WHEN:** your internal team is not prepared — an unprepared executive on your side costs credibility.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — stated as a pattern, no supporting data.

### [SWITH:INVITEDISSENT] Nominate a willing dissenter

### [SWITH:MVCOMMITTEE] Keep a minimally viable committee

### [SWITH:MATHDRAMA] Separate math problems from drama, then name the drama

### [SWITH:TRIANGULATE] Separate the contact's interest from their team's

### [SWITH:EXECNARR] Aim the narrative at shifts, risks and untapped upside
**Move:** Before the meeting, re-point the narrative at the three things executives think in — seismic industry shifts, existential risks, large untapped opportunities — and connect it to trigger phrases and stories they are already sold on internally.

### [SWITH:WHISPERER] Pre-secure the executive whisperer

### [SWITH:PREREAD] Attach a pre-read to the invite and start at :05

**AVOID WHEN:** the executive is known to dislike pre-reads, or the document is not good enough to be the first impression.

## Stage H — Mid-funnel follow-up and the buyer's emotional journey

### [SWITH:FORWARDABLE] Write the forwardable email
**Move:** In roughly 100 words, open on a big company-wide objective that affects the recipient's boss, insert a trigger phrase familiar to their leadership, paint the future in visual language (a mock press-release headline, say), build tension that the future may not happen, link to the one-page narrative, and close with an ask aligned to the decider's actual job plus a nudge that the boss may want to weigh in; the subject line should read like their to-do list.
**APPLY WHEN:** after a strong call with a mid-level contact whose boss controls the outcome; whenever your words must travel without you.
**AVOID WHEN:** you do not know the leadership's language or metric — a guessed trigger phrase marks you as an outsider; and do not pad with case-study links, which kill forwardability.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise

### [SWITH:NEWINFO] Bring something new every touch

**AVOID WHEN:** you have no real new input — manufacturing insight is worse than a short honest note.

## Stage I — Evaluation: demos, mutual action plans, proof of concept

### [SWITH:CONTAINEDDEMO] Contrast outcomes, contain the change
**Move:** Confirm the cost of the current process, pick one narrow scenario inside it, demo only how that scenario works with your product, stop there, state the cost saving or outcome the change produces, then discuss concerns about the change — rather than declaring their whole current process broken and touring dashboards.
**APPLY WHEN:** any demo where a current-versus-future comparison is in play; change-averse teams.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has explicitly asked for broad functional coverage, such as a formal capability review.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — with a paired bad/good example (Excel forecasting versus comparing machine and spreadsheet forecasts on the top 20 key accounts only).

### [SWITH:REVERSEDEMO] Ask the buyer to demo you
**Move:** Find the one or two workflows daily users spend most of their week in and, before opening your product, ask them to share their screen and walk you through it, referencing the differentiator they currently lack; then show that same workflow in your product and, where it fits, hand over screen control so they click through it themselves.
**APPLY WHEN:** below-the-line audiences (daily users); rip-and-replace of a legacy system.
**AVOID WHEN:** executive audiences with no hands-on workflow, or when time is too short to do both halves.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — attributed to what high performers do versus average reps, with a script.

### [SWITH:ENDFIRST] Demo the ending for above-the-line buyers
**Move:** For executives, fast-forward to the final dashboard or outcome state in the first seconds rather than the workflow that produces it, show just enough product to establish confidence you can deliver, and stage multiple pre-loaded windows so you switch views in one click instead of navigating; let them ask for depth.
**APPLY WHEN:** executive-attended demos and short senior slots.
**AVOID WHEN:** technical evaluators need to see the mechanics, or the outcome view is mocked rather than real.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's demo structure, opening with his own failed SVP demo as the counterexample.

### [SWITH:TAGCHAMPION] Pre-agree a tag-team with the champion
**Move:** Before a group call, agree which parts of the conversation the champion will lead and name the specific areas, so you can hand off during the meeting rather than carrying it alone.
**APPLY WHEN:** any group demo or committee meeting the champion convened.
**AVOID WHEN:** the champion has not agreed in advance — surprising them in front of their team burns their capital.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — stated as the default fallback when a demo drifts.

### [SWITH:MAPWHYFIRST] Sequence the memo before the plan
**Move:** Get the decision memo or narrative business case in front of leadership first to establish need and pull the right people in; introduce the project plan afterwards as evidence you will not wing the execution — and recognise that the plan is not the artifact a champion takes to their CTO.
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding what to build with a champion first in a complex cycle.
**AVOID WHEN:** leadership is already sold and the champion is asking for execution detail.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — an extended buyer interview with a senior DevOps director managing $2M of developer-tool spend.

### [SWITH:MAPGRID] Build the MAP as epics, tasks, owners and dates
**Move:** List every step of the customer's real buying process (security review length and participants, whose paper the contract starts on and redline duration, procurement/RFP triggers and vendor requirements, how budget gets created and when finance engages), group them into epics with sub-tasks, split tasks across both companies, and give every task a DRI and a deadline in a simple grid running through onboarding and adoption — not stopping at signature.
**APPLY WHEN:** after the business case lands and the champion has committed to the journey.
**AVOID WHEN:** the plan becomes long enough that perceived effort outweighs value; conversely, do not hide steps to look easy — that is how you sink time into dead deals.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — plus named practitioner Tom Williams (head of Clari Align) on framing it as a plan to solve their problem, not a plan to sell your software.

### [SWITH:MAPADAPT] Adapt the baseline plan with the champion
**Move:** Present the plan as what you typically see customers do, ask how it compares to purchases they have made before, and invite them to cut what does not apply and add what is missing; if they have never led a purchase, ask what in the plan surprises them, which reveals where to educate versus where to edit.
**APPLY WHEN:** first introduction of the MAP to a champion.
**AVOID WHEN:** you would present it as fixed — a non-negotiable plan reads as a close plan.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a scripted introduction is provided.

### [SWITH:BACKDATE] Work backward from a real compelling event

## Stage J — Objections, pricing and the commercial close

### [SWITH:NOHANDLING] Stop "handling" objections
**Move:** Treat a fast rehearsed rebuttal as a cost rather than a save: pushing back raises sales resistance (people dig into their original position), teaches buyers to stop exposing doubts, and accrues emotional debt through dismissiveness that compounds faster than you can repay it; read the number of objections you hear as a measure of the trust you have built.
**APPLY WHEN:** any moment an objection surfaces; coaching reps who pride themselves on rebuttals.
**AVOID WHEN:** a factual error genuinely needs correcting — correct the fact without flattening the person's concern.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=consultative/enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the mechanism is asserted, with the claim that qualified-but-lost deals often trace back to objection-handling moments.

### [SWITH:QNOTOBJ] Test whether it is an objection or an unanswered question
**Move:** Before responding, check whether the pushback is caused by missing or misread information — restate what you understood their goal to be, get confirmation, then ask what specifically they needed to see but did not; if it was a communication gap, own it plainly and offer to show the missing piece.
**APPLY WHEN:** post-demo or post-proposal negative reaction that contradicts what discovery established.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer has already articulated a considered, layered concern — treating that as a misunderstanding is condescending.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — illustrated with a candidate-screening demo dialogue, no outcome data.

### [SWITH:UUR] Understand–unpack–respond instead of handling
**Move:** Run three steps in order: keep pulling the thread until the concern behind the concern surfaces; unpack the tangled sub-parts before answering; only then respond — step one keeps running through step two rather than ending.
**APPLY WHEN:** any substantive objection where you can still get airtime with the person who raised it.
**AVOID WHEN:** a purely mechanical or factual blocker (a security questionnaire item, a missing integration) where digging reads as evasion, or when no time remains in the call to finish the sequence.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's standing framework across enterprise deals.

### [SWITH:UNPACKQ] Unpacking probes and connective questions
**Move:** Widen the objection before answering with probes such as "is there no scenario where this could help?" or "when this comes up it's usually X or Y — is either true here?", then stitch statements together with connective openers (was what you shared related to…, how does this compare to when you said…, this sounds like when you…) and chain them until the buyer restates their own concern in different terms.
**APPLY WHEN:** multi-part objections where several stated priorities may actually be one underlying goal.
**AVOID WHEN:** trust is low or the buyer is short on time — a question chain then reads as interrogation.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — demonstrated with a marketing lead-volume dialogue that ends with the buyer reframing volume as quality.

### [SWITH:INVERT] Invert the objection
**Move:** When unpacking stalls, flip the objection into its opposite context and ask them to define it: what would a large enough return look like, is there a scenario where you would move in Q4, what does a solution that is too cheap look like.
**APPLY WHEN:** dead-end objections where the buyer will not elaborate.
**AVOID WHEN:** used as a rhetorical gotcha — the phrasing is only safe with a calm, non-adversarial tone.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — three stock inversions given as repeated patterns.

### [SWITH:PREMISE] Challenge the built-in assumption
**Move:** Identify the unexamined belief the objection rests on, confirm it out loud, then step one level above the feature debate and name the trade-off that belief carries — surfacing, for example, that all-in-one suites buy breadth at the cost of the depth their team relies on today, then offering the two-point-solution path with integrations.
**APPLY WHEN:** product-shaped objections where an approach was decided internally but never questioned or documented.
**AVOID WHEN:** the assumption is a hard constraint (policy, security, contract) rather than a preference.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — one dialer/all-in-one dialogue.

### [SWITH:COMPLEX] Add complexity to break overconfidence
**Move:** Deliberately reintroduce nuance — if you optimise for X you should do this, but if your goal is Y you actually need that — restoring the buyer's sense of choice rather than simplifying toward your answer.
**APPLY WHEN:** the buyer holds a confident, under-examined position that simplification would only harden.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer is already overwhelmed or the decision is genuinely simple; the author flags this as counter-intuitive, since simplicity is normally the gift.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative

### [SWITH:CALM] Hold a calm posture so objections surface
**Move:** Regulate your own tone and pace so the buyer volunteers concerns; treat a raised objection as something to bring into daylight, not a threat to eliminate quickly.
**APPLY WHEN:** always, but especially late-cycle when the seller's own deal anxiety is highest.
**AVOID WHEN:** calm becomes passivity — the author still expects the question sequence to be run.

### [SWITH:PRICEPRE] Pre-empt the pricing question

### [SWITH:PRICEWHY] Explain the model's logic, then the numbers

**AVOID WHEN:** the rationale is not actually true for your model — the move depends on a defensible reason.

### [SWITH:PRICEPAUSE] Pause and ask how the number lands
**Move:** After giving the number, stop and invite reaction — how does that compare to expectations — then work the gap directly: is that a budget already set aside or something else; is what you have seen interesting enough to keep exploring despite the gap; if you wanted to make this work, what would have to change, and is the problem a big enough priority to work through it.
**APPLY WHEN:** any price reveal, critically when expectation and reality diverge (a $100K expectation versus 500 users at $300K).
**AVOID WHEN:** you cannot tolerate silence — changing the subject while they are still processing the number buries everything you say next.

**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — framed via frustration = reality − expectations.

### [SWITH:PRICEHIGH] Label yourself the expensive option
**Move:** Name the bargain competitor's price out loud, claim the premium position deliberately, then enumerate the costs buyers routinely ignore — time, energy, training, and the opportunity cost of the problem staying unsolved — until cheap starts to look expensive.
**APPLY WHEN:** your category contains an undercutting competitor that sacrifices functionality or UX.
**AVOID WHEN:** you have no substantiated hidden-cost story, or the buyer's constraint is a hard budget ceiling rather than a comparison.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative

### [SWITH:FREETEST] Isolate price with the free hypothetical
**Move:** Ask whether they would move forward if the product were free — and if so, when; if not, why not — to test whether the real blocker is budget or unresolved fit and preference.
**APPLY WHEN:** before any discounting or negotiation, when preferred-vendor status is unconfirmed.
**AVOID WHEN:** the answer would be used to skip the fit work; price is always read in the context of value and trust.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the closing phrase of the five, reinforced by the author's $1,000/hour lawyer anecdote.

### [SWITH:PRICESOLO] Never run pricing as a group call
**Move:** Split scope (needs-driven, a group topic) from price (budget-driven, a small-group topic), and take pricing to the champion plus the economic decider with contract options prepared from both the group's needs and the decider's spend constraint.
**APPLY WHEN:** approaching commercial discussions in a committee deal.
**AVOID WHEN:** procurement mandates a joint commercial session.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the rationale is that people perform their expected role in group settings and will not say what they think.

### [SWITH:PROVIDEROFCHOICE] Do not negotiate until you are the provider of choice
**Move:** Before opening commercial terms, get explicit confirmation that the committee has stopped evaluating alternatives, that stakeholders such as IT and security have signed off, and that a specific scope of work reflects everyone's input; absent that you are bidding, and bidding goes to the lowest price.
**APPLY WHEN:** late-stage deals moving toward pricing and procurement.
**AVOID WHEN:** the buyer's process mandates competitive bidding (formal RFP, public procurement) — the gate cannot be won, only navigated.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a stage rule, with the author's cautionary case of a Fortune 20 MSA that pushed a September close to late February.

### [SWITH:HANDOFF] Close by auditing the handoff, not the signature
**Move:** End the sales process with three checks: does customer success know this account as well as you do, are the buyer's concerns and unique needs documented, and will the handoff preserve the momentum you built.
**APPLY WHEN:** the final weeks before and after signature on any deal with a delivery or onboarding phase.
**AVOID WHEN:** attempted unilaterally — it requires input from both sales and success leadership, and fails without it.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** assertion — a checklist the author attributes to the best sellers, no data.

## Stage K — Urgency, momentum and pipeline judgment

### [SWITH:LIMITERS] Remove the seller's own limiters
**Move:** Accept that no deal is guaranteed and that the only bankable asset is your process; adopt the working definition of sales as generously leaving others better off without a promised payoff, and prioritise the relationship over the revenue.
**APPLY WHEN:** quarter-end pressure is pushing you to lean on buyers.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to rationalise passivity or an unworked pipeline.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=any
**Evidence:** assertion — a values position carried by the governor/jet-fuel analogy.

### [SWITH:SEASONS] Segment pipeline by seasons of pain
**Move:** Classify each deal as Spring (problem barely acknowledged), Summer (desire to fix is heating up) or Fall (time is running out), compare three same-stage deals side by side, and check whether close dates match their season — Fall buyers can run a year's cycle in three months and are lost by under-pacing, over-qualifying or pitching too hard; Summer buyers sit mid-curve, where a quarter-end offer may force their hand but too early breeds resentment; Spring buyers may need twice the normal cycle and help owning a reality they have been avoiding.
**APPLY WHEN:** forecasting and close-date setting, especially when CRM stage makes dissimilar deals look alike.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to excuse indefinite slippage instead of driving the Spring-buyer diagnostic work.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's pipeline segmentation heuristic, no dataset.

### [SWITH:CONFFLIP] Ask what undermines the buyer's confidence
**Move:** Instead of coaching yourself to project confidence, invert it into a diagnostic — what is undermining your buyer's confidence — and be willing to say openly that there may be no fit, which the author claims raises rather than lowers buyer confidence.
**APPLY WHEN:** stalled mid- or late-stage deals where the buying team hesitates without a stated reason.
**AVOID WHEN:** voiced as a bluff or a takeaway close — the mechanism depends on it being genuine.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise/consultative

### [SWITH:COVERAGE] Count opportunities, date deals at the latest likely close
**Move:** Measure pipeline coverage by number of opportunities rather than contract value alone, set each new deal's close date to the latest likely date, and pull it forward only on observed buyer behaviour.
**APPLY WHEN:** building the pipeline posture that makes non-neediness possible.
**AVOID WHEN:** your comp or forecast process penalises conservative dating — organisational friction the book does not address.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=mid/high · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=product/saas/service · market=enterprise

### [SWITH:MOMENTUM] Score and compare deal momentum
**Move:** For each opportunity, multiply the seniority points of engaged contacts (Executive 5, VP 4, Director 3, Manager 2, Analyst 1) by the number of *buyer* interactions by the stage number, divide by weeks elapsed, and compare same-stage deals; adjust the mass model where function matters more than level.
**APPLY WHEN:** pipeline review and forecast, particularly to distinguish two deals that look identical in CRM.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as a real metric — the author says it only becomes a formula if RevOps automates it; otherwise it is a thinking tool.

### [SWITH:CHANGEQ] Audit a deal against D × V × FS > R

**APPLY WHEN:** a logically sound business case is failing to move a committee.
**AVOID WHEN:** used to add more ROI logic — the framework's premise is that buyers set illogical rules and logic alone does not tip them.

### [SWITH:HYPERLINK] Make a first-time decision look like a repeat choice
**Move:** Surface an earlier, similar decision the same person made with a good outcome and connect the new decision to it — ask whether they have tried anything similar before, even manually, and how it went — so the new commitment reads as the next step in an escalating series rather than a novel risk.
**APPLY WHEN:** large perceived-change decisions where novelty is the main resistance.
**AVOID WHEN:** the prior decision's outcome was bad, or the analogy is a stretch the buyer can puncture.

### [SWITH:TRUSTLEDGER] Audit deal history as deposits versus withdrawals
**Move:** Split every email, call and meeting into two columns, weight each by intensity (opening a free testing environment for a technical buyer is a much larger deposit than a prerecorded demo video), apply the doubled denominator for withdrawals, and read the resulting position against the trust hurdle — above it the deal is in a closing position, below it it is not ready.
**APPLY WHEN:** deciding whether to push for a decision, or diagnosing a stall.
**AVOID WHEN:** treated as a precise score — the author concedes it is not a science.

**APPLY WHEN:** any emotionally charged question, especially with skeptics or executives.
**AVOID WHEN:** over-used as verbal padding — it competes directly with the minimum-viable-sentence discipline (see SWITH:MVS).

## Stage L — Managing up and seller craft

### [SWITH:PIPEUPDATE] Send a weekly narrative pipeline update
**Move:** Write a consistent, forwardable weekly update to your manager: QTD closed ARR versus goal; worst/base/best case with the conditions for each; this-quarter discounted pipeline and pacing; next-quarter coverage ratio versus goal and new pipeline built last week; top three priorities; and the dependencies blocking them — scenarios and dependencies rather than a single number, since repeated slippage on called shots is what destroys reputation.
**APPLY WHEN:** any IC seller in a structured sales org; the premise is that if you do not write the narrative someone else will.
**AVOID WHEN:** scenario labels are used to hide a base case you do not believe.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=few/committee · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — a template from the author's leadership practice.

### [SWITH:BADNEWS] Surface bad news early
**Move:** Pass bad news to your manager immediately rather than shielding them, leaving them the choice to intervene, get ahead of it with others, or sit on it.
**APPLY WHEN:** any deal slip, risk or account problem.
**AVOID WHEN:** not contraindicated; the named failure is early-career reps deciding for their manager.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** assertion — presented as a common costly mistake, no data.

### [SWITH:ONEONONE] Reclaim 1:1s for high-leverage asks
**Move:** With routine reporting handled in writing, spend 1:1 time on new project ideas, resource requests and key-account strategy; track commitments in a shared document and, when an important one from your manager has slipped, propose doing it together in five minutes right then.
**APPLY WHEN:** once the weekly written update is established.
**AVOID WHEN:** the written cadence is not yet running — the 1:1 will simply fill back up with status.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise/consultative
**Evidence:** practitioner-n-many — the author's own management practice.

### [SWITH:ASKSPLIT] Split a big ask into escalating small asks
**Move:** Break a large request into a sequence spread over months — permission to spend 20% of time on larger accounts, then product investment to land the first enterprise logo, then dedicated enterprise CSMs — and deliver each in the format the specific manager absorbs (a long in-person meeting with small talk for some, a punchy ten-minute call for others).
**APPLY WHEN:** requests that would be refused as a single large commitment.
**AVOID WHEN:** urgency requires a single decision now.
**Deal-gate:** motion=any · acv=any · stakeholders=any · offer=any · market=enterprise
**Evidence:** practitioner-anecdote — the author's own six-to-nine-month sequence with a past CEO.

### [SWITH:TWOAHEAD] Anticipate and deliver two steps ahead
