# advisor-selling-with — myths and demotions

What this tradition debunks, what must be demoted *inside* it, and which numbers must never leave this lens as facts.

---

## 1. Myths the tradition attacks (import these; they are the lens's cutting edge)

- **"Sellers close deals."** Buyers close; the seller enables. A motivated buyer can close without you; you cannot close without them. Everything in [SWITH:CHAMPBRIEF] and [SWITH:WRITEMEMO] follows from this inversion.
- **"A profile-perfect contact is a champion."** Not until influence converts into forward progress between meetings, and not before discounting for internal believability — [SWITH:CHAMP3I], [SWITH:BELIEVE].
- **"Ghosting means disinterest."** Usually it is an unpaid WIIFM or de-prioritisation of someone who is not helping them do their job — [SWITH:TWOLEVEL], [SWITH:TRIANGULATE]. Buyers do not forget to follow up.
- **"Get to the decision-maker."** Everyone who can say no is a decision-maker, even where one economic buyer signs. And more stakeholders is not more buy-in: it adds people who can say no but not yes, against an enterprise default of doing nothing — [SWITH:MVCOMMITTEE].
- **"A smooth deal is a good deal."** Frictionlessness usually means the champion is absorbing dissent alone — [SWITH:INVITEDISSENT], [SWITH:CONSENSUSILLUSION].
- **"Champions cost only your time."** They spend social capital at every step whether or not the deal closes; each step must return something to them — [SWITH:CHAMPGLORY].
- **"The business case is a late-stage rescue artifact."** Built late, or built by value engineers to save a stalling deal, it fails; it is supposed to *progress* deals to late stage — [SWITH:BCEARLY]. Digital sales rooms stacked with marketing collateral are named as sales enablement in buyer-enablement clothing.
- **"Branding helps materials travel."** Heavily branded material reads as a billboard and rarely gets forwarded — [SWITH:CAMO].
- **"Battlecards win deals."** Every use pulls you into a feature war a loud committee member can redirect — [SWITH:VILLAIN] is offered as the replacement, with its own hazards (see §3).
- **"Templates are efficient / blank pages are relevant."** Templates buy speed at the cost of relevance; blank pages buy relevance at unaffordable cost — hence a per-role framework in between, [SWITH:PROBSTATE].
- **"The approach is: buy our software."** That skips the project-risk conversation; project risk kills more deals than product risk — [SWITH:APPROACHFIRST].
- **"Preparing more material makes exec meetings safer."** Executive meetings go off-agenda by default; over-prepared content leaves no room to adapt — [SWITH:25RULE]. The deadliest in-room sin is not answering the question asked, and a general next step ("we'll need security sign-off") is not a next step — [SWITH:ANSWERQ], [SWITH:NAMEDNEXT].
- **"Winning the exec is enough."** Without the whisperer's nod the deal is uphill — [SWITH:WHISPERER].
- **"Sending links adds value."** Link stacks push work onto the buyer and read as two minutes of effort; automated sequences, check-ins and "Thoughts?" bumps are named as what to abandon as contract values rise — [SWITH:NOLINKSTACK], [SWITH:NEWINFO].
- **"A MAP is a close plan."** Stopping at signature signals "here's what I need to get paid"; and a task list means nothing to a CTO without the memo — [SWITH:MAPGRID], [SWITH:MAPWHYFIRST], [SWITH:MAPADAPT].
- **"More product access helps buyers decide."** It amplifies perceived change and therefore fear; most POCs are overengineered pilots, and running the same POC for every account ignores that perceived risk differs — [SWITH:WHYNOTCHANGED], [SWITH:POCSHRINK].
- **"Objections are risks to remove quickly."** Fast rebuttals raise resistance and teach buyers to hide doubts; silence about objections means they have moved into the internal conversations you are not in — [SWITH:NOHANDLING].
- **"Urgency can be manufactured."** External incentives fit only a buyer already at the decision point; applied to a committee, pressure disperses and damages. Quarter-end discounts to a mid-curve buyer breed resentment — [SWITH:BACKDATE], [SWITH:SEASONS].
- **"Rational ROI cases win."** Blocked good-ROI deals are usually blocked by drama, not math — [SWITH:MATHDRAMA], [SWITH:CHANGEQ].
- **"Acronym frameworks are discovery."** Used as checklists they produce qualification masquerading as discovery; questions tied back to your own product create the illusion of buying interest — [SWITH:CURIOUSBUYER].
- **"Long sentences show listening."** They bury the question and hand the buyer tangents — [SWITH:MVS].
- **"Target new hires; veterans are captured by corporate culture."** Long-tenured employees carry backchannel routes and informal clout — [SWITH:SHADOWORG].
- **"Sales orgs are meritocracies."** Part meritocracy, part bureaucracy: career decisions happen when you are not in the room — [SWITH:PIPEUPDATE].
- **"Sellers are talkers, marketers are writers."** A false dichotomy: writing is what enters the rooms you cannot.

## 2. Demotions inside the tradition — where the technique documentedly fails

**The hard gate: no committee, no method.** Champion enablement, the internal brief, multithreading, the whisperer, the consensus illusion, the shadow org chart and per-contact staging all assume several people must agree and that the deciding conversations happen without you. With a single decision-maker the champion construct is inert and the residue is good discovery plus clear writing. [SWITH:CHAMP3I], [SWITH:TRICONTACT] and [SWITH:CHAMPSKEPTIC] say so in their own AVOID lines. This gate is checked *before* any move from this lens is offered.

**ACV and cycle length.** The artifact stack costs real hours — a business case from call one, per-role problem statements, a communication-flow map, a MAP running through adoption, a hardwired POC. On low-ACV, short-cycle or high-velocity motions it is overhead the deal cannot repay; [SWITH:BCEARLY], [SWITH:OCEANMAP] and [SWITH:HANDSHAKE] each name the small-deal case as the point where the exercise becomes theatre. The source never locates the mid-market boundary.

**Mandated-process deals.** Formal RFPs, public procurement and regulated bidding break [SWITH:PROVIDEROFCHOICE] (you cannot exit the bid), [SWITH:CAMO] (corporate identity is required), [SWITH:PRICESOLO] (joint commercial sessions are mandated) and [SWITH:APPROACHFIRST] (requirements are fixed before you arrive).

**Security- and policy-locked accounts.** Shared external documents, channel-shifting to Slack Connect or text, recording calls for language mining, and gifts all die against strict policy — [SWITH:BUILDLOOP], [SWITH:CHAMPTEST], [SWITH:COORDLANG], [SWITH:WEIRDTEST].

**Product-led, self-serve and transactional motions.** The tradition has nothing for a buyer who evaluates in-product without ever convening a group, and does not address inbound-heavy velocity selling except to dismiss volume-driven pipeline as increasingly expensive.

**No writing latitude.** Where all customer-facing content must be pre-approved by marketing or legal, the Build Loop, the wrong-draft trick, the forwardable and the camouflaged one-pager are simply unavailable.

**Organisational friction the source waves at.** [SWITH:STAGESYNC] (moving deals backward), [SWITH:COVERAGE] (dating deals late) and [SWITH:STAGEEXIT] (behaviour-based stages) collide with how most sales organisations forecast and compensate. The author acknowledges the fight and tells the rep to absorb it personally — which is an answer for one rep, not for a team.

**Selection effect on champions.** The system presumes a competent, motivated, believable insider exists and can be found. Accounts where no such person exists get "no champion, no deal" — an exit, not a strategy for manufacturing one.

**Competition is nearly absent.** Displacement gets one move ([SWITH:BLAMEPROJECT]) plus villain-naming. A well-resourced rival running the same champion playbook inside the same account is not modelled at all.

**Non-US, non-software contexts.** Examples are almost entirely US B2B SaaS. Hierarchical, consensus-mandated or relationship-first buying cultures, channel and partner-led motions, and hardware/services cost structures are unaddressed.

**Post-sale reality.** Signature is called the customer's starting line, then retention, expansion and champion departure after go-live get a handoff checklist and little else — despite the source's own observation that champions and executives leave mid-cycle.

## 3. Anti-recommendations — moves to withhold, downgrade, or hedge, with the reason

- **The ghostwritten CEO email.** The variant of [SWITH:FUNPR] where a real executive's signature is copied and a forwarded timestamp faked is impersonation-adjacent, breaks trust if forwarded onward, and can violate buyer policy. **Never recommend.** The pre-written press release on your own domain is the safe half.
- **Villain-naming aimed at a person.** [SWITH:VILLAIN] against an incumbent your buyer personally chose, or against a colleague, forces the champion to defend a personal attack. The chapter itself concedes the Domino's framing suppressed genuine counter-benefits — i.e. the technique works partly by hiding true information. Offer only against an impersonal obstacle, and say so.
- **Drama-labelling without standing.** [SWITH:MATHDRAMA] means naming what a person is protecting — their sense of being needed, their job security — and then supplying them a replacement story. That is intervening in someone's self-narrative. Without a real relationship it lands as accusation; with one, it still needs the ethical test: would you say this in front of the person being labelled?
- **The back-pocket exclusive framed as rule-breaking.** [SWITH:EXCLUSIVE] is fine as a genuine pre-arranged concession; the "not technically supposed to do this" framing is a manufactured intimacy and an integrity risk if literally true.
- **The wrong-draft trick on the wrong reader.** [SWITH:WRITEMEMO]'s deliberately imperfect draft reads as incompetence to some buyers and is inappropriate on anything formal.
- **The 1–10 fit question at random.** [SWITH:DIPSCORE] depends entirely on being placed just before a known funnel drop. Without stage data it is pressure dressed as curiosity.
- **Loss-framing everything.** [SWITH:FRAMESWITCH] and [SWITH:LOSSFORMULA] used habitually read as manipulation, and the source's own rule is that personal-level consequences must be reached by the buyer, never stated to them.
- **The exclusivity of the champion relay as a substitute for coverage.** Routing everything through one champion ([SWITH:CHAMPSKEPTIC], [SWITH:SPOKESPERSON]) is how you end up with a single point of failure the moment that person leaves — a risk the source names and does not solve.

**Manipulation boundary, stated plainly:** this tradition supplies no test for when influence over a buyer's self-narrative has become deception. It places sincere cautions next to identity moves, drama-labelling and a faked executive email, and credits a therapist for one of the frames. Any move from this lens that the user would not be willing to show the buyer is not offered.

## 4. Numbers that must not be quoted as fact

Everything in this list appears in the source as a figure. None of it is a finding. Cite only with its provenance attached, or not at all.

- **2.6× close-rate lift from buying confidence** — analyst research (Blum & Omale), correlational, not peer-reviewed, not reproduced in the book.
- **13% versus 47% close rate depending on whether a second meeting was booked at the demo** — one of the author's past teams, self-reported, no controls; booking is at least as plausibly a symptom of intent as a cause of closing.
- **Purchase likelihood by stakeholder count (1 → 81%, 2 → 55%, 3 → 60%, 4 → 60%, 5 → 53%, 6 → 31%)** — attributed to Challenger, Inc.; non-monotonic, data stops at six, no methodology given.
- **5–10% of pipeline deals had a business case before engaging, across 190 sales teams in 12 months** — vendor-collected and self-reported by the author's own company, which sells the remedy.
- **An executive minute is worth 16× a non-executive minute** — self-constructed by the author from a Pareto 80:20 split (4 ÷ 0.25). Arithmetic, not measurement.
- **~90% chance an executive already has a prepared line of questioning** — stated by Kellogg, unsourced.
- **Ogilvy: ~5× more people read the headline than the body; 80 cents of the dollar goes into it** — quoted without a source study.
- **POCs strip ~80% of product scope; ~20% of trial activity dispels ~80% of doubts** — explicitly the author's estimate.
- **Carta's influence survey: 9 individual contributors reaching 70% of staff, rising only to 85% with the 9 most influential managers and executives; CEO absent from the top 10** — a single company, self-reported, not peer-reviewed. Do not generalise to other org shapes.
- **63% of sellers struggle with mental health, ~3× the general workforce rate** — attributed to Risely / Sales Health Alliance 2022; a survey, methodology unexamined here.
- **UCLA LEGO experiment (2-person team 36 minutes versus 4-person 56 minutes)** — unnamed study, cited through a secondary source. Unverifiable as printed.
- **Chocolate-versus-fruit choice bracketing (~70% either way depending on timing)** — cited only as "(Read)". Unverifiable as printed.
- **The 15% lifetime-value dip inside the sample follow-up email** — the source states it is illustrative fiction inside a template. It is not data and has been mistaken for data before.
- **Trust-hurdle heights (~9/10 for a $1M / 100-training-hour investment versus ~3/10 for $10K / 5 hours), the doubled weighting of withdrawals, the −5 to +10 trust swing, believability percentages, momentum scores, the 25% material rule, seasons of pain** — all thinking tools by the author's own framing. Their failure cannot be distinguished from bad execution, which is the definition of unfalsifiable.
- **Illustrative arithmetic** — the conversion example ($2.5M/month lost), the philanthropy figures, the $50,000 + $500/user pricing model, the $100K-versus-$300K expectation gap, the "five months late, $500K over" internal build: worked examples invented to demonstrate a formula. Never repeat them as market benchmarks.

**Rule for this lens:** when a number from the source is used in a verdict, it carries its provenance in the same sentence. Self-reported vendor data presented as evidence is the documented failure mode of this whole genre — the author monetises the method through training and tooling, and the book's supporting numbers are collected by that same business.
