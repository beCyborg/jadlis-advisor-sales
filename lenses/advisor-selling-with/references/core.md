# advisor-selling-with — core

The method of the champion/committee layer, decomposed into components and **ranked by the strength of what holds each one up**, strongest first. The claim being ranked is never "the method works" — it is "this component rests on X-grade support; that one is the author's assertion."

Ladder used: `field-study` → `external-research` → `practitioner-n-many` → `practitioner-anecdote` → `assertion`.
Source ceiling for this lens: **external-research**. Nothing here reaches `field-study`; there is no controlled comparison anywhere in the tradition, and no counterfactual for a good seller who skips the artifact stack.

---

## Tier 0 — field-study

**Empty.** No component of this method has been tested against a control group. Every number in the book is either the author's own team data, a client-collected sample, an analyst report, or an illustration. Treat that emptiness as the single most important fact about this lens.

---

## Tier 1 — external-research (weakest form of it: named outside sources, none replicated in-book)

**1. Change requires dissatisfaction × vision × first steps, exceeding resistance.** The deal-diagnostic backbone [SWITH:CHANGEQ]: when a logically sound business case fails to move a committee, one of the three multiplicands is at zero, and adding more ROI logic cannot fix a zero. The equation itself comes from Dannemiller & Jacobs, *Journal of Applied Behavioral Science* 28(4), 1992 — genuinely external and peer-reviewed. Its transfer into B2B deals is the author's, untested. So: the structure is borrowed and sound; the sales application is assertion wearing a citation.

**2. Buying confidence, not information supply, is the closing variable.** The whole emotional-design layer [SWITH:EMOTIONPLAN], [SWITH:DIPSCORE], [SWITH:CONFFLIP] rests on a 2.6× close-rate lift associated with buyer confidence, cited to Blum & Omale. That is analyst research — not peer-reviewed, correlational, and not reproduced in the book. It supports "confidence matters", not "these specific moves produce it".

**3. Complexity, not simplification, unsettles a confident wrong position.** [SWITH:COMPLEX] leans on Adam Grant's *Think Again*. A named popular-science source summarising real work, applied to sales without test.

**4. Executives arrive with a prepared line of questioning and want the answer first.** [SWITH:ANSWERQ] is attributed to Dave Kellogg, a career CEO/CMO speaking from his own executive seat. Labelled external-research in the digest because the source is outside the author, but it is practitioner testimony, not a study — the accompanying ~90% figure is stated and unsourced.

> Everything above is the ceiling. Anything in this lens claiming to be stronger than this tier is an error.

## Tier 2 — practitioner-n-many (the load-bearing mass of the method: the author's repeated practice across his own teams and client teams)

**5. In a committee deal the deciding conversations happen without the seller.** This is the lens's founding premise and everything else is downstream of it. Operationally: your product is a narrative someone else must be able to carry [SWITH:WRITEMEMO], [SWITH:REPEATPROB], [SWITH:ONETRUTH]; your skill is writing plus champion enablement, not pitching. Support: the author's practice and roughly a dozen named buyer interviews. Strong as a design premise, unmeasured as a performance claim.

**6. A champion is a behaviour, not a profile.** Incentive + Influence + Information *simultaneously*, plus forward progress between meetings [SWITH:CHAMP3I]. Influence + Information without stake = influencer; Incentive + Information without power = coach. This is the sharpest operating definition in the tradition and the one most worth importing. It is a definition, not a finding — no dataset supports the tri-part cut over any other cut.

**7. Advocacy must be discounted by internal believability.** [SWITH:BELIEVE] — a contact's influence is worth what their colleagues think it is worth, so treat believability as a multiplier on everything they say internally. Borrowed analogically from Bridgewater's believability weighting. The mechanism is plausible; the percentages are invented by the seller and unfalsifiable.

**8. Coverage across three contact classes is a pass/fail gate.** Daily users, key stakeholders (security, legal, IT, procurement), budget holders — two of three counts as a loss [SWITH:TRICONTACT]. A rule of thumb with worked failure combinations. No data behind the specific threshold.

**9. The one-page business case is the sales process, not a late artifact.** Start it after call one, score its maturity by stage, treat every gap as a live discovery gap [SWITH:BCEARLY], [SWITH:ONEPAGER]; run the demo agenda, the executive pre-read, the negotiation anchor and the forwardable object off the same page [SWITH:BCJTBD]. The 190-teams / 12-months figure behind it is vendor-collected and self-reported by the author's own company — usable as a prevalence observation, not as an outcome result.

**10. Project risk precedes product risk.** Agreement on a vendor-agnostic *approach* removes the risk that nothing gets prioritised at all, and only then does product evaluation matter [SWITH:APPROACHFIRST], [SWITH:THESISBRIDGE]. The author names this as the most-skipped part of his own framework — an honest admission that also means it is the least observed in practice.

**11. Co-creation transfers ownership; delivered polish does not.** The Build Loop [SWITH:BUILDLOOP], [SWITH:SOCIALIZE], [SWITH:IKEA], with three momentum signals as the read-out [SWITH:MOMENTUMCHECK]. Repeated practice across the author's deals. The underlying effort-justification claim is named, not evidenced (see Tier 4).

**12. Writing is the transport layer between meetings.** Forwardables [SWITH:FORWARDABLE], five-part recaps instead of link stacks [SWITH:NOLINKSTACK], camouflaged artifacts [SWITH:CAMO], the buyer's own vocabulary [SWITH:COORDLANG]. This is where the tradition is most distinctive and most operational. Support is the author's teaching practice plus single buyer interviews per claim.

**13. Objections are unpacked, never handled.** Understand → unpack → respond [SWITH:UUR], with widening probes [SWITH:UNPACKQ] and inversion [SWITH:INVERT] when the thread dead-ends; the number of objections you hear is read as a trust measure [SWITH:NOHANDLING]. Repeated framework, no measurement; the resistance mechanism is asserted.

**14. Price is a small-group topic; scope is a group topic.** [SWITH:PRICESOLO], and no commercial conversation at all before provider-of-choice is confirmed [SWITH:PROVIDEROFCHOICE]. Stage rules from the author's practice, with one cautionary case where the rule was ignored.

**15. Urgency must originate inside the buyer's own timeline.** Backdate from a real internal event [SWITH:BACKDATE]; manufactured urgency is explicitly ruled out. Consistent across the author's deals and internally coherent with the rest of the method.

**16. POCs test one perceived risk, not feature coverage.** Build the hypothesis from "why haven't you already changed?" [SWITH:WHYNOTCHANGED], fix X and Y before kickoff [SWITH:GONOGO], let the buyer own the success definition and then stress-test it [SWITH:XOWNERSHIP], shrink scope aggressively [SWITH:POCSHRINK]. The 20%/80% split behind it is stated as the author's estimate.

**17. Stages should be defined by buyer behaviour, and staged per contact.** [SWITH:STAGEEXIT], [SWITH:STAGESYNC]. Sound in principle; the "three buyers who read and agreed" threshold is a working rule, not a measured one, and the author concedes the org-level fight this creates.

**18. The committee should be minimally viable.** Growth of the circle signals falling decision confidence, not progress [SWITH:MVCOMMITTEE]. Supported by cited stakeholder/close-rate figures attributed to Challenger, Inc. — see myths-and-demotions for why that series must not be quoted as a clean finding.

## Tier 3 — practitioner-anecdote (single deals, single interviews, single teams)

**19. Co-authoring the champion's own internal document is the highest-leverage act available.** [SWITH:CHAMPBRIEF] rests on the book's opening story: one six-figure deal closed in a seven-minute call because the CPO had already read the co-written memo. One deal. Compelling as an existence proof, worthless as a base rate.

**20. Branded material does not get forwarded.** [SWITH:CAMO] — one buyer interview, a champion owning a $10M budget at a Fortune 100 company. A single informant's habit, generalised into a design rule.

**21. Buyers test trust with questions they already know the answer to.** [SWITH:THREETS] — one named-role buyer interview. Plausible, unquantified.

**22. Pre-reads attached to the invite, meeting starting at :05.** [SWITH:PREREAD] — taught to the author by one executive assistant during one deal.

**23. Hardwiring go/no-go into the MSA survives post-POC indecision.** [SWITH:HARDWIRE] — adopted after one rep on the author's team pushed for it against the author's own expectation. The honest reporting of that disagreement is a point in its favour; it is still n=1.

**24. Booking the second meeting during the demo predicts close.** [SWITH:DIPSCORE] cites 13% versus 47% on one of the author's past teams, self-reported. Direction plausible, magnitude uninterpretable, causality unestablished — booking a second meeting is at least as likely to be a symptom of interest as a cause of it.

**25. Stuck good-ROI deals are blocked by drama, not math.** [SWITH:MATHDRAMA] — one detailed retailer case, with the frame credited to the author's therapist. Powerful diagnostic, highest manipulation exposure in the lens (see myths-and-demotions).

**26. A structured survey can make committee disagreement visible.** [SWITH:SURVEY] — the philanthropy case, one engagement, later productised as a lead magnet. Note the commercial incentive attached to the story.

## Tier 4 — assertion (named cognitive effects and arithmetic, offered without evidence for the sales application)

These are the components most likely to be mistaken for science because they carry the names of real phenomena. In this lens they are **rhetorical scaffolding, not support**:

- **Coordination cost grows with contacts, not contract value** [SWITH:HANDSHAKE] — n(n−1)/2 is definitional arithmetic; the LEGO-build experiment behind it is attributed only to unnamed UCLA professors via a secondary source.
- **The Law of Large Numbers filter on executive attention** [SWITH:LARGENUM] — argued with arithmetic examples only.
- **Executives are allergic to failure-by-action, so titles confer safety** [SWITH:SENIORFIRST], [SWITH:FIREPRIORITY] — reasoned, with book passages as support, not data.
- **Effort justification / IKEA effect makes co-built cases stick** [SWITH:IKEA] — the effect is real in its own literature; its transfer to buying committees is asserted.
- **Attentional bias and the frequency illusion make repetition work** [SWITH:REPEATPROB] — named, uncited.
- **False consensus and the transparency illusion explain the champion's over-read of alignment** [SWITH:CONSENSUSILLUSION] — named, uncited.
- **Escalation of commitment and the ostrich effect govern displacement** [SWITH:BLAMEPROJECT] — named; the example figures are illustrative.
- **Identity-based decision-making licenses villain-naming** [SWITH:VILLAIN] — cites the Heaths' *Switch* plus brand examples; no sales data, and the chapter itself concedes the framing suppressed real counter-arguments.
- **Framing, renaming and pyramid structure improve reception** [SWITH:FRAMESWITCH], [SWITH:RENAME], [SWITH:MINTO], [SWITH:HEADLINE] — craft conventions with brand anecdotes and an unsourced Ogilvy claim.
- **Multithreading is mirrored by the buyer** [SWITH:MIRROR] — stated as a pattern, no data.
- **Handoff quality determines post-sale momentum** [SWITH:HANDOFF] — a checklist attributed to "the best sellers".
- **Non-neediness is a values position** [SWITH:LIMITERS], [SWITH:CONFFLIP] — explicitly ethical rather than empirical, and healthier for being labelled as such.

## Structural consequences of this ranking

1. **The strongest thing in the lens is a definition, not a result.** [SWITH:CHAMP3I] and [SWITH:TRICONTACT] earn their place by sharpening what counts as a champion and as coverage — a vocabulary gain, which survives even if every effect claim in the book is wrong.
2. **The cognitive-science layer adds no evidential weight.** Strip every named bias from the book and the mechanics are unchanged; they were never resting on the biases.
3. **The artifact stack is unpriced.** Nothing in the source states how many accounts one seller can run this way, so cost-of-method is a live risk on every recommendation this lens makes — flag it whenever the territory is large.
4. **Applicability is gated before quality.** With a single decision-maker the whole apparatus is inert, and what remains is ordinary discovery plus clear writing. That gate outranks every tier above it.
